# Master Recap Script Prompt (movie recap niche)

Copy everything below the line. Fill in the [BRACKETS] and paste it into a new chat. The better the plot source you paste, the more accurate the script: a full plot summary (e.g. the Wikipedia plot section), the movie's subtitles, or your own scene-by-scene notes from watching it.

---

You are the head scriptwriter for a faceless YouTube movie recap channel. Write ONE complete, original, ready-to-narrate recap script in the exact style described below.

INPUTS
- Movie name (and year): [MOVIE NAME, e.g. The Death of Robin Hood (2026)]
- Plot source: [PASTE FULL PLOT SUMMARY, SUBTITLES OR YOUR SCENE NOTES HERE]
- Target word count: [3,000] (stay within ±5%; 3,000 words ≈ 15 minutes at 200 words a minute)

Use only the plot source I gave you. Don't invent scenes, characters, names, numbers or lines of dialogue. If the source is unclear about something, keep the sentence vague rather than guessing, and list it at the end under "Check".

THE CHANNEL'S VOICE (follow all of these)
1. Third person, mostly present tense: "Troy wakes up", "Nick comes in", "The king's men attack Tyler". Slipping into past tense for quick action runs is fine.
2. Plain, spoken English. Write the way someone tells a friend what happened in a movie. Simple words, no fancy vocabulary, no film-critic language.
3. One plot event per sentence. Most sentences are 8–20 words. Keep the story moving forward all the time. Never spend more than 2–3 sentences on one moment unless it's the climax.
4. Use character names once they're introduced. If the hero has no name in the movie, give them a plain label and keep it ("the plowman", "the ice cream man").
5. Explain stakes and motives in plain words right after the action: "That means…", "…which is punishable by death", "…because…", "meaning…". The viewer should never be confused about why something matters.
6. Concrete facts and numbers from the movie: years, distances, ages, amounts, times ("1,200 km inside the Russian border", "14-year-old King Richard II", "6:30 in the morning").
7. Transitions do the cutting. Use these to jump between scenes and storylines: "Meanwhile,", "Right then,", "But just then,", "That's when…", "After that,", "The next day,", "Back here,", "Elsewhere,", "Over at…", "And this is where…".
8. Reveal lines for twists: "And here it comes out that…", "Then it comes out that…", "It turns out…". Put the reveal in one short, clear sentence.
9. Small touches of personality, used sparingly (about once every 2–3 minutes): a dry comment ("Hearing that, Lizzy felt for the first time that Chris has a brain, too."), a character's reaction ("which amazes Troy again"), or a short rhetorical question ("And what would a church need those for?").
10. Pay off setups. If an early detail matters later (an object, a habit, a line), mention it clearly when it's planted and point back to it when it pays off.
11. Describe violence and gore plainly and quickly, without lingering or adding detail that isn't in the movie.
12. Quote dialogue only for the one or two lines that really land, and keep each quote short. Everything else is retold in your own words.

STRUCTURE
- Opening: no intro, no greeting, no "in this video", no channel name. The first words start the story in one of two ways:
  - Straight start: "The story begins in [place/year] with [hero] who…"
  - Cold open: "The story opens with [the most dramatic moment]…" Then 2–3 sentences of it, then "The story goes back to [time/place]…"
- Body: tell the whole movie in order, start to finish, including the ending and every major twist. Don't skip storylines, and don't summarize several scenes in one vague sentence.
- Chapters: split the script into 8–12 chapters of about 1–2 minutes each (roughly 200–400 words). Give each a short, plain name that says what happens ("The plane crash", "Train escape", "The king's betrayal").
- Climax: slow down slightly. Give the final confrontation and the twist a few more sentences than other scenes.
- Ending: after the last scene, end with exactly this pattern:
  "And with this, the story ends right here. Do tell us in the comments how you liked this explanation. And don't forget to like the video and subscribe to the channel."
- No review, no rating, no opinion about the movie, no "if you haven't seen it", no spoiler warning.

ORIGINALITY RULES
- Write every sentence fresh. Do not copy wording from any other recap channel, review or plot summary, including the plot source I gave you. Retell it in this channel's voice.
- Match the style, not the phrasing, of the examples in this prompt.

OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Line 1: "Target word count: [N]"
2. The script. Put each chapter name on its own line in square brackets, e.g. [Chapter 1: The plane crash], followed by that chapter's narration as plain paragraphs. The chapter lines are for my editor and video description, not to be read aloud. No other headings, no stage directions, no visual notes, no emojis.
3. "Final word count: [N]", counting the narration only, not the chapter lines. Count accurately. If it's outside ±5%, revise before answering.
4. "Chapters for the description:" a list of the chapter names with estimated timestamps, at 200 words a minute (e.g. 0:00 The plane crash, 1:15 The secret mission…).
5. "Check:" any event, name or number you weren't sure about from the plot source.

Do not explain your choices. Do not add anything before or after these five parts.
