# THE NIGHT CLEANER
## "She Mopped His Floors. She Owned the Building."

*An original Nigerian romance drama in 32 scenes. About 40 minutes. All dialogue, no narrator. The only unspoken text is short visual notes for the AI scenes.*

*Built on The 1K Plan. Lane: hidden identity, love and marriage. Cold open on the conflict. Title and writer credit go on screen over the first scene. Every scene runs **Establish → Scene → Aftermath → Fade**.*

---

## THE CONTRADICTION (one line)

A billionaire's heiress works as a night cleaner in her own tower to catch the person robbing her late father. The only man who treats her like a human being gets forced into an engagement with the thief's daughter.

## CAST (with locked looks for AI consistency)

Lock each face with one reference image and reuse it in every shot.

- **AMARA "ADA" ELUEMUNOR** (27). The heiress to Crown Eluemunor Holdings. She was sent to school in London at 8 and hasn't been home in nineteen years. *Look:* dark brown skin, oval face, short natural hair under a navy head-tie, small gold stud earrings. *As cleaner:* navy-and-grey Brightline uniform, ID badge "ADA NWOSU — B2". *As chairman:* a cream skirt suit. She always wears a thin gold necklace with a small crown pendant, hidden under her collar until the climax.
- **TOBI ADEYEMI** (30). A junior accountant on the night shift. He's gentle, proud and broke. *Look:* tall and slim, low-cut hair, neat beard, thin silver-rimmed glasses. He always wears a white shirt with rolled sleeves and a brown leather watch strap.
- **CHIEF IKENNA ELUEMUNOR** (58). Amara's uncle and the acting chairman. He smiles warmly with cold eyes. *Look:* heavy build, grey goatee, wine-red agbada with gold embroidery, and a gold ring on his little finger.
- **BISOLA ELUEMUNOR** (28). Ikenna's daughter: beautiful, spoiled, cruel to staff. *Look:* long straight wig, red designer dress, red nails, a phone always in hand.
- **MRS. FOLAKE ADEYEMI** (56). Tobi's mother, a proud widow who wants a rich wife for her son. *Look:* green-and-gold ankara wrapper and matching gele, reading glasses on a chain.
- **MAMA NGOZI** (54). Head cleaner, warm and tired. *Look:* round face, grey-streaked hair under a black scarf. She carries a mop with red tape wrapped around the handle.
- **BARRISTER ODUYA** (65). The late Chief's lawyer, and the only one who knows Amara's secret. *Look:* white hair, black suit, wire glasses, a leather briefcase.
- **DAMI ADEYEMI** (19). Tobi's sister, a university student.
- **MR. KUNLE**. Brightline Facilities supervisor, sweaty, with a clipboard.
- **GARBA**. Head of building security, tall and silent.
- **GUESTS, CLEANERS, OFFICERS**.

## RECURRING DETAILS TO KEEP CONSISTENT

- **Two cups of tea at 2:00 a.m., floor 31.** Tobi always makes two. The night he makes one, we know he's breaking her heart. In the final scene, she makes them.
- **"Learn who cleans the floor."** The late Chief's last letter to Amara.
- **The crown necklace.** It belonged to Amara's mother. Bisola calls it "a Crown family piece" when she accuses Ada of stealing it, and she's right.
- **Mama Ngozi's red-taped mop.** It goes in through the back door at the start and through the front door at the end.
- **"Don't sit on the chairs."** The cleaners' rule. In the last scene, Amara sits on the floor.
- **Midnight.** Amara turns 28 at midnight on the night of the engagement party, and at midnight the building becomes hers.

# COLD OPEN

## SCENE 1: "THIEF!"
**EXT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — ROOFTOP ENGAGEMENT PARTY, LAGOS — NIGHT**
*(~0:00–0:50)*

**ESTABLISH:** *A wide aerial shot of the Lagos skyline at night: Third Mainland Bridge glittering over the lagoon, and one gold-lit glass tower above everything else. Slow push in.*

**ON SCREEN (gold text over the shot):** *THE NIGHT CLEANER*
**ON SCREEN (small white text, lower corner):** *Written by [WRITER'S NAME]*

*The rooftop: fairy lights, a live band, a banner reading "TOBI ♥ BISOLA". A glass of red wine has shattered on the floor. AMARA, in a cleaner's uniform, kneels beside the glass. BISOLA towers over her and grips the thin gold chain at Amara's neck.*

**BISOLA:** Everybody look! Look at what the cleaner is wearing! *(she yanks the pendant into the light)* That's a Crown family piece! Where did a cleaner get real gold?

**AMARA:** *(calm)* Let go of my necklace.

**BISOLA:** *Your* necklace? Thief!

*MRS. FOLAKE ADEYEMI pushes through the crowd and SLAPS Amara across the face. The band stops. Two hundred guests go silent.*

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** At my son's engagement? You followed him here?

*TOBI stands frozen between the two women in a white agbada, a ring box in his hand.*

**TOBI:** *(barely)* Mama… stop.

**CHIEF IKENNA:** *(smiling, unbothered)* Garba. Take her downstairs and call the police.

*GARBA grips Amara's arm. She doesn't fight. She looks at Ikenna, then at the huge clock on the rooftop wall: 11:52.*

**AMARA:** Before you call the police, Uncle… check whose name is on this building.

*The crowd bursts into laughter. Bisola laughs loudest. Only Tobi isn't laughing.*

**TOBI:** *(whispering)* Ada… who are you?

**AFTERMATH:** *Amara touches the red mark on her cheek and holds Tobi's gaze. She doesn't answer.*

**TITLE CARD (white on black):** *SIX MONTHS EARLIER*

*FADE.*

# ACT ONE: THE CONTRADICTION

## SCENE 2: THE WILL
**INT. ELUEMUNOR MANSION — THE LATE CHIEF'S STUDY, IKOYI — DAY**
*(~0:50–2:10)*

**ESTABLISH:** *A white colonial mansion behind tall gates, with palm trees and black funeral bunting on the balcony. Inside, a dark wood study with a huge oil portrait of CHIEF OBINNA ELUEMUNOR, smiling.*

*BARRISTER ODUYA sits behind the desk with an open folder. CHIEF IKENNA wears a black agbada and holds a handkerchief. BISOLA scrolls her phone. A speakerphone sits on the desk.*

**ODUYA:** Miss Amara, can you hear us in London?

**AMARA (V.O., on the speaker):** I can hear you, Barrister.

**ODUYA:** *(reading)* "To my only child, Amara Adaeze Eluemunor, I leave sixty percent of Crown Eluemunor Holdings, including Eluemunor Tower. It passes to her on her twenty-eighth birthday. Until that day, my brother Ikenna will serve as acting chairman."

**BISOLA:** *(not looking up)* Sixty percent. For someone who hasn't been home since she was eight.

**IKENNA:** Bisola. *(softly, toward the phone)* Amara, my daughter. I'm so sorry. Your father was my whole life.

**AMARA (V.O.):** Thank you, Uncle.

**IKENNA:** Don't rush home. Lagos is hot, the traffic is terrible, and you have your studies. Six months will fly. I'll take care of everything.

**AMARA (V.O.):** *(a small pause)* I know you will, Uncle.

**ODUYA:** One more item. A sealed letter, for Amara's eyes only. *(He slides a cream envelope into his briefcase.)*

*Ikenna's eyes follow the envelope all the way into the briefcase.*

**AFTERMATH:** *The call ends. Ikenna dabs dry eyes with the handkerchief, then looks up at his brother's portrait. The grief leaves his face completely.*

**IKENNA:** *(quietly)* Six months.

*FADE.*

## SCENE 3: "I'M STANDING BEHIND YOU."
**EXT. LAGOS INTERNATIONAL AIRPORT — ARRIVALS — DAY**
*(~2:10–3:20)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The airport exterior in harsh sun: yellow taxis, hawkers selling water, families waving placards.*

*Barrister Oduya stands beside a black car with his phone to his ear.*

**ODUYA:** Did your uncle believe you're still in London?

**AMARA (V.O., on the phone):** Completely.

**ODUYA:** Good. When do you land?

**AMARA (V.O.):** I landed yesterday. *(beat)* I'm standing behind you.

*Oduya turns. AMARA stands there in a plain blue dress with a small backpack and no jewelry, except a thin gold chain tucked under her collar.*

**ODUYA:** *(laughing in spite of himself)* Your father's daughter.

*In the back seat he hands her the cream envelope. She opens it, and we hear her father's warm, deep voice.*

**CHIEF OBINNA (V.O.):** "Ada, my heart. Somebody in my house is eating from my plate and calling it love. Every year the company earns more, and every year there is less. Before you sit in my chair, learn who cleans the floor. Find the hand that is stealing, and you'll find the truth."

**AMARA:** *(folding it)* "Learn who cleans the floor."

**ODUYA:** He wanted you trained in finance.

**AMARA:** No. He wanted me invisible. *(She looks up at the gold tower rising over the city.)* Who cleans Eluemunor Tower, Barrister?

**ODUYA:** A contractor. Brightline Facilities. Your uncle signed them two years ago.

**AMARA:** Then from today, my name is Ada Nwosu. And I need a job.

**AFTERMATH:** *Oduya studies her for a long moment, then gives a single nod. Amara unclips the gold earrings and drops them into his palm.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 4: THE RULES OF THE BASEMENT
**INT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — BASEMENT LEVEL B2, BRIGHTLINE OFFICE — NIGHT**
*(~3:20–4:40)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The tower's grand marble lobby, gold and gleaming, then down a grey service ramp to a basement with bare bulbs, mop buckets and a buzzing generator.*

*MR. KUNLE sits at a metal desk and doesn't look up from his clipboard. AMARA stands in front of him in the navy-and-grey uniform.*

**KUNLE:** Salary is seventy thousand naira. Minus uniform, minus training, minus ID card. Night shift, 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. Sign.

**AMARA:** Minus how much?

**KUNLE:** *(finally looking up)* Do you want the job or a lecture?

*She signs "Ada Nwosu". MAMA NGOZI walks over with her red-taped mop.*

**MAMA NGOZI:** New girl? Come. I'll show you. *(walking)* Three rules. Number one: don't look the big people in the eye. Number two: never touch the 40th floor. That's the chairman's floor.

**AMARA:** And number three?

**MAMA NGOZI:** Never sit on their chairs. Even at 4 a.m., when your legs are dying. If they see a cleaner on their chair, they'll throw the chair away. And you with it.

**AMARA:** How long have you worked here, Mama?

**MAMA NGOZI:** Since your fine face was in primary school. Twenty-two years. I cleaned for the old Chief. *(softening)* He used to greet us. By name. He knew my son's name.

**AMARA:** *(carefully)* And the new chairman?

**MAMA NGOZI:** He knows the name of the lift.

**AFTERMATH:** *Mama Ngozi hands Amara a mop and walks off. Amara stares at the mop, then at a framed photo on the basement wall: her father in a hard hat, laughing with the cleaners, Mama Ngozi beside him. She touches the glass.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 5: TWO CUPS
**INT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — 31ST FLOOR, FINANCE DEPARTMENT — 2:00 A.M.**
*(~4:40–6:10)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The tower from outside at night, with almost every window dark except one line of light on the 31st floor.*

*Rows of empty desks. AMARA mops slowly. At the far end, under a single desk lamp, TOBI works through a mountain of printed invoices. A small electric kettle clicks off. He pours two cups and walks one over to her.*

**TOBI:** You've been mopping the same tile for five minutes. Either it's very dirty or you need tea.

**AMARA:** *(cautious)* We're not supposed to…

**TOBI:** Sit on the chairs. I know. *(He sits on the floor with his back against a desk and pats the carpet beside him.)* The floor is nobody's chair.

*She hesitates, then sits. They drink.*

**AMARA:** Why do you make two cups when you work alone?

**TOBI:** Habit. My father was a security guard. Night shifts, twenty years, in a building like this one. When I was small I'd wait up, and we'd drink tea at 2 a.m. when he came home. *(beat)* He's gone now. I still make two.

**AMARA:** I'm sorry.

**TOBI:** When he died, nobody from his company came to the funeral. Twenty years. They sent a text. *(He shrugs, embarrassed.)* Sorry. It's 2 a.m. That's when I talk too much. I'm Tobi.

**AMARA:** Ada.

**TOBI:** Ada. *(raising his cup)* To the people nobody sees.

**AMARA:** *(clinking)* To the people nobody sees.

**AFTERMATH:** *Tobi goes back to his desk. Amara stays on the floor holding the warm cup in both hands. She looks at him as if nobody has ever handed her anything without wanting something back.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 6: "THE CHAIRMAN'S DAUGHTER ASKED ABOUT YOU."
**INT. ADEYEMI FAMILY HOME, SURULERE — MORNING**
*(~6:10–7:20)*

**ESTABLISH:** *A busy Surulere street at sunrise: danfo buses, a woman frying akara. A small, clean two-bedroom flat with a framed photo on the wall of a man in a security uniform.*

*Tobi comes in yawning. MRS. ADEYEMI is ready for church in a green-and-gold gele. DAMI eats bread at the table.*

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Tobi! Guess who asked about you after service on Sunday.

**TOBI:** Mama, I haven't slept…

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Bisola Eluemunor. The *chairman's* daughter. She saw you at the company end-of-year party and she asked, "Who is that tall accountant?"

**TOBI:** She doesn't know my name, Mama. She asked who I was.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** And soon she'll know it! God has remembered this family.

**DAMI:** *(quietly, sliding a paper across the table)* Brother… the school sent this.

*Tobi reads: "OUTSTANDING FEES: ₦850,000. DEADLINE: 30 DAYS."*

**TOBI:** I'll handle it.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** With what? Your night-shift salary? *(She taps the photo of her late husband.)* Your father worked nights for twenty years, and look where it got us. One marriage, Tobi. One right marriage, and your sister finishes school and your mother rests.

**AFTERMATH:** *Mrs. Adeyemi leaves for church. Tobi stares at the fee notice, then at his father's photo. He puts the notice in his shirt pocket over his heart.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 7: THE COFFEE
**INT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — EXECUTIVE LIFT — MORNING**
*(~7:20–8:40)*

**ESTABLISH:** *Morning rush in the gold lobby: suits, ID lanyards, receptionists in red scarves.*

*Amara is finishing her shift and pushes her cleaning cart into a lift. BISOLA steps in holding an iced coffee, on a phone call.*

**BISOLA:** *(on the phone)* …no, I said the 40th floor. My father's floor. *(She looks Amara up and down.)* Hold on. *(to Amara)* This is the executive lift.

**AMARA:** The service lift is broken, ma.

**BISOLA:** Then walk.

*The lift jerks. Bisola's coffee tips, deliberately or not, and splashes across the floor and over her own red shoes.*

**BISOLA:** Look what you've done! Clean it.

*Amara kneels and wipes Bisola's shoes, the cousin who doesn't know her. Her jaw is tight.*

*The doors open on 31. TOBI steps in, sees Amara on her knees, kneels without a word and helps her wipe the floor.*

**BISOLA:** *(suddenly sweet)* Oh, you're the tall accountant.

**TOBI:** *(still wiping)* Tobi Adeyemi. Good morning.

**BISOLA:** You don't have to do that. She's paid for it.

**TOBI:** *(standing, handing Amara the cloth)* So am I. *(to Amara, quietly)* Go and rest, Ada.

*The doors open on 40. Bisola steps out but holds the door.*

**BISOLA:** Tobi Adeyemi. I'll remember that.

**AFTERMATH:** *The doors close on Bisola's interested smile. Inside the lift, Amara and Tobi stand side by side in silence. She looks at him. He looks at the floor numbers. Neither of them hides a small smile.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 8: PAYDAY
**INT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — BASEMENT B2 — DAWN**
*(~8:40–10:00)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The basement at 6 a.m.: a queue of tired cleaners in uniform holding brown envelopes.*

*Amara opens her envelope: ₦38,500. MAMA NGOZI stands at Kunle's desk.*

**MAMA NGOZI:** Mr. Kunle, please. It's two months now. My son Emeka's kidney. The hospital needs a deposit of one million two hundred.

**KUNLE:** Brightline has cash-flow issues, Mama. Next month.

**MAMA NGOZI:** You said that last month.

**KUNLE:** And I'll say it next month. If you don't like it, there are a thousand women outside who want your mop.

*Mama Ngozi walks away. Amara follows her.*

**AMARA:** Thirty-eight thousand. They promised seventy.

**MAMA NGOZI:** Welcome to Brightline, my daughter.

**AMARA:** Mama, how many of us are there? Cleaners. In the whole tower.

**MAMA NGOZI:** *(counting on her fingers)* Day and night together… forty. Forty-one with you.

*Amara looks at the sign-in book on Kunle's desk. While Kunle argues with someone, she photographs the page with a cracked old phone.*

**AFTERMATH:** *Mama Ngozi sits on an upturned bucket and quietly counts the coins in her purse. Amara watches her, then looks down at her own envelope and closes her fist around it.*

*FADE.*

# ACT TWO: THE ROUNDS

## SCENE 9: 300 GHOSTS
**INT. 31ST FLOOR — FINANCE — 2:00 A.M.**
*(~10:00–11:40)*

**ESTABLISH:** *Rain streaks the tower's glass. Lightning flashes over the lagoon.*

*Two cups of tea sit on the floor. Tobi and Amara sit side by side, invoices spread around them.*

**TOBI:** Something's wrong with these. Brightline Facilities. They invoice the company ninety-six million naira every month.

**AMARA:** *(very still)* For what?

**TOBI:** Cleaning. Three hundred cleaners, at three hundred and twenty thousand each. *(He laughs.)* Ada, if you're earning three hundred and twenty thousand, you should be buying the tea.

**AMARA:** I earn thirty-eight thousand five hundred.

*Tobi stops laughing.*

**AMARA:** And there aren't three hundred of us. There are forty. *(She shows him the photo of the sign-in book.)* Forty.

**TOBI:** *(staring at the phone)* That's two hundred and sixty people who don't exist. Getting paid. Every month. *(calculating)* That's… over ninety million naira a month. For two years.

**AMARA:** More than two billion.

**TOBI:** *(looking at her sharply)* How did you calculate that so fast?

**AMARA:** *(caught, then honest in her own way)* My father taught me to read numbers. He said numbers are the only thing in a company that can't smile at you.

**TOBI:** Your father sounds like a wise man.

**AMARA:** He was.

**AFTERMATH:** *Tobi gathers the invoices and locks them in his drawer. When he turns around, Amara is looking at him differently: not as a crush, but as an ally. She holds out her cup. He clinks it.*

**TOBI:** To the people nobody sees.

**AMARA:** And the ones who don't exist.

*FADE.*

## SCENE 10: THE 40TH FLOOR
**INT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — CHAIRMAN'S OFFICE, 40TH FLOOR — NIGHT**
*(~11:40–13:00)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The top of the tower at night: a glass office with the whole of Lagos below it like spilled gold.*

*CHIEF IKENNA pours whisky. MR. KUNLE stands nervously holding a hard hat. GARBA waits at the door.*

**IKENNA:** How many cleaners this month?

**KUNLE:** Forty-one, sir. We hired one new girl.

**IKENNA:** And how many did we invoice?

**KUNLE:** Three hundred, sir. As always.

**IKENNA:** *(smiling)* As always. *(He hands Kunle a thick envelope.)* Pay the new girl less. Say the uniforms went up.

**GARBA:** Sir. The London girl's lawyer has requested the company accounts.

**IKENNA:** *(unbothered)* Send Barrister Oduya the clean version. The girl is twenty-seven and reads novels. She'll see big numbers and be happy.

**GARBA:** And Brightline, sir? The paperwork still lists me as director.

**IKENNA:** Then you'll keep being a rich director, Garba. *(raising his glass)* Four more months. Then my niece gets a building with no money in it, and I'll be long gone with my brother's real fortune.

**AFTERMATH:** *Kunle and Garba leave. Ikenna stands alone in front of his brother's portrait.*

**IKENNA:** You always gave her everything, Obinna. You gave me a salary.

*He turns the portrait to face the wall.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 11: THE INVITATION
**INT. ADEYEMI HOME — EVENING**
*(~13:00–14:00)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The Surulere street at dusk: a generator hums, children play football under a street light.*

*Mrs. Adeyemi waves a cream card with gold edges like a winning lottery ticket.*

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Chief Ikenna Eluemunor requests the pleasure of Mr. Tobi Adeyemi's company. Dinner. At his house. In Ikoyi!

**TOBI:** *(taking the card, suspicious)* Why would the acting chairman invite a junior accountant to dinner?

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Because God has remembered us!

**DAMI:** Or because Bisola wants him.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Is that a crime? A beautiful, rich woman wants my son. Tobi, you'll wear your father's good agbada.

**TOBI:** Mama, I've been asking questions at work. About money. Big money. Maybe this dinner is not about Bisola.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** *(seriously)* Then stop asking questions. Questions are for people who can afford answers.

**AFTERMATH:** *Tobi takes his father's blue agbada out of an old trunk, the one in the framed photo. He holds it against his chest and looks in the mirror. He doesn't look happy.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 12: THE OFFER
**INT. IKENNA'S MANSION, IKOYI — DINING ROOM — NIGHT**
*(~14:00–15:40)*

**ESTABLISH:** *Another Ikoyi mansion, newer and flashier, with a fountain, three Range Rovers in the drive and a crystal chandelier.*

*A long table. IKENNA sits at the head, BISOLA in red beside him, TOBI across from them in his father's blue agbada, too big for him at the shoulders.*

**IKENNA:** Tobi. Your supervisor tells me you work nights. Every night.

**TOBI:** The reconciliations are behind, sir.

**IKENNA:** Behind. *(He smiles.)* I like a hard worker. So I'm promoting you. Head of Finance Operations. Day shift. Your salary goes from two hundred and fifty thousand to one point five million. A month.

*Tobi's fork stops halfway to his mouth.*

**TOBI:** Sir… why me?

**IKENNA:** Because the night floor is for cleaners, Tobi. Not for my future son-in-law.

*Bisola smiles at Tobi over her wine glass.*

**BISOLA:** Daddy, don't scare him.

**IKENNA:** *(laughing)* I'm not scaring him. I'm promoting him. *(beat, softly)* Just one thing. The Brightline invoices on your desk. Leave them. That contract is above your level now.

**TOBI:** *(slowly)* Of course, sir.

**AFTERMATH:** *Later, in the back of a car Ikenna sent to drive him home, Tobi loosens his collar. The streetlights of Ikoyi slide across his face. He types a text.*

**ON SCREEN (phone):** *"Are you on 31?"*

*He looks at it, deletes it, then types it again and sends.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 13: THE BLACKOUT
**INT. 31ST FLOOR — FINANCE — 2:00 A.M.**
*(~15:40–17:30)*

**ESTABLISH:** *Heavy rain. A wide shot of the tower, then the entire tower goes dark at once. Power cut.*

*Darkness. A generator coughs, and dim red emergency lights flicker on. Tobi and Amara sit on the floor, still in his father's agbada, two cups between them.*

**AMARA:** He offered you the job.

**TOBI:** One point five million. And his daughter. *(He laughs with no joy.)* And he knows I've seen the invoices. He told me to leave them.

**AMARA:** Will you take it?

**TOBI:** Dami's fees are due in two weeks. My mother hasn't bought herself new shoes since my father died. *(beat)* I don't know, Ada.

*Silence. The rain beats on the glass.*

**TOBI:** Can I tell you something stupid?

**AMARA:** It's 2 a.m. That's the rule.

**TOBI:** At that dinner, with the chandelier and the wine and the chairman calling me "son"… I felt like I was performing. Every second. *(turning to her)* Here, on this floor, with you, in the dark, with cheap tea… it's the only place in this whole building where I don't perform.

*Amara's eyes fill. She wants to tell him everything, and we see her almost do it.*

**AMARA:** Tobi, there's something I…

*The power snaps back on, and the fluorescent lights blaze. They both squint and laugh.*

**TOBI:** What were you going to say?

**AMARA:** *(swallowing it)* …that your agbada is too big for you.

**TOBI:** It was my father's.

**AMARA:** Then you'll grow into it.

**AFTERMATH:** *She leans her head on his shoulder. He freezes, then rests his head gently on hers. They sit like that, holding each other, as the city lights come back on outside one block at a time.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 14: GOD SENT SOMEBODY
**INT. LAGOS GENERAL HOSPITAL — CORRIDOR — DAY**
*(~17:30–18:40)*

**ESTABLISH:** *A crowded public hospital: a peeling blue sign, women on benches, a nurse pushing a squeaky trolley.*

*MAMA NGOZI sits outside a ward with her face in her hands. A NURSE comes out.*

**NURSE:** Mama Emeka? Your son's deposit has been paid. All of it. The surgery is tomorrow.

**MAMA NGOZI:** *(standing, shaking)* Paid? By who?

**NURSE:** A lawyer brought a bank draft this morning. No name. He said, "From someone who knows your son's name."

*Mama Ngozi drops to her knees in the corridor and lifts her hands.*

**MAMA NGOZI:** God sent somebody! God sent somebody!

**AFTERMATH:** *At the end of the corridor, half hidden behind a pillar, Amara watches in her cleaner's uniform. BARRISTER ODUYA stands beside her.*

**ODUYA:** You know you can't do this for all forty of them.

**AMARA:** Not yet. *(beat)* In four months, Barrister, I'll do it for all forty of them.

*FADE.*

## SCENE 15: THE FRIDGE
**INT. ADEYEMI HOME — DAY**
*(~18:40–19:50)*

**ESTABLISH:** *A delivery truck squeezes down the narrow Surulere street. Neighbors come out to stare as two men carry a huge silver double-door fridge toward the Adeyemi flat.*

*BISOLA sits on the old couch in sunglasses while Mrs. Adeyemi runs her hands over the new fridge.*

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Bisola, my daughter, this is too much!

**BISOLA:** It's nothing, Mummy. There's a bag of rice and a generator coming on Friday.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** "Mummy." *(She clutches her chest.)* You hear how she calls me?

**BISOLA:** *(taking off her sunglasses)* There's just one small problem. Tobi spends his nights with a cleaner.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** *(the smile falling)* A what?

**BISOLA:** A cleaner. Ada something. They drink tea on the floor like beggars. People are talking. It's embarrassing… for both our families.

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** *(cold)* Leave it with me.

**AFTERMATH:** *Bisola leaves, smiling. Mrs. Adeyemi stands in front of the shining new fridge and sees her own reflection in it: her old gele and tired face. She grips the door handle hard.*

*FADE.*

## SCENE 16: "DON'T DRAG HIM INTO A MOP BUCKET."
**INT. ELUEMUNOR TOWER — BASEMENT B2 — NIGHT**
*(~19:50–21:30)*

**ESTABLISH:** *The service entrance at the back of the tower: rubbish bins, a flickering light, a security booth.*

*Mrs. Adeyemi marches down the basement ramp in her gele. Cleaners look up. Amara is wringing out a mop.*

**MRS. ADEYEMI:** Which one of you is Ada?

**AMARA:** *(straightening)* I am, ma. Good evening.
