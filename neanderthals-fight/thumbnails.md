# Did Ancient Humans Really Fight Other Human Species? · Thumbnails

**Myth the video disproves:** "We showed up with better weapons and wiped the Neanderthals out in a war."
**Strongest real finding:** Shanidar 3's rib wound had started to heal, so someone kept him alive for weeks. Add the DNA: most of us carry 1–2% Neanderthal.

## Concept A · SAFE · "NEW NEIGHBORS?"
**Text:** NEW (white) **NEIGHBORS?** (orange #FF8A1F), top-right.

- **Main subject:** a stocky Neanderthal man (heavy brow ridge, wide nose, shaggy red-brown hair and beard, brown fur cloak). He sits at the mouth of his cave and is shown chest-up in the left third. His head takes up about 45% of the frame height, and his shoulder is cropped by the left edge. He leans slightly forward and looks over his shoulder toward the right. **Expression:** worried. The inner eyebrows are pulled up and together, his eyes are wide with small pupils looking right, and his mouth is a flat, slightly crooked line pressed tight.
- **Background and colour:** the warm side is the left: his campfire just inside the cave mouth lights his face orange-gold, and **the fire is the brightest point**. The cold side is the right: a blue dusk valley with snow on it. Far across the valley, 3–4 tiny new campfires glow on a ridge, each with small slim silhouettes (under 12% of the frame height) standing beside it.
- **Supporting details:** only the distant fires and figures. Their spears are held upright or are not shown at all.
- **Emotion the viewer feels:** the modern worry of "someone new just moved in next door and things are about to change."
- **Curiosity gap:** "Who are they, and what happens to him?"
- **How it works with the title:** the title says "fight." The thumbnail suggests something stranger: they were *neighbors*. The video pays this off with 2,600–5,400 years of overlap ("the slowest war in history") and the "squeeze."
- **Main weakness:** the distant figures could vanish at phone size. **Fix:** make the ridge fires the second-brightest spots, line them up along the diagonal from his eyes to the text, and keep them on a clean dark-blue band with no other detail.

## Concept B · CURIOSITY · "WAS IT US?"
**Text:** WAS IT (white) **US?** (orange), top-left, stacked over two lines.

- **Main subject:** an early modern human man representing *us* (slimmer face, high round forehead, dark hair tied back, light fur cloak). He is shown chest-up in the right third. His head takes up about 45% of the frame height, and his face sits between roughly 30% and 75% of the frame height. He is turned three-quarters toward the viewer with one hand hidden behind his back. A plain wooden spear shaft pokes out from behind him, pointing down at the ground, not at anyone. **Expression:** guilty. One eyebrow is raised and the other is lowered, his eyes glance sideways at the viewer, and his mouth is a small, awkward, closed-lip half-smile, like someone caught out.
- **Background and colour:** a bright daytime snowy valley. The warm side is the left: a low golden sun and a Neanderthal family's campfire in the middle distance, and **the sun glow behind the fire is the brightest point**. The cold side is the right: he stands in cool blue snow shadow.
- **Supporting details:** a small Neanderthal family by its fire on the left (each figure under 15% of the frame height). The father is **waving hello** at him, and a child peeks out from behind the father. That wave is the hint of the real answer: contact, not war.
- **Emotion the viewer feels:** guilt and the "what did I do?" feeling of being blamed.
- **Curiosity gap:** "Did we really kill them? Then why are they waving?"
- **How it works with the title:** the title asks whether there was fighting. The text points the finger at the viewer's own species, exactly as the script's hook does ("the main suspect might be the species watching this video"). It's honest because the video asks it, takes it seriously (the Churchill crossbow test), and then complicates it.
- **Main weakness:** a spear plus "WAS IT US?" could read as a straight confirmation of the myth. **Fix:** make the wave large and clear (an open hand, a big friendly smile) and keep the spear tip out of frame so only the plain shaft shows.

## Concept C · BRAND / EVIDENCE · "IT HEALED?"
**Text:** IT (white) **HEALED?** (orange), top-left.

- **Main subject:** the Paper Origins explorer (round face, tan pith helmet, small round glasses, khaki shirt), shown chest-up in the right third. His head, including the helmet, takes up about 45% of the frame height. He holds up a single Neanderthal rib at eye level between both hands, with a smooth, clearly visible healed bump of new bone on it. **Expression:** quietly amazed. His eyebrows are lifted high, his eyes are wide behind his glasses with small shining pupils fixed on the rib, and his mouth is a small round "o."
- **Background and colour:** the inside of a cave. The warm side is the right: an oil lantern beside him lights his face and the rib gold, and **the glow on the rib is the brightest point**. The cold side is the left: blue daylight at the cave mouth and blue-grey cave shadow. On the far-left cave wall is a faint warm painted ochre outline of a small Neanderthal figure lying by a fire with a second figure kneeling beside him (under 15% of the frame height). This hints at the caregiver without showing an injury.
- **Supporting details:** only the rib, the lantern and the faint wall image. No skull and no skeleton.
- **Emotion the viewer feels:** surprise and tenderness: "someone took care of him?"
- **Curiosity gap:** "Who did this to him, and who kept him alive?"
- **How it works with the title:** the title promises violence. The thumbnail shows the twist the video ends on: the wound healed, so someone nursed him for weeks.
- **Main weakness:** a rib with a healed bump is hard to read at 160×90. **Fix:** draw the rib oversized and pale cream against a dark cave wall, with the healed bump as a bright, smooth, glowing knot, and let the explorer's eyeline point right at it.

## Recommended order
**B → A → C.** B has the sharpest hook. "WAS IT US?" turns a distant history question into a personal accusation against the viewer, and the friendly wave creates instant tension against the myth. A is the reliable backup with a clear 1-second story. C is the strongest brand piece and the most moving payoff, but a rib reads less clearly at feed size, so test it third. If you only test two, use B and C so you are comparing two very different ideas.

## Text assembly (Canva / Photopea)
1. Set the canvas to 1280×720 and place the generated image so it fills the canvas.
2. Add the text in **Lilita One**, all caps, 150–190 px tall: the main word in white and the key word in #FF8A1F (A: NEIGHBORS, B: US, C: HEALED).
3. Add a 14–18 px black outline (stroke) and a soft drop shadow (black, ~40% opacity, offset 6–8 px, blur 10–12 px).
4. Place the text inside the empty top ~30% area, on the side opposite the face, so the face and text form one diagonal.
5. Keep at least ~40 px between the text and the face, eyes and helmet. Nothing may overlap the eyebrows or mouth.
6. Check that the bottom-right corner (about 200×100 px) is empty for the video length.
7. Export, then shrink a copy to **160×90 px** and check that the face, the expression and every word are still readable. If any word blurs, cut it down or make it bigger.
