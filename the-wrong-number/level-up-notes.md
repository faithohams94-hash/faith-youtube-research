# The Wrong Number — Level-Up Notes

A comparison of the draft (58 scenes, about 50 minutes) with *Fate Comes Back* (3M views, about 1h51). See `research/fate-comes-back/breakdown.md`.

## What the 3M-view movie does

1. **It pays off its promise in the first 5 minutes.** A villain humiliates a poor girl in public, kicks her food, and costs her "days of my wages." At 4:04 the "beggar" is revealed as the billionaire's grandmother. The thumbnail promise is delivered before most viewers would click away.
2. **Its conflicts happen in public.** The lift, the lobby, the staff room and the gala all have witnesses. The audience watches other characters react to the cruelty.
3. **The villain is punished again and again.** Selena is corrected or exposed at 4:04, 6:57, 23:08, 33:26, 48:03, 1:30:11 and 1:46:34. Each one is a small "yes!" moment that keeps people watching.
4. **A fairytale lift.** A delivery rider goes to the billionaire's office and then to Switzerland. Viewers get to enjoy that rise.
5. **Two leads in the same room.** Most scenes are faces reacting to each other.
6. **A new conflict after the villain leaves.** When Selena is gone (48:13), the loving grandmother's meddling becomes the problem, which gives the film a second "all is lost" moment.
7. **A hidden twist.** Esther fed young Noah years ago. It's planted at 31:16 with "I know that face" and paid off at 1:42:01.
8. **Comedy every 2–4 minutes.** The fake heart attack, 100 copies from the printer, the pillow "fence," the ski fall.

## What your script already does better

- **A stronger single premise.** "She texted the wrong number… 10 years later, the stranger appeared at her wedding" is a clearer and more original hook than "Fate Comes Back."
- **One motif that runs through everything.** Jollof or fried rice runs from the first text to the wedding to "Whatever YOU want." That's tighter than any single callback in *Fate Comes Back*.
- **A great final reveal.** The silence at the wedding, then "Still here ❤️." Keep it exactly as it is.
- **Realistic abuse with real stakes.** The receipts, the collar, the blocked card and the evidence notebooks are believable. The house-with-no-windows drawing is your best image.

So the job isn't to rewrite the story. It's to add the engines the 3M movie has and yours is missing.

## The gaps, with fixes

### 1. The opening is quiet, and the big promise pays off at 47:34
The first scene is a sad woman texting. It's good, but it's a slow burn, and the thing the title promises doesn't happen until minute 47.

**Fix: open with a flash-forward.** Start on the wedding (Scene 54): "I have never met her… Her name is Emma Williams." Silence. Nobody stands. Chloe's smile falters. Cut to black: **"TEN YEARS EARLIER."** Then Scene 1. That's about 30–40 seconds that tell the viewer exactly what they're waiting for, and the "Did she come?" question carries the whole film.

### 2. Kunle is never punished in public
His cruelty is private (bedroom, phone, dining room). His comeuppance is a legal ruling (Scene 38), and then he just fades out. Viewers of this genre wait for the moment the abuser is exposed **in front of the people he performs for.** Scene 11 sets that up (dinner guests, Chief Okeke), but it's never paid off.

**Fixes (pick 1–2):**
- **The dinner party, mirrored.** Put Chief Okeke or the guest's wife from Scene 11 in the courtroom gallery, or have them hear the notebook entry read aloud ("Your mother can't even choose between jollof and fried rice without me"). Show Kunle's face as his audience learns the truth.
- **Supermarket, round two.** In Scene 27 he blocks her card over the phone. Later, in public, his own card or reputation fails: a client or Chief Okeke drops him after the hearing because "a man who treats his wife like that will treat a partner the same."
- **He turns up at the wedding** uninvited, or tries to stop it or make a scene, and **Ada (17) stops him at the gate**: "No." He waits. She says: "No is a full sentence, Daddy." This pays off Scene 47 and gives the wedding a villain beat before the Emma reveal.

### 3. Conflict stops after the divorce
After Scene 39 (34:41) there's about 13 minutes where everyone agrees: Tunde is perfect, Ada approves after one dinner, and Emma says yes at once. *Fate Comes Back* stays strong here because a new problem arrives.

**Fixes:**
- **Turn the "man in Lagos" rumour into a real weapon.** You already planted it: Kunle nearly sees the phone (Scene 7), and Aunty Bola says "a man in Lagos is filling your head with rubbish" (Scene 34). Make Kunle find the FRIDAY STRANGER contact and **use it in court**: "My wife has been having an affair with a man in Lagos for five years." The hearing turns, Chloe's credibility is under attack, and she has to prove who Emma is. Emma sends a sworn statement or a voice note, or appears on video, and the judge hears "Wrong receiver, but jollof rice with turkey sounds better." That's a twist, a trial reversal and a laugh in one beat.
  - It also creates a **cliffhanger at Act Two's midpoint**: Emma, who has "never wanted to meet," is now needed, and for the first time she has to choose to put herself in Chloe's real life.
- **Make Ada push back on Tunde first.** She's 16 and has seen what a man in the house means. Give her one scene where she refuses him (walks out of dinner, or says "We were fine without you"), and let Tunde win her over slowly. Scene 46 currently resolves this in one meal.
- **Give Emma a reason she might not come.** In Scene 51 her only worry is "What if I'm not what she imagines?" Raise it: her mother is back in hospital that week (you already set up her sugar), or the flight is cancelled and she takes a 10-hour bus. Then the silence at the wedding has a real "she might not make it" behind it.

### 4. Too much of the film is two women looking at phones
Count the scenes that are mainly texts: 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 17, 22, 23, 26, 28, 30, 35, 37, 40, 43, 45, 49 and 50. That's 20+ out of 58. If you're making AI images and voiceover, a chat bubble over a woman holding a phone is the weakest visual you can make, and it's where viewers drop off.

**Fixes:**
- **Act out the texts more.** Each text should be followed by something we see happen: Chloe stops apologising to Kunle, Emma walks into the director's office.
- **Turn more texts into calls or voice notes.** The birthday voice note (Scene 14) is one of your best scenes because we *hear* her.
- **Merge or cut light text-only scenes.** For example, 6+7, 40+41 and 49+50. Spend that time on the new conflict scenes above.

### 5. Emma's side needs one in-person moment of cruelty or courage
*Fate Comes Back* works because the heroine **confronts** someone. Emma's best moment (Scene 24, saving four jobs) happens in an office with no opponent. Give her one public confrontation: a client or her director humiliating a junior (Kemi with the new baby) in front of the team, and Emma standing up the way Zara does in the lift. It shows that the person who helped Chloe find her voice uses her own.

### 6. Funny scenes, not just funny lines
Your humour is mostly witty texts. Add 3–4 **visual** comic set pieces that work as images:
- Emma testing a recipe for "jollof with turkey," burning it, and setting off the fire alarm in her flat.
- Tobi (4) secretly texting Friday Stranger from Chloe's phone with toddler gibberish and emojis.
- Segun and the printer that "refuses to work" (Scene 40). Give it a whole gag instead of one line.
- The taxi driver in Scene 52 is great. Give him one more beat at the wedding gate.

### 7. Add one hidden twist
*Fate Comes Back* has a "they were connected all along" twist. You could add a small one without making it cheesy:
- **Emma's studio once made the animated advert for Kunle's company.** She met him once. He was charming. At the hearing or the wedding she recognises him: "That's *him*?" It mirrors "They're judging the person they see" (Scene 12).
- **Or the wrong number was nearly right.** Chloe's real mistake was swapping two digits of an old number Kunle had abandoned, and that number now belongs to Emma.

Plant it early with one line, as *Fate Comes Back* does with "I know that face."

### 8. Length and the "worth the click" feeling
At about 50 minutes, a 3-act story with a 10-year time jump feels rushed in Act 3 (the years go by in montage). Adding items 2, 3 and 5 grows it naturally to about 70–80 minutes. Longer watch time helps long-form drama channels, but only if the new minutes have conflict. Don't pad.

## Priority order (if you only do some)

1. **Flash-forward cold open** (cheap, biggest retention effect).
2. **Kunle uses Emma as an "affair with a man in Lagos" in court** (adds a twist, a midpoint and a reason to see Emma's face).
3. **A public payoff for Kunle in front of his own crowd** (Chief Okeke, church, the wedding gate with Ada's "No").
4. **One obstacle in Act 3** (Ada resists Tunde, or Emma might not make it).
5. **Fewer phone-screen scenes; more acted and voice-note scenes.**
6. **2–3 visual comedy set pieces.**
7. **An optional hidden twist** connecting Emma to Kunle.

## Thumbnail and title angle
*Fate Comes Back* sells injustice plus a reveal. Your title already sells a reveal; add the injustice to the thumbnail:
- Thumbnail idea: Chloe in a marble kitchen, tear-streaked, phone glowing; Kunle's shadow in the doorway. Text: **"He decided what she cooked."** A smaller inset shows the wedding, and a woman in emerald standing up at the back.
- Alt title: *"She Texted the Wrong Number to Escape Her Husband… 10 Years Later, the Stranger Stood Up at Her Wedding."*
