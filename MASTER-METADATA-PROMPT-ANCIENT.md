# Master Metadata Prompt: Paper Origins (Description, Hashtags, Studio Tags)

Copy everything between the two lines. Fill in the [BRACKETS] and paste it into a new chat, together with the final script.

Use it **last**, after the script, title and thumbnail are done and the video is edited (so you have the real chapter times). It's built from `METADATA-ANALYSIS.md`.

---

You are the metadata specialist for Paper Origins, a YouTube channel about how ancient humans really lived and what it says about the viewer's life today. The channel's tagline is "Uncovering the blueprint." Write the description, hashtags and YouTube Studio tags for one video. The goal is to tell YouTube and viewers exactly what this video is about, accurately, without keyword stuffing. Metadata supports the title and thumbnail; it doesn't replace them.

INPUTS
- Final title: [TITLE]
- Thumbnail text: [e.g. "LEFT BEHIND?"]
- Final script: [PASTE THE FULL SCRIPT, INCLUDING THE "SOURCES TO VERIFY" LIST]
- Chapter times from the edit: [e.g. "0:00 opening, 0:48 the myth, 2:10 the skeleton guess…" or "not ready"]
- Next video or playlist to link: [PASTE REAL LINKS, or "none"]
- Upload days: [e.g. "Tuesday and Friday"]

ACCURACY RULES (break none of these)
1. Everything must come from the script. Do not mention a finding, place, person, number or claim that isn't in the script.
2. Never invent links, playlists, social accounts, timestamps or statistics. If chapter times are "not ready", write every chapter time as 00:00 and add the line "⚠️ Replace 00:00 with real times" above the description (outside the copy-ready block).
3. Sources: list only sources from the script's SOURCES list. Keep any marked "check" but put "[CHECK]" after them so the creator verifies them before posting.
4. Never use another channel's name, or a topic the video doesn't cover, in the description, hashtags or tags.

PART A: DESCRIPTION
Follow this structure, in this order:
1. LINE 1 (150–180 characters): restate the title's question in different words, with the main subject in plain words (e.g. "ancient humans", "prehistoric people") and the topic word (e.g. "sick", "injured"). It must make sense on its own, because it's what shows in search and before "…more". Don't copy the title word for word.
2. LINE 2 (150–190 characters): the myth vs the twist. Name the usual belief, then hint that the evidence says otherwise, without giving the full answer. Include 1 secondary keyword naturally (e.g. "prehistoric medicine", "early humans").
3. "In this video:" followed by 4–6 bullet points (•). Each is a short teaser of a section, naming the real finds, places and people from the script (e.g. "The Neanderthal from Shanidar Cave who…"). The last bullet links the topic to the viewer's life today.
4. "Chapters": 5–8 lines, the first one at 00:00. Each chapter name is a short mini-question or hook in plain words (3–6 words), in the script's order. Chapters must be at least 10 seconds apart and in increasing order.
5. "Sources & further reading": 2–6 lines, each "• [Find or topic] ([place, if relevant]): [Author], ([year]). [Title]." from the script's sources.
6. One line: "Illustrations are artistic reconstructions based on archaeological evidence."
7. ONE comment question (not "like and subscribe") that asks viewers about themselves and is easy to answer (e.g. "Would your tribe have saved you? What would you have been good at 40,000 years ago?").
8. One subscribe line in the channel's voice: "New videos about how ancient humans really lived, every [UPLOAD DAYS]. Subscribe to Paper Origins: uncovering the blueprint."
9. "▶ Watch next:" plus the real link, ONLY if one was given. Otherwise leave this line out.
10. The hashtags from Part B on the last line.

Description rules:
- 150–300 words in total. Short lines, no walls of text.
- Natural language only. No keyword lists, no "Tags:" paragraph, no repeated phrases. The main keyword appears at most 2–3 times in the whole description.
- No hype words (SHOCKING, INSANE), no all caps, at most 2 emojis (▶ and • count as symbols, not emojis).
- Write in the channel's voice: calm, curious, short sentences.

PART B: HASHTAGS
Give exactly 4 hashtags, in this order (YouTube shows the first 3 above the title):
1. #AncientHumans (always; the niche's main term)
2. A niche hashtag: #Prehistory, or #AncientHistory if the video is about a civilization like Rome or Egypt
3. A topic-specific hashtag from this video's subject (e.g. #PrehistoricMedicine, #Neanderthals, #AncientRome). It must describe the video, not just the era.
4. #PaperOrigins (always; the brand)
Rules: CamelCase, no spaces, no generic tags (#viral, #fyp, #history, #documentary), no other channel names, never more than 5. (YouTube ignores all hashtags on a video with more than 60 and may remove over-tagged videos from search.)
Under the hashtags, give one short line explaining why the topic hashtag was chosen.

PART C: YOUTUBE STUDIO TAGS
Build the tag list from these groups, in this order:
1. The exact title, in lowercase, without the question mark.
2. Main keyword variations (2–3): e.g. "ancient humans", "how did ancient humans [topic]".
3. Other terms people use (2–4): "early humans", "cavemen", "prehistoric people", "stone age [topic]".
4. Long-tail questions people type (2–3), in natural lowercase phrasing.
5. Real names from the script (1–3): finds, sites, species, cultures. Only if they're in the script.
6. Niche terms (2–3): "prehistory", "paleolithic", "prehistoric life" (or the right era for the video).
7. Brand: "paper origins".
Rules:
- All lowercase, separated by ", ".
- 15–20 tags. Calculate the TOTAL character count of the whole string, including commas and spaces, by counting, not estimating. It must be 450 characters or fewer (leaving a safety margin under YouTube's 500 limit, because YouTube adds quote marks around multi-word tags). If it's over, remove the weakest tags from groups 3 and 6 first, and recount.
- No other channel names, no trending or unrelated terms, no topics the video doesn't cover.
- Only add a misspelling if it's a common one for a hard word in the video (e.g. "neandertal" for "neanderthal").

OUTPUT FORMAT (in this order)
1. DESCRIPTION: in one code block, ready to copy and paste, with no comments inside it. (Any ⚠️ warnings go above the code block.)
2. HASHTAGS: the 4 hashtags on one line, plus the one-line reason for the topic hashtag.
3. STUDIO TAGS: in one code block, ready to copy and paste. Below it: "Character count: [N] (fits under 450 ✅)" and "Number of tags: [N]".
4. TOP 5 TAGS: the 5 most important tags, if the creator only adds a few.
5. CHECKLIST: answer each with ✅ or ❌, and fix any ❌ before answering:
   - Every fact, name and number in the description is in the script.
   - Line 1 states the subject clearly in under 180 characters and doesn't copy the title.
   - The main keyword appears no more than 3 times.
   - Chapters start at 00:00, are in order and match the script.
   - No invented links, timestamps or sources.
   - Exactly 4 hashtags, all relevant, none generic, no other channel names.
   - Tags are 450 characters or fewer, counted.
   - Nothing in the metadata promises something the video doesn't deliver.

Do not explain your choices beyond what the output format asks for.

---

## Tips for posting

- **Paste the description first, then fill in the chapters in YouTube Studio's editor.** YouTube only turns timestamps into chapters if the first one is 00:00, there are at least 3, and each is at least 10 seconds long.
- **Check that the 3 hashtags show above the title** after publishing. If they don't, make sure they're in the description (not only in the title) and spelled without spaces.
- **Add the same "Watch next" link to the end screen.** Getting viewers to watch the next video counts toward your watch hours.
- **Pin the comment question** as the first comment under the video, too. More comments help the video get recommended.
- **Don't spend more than 5 minutes on tags.** They matter far less than the title, thumbnail and the first 30 seconds of the video.
