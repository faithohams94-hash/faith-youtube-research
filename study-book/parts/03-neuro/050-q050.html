Write on the anatomy of the medulla at the upper region.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants "Upper region" = the transverse section of the medulla at the level of the olive (open part, floor of 4th ventricle). Marks go for: a brief intro to the medulla, a big labelled section, the contents arranged as nuclei / tracts / fibres, the inferior olivary nucleus described properly, cranial nerve nuclei in the floor (XII, X, NTS, vestibular), blood supply, and lateral/medial medullary syndromes.

Introduction

The medulla oblongata is the lowest part of the brainstem. It is conical, about 3 cm long and 2 cm wide at its upper end, and extends from the lower border of the pons (above) to just above the first cervical nerve at the foramen magnum (below), where it becomes the spinal cord. Its lower closed part has a central canal; its upper open part forms the lower half of the floor of the fourth ventricle. Chaurasia studies its internal structure at three levels: (1) the level of the pyramidal (motor) decussation, (2) the level of the sensory (lemniscal) decussation, and (3) the level of the olive — the "upper region" asked here.

External features of the upper (open) medulla — orientation

  1. Ventral surface: the pyramids lie on either side of the anterior median fissure; lateral to each is the oval elevation of the olive, separated from the pyramid by the anterolateral (preolivary) sulcus — rootlets of the hypoglossal nerve emerge here.
  2. Lateral surface: behind the olive, in the posterolateral (postolivary) sulcus, emerge the rootlets of the glossopharyngeal, vagus and cranial accessory nerves (IX, X, XI).
  3. Dorsal surface: the lower part of the rhomboid fossa (floor of 4th ventricle) with the hypoglossal triangle, vagal triangle and vestibular area; laterally the inferior cerebellar peduncle (restiform body).

Transverse section at the level of the olive — contents

The central canal has opened out into the fourth ventricle, so the grey matter of the cranial nerve nuclei now lies in the floor of the ventricle. It is convenient to describe the contents as grey matter (nuclei) and white matter (tracts and fibres).

A. Grey matter
  1. Inferior olivary nucleus — the most striking feature. It is a crumpled, purse-like sheet of grey matter with the hilum directed medially. It is accompanied by two small accessory olivary nuclei (medial and dorsal). It receives fibres from the cerebral cortex, red nucleus (via central tegmental tract) and spinal cord (spino-olivary); its efferents, the olivocerebellar fibres, leave through the hilum, cross the midline, and enter the opposite inferior cerebellar peduncle to end as climbing fibres in the cerebellar cortex.
  2. Cranial nerve nuclei in the floor of the fourth ventricle (medial → lateral):
    • Hypoglossal nucleus — beneath the hypoglossal triangle (GSE: muscles of the tongue).
    • Dorsal nucleus of the vagus — beneath the vagal triangle (GVE: parasympathetic to thoracic and abdominal viscera).
    • Nucleus of the tractus solitarius — ventrolateral to the dorsal vagal nucleus, surrounding the tractus solitarius (taste and general visceral afferents of VII, IX, X).
    • Vestibular nuclei (medial and inferior) — beneath the vestibular area, most laterally. At the uppermost level the dorsal and ventral cochlear nuclei lie on the surface of the inferior cerebellar peduncle.
  3. Nucleus ambiguus — deep in the reticular formation, dorsal to the olive (SVE: muscles of pharynx, larynx and soft palate via IX, X, cranial XI).
  4. Inferior salivatory nucleus — near the upper end of the dorsal vagal nucleus (GVE for IX → otic ganglion → parotid gland).
  5. Spinal nucleus of the trigeminal nerve — dorsolateral, medial to its tract (pain and temperature from the face).
  6. Arcuate nuclei — small groups on the anterior surface of the pyramids (displaced pontine nuclei; send fibres to the cerebellum via the inferior peduncle).
  7. Reticular formation — the diffuse network of neurons filling the area between the olive and the floor; contains the vital respiratory and cardiovascular (vasomotor) centres.
B. White matter
  1. Pyramids — corticospinal fibres (uncrossed at this level; they decussate lower down).
  2. Medial lemniscus — a vertical band on each side of the median raphe, dorsal to the pyramid (formed by internal arcuate fibres at the level of sensory decussation; carries fine touch, vibration, position sense).
  3. Tectospinal tract and medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF) — in that order dorsal to the medial lemniscus, near the midline.
  4. Inferior cerebellar peduncle — dorsolateral; contains posterior spinocerebellar, cuneocerebellar, olivocerebellar, reticulocerebellar and vestibulocerebellar fibres.
  5. Spinal lemniscus (lateral and anterior spinothalamic + spinotectal fibres) — lateral, between the olive and the spinal trigeminal nucleus.
  6. Spinal tract of the trigeminal nerve — on the surface, lateral to its nucleus.
  7. Anterior spinocerebellar tract and rubrospinal tract — in the lateral area, near the surface.
  8. Tractus solitarius — surrounded by its nucleus.
  9. Olivocerebellar fibres — crossing the midline to the opposite inferior peduncle.
  10. Emerging nerve fibres: hypoglossal fibres pass ventrally, lateral to the medial lemniscus, to emerge between pyramid and olive; vagus and glossopharyngeal fibres pass ventrolaterally to emerge behind the olive.

Blood supply

Branches of the vertebral artery, anterior spinal artery, posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA), posterior spinal artery and the basilar artery (upper part). Medial area: anterior spinal and vertebral; lateral area: PICA and vertebral.

Clinical significance

  1. Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome — thrombosis of PICA/vertebral artery: damage to nucleus ambiguus (dysphagia, hoarseness), spinal V nucleus and tract (loss of pain/temperature on the same side of the face), spinal lemniscus (loss of pain/temperature on the opposite side of the body), inferior cerebellar peduncle (ipsilateral ataxia), vestibular nuclei (vertigo, nystagmus), descending sympathetic fibres (Horner's syndrome).
  2. Medial medullary syndrome — anterior spinal/vertebral artery: pyramid (contralateral hemiplegia), medial lemniscus (contralateral loss of position and vibration sense), hypoglossal nerve (ipsilateral tongue paralysis — tongue deviates to the side of the lesion).
  3. Damage to the reticular formation (vital centres), e.g. compression from coning of the tonsils of the cerebellum through the foramen magnum, causes death from respiratory failure.

Conclusion

The upper medulla is identified by the olive, an open fourth ventricle and the cranial nerve nuclei in its floor; its small size and vital centres explain why vascular lesions here produce dramatic crossed signs.

Understand it (plain English) Think of the upper medulla as a slice of bread with the top crust peeled off (the fourth ventricle opened). On the "open" top lie the motor and sensory nuclei of the last cranial nerves (XII medially, X, then sensory NTS and vestibular laterally — motor medial, sensory lateral). The big wavy "purse" below is the olive, which talks to the opposite cerebellum. Down the middle runs a column: MLF on top, then tectospinal, then the medial lemniscus, then the pyramid at the bottom.
Memory trick Floor nuclei medial→lateral: "Her Dad Sells Vegetables" = Hypoglossal, Dorsal vagal, Solitarius, Vestibular. Midline column top→bottom: "My Tiny Mother Prays" = MLF, Tectospinal, Medial lemniscus, Pyramid. Lateral medullary syndrome = the "5 D's": Dysphagia, Dysarthria, Dizziness (vertigo), Diplopia/nystagmus, Decreased pain & temperature (crossed).
How to write this in the exam 25-minute plan: Intro (3 levels, 2 min) → diagram (5 min) → grey matter list (6 min) → white matter list (5 min) → blood supply + 2 syndromes (5 min) → conclusion. Use the phrases "open part of medulla", "hilum directed medially", "climbing fibres", "nucleus ambiguus — branchiomotor".
Mistakes that cost marks (1) Drawing a central canal at this level — it is the 4th ventricle now. (2) Saying the pyramids decussate here — they decussate at the lower level. (3) Putting the olive's hilum laterally. (4) Placing nucleus ambiguus in the floor — it lies deep in the reticular formation. (5) Saying olivocerebellar fibres go to the same side — they cross.
