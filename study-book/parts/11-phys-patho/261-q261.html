PART A — HAEMOGLOBINOPATHIES

Definition

Haemoglobinopathies are inherited (genetic) disorders of haemoglobin in which there is either an abnormal structure of the globin chain or a reduced rate of synthesis of a normal globin chain. Normal adult haemoglobin is HbA (α2β2, ~97–98%), with HbA2 (α2δ2, ~2–3%) and traces of HbF (α2γ2). The haem part is normal in haemoglobinopathies — the defect is in the globin.

GroupDefectExamples

2. Quantitative (thalassaemias)Decreased or absent synthesis of a structurally normal α or β chainα-thalassaemia, β-thalassaemia (major, intermedia, minor/trait)
1. Sickle cell disease — pathophysiology
  1. Genetic defect: autosomal recessive point mutation of the β-globin gene on chromosome 11 — codon 6 GAG → GTG, so glutamic acid is replaced by valine at position 6 of the β chain → HbS (α2βS2). Homozygous (HbSS) = sickle cell anaemia; heterozygous (HbAS) = sickle cell trait (usually symptom-free).
  2. Polymerisation: valine is hydrophobic. When HbS is deoxygenated, the valine fits into a hydrophobic pocket of a neighbouring Hb molecule → HbS molecules stick together into long rigid polymers (tactoids/fibres).
  3. Sickling: the polymers distort the red cell into a sickle (crescent) shape. Sickling is favoured by hypoxia, acidosis, dehydration (↑ MCHC), cold, infection and fever. At first it is reversible on re-oxygenation; repeated cycles damage the membrane → irreversibly sickled cells.
  4. Haemolysis: sickled cells are rigid and fragile and are removed by the spleen → RBC lifespan falls from 120 days to ~10–20 days → chronic haemolytic anaemia, unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (jaundice), pigment gallstones, reticulocytosis.
  5. Vaso-occlusion: rigid sickle cells (plus adhesion to endothelium) block small vessels → tissue ischaemia and infarction → painful (vaso-occlusive) crises, hand–foot syndrome (dactylitis) in infants, acute chest syndrome, stroke, avascular necrosis of femoral head, priapism, leg ulcers, autosplenectomy (repeated splenic infarcts).
  6. Consequences of autosplenectomy: susceptibility to encapsulated organisms (pneumococcus) and Salmonella osteomyelitis.
  7. Other crises: aplastic crisis (parvovirus B19 halts erythropoiesis), sequestration crisis (sudden pooling of blood in spleen in children), haemolytic crisis.
  8. Protective note: HbF does not sickle — that is why symptoms start after ~6 months of age when HbF falls. The trait gives some protection against falciparum malaria (hence high frequency in West Africa).
2. Thalassaemia — pathophysiology
  1. Defect: reduced (β+) or absent (β0) synthesis of β chains (β-thalassaemia, chromosome 11), or deletion of α genes (α-thalassaemia, chromosome 16; 4 genes).
  2. Imbalance of chains: the normal partner chain is in excess. In β-thal, excess α chains precipitate inside red cell precursors → membrane damage → precursors die in the marrow = ineffective erythropoiesis; circulating cells are haemolysed in the spleen.
  3. Too little Hb per cell → microcytic hypochromic anaemia; compensatory ↑ HbF and HbA2 in β-thal.
  4. Anaemia → ↑ erythropoietin → massive marrow expansion: frontal bossing, "chipmunk" facies, "hair-on-end" skull X-ray; extramedullary haematopoiesis → hepatosplenomegaly.
  5. Iron overload (↑ absorption + repeated transfusion) → haemosiderosis of heart, liver, endocrine glands.
  6. α-thal severity depends on number of genes deleted: 1 = silent carrier, 2 = trait, 3 = HbH disease (β4), 4 = Hb Bart's (γ4) → hydrops fetalis.

PART B — HEART FAILURE

Definition

Heart (cardiac) failure is the condition in which the heart is unable to pump sufficient blood to meet the metabolic needs of the tissues, or can do so only at an abnormally raised filling pressure. Cardiac output falls relative to need, and blood dams back in the veins behind the failing ventricle.

Causes
  1. Damage to myocardium (↓ contractility): ischaemic heart disease / myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, myocarditis.
  2. Pressure overload (↑ afterload): systemic hypertension, aortic stenosis (left); pulmonary hypertension, pulmonary stenosis, chronic lung disease = cor pulmonale (right).
  3. Volume overload (↑ preload): aortic or mitral regurgitation, septal defects.
  4. Filling defects: mitral stenosis, constrictive pericarditis, cardiac tamponade.
  5. Arrhythmias (very fast or very slow rates) and high-output states: severe anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, beriberi, AV fistula, Paget's disease.

Pathophysiology — compensation and the vicious cycle

The primary event is a fall in stroke volume and cardiac output. The body responds as if there were haemorrhage:

  1. Sympathetic stimulation (via arterial baroreceptors): ↑ heart rate and contractility, and vasoconstriction to maintain arterial pressure. Long-term it ↑ myocardial O2 demand and ↑ afterload, and catecholamines are toxic to myocytes.
  2. Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system: ↓ renal perfusion → renin → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction, ↑ thirst, myocardial fibrosis) and aldosterone (Na+ and water retention). ADH release adds water retention. Result: ↑ blood volume → ↑ venous return.
  3. Frank–Starling mechanism: ↑ end-diastolic volume stretches the fibres → stronger contraction. Useful early, but the failing heart's curve is flattened, so extra filling mostly raises venous pressure.
  4. Myocardial hypertrophy and remodelling: concentric hypertrophy with pressure load, eccentric (dilatation) with volume load. Eventually O2 supply cannot match the thickened muscle → ischaemia, apoptosis, fibrosis → further fall in contractility.
  5. Natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP) are released from stretched atria/ventricles — they promote natriuresis and vasodilatation but are overwhelmed by the RAAS/sympathetic drive.

Thus the compensatory mechanisms that maintain blood pressure in the short term increase preload, afterload and O2 demand in the long term, producing congestion (oedema) and further myocardial damage — the vicious cycle of heart failure.

Forward failure (both): fatigue, weakness, cold peripheries, ↓ urine output, confusion (↓ cerebral perfusion), pulsus alternans; third heart sound (S3) from rapid filling of a dilated ventricle; cardiomegaly.

Principles of management (outline only): treat the cause; reduce preload (diuretics, salt restriction), reduce afterload and block RAAS/sympathetic over-activity, improve contractility where needed.

Conclusion

Haemoglobinopathies arise from a defect of globin structure (HbS) or synthesis (thalassaemia) and cause haemolytic anaemia ± vaso-occlusion. Heart failure is a fall in cardiac output relative to tissue need; neuro-humoral compensations rescue blood pressure in the short run but drive the progressive vicious cycle and congestion.

Understand it (plain English) Sickle cell: one wrong amino acid makes deoxygenated Hb "sticky" — the molecules stack like bricks into rods that bend the cell into a sickle. A bent, stiff cell does two things: it breaks (haemolysis → anaemia, jaundice) and it blocks (vaso-occlusion → pain, infarcts). Thalassaemia is the opposite story — the Hb is normal, there just isn't enough of one chain, so the leftover chain clumps and kills the cell. Heart failure: the body only "sees" low blood pressure and responds as if you were bleeding — squeeze vessels, keep salt and water. Great for a haemorrhage, terrible for a weak pump, which now has to push against tighter vessels with more volume.
Memory trick Sickle = "Break and Block" (haemolysis + vaso-occlusion). Triggers of sickling: "HAD-C" — Hypoxia, Acidosis, Dehydration, Cold. Causes of heart failure: "HEART" — Hypertension, Endocrine/high-output (thyroid, anaemia), Atherosclerosis/IHD, Rheumatic valve disease, Toxins/cardiomyopathy. LVF symptoms: "3 Ds of the Lung" — Dyspnoea, Decubitus dyspnoea (orthopnoea), Dyspnoea at night (PND).

Mistakes that cost marks (1) Saying valine replaces glutamic acid at position 6 of the α chain — it is the β chain. (2) Calling thalassaemia a "structural" defect — it is a quantitative (synthesis) defect. (3) Listing compensations without saying how they become harmful. (4) Mixing LVF and RVF features — pulmonary signs = left, systemic venous signs = right.
