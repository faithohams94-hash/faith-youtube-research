Definition

Deglutition (swallowing) is the process by which food passes from the mouth into the stomach through the pharynx and oesophagus. It starts voluntarily and is finished by reflexes.

Stages of deglutition (Sembulingam)

  1. Oral stage: voluntary
  2. Pharyngeal stage: involuntary (reflex)
  3. Oesophageal stage: involuntary (reflex)

1. Oral stage (voluntary)

  1. Masticated food mixed with saliva is rolled into a bolus on the upper surface of the tongue.
  2. The mouth is closed. The tip of the tongue presses against the hard palate, then the tongue moves upward and backward, squeezing the bolus into the oropharynx.
  3. This stage is under voluntary control (cerebral cortex), but once the bolus touches the pharynx the rest happens by reflex and cannot be stopped.

2. Pharyngeal stage (involuntary, lasts about 1–2 seconds)

The bolus stimulates deglutition receptors around the pharyngeal opening, mainly on the tonsillar pillars. Impulses reach the deglutition centre in the medulla and lower pons, which fires a fixed series of events. The aim is to send the bolus into the oesophagus and away from the nose, mouth and airway.

  1. Closure of the nasopharynx: the soft palate and uvula are pulled upward and meet the posterior pharyngeal wall, which stops food entering the nasal cavity.
  2. Closure of the mouth: the tongue stays pressed against the palate, so food cannot go back into the mouth.
  3. Closure of the larynx: the vocal cords come together, the larynx is pulled upward and forward under the tongue, and the epiglottis swings back over the laryngeal inlet. This stops food entering the trachea.
  4. Deglutition apnoea: respiration is briefly inhibited by the deglutition centre, whatever phase of breathing the person is in.
  5. Opening of the upper oesophageal sphincter (cricopharyngeus): it relaxes as the larynx is lifted.
  6. Pharyngeal peristalsis: the superior, middle and inferior constrictors contract in sequence and drive the bolus into the oesophagus.

3. Oesophageal stage (involuntary)

  1. Primary peristalsis: the pharyngeal peristaltic wave continues into the oesophagus and carries the bolus to the stomach in about 8–10 seconds. Gravity makes this faster when standing, especially for liquids.
  2. Secondary peristalsis: if the primary wave fails to clear the bolus, distension of the oesophagus by the remaining food starts a new wave. It is produced by the myenteric plexus, helped by vagal reflexes, and continues until the oesophagus is empty.
  3. Relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter: the LES (gastro-oesophageal sphincter) relaxes ahead of the peristaltic wave, through inhibitory myenteric neurons (VIP, NO). The proximal stomach relaxes too (receptive relaxation), so the bolus enters easily. The LES then closes again to prevent reflux of acid.

Nervous control: the deglutition (swallowing) reflex

Bolus touches pharynx receptors→Afferent: V, IX, X→Deglutition centre (medulla + lower pons)→Efferent: V, IX, X, XII→Pharyngeal and oesophageal muscles

The centre also inhibits the respiratory centre, which is why breathing stops during swallowing.

Applied physiology

  • Dysphagia: difficulty in swallowing, from mechanical obstruction, neuromuscular disease or bulbar palsy.
  • Achalasia cardia: the LES fails to relax, because the myenteric plexus is lost. Food accumulates and the oesophagus dilates (megaoesophagus).
  • Gastro-oesophageal reflux: an incompetent LES lets acid flow back, causing heartburn.
  • Aspiration: in unconscious or anaesthetised patients the protective laryngeal closure fails.

Conclusion

Deglutition is a voluntary oral stage followed by a reflex pharyngeal and oesophageal stage. The medullary deglutition centre coordinates them so that food reaches the stomach while the airway stays protected.

Understand it (plain English) Kris, the pharynx is a crossroads used by both air and food. For 1–2 seconds the brainstem closes every exit except the oesophagus: it shuts the door to the nose (soft palate), the mouth (tongue), and the airway (larynx up, epiglottis down, cords closed, breathing held), then opens the oesophagus (UES). After that, the oesophagus works like toothpaste being squeezed: a wave pushes the bolus down, and the LES at the bottom opens just before the bolus arrives.
Memory trick Pharyngeal events: "No Mouth Lets Air Up Please", which stands for Nasopharynx closed, Mouth closed, Larynx closed, Apnoea, UES opens, Peristalsis.
How to write this in the exam For 8 marks, spend about 12 minutes. Write the definition, draw the three-box diagram (Fig 311.1), then give each stage as a heading with numbered events. Add the reflex arc as a one-line flow and finish with two applied points. "Deglutition apnoea" and "receptive relaxation" are phrases that earn marks.
Mistakes that cost marks Writing that the whole of swallowing is voluntary (only the oral stage is). Forgetting secondary peristalsis. Saying the epiglottis is the main protection of the airway; the upward movement of the larynx and closure of the vocal cords matter more. Putting the deglutition centre in the cortex instead of the medulla and lower pons.
