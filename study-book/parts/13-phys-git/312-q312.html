What the examiner wants List the organs of the alimentary canal in order, plus the accessory organs, and give each segment's approximate transit (passage) time: pharynx 1–2 s, oesophagus about 8–10 s, stomach 3–4 h, small intestine 3–5 h, large intestine many hours to 1–2 days. Add the factors that change gastric emptying. A table or labelled diagram gets full marks.

Introduction

The gastrointestinal tract (GIT) is a muscular tube about 8–9 m long (in the cadaver) that runs from the mouth to the anus. Its accessory digestive organs empty their secretions into it. Passage (transit) time is the time food or chyme takes to move through each segment. It depends on the type of food, its volume and consistency, and nervous and hormonal control, so all values are approximate.

Organs of the GIT

  1. Alimentary canal: mouth, pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum), large intestine (caecum, appendix, ascending, transverse, descending and sigmoid colon, rectum) and anal canal.
  2. Accessory organs: salivary glands (parotid, submandibular, sublingual), liver, gallbladder and exocrine pancreas.

Passage times

SegmentApproximate passage timeWhat happens there
MouthSeconds to about a minute (depends on chewing)Mastication, mixing with saliva, start of starch digestion
Pharynx1–2 secondsPharyngeal stage of deglutition
OesophagusAbout 8–10 s for solids (liquids reach the stomach in a few seconds)Primary and secondary peristalsis
StomachAbout 3–4 h for a mixed meal (range 2–6 h)Storage, mixing into chyme, protein digestion starts
Small intestineAbout 3–5 h (pylorus to ileocaecal valve)Most digestion and absorption
Large intestineMany hours, usually about 12–48 h (variable)Absorption of water and electrolytes, faeces formed and stored
Total mouth to anusAbout 24–72 hFaster with fibre-rich diet and diarrhoea, slower in constipation

Factors affecting gastric emptying (the longest controlled step)

  1. Nature of food: carbohydrates leave fastest, proteins slower, fats slowest. Liquids leave faster than solids.
  2. Gastric factors that speed emptying: greater volume (stretch), and gastrin.
  3. Duodenal factors that slow emptying: the enterogastric reflex, triggered by acid, fat, protein products, hypertonicity and distension of the duodenum, and hormones (CCK, secretin, GIP).

Applied

Transit through the colon is faster in diarrhoea (less water absorbed) and slower in constipation (more water absorbed, hard stools). Delayed gastric emptying gives a feeling of fullness and vomiting, as in pyloric stenosis.

Conclusion

Food moves through the pharynx and oesophagus in seconds, but spends hours in the stomach and small intestine and the longest time in the large intestine. Total transit is about 1–3 days.

Understand it (plain English) The places where food is only moved (pharynx, oesophagus) take seconds. The places where it is worked on take hours: the stomach mixes it and the small intestine digests and absorbs it. The colon is a slow water-recovery and storage tank, so it takes the longest.
Memory trick "2 seconds, 10 seconds, 4 hours, 5 hours, 2 days" for pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and colon. Gastric emptying speed: "Carbs Can Pass, Fats Freeze" (carbohydrate fastest, protein in between, fat slowest).
How to write this in the exam This is 6 marks, about 8 minutes. Give a one-line introduction, list the organs, write the passage-time table, then two lines on factors affecting gastric emptying. Say the values are "approximate" because examiners accept a range.
Mistakes that cost marks Leaving out the accessory organs when asked for "GIT organs". Quoting one exact figure with no range. Saying fat leaves the stomach fastest (it is the slowest).
