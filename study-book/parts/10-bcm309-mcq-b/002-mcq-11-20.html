11. Control the secretion of hormones from the anterior pituitary:
(a) Thyroid hormones (b) Neurohypophyseal hormones (c) Adrenal cortex hormones (d) Hypothalamic hormone (e) Pituicytes
Answer: (d) Hypothalamic hormones — The releasing and inhibiting hormones (TRH, CRH, GnRH, GHRH, somatostatin, dopamine) reach the anterior pituitary through the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal veins. Thyroid and adrenal hormones only modulate by feedback; the best "controller" is the hypothalamus.
12. Amino acid derived hormone except:
(a) thyroxine (b) Adrenalin (c) Nor adrenalin (d) serotonin (e) parathyroid hormone
Answer: (e) Parathyroid hormone — "Amino-acid-derived" means made from a single amino acid: thyroxine, adrenaline, noradrenaline (from tyrosine), serotonin (from tryptophan). PTH is a polypeptide of 84 amino acids — a peptide hormone.
13. Peptide hormones are produced in all the following organs except:
(a) pancreas (b) parathyroid gland (c) gastrointestinal tracts (d) Neurohypophysis (e) Adrenal cortex
Answer: (e) Adrenal cortex — The adrenal cortex makes only steroid hormones. Strictly, the neurohypophysis (d) only stores and releases ADH and oxytocin (they are made in the hypothalamus), so a purist could argue (d) too — but the lecturer's intended answer is (e).

Catecholamines — adrenal medulla (items 14–18)

14. Involved in the storage of adrenaline and nor adrenaline:
(a) enterochromaffin cells (b) chromaffin cells (c) juxtaglomerular cells (d) parafollicular cells (e) Delta cells of the islets of Langerhans
Answer: (b) Chromaffin cells — The chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla store catecholamines in chromaffin granules (with ATP and chromogranin). Enterochromaffin cells of the gut store serotonin; parafollicular cells = calcitonin; delta cells = somatostatin.
15. Intermediates in the biosynthesis of adrenaline except:
(a) nor epinephrine (b) dopamine (c) L-dopa (d) Tryptophan (e) Phenylalanine
Answer: (d) Tryptophan — Pathway: Phenylalanine → Tyrosine → DOPA (tyrosine hydroxylase, rate-limiting) → Dopamine → Noradrenaline → Adrenaline (PNMT, SAM). Tryptophan is the precursor of serotonin and melatonin, not catecholamines. (Phenylalanine is strictly a precursor rather than an "intermediate", but it is on the pathway.)
16. True except:
(a) the effect of adrenaline on metabolism is about 5–10 times greater than that of nor adrenaline (b) Adrenaline causes greater stimulation of the heart than nor adrenaline (c) Adrenaline causes constriction of the bronchial muscles (d) Glucagon is preferred to adrenaline in the management of cardiac arrest (e) Adrenaline has a positive inotropic and chronotropic effect
Answer: (c) — Adrenaline relaxes bronchial smooth muscle (β2 → bronchodilatation; that is why it is used in acute asthma/anaphylaxis). Flawed item: (d) is also false — adrenaline, not glucagon, is the drug used in cardiac arrest. The lecturer's key is most likely (c), the classic textbook physiology point; (d) was probably written as a "true" statement by mistake. Under negative marking, choose (c).
17. Removal of adrenaline and nor adrenaline from circulation involves:
(a) monoamine oxidase (b) Tyrosine hydroxylase (c) methyl transferase (d) catechol-O-methyl transferase (e) only a and d are correct
Answer: (e) Only a and d — Catecholamines are inactivated by COMT (O-methylation → metanephrines) and MAO (oxidative deamination); the common end-product is vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) in urine. Tyrosine hydroxylase is a synthetic enzyme.
18. Effects of the catecholamines on metabolism except:
(a) increase glycolysis (b) Inhibit glycogenesis (c) Increase lipolysis (d) increase glycogenolysis (e) Inhibit gluconeogenesis
Answer: (e) Inhibit gluconeogenesis — Adrenaline is a stress (anti-insulin) hormone: it increases gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis and lipolysis and inhibits glycogenesis, all raising blood glucose and fuel. Increased glycolysis occurs in muscle (glycogen → lactate during "fight or flight").

Adrenal cortex — steroids and glucocorticoids (items 19–26)

19. Cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene is a common ring structure for which group of hormones:
(a) amino acid hormones (b) peptide hormones (c) hypophyseal hormones (d) GIT hormones (e) steroid hormones
Answer: (e) Steroid hormones — All steroids (cholesterol, corticosteroids, sex hormones, vitamin D precursors, bile acids) share the four-ring (A, B, C, D) cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene nucleus.
20. Not a hormone of the zona reticularis:
(a) Androgens (b) Oestrogens (c) Progestogens (d) All of the above (e) None of the above
Answer: (e) None of the above — The zona reticularis (with inner fasciculata) secretes the adrenal sex steroids: mainly androgens (DHEA, androstenedione), with small amounts of oestrogens and progestogens. So every option IS a zona reticularis product. Caution: progestogens are mainly intermediates rather than secreted products, so a strict marker might argue (c); the textbook list ("androgens, oestrogens, progesterone") supports (e).
