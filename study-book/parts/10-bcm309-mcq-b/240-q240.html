Introduction — where enzymes fit in LFTs

Liver function tests are grouped (Vasudevan) into tests based on: (1) excretory function — serum bilirubin, urine bile pigments/bile salts/urobilinogen; (2) synthetic function — serum albumin, A/G ratio, prothrombin time; (3) detoxification — hippuric acid test, blood ammonia; (4) serum enzymes — markers of hepatocellular damage (ALT, AST) and of cholestasis (ALP, GGT, 5′-nucleotidase). Enzymes are the most sensitive early indicators of liver disease.

1. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT/SGPT)

  1. Alanine + α-ketoglutarate ⇌ pyruvate + glutamate; PLP-dependent.
  2. Cytosolic; liver is by far the richest source → liver-specific.
  3. Normal ≈ 5–40 U/L. Rises 10–100-fold in acute viral and toxic hepatitis, even in the pre-icteric (anicteric) phase; returns to normal as the patient recovers — useful to follow the course of hepatitis.

2. Aspartate aminotransferase (AST/SGOT)

  1. Aspartate + α-ketoglutarate ⇌ oxaloacetate + glutamate; PLP-dependent.
  2. Cytosolic and mitochondrial; also in heart, skeletal muscle, kidney and RBC.
  3. Normal ≈ 5–40 U/L. Raised in hepatitis, alcoholic liver disease, cirrhosis, metastases. AST/ALT (De Ritis) ratio >2 points to alcoholic liver disease; <1 to viral hepatitis.

3. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)

  1. Hydrolyses phosphate esters at pH 9–10; Zn2+/Mg2+ dependent; on the canalicular membrane.
  2. Normal ≈ 40–125 U/L (adult).
  3. Markedly raised in obstructive (post-hepatic) jaundice and intrahepatic cholestasis, and in space-occupying lesions of the liver; only mildly raised in hepatitis. Bone disease also raises it — confirm with GGT.

Additional: γ-glutamyl transferase (GGT)

Most sensitive marker of cholestasis and of chronic alcohol intake; normal in bone disease and pregnancy. Normal ≈ 10–40 U/L.

Conclusion

ALT, AST and ALP (with GGT) together tell us whether the liver lesion is hepatocellular or cholestatic, while albumin and prothrombin time tell how much synthetic function remains.

Understand it (plain English) Strictly speaking, enzymes measure liver damage, not liver work. A cirrhotic liver may have near-normal enzymes but a low albumin and long prothrombin time. Saying this one line shows the examiner real understanding.
Memory trick "Hepatitis Hits the Hepatocyte → ALT/AST; Obstruction Opens the canaliculus → ALP/GGT".
How to write this in the exam Start with the 4-group classification of LFTs (shows breadth), then the three enzymes under numbered headings, then the pattern diagram/table. 20 minutes.
Mistakes that cost marks Listing bilirubin or albumin as "enzymes". Forgetting the bone source of ALP. Not stating which disease pattern each enzyme points to.
