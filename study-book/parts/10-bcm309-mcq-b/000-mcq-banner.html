Negative marking — how to play it, Kris
  1. Decode the stem first. Underline every negative word. "Not … except" = a double negative = "which one IS …". "All are false except" = "which one is TRUE". "True except" = "which one is FALSE".
  2. Eliminate, then decide. If you can confidently strike out all but two options, the odds are in your favour — answer. If you cannot eliminate anything, leave it blank: a wrong answer costs you marks, a blank costs nothing.
  3. "Only a and d", "All of the above", "None of the above" — check each component separately. "All of the above" is only right if every single option is right.
  4. Flawed items exist in this paper (two or even three options can be defended on some). The keys below give the textbook-correct answer AND, where the item is defective, which option the lecturer most likely marked. Where the item is badly broken, consider leaving it blank in a negative-marked exam.
  5. Time: 50 items in about 30 minutes — first pass answer the sure ones, second pass the "eliminate-to-two" ones, never guess blindly.
