21. Plays a major role in protein, lipid and carbohydrate metabolism:
(a) Aldosterone (b) Androgens (c) Cortisol (d) All of the above (e) None of the above
Answer: (c) Cortisol — Cortisol (glucocorticoid) promotes protein catabolism in muscle, gluconeogenesis in liver and lipolysis/fat redistribution. Aldosterone acts on electrolytes; androgens are anabolic for protein only.
22. All the following are false except:
(a) synthesis of glucocorticoids takes place in the mitochondria only (b) synthesis of glucocorticoids takes place in the endoplasmic reticulum only (c) All corticoid hormones are synthesized from cholesterol (d) The step catalysed by 17-hydroxylase is the rate limiting step in the synthesis of glucocorticoids (e) cholesterol can never be synthesized from acetyl CoA
Answer: (c) — "All false except" = find the TRUE one. All corticosteroids come from cholesterol. Synthesis shuttles between mitochondria AND smooth ER (so a, b false); the rate-limiting step is cholesterol side-chain cleavage (desmolase, CYP11A1), not 17-hydroxylase; cholesterol is made from acetyl-CoA via HMG-CoA reductase.
23. Role of ACTH in biosynthesis of glucocorticoids:
(a) increase the conversion of cholesterol to pregnenolone (b) increase the transfer of free cholesterol from plasma lipoproteins into zona fasciculata (c) Activates PPP where NADPH required for hydroxylation is generated (d) All of the above (e) Only a and b are correct
Answer: (d) All of the above — ACTH (via cAMP) increases LDL-cholesterol uptake and cholesterol ester hydrolysis, stimulates the side-chain cleavage step (cholesterol → pregnenolone), and promotes NADPH supply (HMP/pentose phosphate pathway) for the many P450 hydroxylations. The three options read like the lecture-note list, so the key is (d). (If you doubt (c), (e) is the only alternative — but the textbooks list NADPH provision as an ACTH effect.)
24. Which of the following is a metabolic role of glucocorticoids:
(a) hyperglycaemia (b) increase glucose uptake (c) increase glucose utilization (d) All of the above (e) None of the above
Answer: (a) Hyperglycaemia — Cortisol stimulates gluconeogenesis and decreases peripheral glucose uptake and utilisation (anti-insulin effect) → raised blood glucose ("steroid diabetes"). So (b) and (c) are opposites, ruling out (d).
25. Has an anti-inflammatory effect:
(a) insulin (b) thyroxine (c) cortisol (d) glucagon (e) oxytocin
Answer: (c) Cortisol — Glucocorticoids induce lipocortin (annexin-1), which inhibits phospholipase A2 → less arachidonic acid → fewer prostaglandins/leukotrienes; they also stabilise lysosomal membranes and suppress immune cells.
26. Effects of excessive cortisol secretion except:
(a) lymphopaenia (b) thrombocytopenia (c) decreased monocytes (d) increased eosinophils (e) lymphocytosis
Answer (lecturer's most likely key): (d) Increased eosinophils — Excess cortisol causes eosinopenia, lymphopenia and monocytopenia, with neutrophilia and increased RBC and platelets. Badly flawed item: by the textbooks, (b) thrombocytopenia and (e) lymphocytosis are ALSO wrong (three false options). Eosinopenia is the signature fact taught in both texts, so (d) is the safest pick; in a negative-marked exam you may prefer to leave this one blank.

Adrenal cortex — mineralocorticoids and RAAS (items 27–33)

27. The main mineralocorticoid is:
(a) corticosterone (b) 11-deoxycortisol (c) Aldosterone (d) 11-deoxycorticosterone (e) cortisol
Answer: (c) Aldosterone — Made in the zona glomerulosa; accounts for about 90% of mineralocorticoid activity. DOC and corticosterone are weak mineralocorticoids; cortisol is the main glucocorticoid.
28. Mineralocorticoids are:
(a) C21 steroids (b) C19 steroids (c) C18 steroids (d) C22 steroids (e) C16 steroids
Answer: (a) C21 steroids — Remember the carbon rule: C27 cholesterol → C21 pregnane (progesterone, glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids) → C19 androstane (androgens) → C18 estrane (oestrogens).
29. Coenzymes in the synthesis of mineralocorticoids are:
(a) NAD+ (b) FAD (c) NADPH (d) Coenzyme A (e) only a and c are correct
Answer: (e) Only a and c — The hydroxylases (21-, 11β-, 18-) are cytochrome P450 mono-oxygenases needing NADPH + O2; 3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (pregnenolone → progesterone) needs NAD+. (FAD sits inside adrenodoxin reductase as a prosthetic group, but it is not the "coenzyme" the lecturer lists.)
30. In the biosynthesis of aldosterone, which of the following enzymes is found in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum:
(a) side chain cleavage enzyme (b) 11-beta hydroxylase (c) 18-hydroxylase (d) 21-hydroxylase (e) 18-hydroxy steroid dehydrogenase
Answer: (d) 21-hydroxylase — Microsomal (smooth ER) enzymes: 3β-HSD, 17α-hydroxylase and 21-hydroxylase. Mitochondrial enzymes: side-chain cleavage (desmolase), 11β-hydroxylase and aldosterone synthase (18-hydroxylase / 18-oxidase).
