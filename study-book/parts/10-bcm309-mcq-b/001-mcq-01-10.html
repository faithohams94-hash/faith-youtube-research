Peptide hormones and the renin–angiotensin system (items 1–6)

1. Not a uterine-contracting nona-peptide except:
(a) Vasopressin (b) GSH (c) Oxytocin (d) Glucagon
Answer: (c) Oxytocin — Double negative: "not … except" = "which IS a uterine-contracting nonapeptide". Oxytocin (9 amino acids, posterior pituitary) contracts the uterus. Vasopressin is also a nonapeptide but is antidiuretic/pressor, not uterotonic; GSH is a tripeptide; glucagon has 29 amino acids.
2. Insulin chain "A":
(a) 31 (b) 21 (c) 30 (d) 40
Answer: (b) 21 — Human insulin: A chain 21 amino acids, B chain 30 amino acids (total 51), joined by two interchain disulphide bonds (A7–B7, A20–B19) plus one intrachain bond (A6–A11). Option (c) 30 is the B chain — the commonest trap.
3. Angiotensin II is:
(a) Vasoactive (b) Vasodepressant (c) Vasodilator (d) None
Answer: (a) Vasoactive — Angiotensin II is the most potent natural vasoconstrictor (pressor) and stimulates aldosterone release. "Vasoactive" is the only option that fits; (b) and (c) are the opposite action.
4. Renin is:
(a) A Neurotransmitter (b) An Enzyme (c) Hormone (d) Glycoprotein
Answer: (b) An Enzyme — Renin is a proteolytic enzyme (aspartyl protease) secreted by the juxtaglomerular cells; it cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I. It is sometimes loosely called a "hormone" and is glycosylated, but its defining nature, as both textbooks stress, is enzyme.
5. Angiotensinogen is:
(a) An α2-globulin (b) A β2-globulin (c) An α-globulin (d) A β-globulin
Answer: (a) An α2-globulin — Angiotensinogen is a glycoprotein of the α2-globulin fraction, synthesised in the liver. (c) "α-globulin" is too vague when (a) is offered.
6. Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) is produced in:
(a) zona glomerulosa (b) zona fasciculata (c) zona reticularis (d) A&C (e) None of the above
Answer: (e) None of the above — ADH is synthesised in the supraoptic (and paraventricular) nuclei of the hypothalamus and stored/released from the posterior pituitary. All three zones listed are adrenal cortex (aldosterone, cortisol, androgens).

Hormone basics and classification (items 7–13)

7. True concerning hormones except:
(a) Secreted by exocytosis (b) Produced by specialized ductless glands (c) It must exert its effects on specific tissue/organs (d) It must act at a site very close to its site of production (e) Only b and c are correct
Answer: (d) — "True except" = find the FALSE statement. A hormone is classically carried in blood to act on target cells at a distance (endocrine). Acting close to the site of production is paracrine/autocrine (local hormones). Note: (a) is only strictly true for peptide/catecholamine hormones (steroids diffuse out), but the lecturer's intended answer is clearly (d).
8. Gonadal hormones include the following except:
(a) Follicle stimulating hormone (b) Testosterone (c) Estradiol (d) Progesterone (e) Androstenedione
Answer: (a) FSH — FSH is a gonadotropin from the anterior pituitary (it acts ON the gonads but is not made by them). The rest are steroids made in testis/ovary.
9. Thyroid hormones include the following except:
(a) Calcitonin (b) Parathyroid hormone (c) Thyroxine (d) Triiodothyronine (e) Diiodothyronine
Answer: (b) Parathyroid hormone — PTH comes from the chief cells of the parathyroid glands. Calcitonin is from the parafollicular (C) cells of the thyroid; T4, T3 (and diiodothyronine, a deiodination product) are iodothyronines of the follicular cells.
10. Erythropoietin is produced in the:
(a) juxtaglomerular cells (b) parietal cells (c) oxyphil cells (d) chief cells (e) leydig cells
Answer: (a) juxtaglomerular cells — Erythropoietin is made mainly (about 85–90%) by the kidney; the precise site is the peritubular interstitial cells, but of the options only the renal JG cells fit (several lecture notes state "juxtaglomerular apparatus"). Parietal cells = HCl and intrinsic factor; oxyphil and chief cells = parathyroid/stomach; Leydig cells = testosterone.
