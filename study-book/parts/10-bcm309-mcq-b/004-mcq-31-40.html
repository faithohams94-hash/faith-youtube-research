31. Metabolic effect of mineralocorticoids:
(a) hypervolaemia (b) Acidosis (c) Hyponatraemia (d) hypokalaemia (e) None of the above
Answer (most likely key): (d) Hypokalaemia — Aldosterone causes Na+ reabsorption in exchange for K+ and H+ secretion in the distal tubule/collecting duct → hypokalaemia, metabolic alkalosis (not acidosis) and sodium retention (not hyponatraemia). Two-answer item: (a) hypervolaemia (ECF expansion from Na+/water retention) is also a genuine effect. Hypokalaemia is the specific, always-tested "metabolic/electrolyte" effect, so (d) is the safer choice.
32. Conversion of angiotensinogen to angiotensin I takes place in the:
(a) juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney (b) liver (c) lungs (d) heart (e) intestine
Answer (lecturer's key): (a) Juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney — The converting enzyme is renin, made by the JG cells. (Strictly, renin cleaves angiotensinogen in the circulating plasma; angiotensinogen itself is made in the liver, and the lungs carry ACE for angiotensin I → II.) Do not choose (c) — that is the NEXT step.
33. Clinical features of Addison's disease except:
(a) hypertension (b) hypercalcaemia (c) hyperkalaemia (d) darkening of the skin (e) hyponatraemia
Answer: (a) Hypertension — Addison's disease (primary adrenocortical insufficiency) → loss of aldosterone and cortisol → Na+ loss, hypovolaemia and hypotension, hyperkalaemia, hypoglycaemia; high ACTH/MSH → pigmentation. Mild hypercalcaemia does occur in some patients.

Adrenal medulla disorder (items 34–35)

34. In phaeochromocytoma, which of the hormones is affected:
(a) Aldosterone (b) cortisol (c) Epinephrine (d) glucagon (e) insulin
Answer: (c) Epinephrine — Phaeochromocytoma is a catecholamine-secreting tumour of the chromaffin cells (adrenaline/noradrenaline excess); diagnosed by raised urinary VMA and metanephrines.
35. Not seen in phaeochromocytoma except:
(a) hypotension (b) decreased cardiac output (c) anxiety (d) bradycardia (e) All of the above
Answer: (c) Anxiety — Double negative = "which IS seen". Catecholamine excess → paroxysmal hypertension, headache, palpitations/tachycardia, sweating, anxiety, hyperglycaemia. Hypotension, low cardiac output and bradycardia are the opposite picture.

Gonadal hormones — androgens (items 36–42)

36. The following are sites of production of gonadal hormones except:
(a) placenta (b) corpus luteum (c) adrenal cortex (d) adrenal medulla (e) testes
Answer: (d) Adrenal medulla — The medulla makes catecholamines only. Sex steroids come from testes, ovary (follicle, corpus luteum), placenta (progesterone, oestriol) and adrenal cortex (zona reticularis androgens).
37. True about androgens except:
(a) found in males only (b) found in females only (c) found more in males than females (d) found more in females than males (e) None of the above
Lecturer's most likely key: (c) Found more in males than females — Defective stem: as printed ("true … except" = pick the FALSE one), options (a), (b) and (d) are all false, so no single answer fits. Only (c) is a true statement, which strongly suggests the intended question was "True about androgens" and the "except" crept in. Androgens are present in both sexes (in females from the adrenal zona reticularis and ovarian theca cells) but are much higher in males. In a negative-marked paper this is a reasonable item to leave blank unless the invigilator clarifies.
38. Conversion of testosterone to the more active dihydrotestosterone is catalyzed by:
(a) 17-alpha hydroxylase (b) 17,20-lyase (c) 5-alpha reductase (d) dehydrogenase + isomerase (e) cyt P450 side chain cleavage enzyme
Answer: (c) 5α-reductase — An NADPH-dependent enzyme in prostate, external genitalia and skin. Its deficiency gives ambiguous genitalia in genetic males; its inhibitors (finasteride) treat benign prostatic hyperplasia.
39. Actions of androgens except:
(a) penile enlargement (b) baldness (c) increased musculature (d) decreased libido (e) relaxation of myometrium
Answer: (d) Decreased libido — Androgens increase libido. They enlarge the penis, build muscle (anabolic) and cause male-pattern baldness in genetically prone men. Note: (e) "relaxation of myometrium" is a progesterone action rather than a recognised androgen action, so (e) is a second possible answer; the lecturer clearly built (d) as the reversed fact, so choose (d).
40. Involved in doping in sports:
(a) Anabolic steroids (b) glucagon (c) insulin (d) leutinizing hormone (e) follicle stimulating hormone
Answer: (a) Anabolic steroids — Synthetic testosterone derivatives increase muscle mass and strength; side-effects include testicular atrophy (feedback suppression of LH/FSH), infertility, liver damage, acne and gynaecomastia.
