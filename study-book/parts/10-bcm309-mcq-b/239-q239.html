Examine three specific enzyme markers for the diagnosis of liver dysfunction.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Definition of a diagnostic (marker) enzyme; three named markers — ALT and AST (hepatocellular damage) and ALP (cholestasis), with GGT as a bonus — each with: location in the cell, reaction catalysed, normal range, conditions that raise it, and how to interpret (De Ritis ratio, pattern). A diagram of the hepatocyte showing where each enzyme lives.

Introduction

Enzymes are normally intracellular. When liver cells are damaged, or bile flow is blocked, specific enzymes leak or are over-produced into plasma, where their activity can be measured. Such non-functional plasma enzymes act as markers of liver dysfunction. Liver enzymes fall into two groups: (1) markers of hepatocellular injury — ALT, AST; (2) markers of cholestasis (biliary obstruction) — ALP, GGT, 5′-nucleotidase.

1. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT; formerly SGPT)

  1. Reaction: Alanine + α-ketoglutarate ⇌ Pyruvate + Glutamate (coenzyme pyridoxal phosphate, vitamin B6).
  2. Location: cytosol; highest concentration in the liver → it is the most liver-specific aminotransferase.
  3. Normal serum level: roughly 5–40 U/L (laboratory dependent).
  4. Raised in: acute viral hepatitis (very high, often >10–20 times normal, rising even before jaundice appears), toxic/drug hepatitis (e.g. paracetamol), chronic hepatitis, fatty liver, cirrhosis (mild).

2. Aspartate aminotransferase (AST; formerly SGOT)

  1. Reaction: Aspartate + α-ketoglutarate ⇌ Oxaloacetate + Glutamate (PLP-dependent).
  2. Location: both cytosol (c-AST) and mitochondria (m-AST); found in liver, heart, skeletal muscle, kidney, RBC — therefore less specific than ALT.
  3. Normal serum level: roughly 5–40 U/L.
  4. Raised in: hepatitis, alcoholic liver disease (mitochondrial damage), cirrhosis, hepatic metastases; also myocardial infarction and muscle injury (non-hepatic).
  5. De Ritis ratio (AST/ALT): < 1 in viral hepatitis (ALT rises more); > 2 in alcoholic hepatitis and in cirrhosis (mitochondrial AST released; alcohol also causes B6 deficiency that lowers ALT).

3. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)

  1. Reaction: hydrolyses phosphate monoesters at alkaline pH (about 9–10); requires Zn2+ and Mg2+.
  2. Location in liver: the bile canalicular (sinusoidal-canalicular) membrane of hepatocytes and biliary epithelium.
  3. Normal serum level: about 40–125 U/L in adults (higher in growing children and in pregnancy).
  4. Raised in liver disease: obstructive (cholestatic) jaundice — gallstones, carcinoma head of pancreas — rises markedly (often >3–4 times normal) because bile salts stimulate its synthesis and it regurgitates into blood; also space-occupying lesions (abscess, metastases, hepatoma). Only mild rise in viral hepatitis.
  5. Limitation: bone also releases ALP (rickets, Paget's disease, osteoblastic tumours) — confirm hepatic origin with GGT or 5′-nucleotidase.

Supporting marker: γ-Glutamyl transferase (GGT)

A microsomal/canalicular membrane enzyme that transfers the γ-glutamyl group of glutathione to amino acids (γ-glutamyl cycle). Very sensitive marker of cholestasis and of alcohol intake and enzyme-inducing drugs (phenytoin, phenobarbitone). It is not raised in bone disease, so a high ALP with a high GGT means the ALP is from the liver.

Conclusion

ALT (most liver-specific) and AST indicate hepatocyte injury; ALP (confirmed by GGT) indicates cholestasis. Measuring them together, and reading the pattern rather than a single value, localises the liver lesion.

Understand it (plain English) Think of the hepatocyte as a shop. ALT and AST are goods inside the shop — if the walls break (hepatitis), they spill into the street (blood). ALP and GGT are fixed on the back door that opens into the bile duct — if the drain behind the shop blocks (obstruction), the shop makes more of them and they back up into blood.
Memory trick "ALT = Liver" (L in ALT). "AST = Also Sits in the Ticker" (heart, muscle too). De Ritis: "Scotch > 2" — AST/ALT >2 in alcohol. ALP = "Alkaline = Above the blockage".
How to write this in the exam Define marker enzymes (2 lines) → three numbered headings, each with reaction, location, normal value, conditions → diagram → pattern table → conclusion. Name the coenzyme (PLP) for the aminotransferases — easy extra mark.
Mistakes that cost marks Calling AST "liver-specific" (it is ALT). Forgetting that ALP also comes from bone and placenta. Writing only names without saying what each one diagnoses.
