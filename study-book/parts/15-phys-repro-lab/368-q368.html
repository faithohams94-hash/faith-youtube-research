State the role of testosterone in secondary sexual characteristics (10mks).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants A definition of testosterone and of secondary sexual characters, then a system-by-system list (hair, voice, skin, muscles, bones/body shape, external genitalia, behaviour, blood/metabolism) with the mechanism in one line each. 10 marks = about 8–10 good points plus a diagram.

Introduction

Testosterone is the principal male sex hormone (androgen). It is a C-19 steroid secreted by the interstitial cells of Leydig of the testis under the influence of LH (ICSH) from the anterior pituitary. Normal plasma level in adult male ≈ 300–1000 ng/dL. In many target tissues it is converted by 5α-reductase into the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT).

Secondary sexual characteristics are the features that distinguish the male from the female but are not directly concerned with reproduction. In the male they appear at puberty (12–14 years) when testosterone secretion rises, and are maintained by it throughout adult life.

Role of testosterone in male secondary sexual characteristics

1. Hair distribution (androgenic hair)
  1. Growth of hair on the face (beard, moustache), chest, axilla, limbs and back.
  2. Pubic hair with the male pattern — extends upwards along the linea alba towards the umbilicus (rhomboid/diamond shape) instead of the flat-topped female triangle.
  3. Recession of the hairline at the temples and, in genetically predisposed men, baldness (androgen + genetic factor).
2. Voice
  1. Testosterone causes hypertrophy of the laryngeal mucosa and enlargement of the larynx (prominent Adam's apple); the vocal cords become longer and thicker → voice first cracks, then becomes deep and low-pitched (masculine).
3. Skin
  1. Skin becomes thick and rough; increased melanin → slightly darker skin.
  2. Increased secretion of sebaceous glands → oily skin and, when ducts block, acne at puberty.
4. Muscles
  1. Testosterone is anabolic: increases protein synthesis and decreases breakdown → muscle mass increases by about 50% compared with the female; muscular, broad body build.
5. Bones and body shape
  1. Increases the bone matrix and deposition of calcium → bones become thicker and heavier.
  2. Broad shoulders; the pelvis becomes narrow, long and funnel-shaped (android pelvis) with a narrow outlet.
  3. Causes the pubertal growth spurt, then early fusion of epiphyses with the shafts of long bones, so linear growth stops.
6. External genitalia
  1. Enlargement of the penis and scrotum (scrotum becomes pigmented and rugose); growth of testes and accessory sex glands (prostate, seminal vesicles) at puberty.
7. Blood and metabolism
  1. Increases BMR by about 5–10%.
  2. Stimulates erythropoietin secretion → higher RBC count and haemoglobin in males than females.
  3. Slight retention of sodium and water (mineralocorticoid-like effect).
8. Behaviour (psychic effects)
  1. Development of libido (sexual desire), aggressive and assertive behaviour, and male attitude.

Mechanism of action (one line)

Testosterone (or DHT) enters the target cell, binds the cytoplasmic/nuclear androgen receptor; the hormone–receptor complex binds DNA → transcription of mRNA → synthesis of new proteins (e.g. structural proteins of muscle, enzymes of sebaceous glands).

Applied

  • Hypogonadism / castration before puberty (eunuchism): secondary sexual characters fail to appear — infantile genitalia, high-pitched voice, scanty hair, long limbs (delayed epiphyseal fusion), feminine fat distribution.
  • Testosterone-secreting tumours in a child cause precocious puberty.

Conclusion

Testosterone, secreted by Leydig cells under LH control, is responsible for the masculine hair pattern, deep voice, thick oily skin, muscular build, heavy bones with narrow pelvis, adult genitalia and male behaviour that appear at puberty.

Understand it (plain English) Before puberty, a boy and a girl look much alike. At puberty LH rises, Leydig cells pour out testosterone, and testosterone "builds" a man: it is anabolic (more muscle, heavier bone), it acts on skin glands (oil, acne), on hair follicles (beard), on the larynx (deep voice) and on the brain (libido). Remove testosterone before puberty and none of this happens — that is why eunuchs keep a child-like voice.
Memory trick "HV-SMB-GB" — Hair, Voice, Skin, Muscle, Bone, Genitalia, Behaviour (+ blood). Say: "His Voice Sounds Manly; Big Genitals, Bold Behaviour".
How to write this in the exam 12 minutes. Intro (source, LH control, DHT) → numbered sub-headings for each system → spoke diagram → applied (eunuchism) → one-line conclusion. Use the phrases "anabolic", "androgen receptor", "android pelvis", "epiphyseal fusion".
Mistakes that cost marks (1) Listing primary sex characters (spermatogenesis, testis) as secondary. (2) Saying testosterone comes from Sertoli cells — it is Leydig. (3) Saying testosterone makes people taller for ever — it causes a spurt and then stops growth by fusing epiphyses.
