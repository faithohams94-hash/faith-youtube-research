Mention the functions of Sertoli cells (8mks).

Introduction

Sertoli cells (sustentacular or "nurse" cells) are tall columnar cells of the seminiferous tubule extending from the basement membrane to the lumen. Developing germ cells are embedded in recesses of their cytoplasm. Adjacent Sertoli cells are joined near the base by tight junctions, which divide the epithelium into a basal and an adluminal compartment. They are the target cells of FSH and testosterone.

Functions of Sertoli cells

A. Supportive / protective functions
  1. Support and nutrition of developing germ cells — provide mechanical support and supply nutrients (lactate, glucose) and other substances to spermatocytes and spermatids, which are cut off from blood.
  2. Blood–testis barrier — tight junctions prevent entry of toxic substances and large molecules from blood into the adluminal compartment, and keep sperm antigens away from the immune system (prevent auto-immune destruction of sperm).
  3. Phagocytosis — engulf the residual bodies (excess cytoplasm) shed by spermatids and degenerating germ cells.
  4. Spermiation — release of mature spermatozoa from the Sertoli cell into the lumen; also assist movement of germ cells towards the lumen.
  5. Secretion of tubular fluid rich in K⁺, bicarbonate and ABP, which carries sperm towards the epididymis.
B. Secretory (endocrine/paracrine) functions
  1. Androgen-binding protein (ABP) — secreted under FSH; binds testosterone and keeps its concentration high in the tubule and epididymis.
  2. Inhibin — inhibits FSH secretion from the anterior pituitary (negative feedback).
  3. Activin — stimulates FSH secretion.
  4. Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH / Müllerian inhibiting substance) — in fetal life causes regression of the Müllerian ducts in the male.
  5. Oestrogen — Sertoli cells contain aromatase that converts testosterone to oestradiol, needed for spermiogenesis.
  6. Other products: transferrin (iron transport to germ cells), growth factors.
C. Mediation of hormonal control
  1. Possess receptors for FSH and testosterone; these hormones act on spermatogenesis through Sertoli cells.

Applied

Sertoli-cell-only syndrome: tubules contain only Sertoli cells, no germ cells → azoospermia and infertility; FSH is raised (no inhibin feedback).

Conclusion

The Sertoli cell is the "nurse" of spermatogenesis — it supports, protects (blood–testis barrier), cleans up (phagocytosis), releases sperm and secretes ABP, inhibin, activin, AMH and oestrogen.

Understand it (plain English) Germ cells after meiosis are "foreign" to your own immune system (they carry new gene combinations). The Sertoli cells build a wall (tight junctions) so immune cells cannot reach them, then feed them through the wall like a nurse feeds a patient in isolation, and finally push the finished sperm out of the door (spermiation).
