Highlight the necessary parameters in semen analysis (9mks).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Definition of semen and semen analysis, collection conditions, then parameters in three groups — macroscopic (volume, colour, pH, viscosity, liquefaction), microscopic (count, motility, morphology, vitality, other cells) and biochemical (fructose etc.) — each with normal values, plus the terms for abnormal results.

Definition

Semen is the white, opalescent fluid ejaculated during orgasm, made of spermatozoa suspended in seminal plasma (≈60% from seminal vesicles, ≈20–30% prostate, small amounts from vas, bulbourethral and urethral glands). Semen analysis (seminogram) is the laboratory evaluation of semen and is the first investigation of male infertility.

A. Collection of the sample

  1. Sexual abstinence for 2–7 days (usually 3–5 days) before collection.
  2. Collected by masturbation into a clean, dry, wide-mouthed sterile container (not an ordinary condom — contains spermicides).
  3. The whole ejaculate must be collected; delivered to the lab within 1 hour, kept near body temperature.
  4. Two samples, 2–3 weeks apart, are examined because counts vary.

D. Biochemical parameters

  1. Fructose (from seminal vesicles; energy source of sperm) — absent in congenital absence of vas deferens/seminal vesicles or ejaculatory duct obstruction.
  2. Acid phosphatase, citric acid, zinc — markers of prostatic function.
  3. Neutral α-glucosidase/carnitine — markers of epididymal function.

Terminology to quote

  • Normozoospermia — all parameters normal. Oligo-astheno-teratozoospermia (OAT) — all three abnormal.
  • Azoospermia — no sperm (obstructive vs non-obstructive/testicular failure). Aspermia — no ejaculate.

Conclusion

A complete semen analysis records volume, appearance, pH, liquefaction, viscosity, sperm count, motility, morphology, vitality, leucocytes, agglutination and fructose, each compared with reference values.

Understand it (plain English) Ask three questions of the semen: How much and what is it like? (volume, colour, pH, liquefaction — tells you about the glands), How many sperms and are they good? (count, motility, shape, alive — tells you about the testis and epididymis), and What is in the fluid? (fructose — tells you the seminal vesicles are present and connected).
Memory trick Microscopy = "CMMV" → Count, Motility, Morphology, Vitality. WHO 2010 numbers: "1.5 – 15 – 40 – 32 – 4 – 58" (volume mL, million/mL, total motility %, progressive %, normal forms %, live %).
How to write this in the exam Start with collection (examiners love "3–5 days abstinence, within 1 hour"). Then two tables. "Highlight" means you can be brief per item but must have the normal value next to each parameter.
Mistakes that cost marks Giving counts without units (million per mL). Confusing aspermia (no semen) with azoospermia (no sperm in semen). Forgetting liquefaction time and fructose.
