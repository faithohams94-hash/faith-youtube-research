(a) Role of FSH and LH in the development of the uterine endometrium

FSH and LH from the anterior pituitary do not act directly on the uterus; they act on the ovary, and the ovarian hormones (oestrogen and progesterone) bring about the endometrial changes of the uterine (menstrual) cycle (28 days).

  1. FSH (follicular phase, days 1–14): stimulates growth of primordial follicles into primary, secondary and Graafian follicles; granulosa cells (with aromatase) convert theca androgens into oestrogen. FSH also induces LH receptors on granulosa cells.
  2. LH: stimulates theca interna cells to produce androgens (substrate for oestrogen). The LH surge (day 13–14, triggered by positive feedback of high oestrogen) causes ovulation and converts the ruptured follicle into the corpus luteum; LH then maintains the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone and oestrogen.
  3. Oestrogen → proliferative phase (days 5–14): regeneration of the endometrium from the basal layer; proliferation of epithelial and stromal cells; elongation of straight tubular glands; growth of spiral arteries; thickness increases to ≈3–4 mm; induces progesterone receptors.
  4. Progesterone (+ oestrogen) → secretory phase (days 15–28): glands become tortuous and coiled, secrete glycogen-rich "uterine milk"; stroma becomes oedematous; spiral arteries coil further; endometrium becomes ≈5–6 mm thick and ready for implantation.
FSH→Follicle → oestrogen→Proliferative endometrium→LH surge → corpus luteum→Progesterone → secretory endometrium

(b) Effect of decreased progesterone and oestrogen secretion by the ovary

Within a cycle (no pregnancy): if the ovum is not fertilised, the corpus luteum degenerates (luteolysis) about day 24–26 → sudden fall of progesterone and oestrogen. Effects:

  1. Involution of the endometrium — loss of hormonal support → endometrium shrinks to 65% of its thickness.
  2. Vasospasm of spiral arteries — caused by vasoconstrictor prostaglandins (PGF₂α) released from the endometrium → ischaemia.
  3. Necrosis and desquamation of the functional (superficial) layer; lysosomal enzymes released; vessels rupture → menstrual bleeding (≈35 mL blood + 35 mL serous fluid; non-clotting because of fibrinolysin).
  4. Uterine contractions (prostaglandins) expel the debris.
  5. Loss of negative feedback on hypothalamus and pituitary → FSH and LH rise → a new set of follicles begins to grow → next cycle.

Permanent decrease (menopause, 45–55 yrs; or ovariectomy): cessation of menstruation; hot flushes (vasomotor instability due to raised FSH/LH); atrophy of uterus, vagina and breasts; dryness of vagina; osteoporosis (loss of oestrogen's bone-protective action); increased risk of atherosclerosis (loss of HDL-raising effect); psychological changes (irritability, depression).

(c) Mechanism by which the sperm enters the egg (fertilisation)

  1. Capacitation — in the female genital tract (5–6 hours), the decapacitation factor and cholesterol are removed from the sperm membrane; membrane becomes permeable to Ca²⁺; sperm become hyperactive and able to undergo the acrosome reaction.
  2. Penetration of corona radiata — by tail movements and hyaluronidase released from the acrosome, which dissolves the hyaluronic acid cementing the corona cells.
  3. Binding to zona pellucida — the sperm head binds to the glycoprotein receptor ZP3.
  4. Acrosome reaction — ZP3 binding triggers Ca²⁺ influx; the outer acrosomal membrane fuses with the sperm plasma membrane and releases acrosin (proteolytic) and other enzymes, which digest a path through the zona pellucida.
  5. Fusion with oocyte membrane — the sperm lies in the perivitelline space; its plasma membrane fuses with the oolemma, and the head and tail enter the cytoplasm.
  6. Cortical reaction / zona reaction — Ca²⁺ wave in the oocyte causes cortical granules to release enzymes into the perivitelline space; they alter ZP3/ZP2 so no further sperm can bind → prevents polyspermy.
  7. Completion of meiosis II by the secondary oocyte → mature ovum + second polar body; male and female pronuclei form, fuse → diploid zygote (46 chromosomes).

(d) Differentiation of the internal and external genitalia

Up to the 7th week the embryo is indifferent (bipotential): it has both Wolffian (mesonephric) and Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts and an undifferentiated genital tubercle, folds and swellings. The default (no hormones) pathway is female.

Key point: internal male ducts need testosterone + AMH; external male genitalia need DHT (testosterone converted by 5α-reductase). In 5α-reductase deficiency, internal ducts are male but external genitalia are ambiguous/female-like.

(e) Differentiation of the gonads

  1. The gonad starts as an indifferent gonad (genital ridge) with a cortex and medulla; primordial germ cells migrate into it from the yolk sac wall (week 5–6).
  2. Male: the SRY gene on the short arm of the Y chromosome codes for testis-determining factor (TDF). In week 6–7, TDF causes the medulla to develop into seminiferous (testis) cords with Sertoli cells, while the cortex regresses → testis. Leydig cells appear and secrete testosterone by week 8–9.
  3. Female: in the absence of the Y chromosome/SRY (XX), the cortex develops and the medulla regresses → ovary (later, around week 10); germ cells become oogonia and primary oocytes that enter meiosis and arrest in prophase I.
  4. Thus genetic sex (XX/XY) determines gonadal sex, and gonadal hormones determine phenotypic sex (ducts and genitalia).

Conclusion

Pituitary FSH/LH build the endometrium through ovarian steroids, whose withdrawal causes menstruation; fertilisation depends on the acrosome and cortical reactions; and the Y-chromosome SRY gene directs the testis, whose AMH, testosterone and DHT masculinise the ducts and genitalia.

Understand it (plain English) The female body is the "default setting". To make a male you need three switches: SRY (turns the gonad into a testis), AMH (deletes the female ducts) and testosterone/DHT (builds the male ducts and outside parts). For the endometrium: oestrogen "builds the walls", progesterone "furnishes the room" for the baby, and when both are withdrawn the furniture and walls are thrown out — that is menstruation.
