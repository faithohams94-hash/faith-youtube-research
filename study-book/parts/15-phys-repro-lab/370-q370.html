Enumerate the necessary hormones for spermatogenesis (8mks).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants "Enumerate" = a numbered list, but each hormone needs its source and exact role in spermatogenesis for full marks. The key list (Sembulingam): FSH, LH (via testosterone), testosterone, oestrogen, growth hormone, inhibin, activin — plus GnRH as the master controller. Add the feedback diagram.

Definition

Spermatogenesis is the process by which spermatogonia in the seminiferous tubules develop into mature spermatozoa. It begins at puberty, takes about 74 days in man, and is controlled by hormones acting mainly through the Sertoli cells and Leydig cells.

Hormones necessary for spermatogenesis

#Hormone (source)Role in spermatogenesis
1GnRH (hypothalamus, pulsatile)Stimulates the anterior pituitary to secrete FSH and LH; without it neither is released (e.g. Kallmann syndrome → no spermatogenesis).
2FSH (anterior pituitary)Acts on Sertoli cells: stimulates their proliferation and secretion of androgen-binding protein (ABP), nutrients and fluid; converts testosterone to oestrogen (aromatase); essential for initiation of spermatogenesis and for spermiogenesis (conversion of spermatids to sperm).
3LH / ICSH (anterior pituitary)Acts on Leydig cells to secrete testosterone; hence indirectly essential.
4Testosterone (Leydig cells)Necessary for growth and division of germ cells, especially the meiotic division of primary spermatocytes into secondary spermatocytes; ABP keeps a very high local concentration in the tubule; maintains spermatogenesis once started.
5Oestrogen (formed from testosterone in Sertoli cells)Needed for spermiogenesis; in small amounts essential for normal spermatogenesis.
6Growth hormone (anterior pituitary)Needed for the general metabolic functions of the testis and promotes early division of spermatogonia; in GH deficiency (pituitary dwarfism) spermatogenesis is severely deficient.
7Inhibin (Sertoli cells)Negative feedback on FSH secretion from the anterior pituitary — regulates the rate of spermatogenesis (high rate → more inhibin → less FSH).
8Activin (Sertoli cells)Opposite of inhibin — increases FSH secretion.

(Other permissive hormones: thyroxine for general metabolism; prolactin enhances LH receptor expression on Leydig cells.)

Summary of sequence

GnRH→FSH + LH→Sertoli (ABP) + Leydig (testosterone)→Spermatogonia → sperm

Conclusion

FSH initiates and testosterone maintains spermatogenesis; LH is needed for testosterone, GH and oestrogen are permissive, and inhibin/activin fine-tune FSH by feedback.

Understand it (plain English) Two cells, two hormones: FSH talks to Sertoli ("F–S"), LH talks to Leydig ("L–L"). Leydig makes testosterone, which seeps into the tubule; Sertoli makes ABP that "grabs" testosterone and keeps it concentrated around the dividing germ cells. Sertoli also reports back to the pituitary with inhibin: "enough sperm, slow down FSH".
Memory trick "LL – FS": LH→Leydig, FSH→Sertoli. Hormone list = "Good Females Like Tall Equal Guys In Africa": GnRH, FSH, LH, Testosterone, Estrogen, GH, Inhibin, Activin.
How to write this in the exam 8 marks ≈ 10 minutes. Write the table (hormone · source · role) — one mark per hormone — then the feedback diagram for the last marks.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying LH acts on Sertoli cells or FSH on Leydig cells. Forgetting that inhibin inhibits FSH only, not LH. Writing "ICSH" without explaining it is LH in the male.
