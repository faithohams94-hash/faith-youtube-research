Classify the cartilages of the larynx. [MK2010]

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Nine cartilages classified in two ways: (1) unpaired (thyroid, cricoid, epiglottis) vs paired (arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform); (2) by histology — hyaline vs elastic, with the note that hyaline ones may calcify with age. One line on each cartilage + a sketch completes it.

Introduction

The larynx is the organ of phonation and the guarded entrance to the lower airway, lying opposite C3–C6 vertebrae. Its skeleton is made of nine cartilages — three unpaired and three paired — joined by joints, ligaments and membranes.

Classification 1 — by number

Unpaired (3)Paired (3 pairs = 6)
1. Thyroid cartilage — largest; two laminae meet in front at the laryngeal prominence (Adam's apple)
2. Cricoid cartilage — signet-ring shaped; the only complete ring in the airway
3. Epiglottis — leaf-shaped, behind the tongue and hyoid		 1. Arytenoid — pyramidal; sit on the upper border of cricoid lamina; vocal process (vocal ligament) and muscular process
2. Corniculate — small nodules on the apex of each arytenoid
3. Cuneiform — rod-like nodules in the aryepiglottic folds

Classification 2 — by type of cartilage (histology)

Hyaline cartilageElastic cartilage
1. Thyroid
2. Cricoid
3. Arytenoid (most of it — the base / bulk)		 1. Epiglottis
2. Corniculate
3. Cuneiform
4. Apex and vocal process of the arytenoid
May calcify / ossify in later life (seen on X-ray)Do not calcify; remain flexible

Note: small inconstant triticeal cartilages may lie in the lateral thyrohyoid ligaments; they are not counted among the nine.

Clinical note

  • The cricoid is the only complete cartilaginous ring of the airway — pressure on it (cricoid pressure) occludes the oesophagus behind it.
  • The cricothyroid membrane (between thyroid and cricoid) is the site for emergency airway (cricothyrotomy).
  • Calcified hyaline cartilages may be mistaken for foreign bodies on X-ray.

Conclusion

Nine cartilages: 3 unpaired (thyroid, cricoid, epiglottis) and 3 paired (arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform); hyaline ones (thyroid, cricoid, most of arytenoid) can calcify, elastic ones do not.

Understand it (plain English) The big, load-bearing cartilages (thyroid, cricoid, arytenoid body) are made of hyaline cartilage — rigid, and like any hyaline cartilage they can calcify with age. The small flexible ones that must bend when you swallow or move the folds (epiglottis, corniculate, cuneiform, arytenoid tip) are elastic cartilage.
Memory trick Unpaired = "TCE" — The Cricket Eye (Thyroid, Cricoid, Epiglottis). Paired = "ACC" — Arytenoid, Corniculate, Cuneiform (the three C/A pairs on the back).
Elastic = the "bendy" ones: E-C-C (Epiglottis, Corniculate, Cuneiform).
How to write this in the exam "Classify" means a classification, not a description. Give BOTH classifications as two small tables, then one line on each cartilage. The diagram is optional but quick and wins the extra mark.
Mistakes that cost marks Including the hyoid bone among laryngeal cartilages (it is a bone, suspended above the larynx). Saying the epiglottis is hyaline. Forgetting that the arytenoid is mixed (hyaline body, elastic apex/vocal process).
