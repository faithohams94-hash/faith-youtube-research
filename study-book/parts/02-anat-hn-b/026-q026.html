Give the three coats of the eyeball, their disposition, and a function of each. [MK2010]

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
Asked before The whole eyeball in section is asked in Q22. Below is the answer tailored to THIS wording — coats, their arrangement ("disposition"), and one function each.
What the examiner wants Three coats named correctly — outer fibrous (sclera + cornea), middle vascular / uvea (choroid, ciliary body, iris), inner nervous (retina) — with the parts of each and where they lie (posterior 5/6 vs anterior 1/6, etc.), plus a function for each part. A labelled section earns the remaining marks.

Introduction

The eyeball is the organ of sight, about 24 mm in diameter, lying in the anterior part of the orbit. Its wall is made of three concentric coats, which enclose the refracting media (aqueous humour, lens, vitreous body).

CoatParts and dispositionFunction(s)
1. Outer fibrous coat Sclera — opaque, white, forms the posterior five-sixths; pierced behind by the optic nerve at the lamina cribrosa.
Cornea — transparent, forms the anterior one-sixth; more convex; joins sclera at the sclerocorneal junction (limbus).		 Sclera: protects the eye, maintains its shape (with intraocular pressure) and gives attachment to the extraocular muscles.
Cornea: transparent window that refracts light — the chief refracting surface of the eye; avascular.
2. Middle vascular coat (uvea / uveal tract) Choroid — thin pigmented vascular layer lining the posterior five-sixths, between sclera and retina; ends in front at the ora serrata.
Ciliary body — thickened middle part, a ring behind the sclerocorneal junction; contains ciliary muscle and ciliary processes.
Iris — the anterior-most part, a coloured diaphragm in front of the lens, with a central aperture, the pupil.		 Choroid: nourishes the outer layers of the retina; its pigment absorbs stray light.
Ciliary body: ciliary muscle brings about accommodation for near vision; ciliary processes secrete aqueous humour and anchor the suspensory ligament of the lens.
Iris: regulates the amount of light entering the eye by altering pupil size (sphincter pupillae — constricts, dilator pupillae — dilates).
3. Inner nervous coat Retina — lines the inner surface of the choroid. Optic part (photosensitive) extends from the optic disc to the ora serrata; the non-nervous ciliary and iridial parts continue forward over the ciliary body and back of the iris. Photoreception: rods (dim light) and cones (bright light, colour) convert light into nerve impulses which leave by the optic nerve. Macula lutea / fovea centralis = area of most acute vision.

Clinical note

  • Uveitis = inflammation of the vascular coat (iritis, cyclitis, choroiditis).
  • Retinal detachment occurs between the pigment layer and the rest of the retina (the plane of the embryonic optic vesicle cavity).
  • Corneal transparency depends on its avascularity and regular arrangement of collagen; corneal grafts are rarely rejected because it is avascular.

Conclusion

Outside-in: fibrous coat to protect and refract, vascular coat to nourish, accommodate and control light, nervous coat to receive the image.

Understand it (plain English) Picture a camera. The tough white outer shell (sclera) is the camera body; the clear cornea is the front glass. The middle "uvea" is the dark, blood-rich lining that feeds the film and blackens the inside — and in front it becomes the focusing ring (ciliary body) and the aperture (iris). The retina is the film (or sensor) that actually catches the picture.
Memory trick Coats from outside in: "F-V-N" — Fibrous, Vascular, Nervous. Uvea from back to front: "CCI" — Choroid, Ciliary body, Iris. Fractions: sclera 5/6, cornea 1/6.
How to write this in the exam Use the 3-column table — it answers "coat, disposition, function" in one glance. Then the diagram. Use the word uveal tract and ora serrata — they mark you out.
Mistakes that cost marks Calling the iris part of the fibrous coat; forgetting that the retina has non-nervous ciliary and iridial parts in front of the ora serrata; giving "vision" as the function of the choroid.
