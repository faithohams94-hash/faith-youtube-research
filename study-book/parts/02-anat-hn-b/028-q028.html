Name the three types of papillae of the tongue, giving two examples of each. [MK2010]

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
Asked before Papillae are part of every tongue essay — see Q13, Q14 and Q35. Below is the answer tailored to THIS wording.
What the examiner wants The three main types — vallate, fungiform, filiform (plus mention of the rudimentary foliate) — and for each, two distinguishing points ("examples" here means two features: number/site and structure/taste buds). Show them on a dorsum sketch.

Introduction

Lingual papillae are projections of the mucous membrane (lamina propria covered by epithelium) on the anterior two-thirds (presulcal part) of the dorsum of the tongue, in front of the sulcus terminalis. They make the surface rough for handling food and many of them carry taste buds. The posterior one-third has no papillae; instead it has lymphoid follicles (lingual tonsil).

Interpretation: since each type has no "sub-examples", the two points asked for each type are best given as two characteristic features.

TypePoint 1 — number & sitePoint 2 — structure & taste
1. Vallate (circumvallate)Large, 8–12 in number, arranged in a V-shaped row just in front of the sulcus terminalisCylindrical, sunk in the surface and surrounded by a circular trench (hence "walled"); taste buds on their side walls; serous glands (of von Ebner) open into the trench. Supplied by the glossopharyngeal nerve even though they lie in front of the sulcus.
2. FungiformNumerous, scattered mainly at the tip and margins of the tongueMushroom-shaped (narrow stalk, broad top); seen as bright red dots (thin epithelium over vascular core); carry taste buds on their upper surface.
3. Filiform (conical)Most numerous, cover the whole presulcal dorsum, arranged in rows parallel to the sulcus terminalisSmall, pointed/conical, keratinised tips that give the tongue its velvety appearance; help grip food; no taste buds — purely tactile/mechanical.
(4. Foliate)On the sides of the posterior part of the tongue, in front of the palatoglossal foldSeries of leaf-like folds; rudimentary in man (well developed in some animals); may carry taste buds.

Taste pathway note

Taste from fungiform papillae (anterior two-thirds) → chorda tympani (facial nerve); from vallate papillae and posterior one-third → glossopharyngeal nerve.

Conclusion

Vallate (large, walled, V-row, taste), fungiform (red, mushroom, tip/margins, taste) and filiform (numerous, conical, velvety, no taste) papillae — with foliate rudimentary in man.

Understand it (plain English) Run your tongue on the roof of your mouth: the rough velvet feel comes from the countless tiny filiform cones (grip, not taste). The red dots at the tip are fungiform "mushrooms" (taste). At the back of the front part sits a "V" of big walled papillae — vallate — like castles with a moat; taste buds line the moat walls.
Memory trick "V-F-F": Vallate = V-shaped row, walled like a Vault; Fungiform = Fungus (mushroom) at the Front (tip); Filiform = Fine threads, Feel only (no taste).
How to write this in the exam A 3-row table (type, site/number, structure/taste) answers "two examples of each" cleanly. Add foliate as a footnote to show depth. Finish with the dorsum diagram.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying filiform papillae carry taste buds; placing vallate papillae behind the sulcus (they are just in front of it); saying vallate papillae are supplied by chorda tympani (they are glossopharyngeal).
