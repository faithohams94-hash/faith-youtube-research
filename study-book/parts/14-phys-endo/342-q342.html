Explain: Diabetogenic and Ketogenic effects of growth hormone; Relationship between Parathyroid hormone and Osteolytic pump; Antagonism between Parathyroid hormone and Calcitonin (9mks).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Three short explanations of ~3 marks each. (a) GH ↑ blood glucose (anti-insulin, "metahypophyseal diabetes") and ↑ ketone bodies from fat mobilisation. (b) PTH activates the calcium pump of the osteocytic membrane system → rapid phase of bone resorption. (c) PTH raises, calcitonin lowers plasma Ca2+ — opposite actions on bone and kidney, both controlled by plasma Ca2+.

(a) Diabetogenic and ketogenic effects of growth hormone

Diabetogenic effect — GH raises blood glucose (an anti-insulin, "diabetogenic" hormone):

  1. Decreases peripheral utilisation of glucose by skeletal muscle and adipose tissue (reduces glucose uptake — insulin resistance).
  2. Increases hepatic glucose output — increases gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis in the liver.
  3. Increases glycogen deposition in cells because glucose is not used (fat is used instead).
  4. The resulting hyperglycaemia stimulates the β-cells of the islets to secrete more insulin; prolonged excess GH exhausts the β-cells → permanent diabetes, called metahypophyseal (pituitary) diabetes — seen in acromegaly/gigantism.

Ketogenic effect — GH mobilises fat:

  1. Increases lipolysis — release of free fatty acids from adipose tissue into the blood.
  2. Increases conversion of fatty acids to acetyl-CoA (β-oxidation) and their use for energy (protein- and glucose-sparing).
  3. When fat mobilisation is excessive, acetyl-CoA exceeds the capacity of the citric acid cycle → ketone bodies (acetoacetic acid, β-hydroxybutyric acid, acetone) → ketosis. This is the ketogenic effect.
↑ GH→↓ glucose uptake + ↑ hepatic output→Hyperglycaemia (diabetogenic)
↑ GH→↑ lipolysis → ↑ FFA→↑ acetyl-CoA→Ketone bodies (ketogenic)

(b) Parathyroid hormone and the osteolytic (calcium) pump

PTH raises plasma calcium by resorbing bone in two phases:

  1. Rapid phase (osteolysis) — within minutes to hours. Osteoblasts and osteocytes form an interconnected osteocytic membrane system that separates the bone fluid (around bone crystals) from the extracellular fluid. Its membranes carry a calcium pump ("osteolytic pump"). PTH binds receptors on these cells and activates the pump → Ca2+ (and phosphate) is pumped from the bone fluid into the ECF. Removal of calcium from bone fluid lets more calcium dissolve from the labile, amorphous bone salts. No new cells are needed, so the effect is fast — this is osteocytic osteolysis.
  2. Slow phase — days to weeks. PTH (acting via osteoblasts, which have the receptors) activates existing osteoclasts and promotes formation of new osteoclasts → resorption of bone matrix and mineral.

(c) Antagonism between PTH and calcitonin

Normal plasma calcium is 9–11 mg/dL. PTH (parathyroid chief cells) is the hypercalcaemic hormone; calcitonin (parafollicular "C" cells of the thyroid) is the hypocalcaemic hormone. They act on the same targets in opposite directions — physiological antagonism.

Conclusion

GH spares glucose by burning fat (diabetogenic + ketogenic); PTH defends plasma calcium rapidly through the osteocytic calcium pump; PTH and calcitonin antagonise each other to keep plasma Ca2+ in the narrow 9–11 mg/dL range.

Understand it (plain English) GH wants the body to grow, so it saves glucose and protein for building and pushes the body to burn fat instead. Burning lots of fat produces ketones, and starving cells of glucose raises blood sugar. For PTH: bone is like a bank. The pump is the ATM — instant cash (calcium) from the loose fluid around bone crystals. Osteoclasts are the bank officers who later break into the vault (bone matrix) — slower but bigger.
Memory trick "Calci-TONE-in tones down calcium"; "PTH = Pulls The calcium Higher". PTH: "Bones, Stones (kidney), Groans (gut)" — three targets.
How to write this in the exam Three sub-headings, ~4 minutes each. For (a) give 3–4 numbered mechanisms each and name "metahypophyseal diabetes". For (b) the phrase "osteocytic membrane system" and "rapid phase vs slow phase". For (c) a table is the fastest way to score — then one sentence that both respond to plasma Ca2+ (negative feedback).
Mistakes that cost marks Saying GH lowers blood sugar (that is IGF-1/insulin-like at very high doses — not the classical effect). Saying calcitonin is the main calcium regulator in adults. Forgetting that PTH increases phosphate excretion (not retention).
