Differentiate between: Gigantism and Acromegaly; Dwarfism and Cretinism (6mks).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Two comparison tables. Key discriminators: epiphyses open vs fused (gigantism vs acromegaly); GH deficiency with normal intelligence and proportionate body vs congenital thyroid deficiency with mental retardation and disproportionate body (dwarfism vs cretinism).

Definitions

Gigantism — hypersecretion of GH before fusion of the epiphyses (in childhood/adolescence). Acromegaly — hypersecretion of GH after fusion of the epiphyses (in adults). Pituitary dwarfism — deficiency of GH in childhood. Cretinism — deficiency of thyroid hormone from birth or infancy (congenital hypothyroidism).

(a) Gigantism versus acromegaly

FeatureGigantismAcromegaly
Age at onsetChildhood, before puberty endsAdult life
EpiphysesNot fused — long bones grow in lengthFused — bones cannot lengthen; grow in thickness (periosteal growth)
HeightVery tall (may exceed 7–8 feet); body proportionateNormal height
Characteristic featuresGeneral overgrowth of all tissues and organsEnlarged hands and feet (acral parts), prognathism (protruding lower jaw), prominent supraorbital ridges, broad nose, thick lips, enlarged tongue, kyphosis, thick skin, visceromegaly
Common causeTumour (adenoma) of GH-secreting acidophil (somatotroph) cells of the anterior pituitary
MetabolicBoth may show hyperglycaemia → metahypophyseal diabetes; later pituitary failure may follow tumour growth

(b) Dwarfism (pituitary) versus cretinism

FeaturePituitary dwarfismCretinism
CauseDeficiency of GH in childhood (pituitary tumour, atrophy, infection; or GH-receptor/IGF defects)Deficiency of thyroid hormone from birth/infancy — congenital absence of thyroid, iodine deficiency, defective hormone synthesis
Body proportionsProportionate — small but well proportionedDisproportionate — short limbs, relatively long trunk
Mental statusNormal intelligenceMental retardation (thyroid hormone needed for brain development)
AppearanceChild-like face; normal skinCoarse dry skin, large protruding tongue, flat nose, potbelly, umbilical hernia, thick lips
Sexual developmentUsually normal (delayed if gonadotropins are also deficient)Delayed / retarded
BMRNormalLow; cold intolerance, sluggishness
Bone ageDelayedMarkedly delayed; delayed dentition

Note: Other types of dwarfism (Sembulingam): Laron dwarfism (GH receptor defect — high GH, low IGF-1) and African pygmies (inability to produce IGF-1); achondroplasia is non-endocrine.

Conclusion

Same hormone (GH) excess gives gigantism or acromegaly depending on epiphyseal fusion; short stature with normal mind suggests GH deficiency, with mental retardation suggests cretinism.

Understand it (plain English) GH can only make a bone longer while the growth plate is still open. After puberty the plate closes, so extra GH just makes bones thicker and soft tissues bigger — hands, feet, jaw. Thyroid hormone is needed for the brain as well as bones, so a thyroid-deficient child is both short and mentally slow; a GH-deficient child is short but bright.
Memory trick Acro = extremities, megaly = enlargement → big hands, feet, jaw. Cretinism "3 Ps": Pot belly, Protruding tongue, Poor mind (mental retardation).
How to write this in the exam 6 marks ≈ 8 minutes. One-line definitions of all four, then two tables with at least 5 rows each. Put "epiphyses fused/unfused" and "intelligence normal/retarded" in the first rows — those are the marking points.
Mistakes that cost marks Writing that acromegalics are tall. Calling cretinism "thyroid dwarfism" without saying mental retardation. Mixing up "proportionate" (pituitary) and "disproportionate" (cretin).
