What the examiner wants The stages in correct order: thyroglobulin synthesis → iodide trapping (pump) → oxidation (thyroid peroxidase) → transport into colloid → iodination of tyrosine (MIT, DIT) → coupling (T3, T4) → storage, then a word on release. One labelled follicular-cell diagram earns several marks by itself.

Introduction

The thyroid gland secretes two iodine-containing hormones derived from the amino acid tyrosine: tetra-iodothyronine (T4, thyroxine) and tri-iodothyronine (T3). About 93% of the secretion is T4 and 7% is T3; T3 is the more potent (about 4 times). Synthesis takes place in the follicular (acinar) cells and in the colloid of the thyroid follicle. Raw materials: iodine (dietary requirement about 1 mg/week, i.e. ~150 µg/day) and tyrosine (carried in thyroglobulin).

Stages of synthesis

  1. Synthesis of thyroglobulin. The follicular cell synthesises thyroglobulin, a large glycoprotein rich in tyrosine residues, on the rough endoplasmic reticulum; it is glycosylated in the Golgi apparatus, packed into vesicles and released by exocytosis into the colloid. Tyrosine is never iodinated free — it is iodinated while it is part of the thyroglobulin molecule.
  2. Iodide trapping (iodide pump). Iodide in the blood is actively transported into the follicular cell across the basal membrane by the sodium–iodide symporter (NIS) — 2 Na+ with 1 I−, energy from the Na+–K+ ATPase (secondary active transport). Iodide is concentrated about 30 times its blood level. TSH stimulates this pump.
  3. Oxidation of iodide. Iodide is converted to the active form (nascent iodine, I2) by the enzyme thyroid peroxidase (TPO) with hydrogen peroxide, at the apical membrane.
  4. Transport of iodine into the follicular cavity. Iodide leaves the cell across the apical membrane into the colloid by a chloride–iodide exchanger called pendrin.
  5. Iodination of tyrosine (organification). Iodine binds to the tyrosine residues of thyroglobulin (catalysed by TPO / iodinase):
    • Tyrosine + I → mono-iodotyrosine (MIT)
    • MIT + I → di-iodotyrosine (DIT)
  6. Coupling reactions. Iodotyrosines couple within thyroglobulin (TPO-catalysed):
    • DIT + DIT → T4 (tetra-iodothyronine)
    • MIT + DIT → T3 (tri-iodothyronine)
    • DIT + MIT (other orientation) → reverse T3 (inactive, small amount)
  7. Storage. The hormones stay bound to thyroglobulin in the colloid. The thyroid is the only endocrine gland that stores its hormone extracellularly and in large amounts — enough for 2–3 months.

Release (secretion) — in brief

  1. Under TSH, the apical membrane sends pseudopodia that engulf colloid by endocytosis (pinocytosis) → colloid droplets.
  2. Droplets fuse with lysosomes; proteases digest thyroglobulin → free T4 and T3 diffuse into the capillaries.
  3. Released MIT and DIT are not secreted — the enzyme iodotyrosine deiodinase removes their iodine, which is reused.

Transport and fate

In plasma, T3/T4 are bound mostly to thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG), and also to thyroxine-binding prealbumin and albumin; only a tiny fraction is free (active). Most circulating T3 is formed in tissues by deiodination of T4.

Regulation (one line)

Hypothalamic TRH → anterior pituitary TSH → stimulates every step above (trapping, iodination, coupling, endocytosis, gland growth); T3/T4 exert negative feedback on TSH and TRH.

Applied

  • Iodine deficiency → ↓ hormone → ↑ TSH → endemic (simple) goitre.
  • Thiocyanate and perchlorate block the iodide pump; thiourea drugs (propylthiouracil, carbimazole) block peroxidase — used in hyperthyroidism.

Conclusion

Thyroid hormone synthesis is a TSH-driven sequence of trapping, oxidation, iodination and coupling on thyroglobulin, with large extracellular storage in the colloid.

Understand it (plain English) Think of thyroglobulin as a long conveyor belt full of tyrosines. The cell builds the belt and pushes it into the colloid. Meanwhile it "catches" iodide from blood, activates it with peroxidase, and sticks iodine onto the tyrosines (MIT, DIT). Neighbouring iodinated tyrosines on the same belt then join (coupling) to make T4/T3 — still attached to the belt. When the body needs hormone, the cell swallows a bit of belt, digests it in lysosomes and releases T4/T3.
Memory trick "T-TOT-IC-S" — Thyroglobulin, Trapping, Oxidation, Transport, Iodination, Coupling, Storage. And "2 + 2 = 4, 1 + 2 = 3": DIT+DIT = T4; MIT+DIT = T3.
How to write this in the exam 10 marks ≈ 15 minutes. Intro (2 lines, with 93%/7%) → diagram first → numbered stages with enzyme names in bold → 3 lines on release → one-line regulation → conclusion. "Sodium–iodide symporter", "thyroid peroxidase", "organification" and "coupling" are the marks-winning words.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying free tyrosine is iodinated (it is tyrosine in thyroglobulin). Writing MIT + MIT = T3 (wrong — it is MIT + DIT). Forgetting the storage step. Calling calcitonin a "thyroid hormone" in this context — it is from parafollicular C cells and contains no iodine.
