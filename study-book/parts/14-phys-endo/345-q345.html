What the examiner wants Part 1 (~8 marks): Leydig cell, cholesterol → pregnenolone (rate-limiting, LH-stimulated) → DHEA/androstenedione → testosterone; secretion rate; transport (SHBG, albumin, free); "bioavailable" = free + albumin-bound; conversion to DHT/oestradiol; liver metabolism (17-ketosteroids). Part 2 (~7 marks): classified causes — primary/secondary/tertiary for each, with Graves' disease and iodine deficiency/Hashimoto's named.

Part A — Synthesis of testosterone

Site: Leydig (interstitial) cells lying between the seminiferous tubules (≈95% of testosterone); small amounts from the adrenal cortex. Precursor: cholesterol (from plasma LDL or made from acetate in the cell). Stimulus: LH (interstitial-cell-stimulating hormone, ICSH) acting via receptor → Gs → cAMP → protein kinase A.

  1. Cholesterol is moved into the mitochondria (by steroidogenic acute regulatory protein, StAR) — the step LH speeds up.
  2. Cholesterol → pregnenolone by side-chain cleavage enzyme (cholesterol desmolase, P450scc) — rate-limiting step.
  3. In the smooth ER, pregnenolone is converted along two routes:
    • Δ5 pathway (main in man): pregnenolone → 17α-hydroxypregnenolone → DHEA → androstenediol / androstenedione
    • Δ4 pathway: pregnenolone → progesterone → 17α-hydroxyprogesterone → androstenedione
  4. Androstenedione → testosterone by 17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase.
  5. Testosterone is not stored; it diffuses out as soon as it is made. Adult male secretion ≈ 4–9 mg/day.

Bio-availability of testosterone

  1. Transport: about 97–98% of plasma testosterone is protein-bound — loosely to albumin and tightly to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG); only about 2% is free.
  2. Bioavailable testosterone = free + albumin-bound fraction (albumin releases it readily in tissue capillaries). SHBG-bound testosterone is not readily available. Normal adult male plasma testosterone ≈ 300–1000 ng/dL.
  3. Factors altering SHBG (and hence free testosterone): SHBG ↑ by oestrogens, thyroid hormone excess, ageing, liver cirrhosis (↓ free T); SHBG ↓ by androgens, obesity, hypothyroidism (↑ free fraction).
  4. Activation in target tissue: testosterone → DHT (5α-reductase; more potent) or → oestradiol (aromatase). In muscle it acts as testosterone itself.
  5. Fate: half-life is short; most is inactivated in the liver to 17-ketosteroids (androsterone, etiocholanolone), conjugated with glucuronic acid/sulphate and excreted in urine (and bile).
  6. Route: oral testosterone has poor bioavailability — rapid first-pass hepatic metabolism.

Part B — Causes of hyperthyroidism

Hyperthyroidism = increased secretion of thyroid hormones (thyrotoxicosis).

CategoryCauses
Primary (thyroid itself)1. Graves' disease (exophthalmic goitre) — autoimmune; thyroid-stimulating antibodies (TSI / LATS) mimic TSH — commonest cause
2. Toxic multinodular goitre
3. Toxic adenoma (single hot nodule)
4. Thyroiditis (early, destructive phase — leakage of stored hormone)
5. Iodine-induced (Jod–Basedow) in a nodular gland
Secondary (pituitary)TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma
Tertiary (hypothalamus)Excess TRH (rare)
OthersExcess thyroxine intake (thyrotoxicosis factitia); hCG-secreting tumours (hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma); struma ovarii

Part B — Causes of hypothyroidism

Hypothyroidism = decreased secretion of thyroid hormones; in children → cretinism, in adults → myxoedema.

CategoryCauses
Primary (thyroid failure; TSH high)1. Iodine deficiency — endemic goitre
2. Hashimoto's (autoimmune) thyroiditis
3. Thyroidectomy or radioactive iodine ablation
4. Antithyroid drugs; goitrogens (e.g. thiocyanate from cassava, cabbage)
5. Congenital absence of thyroid or enzyme defects of synthesis
Secondary (pituitary; TSH low)Pituitary tumour, postpartum pituitary necrosis (Sheehan's syndrome), hypophysectomy
Tertiary (hypothalamus; TRH low)Hypothalamic lesions — tumour, trauma, infection
PeripheralTissue resistance to thyroid hormone (receptor defect)

Conclusion

Testosterone is a Leydig-cell steroid synthesised from cholesterol under LH, carried largely bound (SHBG/albumin) with only the free + albumin fraction bioavailable. Thyroid dysfunction is classified by the level of the defect — thyroid (primary), pituitary (secondary) or hypothalamus (tertiary).

Understand it (plain English) Testosterone in blood is like money: SHBG-bound = locked in a fixed deposit; albumin-bound = in a current account (easy to withdraw); free = cash in hand. "Bioavailable" means cash + current account. For thyroid causes, always ask "where is the fault?" — gland, pituitary or hypothalamus — and you will never forget a category.
Memory trick Testosterone pathway: "C-P-D-A-T" — Cholesterol, Pregnenolone, DHEA, Androstenedione, Testosterone ("Cool People Don't Act Tough"). Hyperthyroid "GTT": Graves', Toxic goitre/adenoma, Thyroiditis.
How to write this in the exam 15 marks ≈ 22 minutes: 11 min on testosterone (diagram + steps + bioavailability), 11 min on causes using two tables classified primary/secondary/tertiary. Mention "LH via cAMP", "rate-limiting", "SHBG" and "17-ketosteroids".
Mistakes that cost marks Saying Sertoli cells make testosterone (Leydig cells do). Listing causes without a classification. Forgetting that bioavailable includes albumin-bound testosterone, not only free.
