Explain Ferguson and Milk Let-down Reflexes as they relate to Parturition and Lactation respectively (6mks).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Both are neuroendocrine reflexes: neural afferent limb → hypothalamus (supraoptic & paraventricular nuclei) → oxytocin from posterior pituitary (hormonal efferent limb). Ferguson = cervical stretch → uterine contraction (positive feedback); let-down = suckling → contraction of myoepithelial cells → milk ejection.

Introduction

A neuroendocrine reflex is a reflex whose afferent limb is nervous and whose efferent limb is a hormone. The hormone in both reflexes here is oxytocin, synthesised mainly in the paraventricular nucleus (and supraoptic nucleus) of the hypothalamus, carried by axoplasmic flow (bound to neurophysin) along the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract and released from the posterior pituitary.

(a) Ferguson reflex — parturition

  1. Stimulus: as labour begins, the fetal head stretches the cervix and vagina.
  2. Afferent: stretch receptors send impulses via sensory (pelvic) nerves → spinal cord → hypothalamus.
  3. Centre: paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei → oxytocin released from posterior pituitary.
  4. Efferent (hormonal): oxytocin acts on myometrium (whose oxytocin receptors are greatly increased by oestrogen at term) → strong uterine contractions.
  5. Contractions push the fetus further → more cervical stretch → more oxytocin. This positive feedback cycle continues until the baby is delivered, and later helps contraction of the uterus after delivery (reducing bleeding).

(b) Milk let-down (milk ejection) reflex — lactation

  1. Stimulus: suckling stimulates touch receptors in the nipple and areola.
  2. Afferent: somatic sensory nerves → spinal cord → hypothalamus.
  3. Centre: paraventricular/supraoptic nuclei → oxytocin from posterior pituitary.
  4. Efferent: oxytocin causes contraction of the myoepithelial cells around the alveoli and ducts → milk is squeezed into the ducts and sinuses and ejected within 30 seconds to 1 minute of suckling; milk is ejected from both breasts.
  5. Conditioning: the reflex can be triggered by the sight, sound (cry) or thought of the baby; it is inhibited by fear, pain and emotional stress (via adrenaline and inhibition of hypothalamus).
  6. Simultaneously suckling stimulates prolactin release from the anterior pituitary for milk secretion for the next feed.

Conclusion

Both reflexes are oxytocin-mediated neuroendocrine reflexes — the Ferguson reflex completes parturition and the let-down reflex delivers stored milk to the baby.

Understand it (plain English) Kris, the nervous system "calls" the hypothalamus, and the hypothalamus answers by posting a hormone letter (oxytocin) through the blood. In labour, every push stretches the cervix, which calls for more oxytocin, which gives a stronger push — a snowball that only stops when the baby is out. In feeding, the baby's sucking calls for oxytocin, which squeezes the milk sacs like a hand squeezing a sponge.
Memory trick Ferguson = Fetus (cervix, uterus). Oxytocin = "Oxy-tocin = quick birth" (Greek okys = swift, tokos = birth). "Prolactin Produces, Oxytocin eOjects."
How to write this in the exam 6 marks ≈ 8 minutes: define neuroendocrine reflex, then the two reflexes as numbered arcs (stimulus → afferent → centre → hormone → effector → response), the shared diagram, and a 4-row comparison table.
Mistakes that cost marks Writing that prolactin causes milk ejection (it causes secretion). Saying oxytocin is made in the posterior pituitary (it is only stored and released there). Forgetting "positive feedback" for Ferguson.
