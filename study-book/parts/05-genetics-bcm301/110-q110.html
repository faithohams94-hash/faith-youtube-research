Write on the Mendelian Laws of Inheritance up to third filial generation.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
Asked before The three laws are also asked in Q106 — full Punnett squares (monohybrid and dihybrid 9:3:3:1) there. Below is the answer tailored to THIS wording: the key extra is carrying the cross through to the F3 generation.
What the examiner wants The laws stated, then a worked cross P → F1 → F2 → F3 by self-fertilisation, showing that F2 dwarfs and one-third of F2 talls breed true while two-thirds of F2 talls segregate 3:1 again — this is how Mendel PROVED segregation and the 1 : 2 : 1 genotype ratio.

Introduction

Gregor Mendel (1822–1884) crossed pure-breeding garden peas (Pisum sativum) differing in contrasting characters and followed the offspring for three generations (F1, F2, F3). From the ratios he derived the laws of inheritance. Mendel's "factors" are today's genes; alternative forms are alleles.

The laws

  1. Law of dominance — in a cross between pure parents with contrasting characters, only one character (the dominant) appears in F1; the other (recessive) is masked.
  2. Law of segregation (purity of gametes) — the two alleles of a pair separate during gamete formation, each gamete receiving only one.
  3. Law of independent assortment — alleles of different gene pairs segregate independently of each other (dihybrid F2 ratio 9:3:3:1).

Worked monohybrid cross up to F3 (tall × dwarf)

Parental (P) generation

Pure tall (TT) × pure dwarf (tt). Gametes: T and t.

First filial generation (F1)

All Tt — tall. Demonstrates dominance: T masks t.

Second filial generation (F2) — F1 self-fertilised (Tt × Tt)

Gametes T and t (½ each) from both sides → ¼ TT : ½ Tt : ¼ tt; phenotype 3 tall : 1 dwarf. Reappearance of dwarfs demonstrates segregation (t was never lost or blended).

Third filial generation (F3) — each F2 plant self-fertilised
  1. F2 dwarfs (tt) → produce only dwarfs — they breed true.
  2. F2 talls are of two kinds, indistinguishable by appearance:
    • ⅓ are TT → produce only tall plants (breed true);
    • ⅔ are Tt → segregate again into 3 tall : 1 dwarf.
  3. This F3 test proved that the F2 "3 : 1" is really 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt — the genotypic ratio.

Note: with each generation of selfing the proportion of heterozygotes halves (½ in F2 → ¼ in F3), so self-fertilisation leads towards pure (homozygous) lines.

Dihybrid cross to F3 (independent assortment)

P: RRYY (round yellow) × rryy (wrinkled green) → F1 RrYy (all round yellow) → F2 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (16 combinations, 9 genotypes). On selfing in F3, only 1 of every 9 round-yellow F2 plants (RRYY) breeds true; the others (RRYy, RrYY, RrYy) segregate again — e.g. RrYy gives 9:3:3:1 again. The double recessive rryy always breeds true. This confirmed that the two gene pairs assort independently.

Applications in man

  • Carrier × carrier of sickle cell (HbAS × HbAS) mirrors Mendel's F1 × F1: 1 HbAA : 2 HbAS : 1 HbSS — 25% sickle cell disease, 50% carriers.
  • Phenotypically normal children of carrier parents are carriers with a probability of ⅔ — exactly Mendel's ⅔ of F2 talls being Tt. This figure is used in genetic counselling.

Conclusion

Following crosses into F3 showed Mendel that dominant-looking F2 individuals are of two genotypes (TT and Tt in 1 : 2), confirming segregation; dihybrid crosses confirmed independent assortment.

Understand it (plain English) In F2 you cannot tell a TT tall from a Tt tall by looking. Mendel's trick was to let each F2 plant fertilise itself and watch its children (F3). Plants whose children were all tall were TT; plants whose children split 3:1 were Tt. He found twice as many of the second kind — so the hidden ratio is 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt.
Memory trick F3 talls: "One-third true, two-thirds split". Pooled F3: "3-2-3, five over three" (TT 3/8, Tt 2/8, tt 3/8 → 5 tall : 3 dwarf).
How to write this in the exam State the 3 laws briefly (1 line each) then spend most time on the worked cross to F3 — that is what "up to third filial generation" asks. Draw Fig 110.1, then add the ⅓ / ⅔ statement in a box. A small F2 Punnett square (as in Q106) adds marks.
Mistakes that cost marks Stopping at F2. Saying all F2 talls breed true. Crossing F2 plants with each other randomly instead of self-fertilising (then the ratio stays 3:1 under random mating — not what Mendel did).
