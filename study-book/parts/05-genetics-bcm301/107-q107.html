Introduction / Definitions

Chromosomes are thread-like structures in the nucleus made of DNA wound on histone proteins (chromatin); they carry the genes. Normal human somatic cells are diploid (2n = 46): 22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes (XX female, XY male). Gametes are haploid (n = 23).

Chromosome analysis (cytogenetics) is the study of the number and structure of chromosomes. Its standard product is the karyotype — the systematic arrangement of the photographed metaphase chromosomes of one cell in pairs, in descending order of size and according to centromere position. A diagrammatic karyotype is an idiogram.

A. Classification

(i) Classification of chromosomes by position of the centromere
  1. Metacentric — centromere in the middle, arms equal (e.g. 1, 3, 16, 19, 20).
  2. Submetacentric — centromere off-centre, short arm (p) and long arm (q) distinct (e.g. 2, 4–12, 17, 18, X).
  3. Acrocentric — centromere near one end, very short p arm often carrying satellites (e.g. 13, 14, 15, 21, 22, Y — Y has no satellites).
  4. Telocentric — centromere at the very end; not found in normal humans.

C. Significance of chromosome analysis

  1. Confirms the normal chromosome number (46) and sex chromosome constitution of an individual.
  2. Detects numerical abnormalities — aneuploidy (trisomy, monosomy) and polyploidy (triploidy).
  3. Detects structural abnormalities — deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation (balanced/unbalanced), ring and isochromosome.
  4. Identifies balanced translocation carriers who are healthy but at risk of abnormal offspring (e.g. Robertsonian 14;21 → familial Down syndrome).
  5. Detects mosaicism (two cell lines, e.g. 45,X/46,XX).
  6. Provides the basis for gene mapping (band nomenclature, e.g. CFTR on 7q31) and for genetic counselling.

Conclusion

Chromosomes are classified by centromere position and size (Denver groups A–G); their analysis by karyotyping, banding and FISH is an essential tool for diagnosing chromosomal disorders, prenatal diagnosis, cancer, infertility and genetic counselling.

Understand it (plain English) Chromosomes are only visible as neat "X" shapes when a cell is dividing. So we take cells that divide easily (white cells stimulated by PHA), freeze them in metaphase with colchicine, swell them in a weak salt solution so the chromosomes spread out, stain them so each pair gets a "barcode" of bands, then line them up like socks in pairs. Any extra, missing or broken sock is the diagnosis.
Memory trick Acrocentrics = "13, 14, 15, 21, 22 — the D and G crew"; they carry satellites and are the ones in Robertsonian translocations. Denver groups: "A Big Cat Dances Every Friday Gaily" (A–G). Banding: "Giemsa is General".
How to write this in the exam Use the three headings of the question exactly: Classification → Significance → Medical applications. Put the Denver groups in a table (5 marks in 2 minutes). Draw the karyotyping flow diagram. Always quote at least 4 karyotypes in ISCN style (47,XX,+21; 45,X; 47,XXY; t(9;22)).
Mistakes that cost marks Saying telocentric chromosomes occur in humans. Putting X in group G (X is in group C; Y is in group G). Writing "47 XX +21" without commas — ISCN is: number, sex chromosomes, abnormality. Forgetting colchicine's role (spindle poison → metaphase arrest).
