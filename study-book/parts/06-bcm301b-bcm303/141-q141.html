Introduction

Nucleotides are phosphate esters of nucleosides (base + pentose sugar + 1–3 phosphates). They are synthesised by de novo pathways (from amino acids, CO2, one-carbon units from folate and PRPP) and by salvage of preformed bases; they are degraded to uric acid (purines) or to soluble β-amino acids (pyrimidines).

Principle: rapidly dividing cells (tumours, bone marrow, gut epithelium, hair follicles) need large amounts of nucleotides, so these drugs kill them preferentially — and also explain toxic effects (marrow suppression, mucositis, alopecia).

B. Degradation of nucleic acids

  1. Nucleases (pancreatic DNase, RNase; endonucleases and exonucleases; phosphodiesterases) hydrolyse nucleic acids to oligonucleotides → mononucleotides.
  2. Nucleotidases (phosphatases) remove phosphate → nucleosides.
  3. Nucleosidases / phosphorylases split nucleosides → free base + ribose (or ribose-1-phosphate). Bases are either salvaged or degraded.
Purine catabolism (end product uric acid — humans lack uricase)
AMP→IMP / adenosine→inosine (adenosine deaminase)→hypoxanthine (PNP)→xanthine (xanthine oxidase)→URIC ACID (xanthine oxidase)
GMP→guanosine→guanine (PNP)→xanthine (guanase)→uric acid

Clinical: ↑ uric acid → gout; ADA deficiency → SCID; PNP deficiency → T-cell immunodeficiency.

Pyrimidine catabolism (soluble products, mainly in liver)
  • Cytosine → (deamination) uracil → dihydrouracil → β-ureidopropionate → β-alanine + CO2 + NH3.
  • Thymine → dihydrothymine → β-aminoisobutyrate + CO2 + NH3 (excretion ↑ in leukaemia and after radiation/chemotherapy because of increased DNA breakdown).

C. Metabolism of folate

  1. Structure: folic acid (pteroylglutamic acid) = pteridine ring + para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) + glutamate; dietary folate is a polyglutamate — deconjugated in the jejunum, absorbed as monoglutamate, circulates as N5-methyl-THF.
  2. Activation: folate → dihydrofolate (DHF) → tetrahydrofolate (THF), both steps by dihydrofolate reductase using NADPH. THF is the active coenzyme — the carrier of one-carbon units (attached at N5 and/or N10).
  3. One-carbon forms: N5-methyl, N5,N10-methylene, N5,N10-methenyl, N10-formyl, N5-formimino THF (interconvertible except N5-methyl, which is formed irreversibly).
  4. Sources of one-carbon units: serine (major — serine hydroxymethyltransferase, PLP), glycine (glycine cleavage), histidine (via FIGLU → formimino-THF), formate, tryptophan.
  5. Uses: purine C-2 and C-8 (N10-formyl-THF); thymidylate synthesis dUMP → dTMP (N5,N10-methylene-THF, which is oxidised to DHF and must be reduced again by DHFR); methionine regeneration from homocysteine (N5-methyl-THF + vitamin B12); serine ⇌ glycine.
  6. Methyl-folate trap: in B12 deficiency, folate is trapped as N5-methyl-THF → functional folate deficiency → megaloblastic anaemia.
  7. Deficiency: megaloblastic anaemia, neural tube defects in the fetus, hyperhomocysteinaemia; FIGLU excretion after histidine load is a test.

Other points: (1) PRPP availability links purine and pyrimidine synthesis (activation of CPS II by PRPP balances both); (2) multienzyme complexes — CAD (CPS II, ATCase, dihydroorotase) and UMP synthase (orotate phosphoribosyltransferase + OMP decarboxylase) channel intermediates; (3) gene expression of CAD rises in growing cells. Defect of UMP synthase → orotic aciduria (megaloblastic anaemia not responding to B12/folate; treated with uridine).

E. Metabolism and functions of nucleotides

Metabolism (outline)
  • Purines: ring built on ribose-5-phosphate (from PRPP) → IMP (10 steps; sources: glycine, glutamine, aspartate, CO2, N10-formyl-THF) → AMP and GMP. Salvage by HGPRT and APRT (HGPRT deficiency = Lesch–Nyhan).
  • Pyrimidines: ring made first (carbamoyl phosphate + aspartate → orotate) and then attached to PRPP → UMP → UTP → CTP; dTMP from dUMP by thymidylate synthase.
  • Deoxyribonucleotides: from ribonucleoside diphosphates by ribonucleotide reductase (thioredoxin, NADPH).
  • Interconversion by kinases: NMP → NDP → NTP (nucleoside mono- and diphosphate kinases).
Functions of nucleotides
  1. Building blocks of nucleic acids — dNTPs for DNA; NTPs for RNA.
  2. Energy currency — ATP (universal); GTP (protein synthesis, gluconeogenesis), UTP (glycogen synthesis), CTP (phospholipid synthesis).
  3. Components of coenzymes — NAD+, NADP+, FAD, coenzyme A all contain adenine nucleotide.
  4. Second messengers — cAMP and cGMP mediate hormone action.
  5. Activated biosynthetic intermediates — UDP-glucose (glycogen), UDP-glucuronic acid (conjugation of bilirubin), CDP-choline and CDP-diacylglycerol (phospholipids), S-adenosylmethionine (methyl donor), PAPS (sulphate donor).
  6. Allosteric regulators — ATP, AMP, ADP control PFK-1, isocitrate dehydrogenase, glycogen phosphorylase, etc.
  7. Signalling and others — GTP in G-proteins; ATP in protein phosphorylation (kinases); adenosine as a neuromodulator/vasodilator.

Conclusion

Nucleotide synthesis depends critically on folate-derived one-carbon units, PRPP and feedback-regulated enzymes (CPS II for pyrimidines); blocking these steps is the basis of many anticancer, antimicrobial and immunosuppressive drugs, while degradation ends in uric acid (purines) and soluble β-amino acids (pyrimidines).

Understand it (plain English) Folate is a "one-carbon delivery van". Every time thymidylate synthase uses a methylene group to make dTMP for DNA, the van is left damaged (DHF) and must be repaired by DHFR. Methotrexate jams the repair shop, so the vans run out, and dividing cells cannot make DNA. That single idea explains methotrexate, trimethoprim, 5-FU and megaloblastic anaemia.
Memory trick Pyrimidine control in humans: "CPS-2 is the 'U-turn'" — inhibited by UTP, turned on by PRPP. Bacteria: "CTP Cuts ATCase". Pyrimidine end products: "Cytosine/Uracil → β-Alanine; Thymine → β-amino-Iso-butyrate" (Think Isobutyrate, the "T-I" pair).
