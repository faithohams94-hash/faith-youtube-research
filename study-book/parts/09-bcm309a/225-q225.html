(a) Microsomal enzymes

Microsomes are not true organelles. They are small vesicles formed from fragments of the endoplasmic reticulum (mainly smooth ER) when cells are homogenised. They are recovered by ultracentrifugation (about 100,000 × g) of the post-mitochondrial supernatant. Enzymes bound to these membranes are called microsomal enzymes. They are most plentiful in the liver.

Key features:

  1. Inducible: phenobarbitone, rifampicin, alcohol and polycyclic hydrocarbons proliferate the SER and raise the enzyme content. This causes drug tolerance and drug interactions.
  2. Can be inhibited (cimetidine, ketoconazole).
  3. Low substrate specificity: one enzyme handles many drugs.
  4. Low at birth (e.g. UGT), which causes neonatal jaundice and the grey-baby syndrome with chloramphenicol.

(b) Health impact of climate change

Climate change is the long-term change in global temperature and weather patterns, driven mainly by greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane) from burning fossil fuels and from deforestation. It affects health directly and indirectly.

EffectHealth impact (and biochemical link)
Heat waves (direct)Heat exhaustion and heat stroke, dehydration and electrolyte loss, acute kidney injury, worsening of heart and lung disease. Biochemically: protein denaturation and enzyme failure above about 42 °C, rhabdomyolysis (↑CK, myoglobinuria), induction of heat-shock proteins.
Floods (e.g. the 2012 and 2022 Nigerian floods)Drowning and injury, displacement, contamination of water → cholera, typhoid, diarrhoeal disease, hepatitis A/E, leptospirosis
Vector-borne diseaseWarmer, wetter conditions extend mosquito breeding and range → more malaria, dengue, yellow fever; also schistosomiasis
Drought, desertification (northern Nigeria, shrinking Lake Chad), crop failureFood insecurity → protein-energy malnutrition (kwashiorkor, marasmus), micronutrient deficiencies (iron, vitamin A, zinc), stunting; conflict and migration

Ozone depletion / UV radiation (related)Skin cancers, cataract, thymine-dimer DNA damage

Vulnerable groups: children, the elderly, pregnant women, the poor and people with chronic disease. Response: mitigation (cut emissions, afforestation) and adaptation (early-warning systems, flood control, vector control, climate-resilient health services).

(c) Detoxification of acetaminophen (paracetamol)

  1. Therapeutic dose: most of the drug is conjugated directly (paracetamol already has an –OH group) by phase II: glucuronidation (largest share) and sulphation. These inactive conjugates are excreted in urine. A small amount is excreted unchanged.
  2. A small fraction (~5–10%) is oxidised by CYP2E1 (also CYP3A4, 1A2) to the highly reactive electrophile N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI).
  3. NAPQI is immediately conjugated with reduced glutathione (GSH) by glutathione S-transferase. The product is converted to cysteine and mercapturic acid conjugates and excreted in urine (phase III). So at normal doses, paracetamol is safe.
  4. In overdose: the glucuronide and sulphate pathways become saturated (PAPS especially is limited), so more drug goes through CYP2E1 → excess NAPQI. Hepatic GSH is depleted (toxicity follows when stores fall to roughly 30% of normal).
  5. Free NAPQI then binds covalently to sulphydryl (–SH) groups of hepatocyte proteins (including mitochondrial proteins), and causes oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation. The result is centrilobular (zone 3) hepatic necrosis, where CYP2E1 is most concentrated. Very high ALT/AST and acute liver failure follow, and sometimes renal tubular necrosis.
  6. Risk is increased by chronic alcohol use (induces CYP2E1 and depletes GSH), enzyme-inducing drugs (phenobarbitone, rifampicin), fasting and malnutrition (low GSH).
  7. Antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) supplies cysteine for glutathione synthesis (γ-glutamyl-cysteinyl-glycine), so GSH is replenished. It also directly binds NAPQI and promotes sulphation. It works best when given early, within about 8–10 hours.

Conclusion

Microsomal enzymes are the inducible drug-handling machinery of the SER. Climate change harms health through heat, infection, malnutrition and pollution. Paracetamol shows how one detoxifying system (CYP) can create a toxin, and how glutathione protects the liver.

Understand it (plain English) Paracetamol has three "exits". Two are safe (glucuronide and sulphate). One (CYP2E1) makes a dangerous chemical, NAPQI, but glutathione is a bodyguard that grabs it at once. In overdose the safe exits are jammed, so too much NAPQI is made, the bodyguards run out, and NAPQI attacks liver proteins. NAC simply brings more bodyguards.
Memory trick Paracetamol fates "G-S-N" = Glucuronide, Sulphate, NAPQI (+ GSH). Toxic zone = zone 3 ("3 = CYP-rich, near the central vein").
Microsomal enzymes = "CUBE-FM": CYP450, UGT, b₅, Epoxide hydrolase, FMO, MEOS.

(3) Writing "NAPQI is excreted". It must first be conjugated with GSH. (4) Making the climate-change note a geography essay with no health or biochemical points.
