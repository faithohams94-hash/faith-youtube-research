What is Recombinant DNA technology?

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants A precise definition, the tools (enzymes, vectors, hosts), the steps (isolate → cut → join → introduce → select/screen → express) with the classic labelled diagram, and a short list of applications. This is the core diagram of the whole course — draw it well.

Definition

Recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology (genetic engineering, gene cloning) is the set of techniques by which a DNA fragment (gene) from one source is cut out and joined in vitro to a vector DNA, forming a new combination — recombinant DNA (chimeric DNA) — which is then introduced into a host cell where it replicates (cloning) and may be expressed to give a useful product.

History: restriction enzymes discovered (Arber, Smith, Nathans — Nobel 1978); Paul Berg (1972) made the first recombinant DNA (SV40 + λ DNA); Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer (1973) cloned recombinant plasmid in E. coli.

Tools of recombinant DNA technology

ToolExamples / role
Restriction endonucleases ("molecular scissors")Cut DNA at specific palindromic sites — EcoRI (G↓AATTC), BamHI, HindIII; give sticky or blunt ends
DNA ligase ("molecular glue")T4 DNA ligase seals phosphodiester bonds between insert and vector
Other enzymesReverse transcriptase (mRNA → cDNA), DNA polymerase (Klenow, Taq for PCR), alkaline phosphatase (prevents vector self-ligation), terminal transferase (homopolymer tails), S1 nuclease
Vectors ("vehicles")Plasmids (pBR322, pUC), bacteriophage λ, cosmids, BAC, YAC, viral vectors (retro-, adeno-viruses) — Q205
Host cellsE. coli (commonest), Bacillus subtilis, yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae), mammalian cells (CHO cells), plant cells
Gene of interest (insert)From genomic DNA, cDNA (via reverse transcriptase), chemical synthesis, or PCR
Probes and markersLabelled DNA/RNA probes; antibiotic resistance genes, lacZ for selection

Steps of recombinant DNA technology

  1. Isolation of the gene of interest — extract DNA from donor cells; or isolate mRNA and convert it to cDNA with reverse transcriptase (cDNA has no introns, so bacteria can express it); or amplify by PCR; or synthesise chemically.
  2. Isolation of the vector — e.g. plasmid from bacteria.
  3. Cutting — both donor DNA and vector are digested with the same restriction endonuclease to produce complementary sticky (cohesive) ends.
  4. Joining (ligation) — the insert and opened vector are mixed; sticky ends base-pair (anneal) and DNA ligase seals the nicks → recombinant DNA. (Blunt ends are joined using linkers, adaptors or homopolymer tailing — Q215.)
  5. Introduction into host — transformation (CaCl2-treated competent cells + heat shock at 42 °C), electroporation, transfection, transduction by phage, microinjection, gene gun, liposomes.
  6. Selection and screening of recombinants — host cells are grown on antibiotic medium: only cells with the plasmid survive. Recombinant vs non-recombinant is distinguished by insertional inactivation (e.g. loss of tetracycline resistance in pBR322) or blue–white screening (lacZ in pUC — white colonies carry the insert). Confirmed by colony hybridisation with labelled probes, PCR, restriction analysis or sequencing.
  7. Cloning and expression — selected cells multiply into a clone; with an expression vector (strong promoter, ribosome binding site) the gene is transcribed and translated. The protein is harvested from large fermenters and purified (downstream processing).

Applications (brief)

  1. Recombinant drugs: insulin, growth hormone, erythropoietin, interferons, tPA, factor VIII.
  2. Vaccines: hepatitis B (HBsAg in yeast), HPV.
  3. Diagnosis: DNA probes, prenatal diagnosis of sickle cell disease and thalassaemia, PCR for infections.
  4. Gene therapy (ADA-SCID), transgenic animals and plants (Bt cotton, golden rice), forensic DNA fingerprinting, and research on gene structure and function.

Conclusion

Recombinant DNA technology cuts, pastes and multiplies genes using restriction enzymes, ligase, vectors and hosts. It has transformed medicine by making human proteins, vaccines and molecular diagnostics available on a large scale.

Understand it (plain English) It is "cut-and-paste" for DNA. The restriction enzyme is the scissors that always cuts at the same "word", leaving sticky ends like Velcro. Cut the human gene and the plasmid with the same scissors and their Velcro ends match; ligase is the glue. Put the new plasmid into bacteria, which copy it every time they divide — billions of copies, and the bacteria make the human protein for you.
Memory trick Steps: "I Cut, Join, Transform, Select, Express" → "I Can Join The Shy Elephant". Tools: "SLV-H" — Scissors (restriction enzyme), Ligase, Vector, Host.
