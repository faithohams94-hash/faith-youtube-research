Differentiate between apoptosis and necrosis.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Two definitions, then a big comparison table — cause, number of cells, cell size, membrane, nucleus, DNA pattern (ladder vs smear), energy (ATP), enzymes (caspases), inflammation, examples — and a labelled diagram of both processes.

Definitions

Apoptosis (Greek: "falling off", like leaves from a tree; Kerr, Wyllie and Currie, 1972) is programmed, energy-dependent cell death, genetically controlled, in which a cell activates enzymes (caspases) that dismantle it into membrane-bound apoptotic bodies that are eaten by phagocytes without inflammation.

Necrosis is accidental, uncontrolled cell death caused by severe injury (ischaemia, toxins, burns, infection) in which cells swell and burst, releasing their contents and provoking inflammation.

Similarities

Both end in cell death; both may show pyknosis and karyorrhexis; a stimulus may cause apoptosis at low dose and necrosis at high dose.

Clinical significance

  • Too little apoptosis: cancer (Bcl-2 over-expression in follicular lymphoma; p53 mutation), autoimmune disease.
  • Too much apoptosis: neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer, Parkinson), CD4 cell loss in AIDS.
  • Necrosis releases intracellular enzymes — the basis of cardiac and liver enzyme tests.

Conclusion

Apoptosis is a tidy, gene-directed, ATP-requiring suicide of single cells without inflammation; necrosis is a messy, accidental death of many cells with swelling, rupture and inflammation.

Understand it (plain English) Apoptosis is like a tenant who packs his things into neat boxes and leaves quietly — the cleaner (macrophage) just carries the boxes away. Necrosis is a house that explodes: debris everywhere, and the fire service (neutrophils, inflammation) has to come.
Memory trick Apoptosis = "S": Shrink, Single cells, Self-programmed, Silent (no inflammation), Steps ladder (DNA). Necrosis = "S" for the opposite: Swell, Sheets of cells, Spill contents, Smear DNA, Scar.
How to write this in the exam Definitions → diagram → table of at least 12 rows → one line on clinical significance → conclusion. The "DNA ladder vs smear" and "inflammation absent vs present" rows are the examiner's favourites. 15 minutes.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying apoptosis always means disease (it is mostly physiological); saying the membrane ruptures in apoptosis; forgetting that apoptosis needs ATP.
