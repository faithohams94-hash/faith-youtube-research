Significance of biotechnology to medicine in general.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Definition, then organised headings — therapeutics (recombinant proteins), vaccines, diagnosis, gene therapy, monoclonal antibodies, antibiotics/enzymes, forensic, research/models — each with named products. A mind-map diagram and a short note on limitations make it a distinction answer.

Introduction

Biotechnology is the use of living organisms, cells or their molecules to produce goods and services. Medical (red) biotechnology applies it to the prevention, diagnosis and treatment of disease. Its modern power comes from recombinant DNA technology, hybridoma technology, PCR, cell culture and fermentation.

2. Vaccines

Recombinant hepatitis B vaccine (HBsAg made in yeast, 1986), HPV vaccine (virus-like particles), subunit, conjugate (Hib), DNA and mRNA vaccines (COVID-19). They are safer — no live pathogen — and can be mass-produced (see Q218).

3. Diagnosis

  1. PCR — detection of HIV, HBV, TB, COVID-19; viral load; prenatal diagnosis from tiny samples.
  2. DNA probes and blotting (Southern, Northern), FISH, microarrays, and allele-specific oligonucleotides (sickle cell, thalassaemia).
  3. ELISA and rapid kits using monoclonal antibodies — HIV, HBsAg, pregnancy test (β-hCG), tumour markers (AFP, PSA, CEA).
  4. Biosensors — glucose oxidase strips for glucometers.
  5. Newborn and mass screening (PKU, hypothyroidism, sickle cell) — Q216.

4. Monoclonal antibodies (hybridoma technology)

Köhler and Milstein (1975) fused antibody-producing B cells with myeloma cells. Uses: diagnosis (blood grouping reagents, ELISA), imaging, targeted cancer therapy (trastuzumab, rituximab), prevention of graft rejection, anti-D for Rh prophylaxis.

5. Gene therapy and cell therapy

Correction of monogenic disorders (ADA-SCID, haemophilia, β-thalassaemia, sickle cell disease), engineered CAR-T cells for leukaemia, stem cell therapy and tissue engineering (artificial skin for burns).

6. Antibiotics and other fermentation products

Penicillin, streptomycin, tetracyclines produced by improved microbial strains; vitamins (B12, riboflavin); steroid biotransformation (cortisone).

7. Forensic medicine

DNA fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys) for crime investigation, paternity and identification of mass-disaster victims (Q202).

8. Research and drug development

Transgenic and knockout animal models of human disease, pharmacogenomics (personalised medicine), Human Genome Project, gene mapping and drug-target discovery.

Limitations / concerns

  • High cost — many products unaffordable in developing countries.
  • Biosafety (escape of GMOs), immunogenicity of foreign proteins.
  • Ethical issues: germline editing, cloning, genetic privacy and discrimination.

Conclusion

Biotechnology has given medicine safer and abundant drugs, new vaccines, rapid and precise diagnostics, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapy and DNA forensics; it is the foundation of modern molecular medicine.

Understand it (plain English) Before biotechnology, insulin came from pig pancreas and growth hormone from dead human pituitaries (which once spread a fatal prion disease). Now bacteria make pure human proteins by the tonne. The same toolkit also lets us read a patient's DNA (diagnosis) and even fix it (gene therapy).
