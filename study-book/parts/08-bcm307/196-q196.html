Differentiate between genetic engineering and biotechnology.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Two clean definitions, the key idea that genetic engineering is one tool inside biotechnology (not a synonym), a comparison table of 8–10 points, examples of each, and a one-line conclusion.

Definitions

Biotechnology is the application of living organisms, their cells, or their components (enzymes, antibodies, genes) to make products or provide services useful to humans — in medicine, agriculture, industry and the environment. It is very old: brewing, bread-making, cheese, yogurt and vinegar production by fermentation are traditional biotechnology. The term was coined by Karl Ereky (1919).

Genetic engineering (also called recombinant DNA technology or gene manipulation) is the deliberate, in vitro alteration of the genetic material of an organism. A gene is cut out of one DNA, joined to a vector, and put into a host cell where it is copied and/or expressed. It began in the early 1970s (Paul Berg 1972; Cohen and Boyer 1973).

Similarities (one line earns a mark)

  1. Both use living cells or their molecules to benefit man (medicine, agriculture, industry).
  2. Both depend on microbes (E. coli, yeast) and on biochemistry of DNA, enzymes and proteins.

Conclusion

Biotechnology is the broad, ancient application of living systems; genetic engineering is the modern molecular technique within it that directly alters DNA. Genetic engineering has turned biotechnology into the source of recombinant drugs, vaccines and diagnostics.

Understand it (plain English) Biotechnology is the whole "kitchen" of using living things — from palm-wine fermentation to monoclonal antibodies. Genetic engineering is one very powerful "knife" in that kitchen: it lets you cut a gene out of one organism and paste it into another. All knife-work is cooking, but not all cooking needs the knife.
