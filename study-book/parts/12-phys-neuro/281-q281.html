Write short note on: Eyelids, Lacrimal gland, conjunctiva, & optic disk.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Four crisp mini-answers: each with definition/location, structure, function and one applied point. The lacrimal apparatus diagram (gland → ducts → puncta → canaliculi → sac → nasolacrimal duct) and the "blind spot" explanation of the optic disc are the high-yield marks.

(a) Eyelids (palpebrae)

Definition: two movable musculo-fibrous folds (upper and lower) in front of the eyeball. The gap between them is the palpebral fissure; they meet at the medial and lateral canthi.

Layers (front to back):

  1. Skin — thin, loose; eyelashes (cilia) at the margin.
  2. Subcutaneous areolar tissue — no fat (so oedema collects easily).
  3. Orbicularis oculi (palpebral part) — closes the lids; facial nerve (VII).
  4. Submuscular areolar tissue.
  5. Tarsal plate (dense fibrous "skeleton" of the lid) containing tarsal (Meibomian) glands; levator palpebrae superioris (oculomotor nerve) and the smooth superior tarsal (Müller's) muscle (sympathetic) insert into the upper lid and raise it.
  6. Palpebral conjunctiva.

Glands of the lid: Meibomian (modified sebaceous; oily layer of the tear film), glands of Zeis (sebaceous, with lashes), glands of Moll (modified sweat glands).

Functions: protect the eye from injury, dust and excessive light; blinking spreads the tear film over the cornea and pushes tears toward the puncta; the corneal (blink) reflex (afferent — ophthalmic division of trigeminal; efferent — facial nerve) protects the cornea; closure during sleep prevents drying.

Applied: stye (hordeolum — infection of a gland of Zeis), chalazion (Meibomian cyst), ptosis (oculomotor palsy or loss of sympathetic tone to Müller's muscle in Horner's syndrome), inability to close the eye in facial palsy → exposure keratitis.

(b) Lacrimal gland

Location: serous gland in the lacrimal fossa at the upper, lateral part of the orbit. The levator aponeurosis divides it into a larger orbital part and a smaller palpebral part; about 10–12 ducts open into the superior conjunctival fornix.

Nerve supply (secretomotor): parasympathetic — lacrimal (superior salivatory) nucleus → facial nerve → greater petrosal nerve → nerve of pterygoid canal → pterygopalatine ganglion → zygomatic nerve → lacrimal nerve → gland. Sympathetic fibres are mainly vasomotor.

Tears: watery fluid containing electrolytes (mainly NaCl), lysozyme, lactoferrin and IgA. The tear film has three layers: outer lipid (Meibomian glands), middle aqueous (lacrimal gland), inner mucous (goblet cells of conjunctiva).

Functions of tears: keep the cornea and conjunctiva moist; give the cornea a smooth optical surface; supply O2 to the avascular cornea; wash away foreign particles; antibacterial action (lysozyme, IgA); emotional (psychic) weeping.

Drainage (lacrimal passages): lacrimal lake at medial canthus → lacrimal puncta → lacrimal canaliculi → lacrimal sac → nasolacrimal duct → inferior meatus of the nose (that is why you sniff when you cry).

Applied: dry eye (xerophthalmia) when secretion fails; epiphora (watering) when drainage is blocked; facial nerve lesion proximal to the geniculate ganglion → reduced lacrimation ("crocodile tears" after faulty regeneration).

(c) Conjunctiva

Definition: thin, transparent mucous membrane that lines the inner surface of the eyelids and is reflected onto the front of the eyeball.

  • Parts: palpebral conjunctiva (lines lids, firmly adherent to tarsal plate); bulbar conjunctiva (covers anterior sclera, loosely attached, ends at the limbus — over the cornea only its epithelium continues as corneal epithelium); fornices (superior and inferior) — the lines of reflection.
  • The potential space between lids and eyeball = conjunctival sac; it opens at the palpebral fissure.
  • Epithelium: non-keratinised stratified epithelium with goblet cells (secrete mucin — the inner layer of the tear film).
  • Functions: protection and lubrication; allows the eyeball to move smoothly under the lids; contains lymphoid tissue for defence.
  • Applied: conjunctivitis ("Apollo"); subconjunctival haemorrhage; pallor of palpebral conjunctiva = sign of anaemia; yellow bulbar conjunctiva/sclera = jaundice; Bitot's spots and conjunctival xerosis in vitamin A deficiency.

(d) Optic disc (optic papilla)

  • Definition: the circular pale-pink area of the retina, about 1.5 mm in diameter, situated about 3 mm medial (nasal) to the macula/posterior pole, where the optic nerve fibres leave the eyeball.
  • Central artery and vein of the retina enter and leave at its centre; the central depression is the physiological cup.
  • It has no rods or cones, so light falling on it is not perceived → blind spot. In the visual field the blind spot lies about 15° to the temporal side of the point of fixation. It is demonstrated by Mariotte's experiment (a dot disappears when its image falls on the disc).
  • We do not notice the blind spot because the two blind spots do not correspond (each eye covers the other's gap) and the brain "fills in" the gap.
  • Applied: seen with the ophthalmoscope — papilloedema (swollen disc in raised intracranial pressure), optic atrophy (pale disc), glaucomatous cupping (enlarged cup).
Understand it (plain English) The eyelids are wipers, the lacrimal gland is the washer-fluid tank (upper-outer corner), and the tears drain like a sink into the nose at the inner corner. The conjunctiva is the thin "cling film" lining the lids and the white of the eye. The optic disc is the exit hole for the cables of the retina — no sensors there, so it is blind.
Memory trick Tear drainage: "Please Can Somebody Drain" = Puncta, Canaliculi, Sac, Duct (→ inferior meatus). Lid glands: "MZ-M" = Meibomian (oil), Zeis (sebaceous), Moll (sweat). Tear film layers outside-in: "LAM" = Lipid, Aqueous, Mucin.
How to write this in the exam Give each structure its own heading and roughly equal space (about 5 minutes each). Use a pattern: definition → structure → function → applied. Draw the lacrimal apparatus once; it illustrates both the gland and the drainage.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying tears drain into the middle meatus — it is the inferior meatus. Placing the optic disc lateral to the fovea (it is nasal/medial). Saying the conjunctiva covers the cornea fully — only its epithelium continues over it.
