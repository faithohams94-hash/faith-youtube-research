Introduction

Aqueous humour is the clear, watery fluid filling the anterior and posterior chambers of the eye (the anterior segment, in front of the lens). It is continuously formed and drained, at about 2–3 µL/min; the balance between formation and drainage keeps the intraocular pressure (IOP) at 12–20 mm Hg (average about 15 mm Hg).

Formation

Formed by the epithelium of the ciliary processes of the ciliary body by three processes: (1) active secretion (major — Na+ pumped into the posterior chamber, Cl− and HCO3− follow, water follows osmotically; carbonic anhydrase is needed), (2) diffusion, and (3) ultrafiltration from the ciliary capillaries.

Flow chart of circulation and drainage

Ciliary processes (ciliary body) — secretion↓ Posterior chamber (between iris and lens)↓ Pupil↓ Anterior chamber (between cornea and iris)↓ Iridocorneal (filtration) angle → trabecular meshwork (spaces of Fontana)↓ Canal of Schlemm (sinus venosus sclerae)↓ Aqueous veins → episcleral (anterior ciliary) veins → venous circulation

Routes of drainage

RoutePathwayShare
Conventional (trabecular)Angle → trabecular meshwork → canal of Schlemm → aqueous / episcleral veinsMajor (about 80–90%)
Uveoscleral (unconventional)Through the ciliary muscle into the suprachoroidal space → venous circulation of ciliary body, choroid and scleraMinor (about 10–20%)

Applied physiology — glaucoma

  • If drainage is obstructed while secretion continues, IOP rises above normal → glaucoma. Persistent high IOP compresses the optic nerve fibres and their blood supply at the optic disc (cupping) → progressive loss of visual field and blindness.
  • Open-angle glaucoma: angle open but trabecular meshwork / canal of Schlemm resistance increased. Closed-angle glaucoma: the iris is pushed forward and blocks the angle (may be precipitated by pupil dilatation).
  • IOP is measured with a tonometer. Secretion can be reduced by carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide) — this follows from the role of carbonic anhydrase in formation.

Conclusion

Aqueous humour is secreted by the ciliary processes, flows from the posterior chamber through the pupil into the anterior chamber, and drains at the iridocorneal angle through the trabecular meshwork into the canal of Schlemm; this steady turnover maintains the intraocular pressure.

Understand it (plain English) Picture a tap and a sink. The ciliary processes are the tap, constantly running behind the iris. The water flows forward through the pupil "doorway" into the front room (anterior chamber) and drains out through the sieve (trabecular meshwork) at the corner where the cornea meets the iris, into a ring-shaped drain pipe (canal of Schlemm). Block the sink and the pressure in the room rises — that is glaucoma.
Memory trick "Can People Push Against The Swinging Exit?" = Ciliary processes, Posterior chamber, Pupil, Anterior chamber, Trabecular meshwork, Schlemm's canal, Episcleral veins.
How to write this in the exam The question says "schematic" — the diagram IS the answer. Draw it first and large, number the arrows, then give the flow chart, then 3–4 lines on formation, IOP value and glaucoma. 12 minutes.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying aqueous is formed in the anterior chamber (it is formed in the posterior chamber). Saying aqueous fills the space behind the lens (that is vitreous). Draining Schlemm's canal into lymphatics — it drains into veins.
