List the six components of visual pathway.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants The six components in correct order, each with a one-line description (which fibres cross, where the relay is, Brodmann area). A labelled diagram turns a 5-mark list into full marks.

Definition

The visual pathway is the chain of neurons that carries impulses from the retina to the visual cortex in the occipital lobe. Within the retina: rods and cones (receptors) → bipolar cells (first-order neurons) → ganglion cells (second-order neurons), whose axons form the optic nerve. The third-order neurons are in the lateral geniculate body.

The six components (in order)

  1. Optic nerve — axons of the ganglion cells of one retina; leaves the eye at the optic disc, passes through the optic canal. Carries fibres from both the nasal and temporal halves of that retina.
  2. Optic chiasma — X-shaped junction above the pituitary (sella turcica). Fibres from the nasal half of each retina cross to the opposite side; temporal fibres stay uncrossed.
  3. Optic tract — from chiasma to lateral geniculate body. Each tract carries temporal fibres of the same eye + nasal fibres of the opposite eye, i.e. the whole opposite half of the visual field. Some fibres leave for the pretectal nucleus (light reflex) and superior colliculus.
  4. Lateral geniculate body (LGB) — relay nucleus in the thalamus; six layers. Crossed (contralateral) fibres end in layers 1, 4, 6; uncrossed (ipsilateral) in layers 2, 3, 5. Layers 1–2 magnocellular (movement), 3–6 parvocellular (colour, fine detail).
  5. Optic radiation (geniculocalcarine tract) — axons of LGB neurons; pass through the retrolentiform part of the internal capsule. Lower fibres loop forward into the temporal lobe (Meyer's loop); upper fibres pass through the parietal lobe.
  6. Visual cortex — primary visual area (Brodmann area 17, striate cortex) on the banks of the calcarine sulcus; visual association areas 18 and 19 around it.
Understand it (plain English) Each eye's nasal retina looks out to the side (temporal field). To make each half of the brain handle one half of the world, only the nasal fibres need to swap sides at the chiasma. After the chiasma, everything on the left side of the brain is "seeing" the right side of the world.
Memory trick Order: "Never Cross The Line, Run Clever" = Nerve, Chiasma, Tract, LGB, Radiation, Cortex. LGB layers: "See I? I see. See I see I" — 1 C, 2 I, 3 I, 4 C, 5 I, 6 C (C = contralateral, I = ipsilateral). Meyer's loop: "pie in the sky" — temporal lesion → upper quadrant defect.
How to write this in the exam Number the six components exactly in order, one line each, then add the diagram. If time allows, add the three-neuron chain (bipolar → ganglion → LGB) and one line on the light-reflex branch. 8–10 minutes.
Mistakes that cost marks Writing "medial geniculate body" (that is hearing). Saying temporal fibres cross (only nasal fibres cross). Giving the visual cortex as area 4 or 41 — it is area 17.
