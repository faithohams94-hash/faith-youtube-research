What are the deficiency syndromes found in ascorbic acid deficiency.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Name the syndromes — scurvy (adult), infantile scurvy (Barlow's disease), plus anaemia, poor wound healing, bone changes, lowered immunity — and explain EACH by its biochemical cause (defective collagen hydroxylation, iron absorption, folate, antioxidant role).

Introduction

Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) is a water-soluble vitamin, a hexose derivative (6-carbon) and strong reducing agent. Humans, other primates and guinea pigs cannot synthesise it because they lack L-gulonolactone oxidase, so it must come from the diet (citrus fruits, guava, amla, green leafy vegetables, tomatoes). RDA ≈ 75 mg/day (adult). Deficiency produces scurvy.

Biochemical functions whose loss causes the syndromes

  1. Collagen synthesis — cofactor (reductant) for prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase, keeping their iron in the Fe2+ state. Hydroxyproline forms H-bonds that stabilise the triple helix; hydroxylysine is needed for cross-links and glycosylation.
  2. Iron absorption — reduces dietary Fe3+ to absorbable Fe2+; also helps mobilise ferritin iron.
  3. Folate metabolism — keeps tetrahydrofolate in reduced form (helps formation of active THF).
  4. Antioxidant — scavenges free radicals; regenerates vitamin E.
  5. Others: dopamine β-hydroxylase (noradrenaline synthesis), carnitine synthesis, steroid hydroxylation in adrenal cortex, tyrosine catabolism (p-hydroxyphenylpyruvate hydroxylase), bile acid formation (7α-hydroxylase), immune function (phagocyte activity).

Deficiency syndromes

1. Scurvy (adult)
  1. Haemorrhagic signs — swollen, spongy, bleeding gums (only if teeth present); perifollicular haemorrhages; petechiae and bruising; haemorrhage into joints and muscles. Cause: fragile capillaries from defective collagen in vessel walls.
  2. Skin — perifollicular hyperkeratosis with corkscrew hairs; dry rough skin.
  3. Teeth — loosening and loss of teeth (defective dentine and periodontal collagen).
  4. Delayed wound healing — old scars may break down; poor tensile strength of new scars.
  5. Bones — osteoporosis (defective osteoid matrix), easy fractures.
  6. Anaemia — see below.
  7. General — weakness, fatigue, lowered resistance to infection (impaired leucocyte function).
2. Infantile scurvy (Barlow's disease)
  • Usually 6–12 months, in infants fed only boiled/processed milk without fruit juice (boiling destroys vitamin C).
  • Subperiosteal haemorrhages → painful swollen limbs; infant lies in "frog-leg" position and cries on handling (pseudoparalysis).
  • Defective bone formation at growing ends; "scorbutic rosary" at costochondral junctions (sharp, angular — unlike the rounded rachitic rosary).
  • Bleeding gums if teeth have erupted; irritability, failure to thrive.
3. Anaemia of vitamin C deficiency
  • Microcytic hypochromic — reduced absorption of non-haem iron (Fe3+ not reduced) + blood loss from haemorrhages.
  • Megaloblastic may also occur — impaired formation/maintenance of THF.
4. Other biochemical effects
  • Reduced catecholamine (noradrenaline) synthesis and adrenal steroid production under stress.
  • Reduced carnitine → fatigue (impaired fatty acid oxidation).
  • Increased oxidative damage; impaired immunity.

Laboratory pointers

Low plasma / leucocyte (buffy coat) ascorbate — leucocyte level reflects tissue stores best; positive capillary fragility (Hess tourniquet) test; ascorbic acid saturation (load) test.

Conclusion

Almost every feature of ascorbic-acid deficiency traces back to one lesion — failure of prolyl and lysyl hydroxylation of collagen — plus impaired iron absorption and folate metabolism, producing scurvy, Barlow's disease and anaemia.

Understand it (plain English) Collagen is a rope made of three strands. The "-OH" groups that vitamin C helps add are the knots that hold the strands together. No vitamin C → no knots → rope frays. Capillaries leak (bleeding gums), scars come apart, bone matrix is poor. The anaemia is the bonus problem: vitamin C is what turns food iron into the absorbable Fe2+ form.
Memory trick Scurvy = the 4 H's: Haemorrhage, Hyperkeratosis (corkscrew hairs), Hypochondriasis/weakness, Haematological (anaemia). Add "gums, guns (wounds), bones".
How to write this in the exam Spend 3 lines on functions FIRST, then list syndromes — every sign must be tied back to a function ("because collagen is under-hydroxylated..."). Draw Fig 169.1. Mention Barlow's disease by name — it separates you from the average script.
Mistakes that cost marks Saying vitamin C is a coenzyme of prolyl hydroxylase in the same way as B vitamins — say it is a reducing cofactor keeping Fe2+. Writing that bleeding gums occur in edentulous people — they do not. Confusing scorbutic rosary (sharp) with rachitic rosary (rounded).
