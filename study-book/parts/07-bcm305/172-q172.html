How would you determine BMR.

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants Definition of BMR + the basal conditions → principle (direct vs indirect calorimetry) → Benedict–Roth procedure step by step → calculation (O2 × 4.825 kcal/L; DuBois surface area; kcal/m²/h) → normal values and % deviation → factors affecting BMR.

Definition

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the minimum amount of energy required to maintain the vital functions of the body (heart beat, respiration, kidney function, ion pumps, body temperature) when the subject is at complete physical and mental rest, in the post-absorptive state, under comfortable conditions. It is expressed as kcal per square metre of body surface area per hour (kcal/m²/h). Normal: adult male ≈ 40 kcal/m²/h, adult female ≈ 37 kcal/m²/h; roughly 1500–1800 kcal/day for an adult.

Basal conditions (must be satisfied)

  1. Post-absorptive — 12–14 h after the last meal (no specific dynamic action of food).
  2. Physical rest — lying supine; no strenuous exercise the day before; rested 30 min before the test.
  3. Mental rest — relaxed, awake, free of anxiety (adrenaline raises BMR); after a good night's sleep.
  4. Thermoneutral room — comfortable temperature (≈ 20–25 °C) so no shivering or sweating.
  5. No fever, no drugs affecting metabolism (thyroxine, caffeine, nicotine); usually done in the early morning.

Principle — methods

MethodPrincipleComment
Direct calorimetry (Atwater–Benedict respiration chamber)Subject inside an insulated chamber; heat given off is absorbed by water circulating in pipes; heat = mass of water × rise in temperatureAccurate but bulky, expensive; research only
Indirect calorimetry — closed circuit (Benedict–Roth spirometer)Measure O2 consumed; heat produced = O2 (L) × 4.825 kcal/L (the calorific equivalent of O2 at RQ 0.82 of a mixed diet)Routine clinical/practical method
Indirect — open circuit (Douglas bag)Expired air collected; analysed for O2 and CO2 → O2 consumed and RQAllows RQ calculation; field use

Procedure (Benedict–Roth apparatus)

  1. Subject prepared under basal conditions; weight and height recorded.
  2. Bell filled with pure O2; subject lies supine, nose clip applied and mouthpiece inserted; breathes from the bell for a few minutes to accustom.
  3. The kymograph is started and breathing recorded for 6 minutes. Expired CO2 is absorbed by soda lime, so the bell falls in proportion to O2 used.
  4. A line is drawn through the troughs (or peaks); the vertical fall over 6 min gives O2 consumed, read from the calibration (mL per mm).
  5. Room temperature and barometric pressure are noted to correct volume to STP (0 °C, 760 mmHg, dry).

Calculation

O2 consumed in 6 min (L, corrected to STP)↓ × 10O2 consumed per hour (L/h)↓ × 4.825 kcal/LHeat produced (kcal/h)↓ ÷ body surface area (m²)BMR (kcal/m²/h)↓ compare with normal for age & sex% deviation = (observed − normal) / normal × 100

Body surface area — DuBois formula: A (m²) = W0.425 × H0.725 × 0.007184 (W in kg, H in cm), or read from a DuBois nomogram. Normal range: ±15% (some texts ±10%) of the standard value. See the worked example in Q173.

Factors affecting BMR

Increase BMRDecrease BMR
Larger surface area; males; children/adolescents (growth); pregnancy (later months); fever (≈ 12–13% per °C rise); cold climate; hyperthyroidism (+50 to +100%); adrenaline, stress; tumours, leukaemia; polycythaemia; high-protein dietFemales; old age; starvation/undernutrition; sleep; hypothyroidism (−30 to −50%); Addison's disease; hypopituitarism; warm climate

Applications

Calculating daily energy requirement (BMR + SDA + physical activity); historically used to assess thyroid function; studying obesity and malnutrition.

Conclusion

BMR is best determined clinically by indirect calorimetry with the Benedict–Roth apparatus: O2 consumed under basal conditions × 4.825 kcal/L, divided by surface area, gives kcal/m²/h, compared with the normal for age and sex.

Understand it (plain English) Burning food uses oxygen. For an average mixed diet, every litre of O2 burnt releases about 4.8 kcal of heat. So if you measure how much O2 someone uses while lying still and fasting, you know how much heat (energy) their body is producing just to stay alive. We divide by body surface area because heat is lost through the skin, so big and small people can be compared fairly.
Memory trick Basal conditions — "FRESH": Fasting 12 h, Rest (physical), Emotional calm, Supine & awake, Heat-neutral room. Numbers: "6 × 10 × 4.825" (6-min test, ×10 for hour, ×4.825 kcal/L).
