(a) Seasonal ataxia and thiaminase

  1. Definition — Seasonal (acute) ataxia is an outbreak-type acute cerebellar-type ataxia reported in south-western Nigeria, occurring each year in the rainy season (roughly July–September).
  2. Cause — consumption of roasted larvae of the African silkworm, Anaphe venata, a popular protein snack in that season when other foods are scarce. The larvae contain a heat-resistant thiaminase (survives roasting).
  3. Thiaminase — an enzyme that cleaves thiamine at the methylene bridge into its pyrimidine and thiazole halves, inactivating it. Other dietary sources: raw fish and shellfish; tea and coffee contain heat-stable anti-thiamine polyphenols.
  4. Mechanism — destroyed thiamine → ↓ thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) → ↓ pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase → neurons (which rely on glucose) cannot make enough ATP; pyruvate and lactate accumulate → acute dysfunction, especially of the cerebellum and brainstem.
  5. Features — sudden unsteady gait, inability to stand, tremors, dysarthria, vomiting, altered consciousness; often several members of a household; poorly-nourished people (already marginal thiamine stores) are most affected.
  6. Proof of thiamine link — rapid improvement after thiamine; low erythrocyte transketolase activity. Prevention: avoid/limit larvae, improve thiamine intake.

(b) Spina bifida

  1. Definition — a neural tube defect (NTD) in which the vertebral arches fail to fuse posteriorly, due to failure of closure of the caudal (posterior) neuropore around the 4th week (day ~26–28) of embryonic life.
  2. Types — spina bifida occulta (bony defect only; tuft of hair/dimple), meningocele (meninges + CSF herniate), meningomyelocele (cord/nerve roots also herniate — most common severe form), myeloschisis/rachischisis (open neural plate).
  3. Biochemical link to folate — neural tube closure involves very rapid cell division. Folate (as N5,N10-methylene-THF) is needed for thymidylate and purine synthesis (DNA) and, via methionine, for SAM-dependent methylation. Maternal folate deficiency or impaired folate metabolism (and hyperhomocysteinaemia) increases NTD risk. Antifolate drugs in early pregnancy also raise risk.
  4. Detection — raised α-fetoprotein in maternal serum and amniotic fluid (open defects); ultrasound.
  5. Prevention — periconceptional folic acid supplementation (begun before conception, because the tube closes before many women know they are pregnant) markedly reduces NTDs.

(c) Folate pathway in chemotherapy

Rapidly dividing cells (cancer cells, bacteria, malaria parasites) need large amounts of THF for DNA synthesis. Drugs that block folate enzymes therefore stop their multiplication. Selectivity comes from (i) microbes having to synthesise folate from PABA (humans cannot — they take preformed folate), and (ii) species differences in DHFR.

(d) Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome

  1. Definition — a neuropsychiatric disorder due to thiamine deficiency, seen mainly in chronic alcoholics (poor intake, impaired absorption and storage of thiamine). Also seen in prolonged vomiting and malnutrition.
  2. Wernicke's encephalopathy (acute) — triad: ophthalmoplegia (nystagmus, lateral rectus palsy), ataxia, global confusion.
  3. Korsakoff's psychosis (chronic) — severe anterograde and retrograde amnesia with confabulation; largely irreversible.
  4. Biochemistry — TPP is the coenzyme of pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and transketolase. The brain, which depends on glucose oxidation, suffers energy failure → lesions in mamillary bodies, periaqueductal grey and thalamus. A genetic variant of transketolase with low affinity for TPP predisposes some people.
  5. Diagnosis — ↓ erythrocyte transketolase activity, ↑ TPP effect; raised blood pyruvate/lactate.
  6. Giving glucose alone to a thiamine-deficient patient can precipitate or worsen Wernicke's (more glucose load, no coenzyme to oxidise it) — thiamine is given first.

Conclusion

Two of these notes are about thiamine (destroyed by thiaminase in seasonal ataxia; deficient in alcoholics in WKS) and two about folate (DNA synthesis in the embryo — spina bifida; and the same pathway exploited by antifolate drugs).

Understand it (plain English) The silkworm larvae carry a "thiamine-shredding" enzyme that survives roasting, so eating them wipes out the little thiamine a poorly-fed person has; the brain's sugar engine stalls and the cerebellum (balance) fails first. For folate: every dividing cell needs THF to make thymine. Block THF production in a bacterium, parasite or tumour and it cannot copy its DNA.
Memory trick Antifolates — "Super Team Plays Match": Sulphonamide (DHPS, bacteria) · Trimethoprim (bacterial DHFR) · Pyrimethamine (Plasmodium DHFR) · Methotrexate (Man's DHFR). WKS triad: "COAT" — Confusion, Ophthalmoplegia, Ataxia, Thiamine.
How to write this in the exam If time is short choose (a), (c), (d): they are pure biochemistry. ~7 minutes per note. For (c) the diagram + table alone are worth most marks. Write "Anaphe venata" and "heat-resistant thiaminase" for (a).
Mistakes that cost marks Saying sulphonamides act on human cells (humans don't make folate from PABA). Saying methotrexate inhibits thymidylate synthase (that is 5-FU; methotrexate inhibits DHFR). Calling seasonal ataxia an infection.
