A 30yr old male, weight 70kg and height 170cm consumed 1.2L of O2 under basal conditions. Calculate the BMR (Add a note on the clinical aspect).

Revision tracker: Read Drew diagram Wrote from memory
What the examiner wants A clearly stated assumption about the recording time → O2/h → kcal/h (× 4.825) → kcal/day → surface area by DuBois → kcal/m²/h → % deviation from the normal (≈ 40 kcal/m²/h for a 30-yr male) → interpretation and clinical note. Units at every line.

Data given

  • Male, age 30 years; W = 70 kg; H = 170 cm.
  • O2 consumed under basal conditions = 1.2 L.
  • Constants: calorific value of O2 (mixed diet, RQ ≈ 0.82) = 4.825 kcal/L; normal BMR for a 30-year-old male ≈ 40 kcal/m²/h (normal range ±15%).

Assumption (state it in the script)

The question gives the volume but not the time. In the standard Benedict–Roth test, O2 consumption is recorded for 6 minutes (and the volume is taken as already corrected to STP). I therefore take 1.2 L as the O2 consumed in 6 minutes. (1.2 L per minute is impossible at rest — it would give ≈ 347 kcal/h, a level seen only during exercise — so the 6-min interpretation is the sensible one.)

Step 1 — O2 consumed per hour

O2/h = 1.2 L × (60 ÷ 6) = 1.2 × 10 = 12.0 L/h (= 200 mL/min).

Step 2 — Heat produced (energy expenditure)

Per hour = 12.0 L/h × 4.825 kcal/L = 57.9 kcal/h
Per day = 57.9 × 24 = 1389.6 kcal/day (≈ 1390 kcal/day ≈ 5.8 MJ/day).

Step 3 — Body surface area (DuBois formula)

A = W0.425 × H0.725 × 0.007184

W0.425log 70 = 1.8451; × 0.425 = 0.7842; antilog6.084

Step 4 — BMR

BMR = 57.9 kcal/h ÷ 1.81 m² = 32.0 kcal/m²/h
(per day per m²: 32.0 × 24 ≈ 768 kcal/m²/day)

Step 5 — Compare with normal

% deviation = (observed − normal) ÷ normal × 100 = (32.0 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = −20%

Clinical aspect

  1. Interpretation — a BMR of −20% lies outside the normal ±15%, so the man is hypometabolic.
  2. Causes of low BMR (to consider): hypothyroidism/myxoedema (classically −30 to −50%), prolonged starvation/undernutrition (adaptive fall in BMR, ↓ T3), Addison's disease, hypopituitarism, and technical error (subject asleep, wrong STP correction, leak in the circuit).
  3. Causes of high BMR (for contrast): hyperthyroidism (+50 to +100%), fever (≈ +12% per °C), pregnancy, leukaemia, phaeochromocytoma, anxiety.
  4. Uses of BMR — calculating daily energy requirement for diet planning (BMR + SDA ≈ 10% + activity); BMR was historically the main test of thyroid function, now replaced by serum TSH, T3, T4. A low BMR in this man should therefore be followed by thyroid function tests.
  5. Nutritional significance — a lower BMR means fewer calories needed; if intake is not reduced, weight gain follows (one reason hypothyroid patients gain weight).
Understand it (plain English) Four multiplications and a division: minutes → hour (×10), litres → kcal (×4.825), then share it out per square metre of skin (÷1.81). Then ask: is 32 close to 40? It is 8 units short — that is 20% short — too low to be normal.
Memory trick "Ten, Four-point-eight, DuBois, Forty" — the four numbers that solve every BMR question: ×10 (6-min test), ×4.825, ÷ DuBois area, compare with 40 (male) / 37 (female).
How to write this in the exam Write "Data", "Assumption", Steps 1–5 as headings, each with its formula then the numbers then units. Box the final answer. Put the clinical note last. If allowed a calculator, still show the DuBois formula; if not, log tables (as shown) earn full method marks.
Mistakes that cost marks Not stating the time assumption. Dividing by body weight instead of surface area. Reporting kcal/day as "BMR" without the per-m² figure. Forgetting to say whether the result is normal.
