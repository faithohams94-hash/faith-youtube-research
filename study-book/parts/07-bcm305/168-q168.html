Explain the biochemical forms of cobalamin found in metabolism.

Introduction

Cobalamin (vitamin B12) is a water-soluble vitamin, the only vitamin that contains a metal (cobalt). It is synthesised only by micro-organisms; humans obtain it solely from animal foods (liver, meat, fish, egg, milk). Its different "forms" differ only in the group attached to the cobalt atom on the upper (β) side of the molecule.

1. Structure (common to all forms)

  1. Corrin ring — four reduced pyrrole rings (A–D) joined by three methene bridges; rings A and D are linked directly (this is what makes corrin differ from porphyrin).
  2. Cobalt at the centre, coordinated to the four pyrrole nitrogens. It can exist as Co3+, Co2+, Co+.
  3. Lower (α) axial ligand — 5,6-dimethylbenzimidazole (DMB) ribonucleotide, attached through an aminopropanol link to the corrin ring.
  4. Upper (β) axial ligand "R" — variable; it names the form of cobalamin.

3. Coenzyme functions — the two B12 reactions in humans

  1. Methionine synthase (N5-methyl-THF–homocysteine methyltransferase): methyl group passes N5-methyl-THF → cobalamin → homocysteine, giving methionine and regenerating free THF. This is the ONLY reaction that can release THF from N5-methyl-THF.
  2. Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase: intramolecular rearrangement (isomerisation) of L-methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA, so that propionate from odd-chain fatty acids and some amino acids enters the TCA cycle.

4. Transport and storage forms (the protein-bound forms)

Food B12 (protein-bound)→Stomach: HCl/pepsin free it; binds R-protein (haptocorrin)→Duodenum: pancreatic proteases release it → binds Intrinsic Factor→Terminal ileum: IF–B12 receptor (cubilin)→Blood: Transcobalamin II → tissues
  • Transcobalamin II — the main transport protein delivering B12 to tissues (receptor-mediated endocytosis).
  • Transcobalamin I (haptocorrin) — binds most plasma B12; storage/circulating reserve.
  • Liver store ≈ 4–5 mg, mostly as adenosylcobalamin — enough for several years, so dietary deficiency appears slowly.

5. Biochemical consequences of deficiency

  1. Megaloblastic anaemia — via the methyl-folate trap: THF is locked as N5-methyl-THF → less N5,N10-methylene-THF → impaired thymidylate (DNA) synthesis.
  2. Neurological damage (subacute combined degeneration of the cord, peripheral neuropathy) — attributed to methylmalonyl-CoA accumulation (abnormal/branched fatty acids in myelin) and reduced methionine/SAM for methylation.
  3. Methylmalonic aciduria and homocystinuria — diagnostic markers.
  4. Pernicious anaemia — autoimmune loss of intrinsic factor; also gastrectomy, ileal resection, fish tapeworm (Diphyllobothrium latum), strict vegetarians.

Conclusion

Of the five forms of cobalamin, cyano-, hydroxo- and aquo-cobalamin are precursor/pharmacological forms, while methylcobalamin and 5'-deoxyadenosylcobalamin are the two coenzyme forms, serving methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase respectively.

Understand it (plain English) Think of B12 as a cobalt "hand" sitting in a ring. The lower side is always held by DMB; the upper side holds whatever it is carrying. When it carries a methyl group it works in the cytosol making methionine; when it carries an adenosyl group it works in mitochondria moving atoms around in methylmalonyl-CoA. The CN and OH versions are just "packaging" forms the body has to unwrap.
Memory trick "CHAMA" — Cyano, Hydroxo, Aquo, Methyl, Adenosyl. The last two (Methyl → Methionine; Adenosyl → mutAse) are the working coenzymes.
How to write this in the exam Intro (1 line) → Fig 168.1 → table of forms → Fig 168.2 with both reactions → transport (TC II) → deficiency (methyl-folate trap) → conclusion. 20 minutes. Use the phrase "methyl-folate trap" — examiners look for it.
Mistakes that cost marks (1) Calling cyanocobalamin the "active form" — it is not. (2) Mixing up locations: methyl-Cbl = cytosol, adenosyl-Cbl = mitochondria. (3) Saying transcobalamin I is the main tissue-delivery protein — it is TC II. (4) Saying corrin = porphyrin; corrin lacks one methene bridge (A–D directly linked).
