The 3-pass plan for a busy student

  1. Pass 1 (skim): for each question read only the examiner box, the headings of the model answer, and the diagram. About 3 minutes per question.
  2. Pass 2 (understand): read the full answer plus the Understand it box. Redraw every diagram once from memory.
  3. Pass 3 (exam mode): pick a question, set a timer, write the answer from memory, then compare. Do the self-tests the night before the paper.

Exam-writing masterclass: how distinction scripts look

1. The universal answer skeleton

Definition / Introduction→Headings from the question's keywords→Labelled diagram→Table (compare/classify)→Applied / clinical→Conclusion
  • Start with a one- or two-line definition. The examiner decides your grade band in the first few lines.
  • Turn the question's own words into headings. If the question says "blood supply, nerve supply and lymphatic drainage", you write three headings with those exact words.
  • Use numbered points, not long paragraphs. One fact per line, with the keyword underlined.

2. Diagrams: where many of the marks are

  • Draw in pencil, with large, clean outlines and labels in ink, written horizontally, with straight leader lines that never cross.
  • Every diagram needs a title ("Fig 1: Coronal section of the scalp showing its five layers") and, where relevant, a key.
  • Put the diagram near the text it explains and refer to it: "(see Fig 1)". About a third to half a page is the right size.
  • Keep flowcharts for pathways and mechanisms: biochemistry and physiology examiners love them.

Short notesMarks ÷ total marks × time. Never spend twice the allotted time on a favourite topic.

4. What lecturers reward

  • Correct textbook terminology (Chaurasia / Vasudevan / Sembulingam wording).
  • Classification tables, "Applied anatomy" or "Clinical significance" sections, and a conclusion.
  • Neat presentation: margins, headings, spacing, and starting each question on a new page.

5. Negative-marking MCQs / True–False

  • Answer only when you can rule out the other options. A blind guess loses more marks on average than leaving it blank.
  • Circle the words EXCEPT / NOT / FALSE in the stem before choosing.
