How to use this pack

StepWhat to do
1. Set up ChatGPT onceOpen a new ChatGPT chat for each scene. Paste the ChatGPT setup message below first, and attach the approved character images for the characters in that scene. Then paste the shot image prompts one at a time.
2. Make the imageFor each shot, attach the character reference images listed on the shot ("Attach refs") and paste the IMAGE PROMPT. If something is wrong, reply: "Same image, only fix: [one thing]."
3. Make the video in GrokUpload the image to Grok Imagine, choose 10 seconds, paste the VIDEO PROMPT exactly. Don't shorten the RULES part. That part is what stops glitches.
4. No breaks between clipsWhen a shot says Start: last frame of the previous shot, pause the previous Grok clip on its very last frame, save it as an image, and use that image as the start for this clip instead of a new ChatGPT image. Use the image prompt only if you need a fresh angle. This makes the two clips join perfectly.
5. EditPut the clips in shot order. Add the on-screen titles ("SUPER" text), music and the opening title card in CapCut. AI tools spell words badly, so text is never generated inside the images.
One speaker per clipGrok mixes up who is talking when two people speak in one clip, so every conversation is split into clips with exactly one speaker (shot numbers like 1.3.1, 1.3.2). The speaker is the focus of the image; the listener stays silent at the edge of the frame. Cut them together in order and it plays as one natural conversation.
Phone calls and off-screen voicesGrok always moves the mouth of the person it can see when there is speech in a clip, so listening shots are made silent: the person on screen just listens and reacts. The other voice (Segun on the phone, the TV, someone off-screen) is added in CapCut: (1) make that character's voice with the Step 1 prompt under the clip (their own face, saying their own line), (2) in CapCut use Extract audio, put it under the listening clip, and choose Audio > Voice effects > Telephone (or the effect named under the clip). The voice then sounds like it comes from the phone, and the listener's mouth never moves.
Quality settingAlways choose Grok's highest quality or resolution option. Your test clips came out around 700 pixels wide, which looks soft. If Grok has no HD option on your plan, upscale the clips in CapCut (Enhance quality or Upscale) before exporting at 1080p.
Faces and brightnessMake sure the face of anyone who speaks is clearly visible in the start image. Grok invents a new face when a face is hidden and the person turns around. Every video prompt now orders Grok to keep hidden faces hidden and to keep the exact brightness and darkness of the image.
Shot typesEvery shot is marked SCENERY (the place before the action, no faces), DIALOGUE (someone speaks, the others react), or SILENT ACTION / AFTER-ACTION (the physical action, or how people react to it). Never skip the scenery and after-action shots: they are what make the film feel real and flowing.
Final frameEvery video prompt tells Grok how the last 2 seconds must look. When the next shot starts from the last frame, the prompt describes that exact picture, so the cut is invisible.
One scene at a timeThe by_scene folder has one text file per scene (Scene_01.txt to Scene_32.txt), with the upload list at the top. Work through one scene from start to finish before starting the next.
If Grok glitchesRegenerate the same clip. Don't change the prompt. If a door, curtain or face still changes, add at the start: "Camera locked, almost no movement."

ChatGPT setup message (paste once at the start of every new chat)

You are the director of photography on my Nigerian drama film. For every image I ask for in this chat, you must follow these rules with no exceptions: 1. Every image is a photorealistic still from a real cinema film, wide 16:9, never an illustration, never a 3D render. 2. My characters must be the SAME people as in the approved character images and character sheets I attach, not lookalikes: the same face, hair, skin tone, age, height, weight and body build. Never change a face. Never make anyone younger, slimmer, lighter-skinned or more glamorous. Match the warm, natural cinematic light and colour of those approved images. 3. Light comes only from real sources in the scene. Soft, natural, warm-neutral movie grade, gently muted. Matte natural skin with real texture. No shine, no glow, no HDR, no airbrushing, no oversaturated colours. 4. The setting, props, wardrobe and time of day I describe for a scene stay identical in every image of that scene, unless I say they change. 5. No readable text, logos or watermarks unless I give the exact words. 6. All characters are fictional adults (one is a 17-year-old boy in ordinary school scenes). Do not add anything I did not ask for. If you can't follow a rule, tell me instead of changing the image. Reply "Ready" and wait for my first shot.
