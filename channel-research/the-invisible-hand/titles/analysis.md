**What the numbers say**
- 6 of 9 titles start with **"Why"** or **"If… Who"**. Every title promises an explanation.
- **All 4 videos above 2M are paradoxes**: they contradict something people believe.
- **The biggest topics are global or huge-audience ones** (the whole world, the UK, Africa). Single non-English-speaking countries (France, Saudi Arabia) get hundreds of thousands, not millions.
- **Remixing works:** the 2.1M video is the 2.9M title reworded, and it reached 2.1M in a month.
- **No numbers in any title.** Numbers go on the thumbnail.

## The 5 title moves

### 1. Promise an explanation with "Why…" or "If… who…?"
A "Why" title promises to explain the cause behind something everyone has noticed. "If X… who/why Y?" builds a logical puzzle the viewer wants to solve.

### 2. Contradict what people believe
- "Giving money to Africa" should help, but the title says it makes it poor.
- The UK is rich, but "no one wants to live there".
- If everyone is in debt, who has the money?

The gap between belief and claim is what drives millions of clicks. **Every 2M+ title uses this.**

### 3. Big stakes, rippling outward (country → continent → world)
"France's debt crisis is about to **break Europe**." "Germany is **risking Europe**." The problem starts in one country and threatens something bigger, which makes the video relevant to viewers who don't live there. Verbs: break, destroy, risk, dying, collapse.

### 4. A one-word opinion in brackets, plus "…" pauses
"(Finally)", "(Overdue)". One bracketed word turns a news headline into a **take**: we saw this coming. It sounds confident and makes the viewer curious about the reasoning. The "…" in the If-titles adds a dramatic pause before the question.

### 5. Short, simple words, and they reuse their own winners
- 5–12 words, everyday vocabulary, the country or region named (except "How to Destroy an Economic Miracle", which hides it so the thumbnail and the click reveal it).
- When a title becomes an outlier, **they make the same idea again** with a fresh angle ("…Who's the Money Owed To?" → "…Who's Lending the Money?").

## Title rules checklist
- [ ] Starts with Why / If… / How to / The (…) Collapse.
- [ ] Contains a contradiction or a stake bigger than one country.
- [ ] 5–12 words, no jargon, and no numbers (numbers go on the thumbnail).
- [ ] Names a country or region with a big audience, or hides it on purpose and reveals it on the thumbnail.
- [ ] The video can **prove** the claim. A paradox title is a promise, not a lie.
- [ ] Different words from the thumbnail text.
- [ ] Not a copy of a competitor's title. Use the same pattern on a different subject or angle.
