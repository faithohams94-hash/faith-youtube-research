## 1. What we can tell about Ink Explainer's discovery strategy

- 🔎 **Titles are browse-first.** Every title is a curiosity question in plain words ("What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Day?"). None are keyword-stuffed ("Ancient Humans | Prehistory | Documentary").
- 💡 **Most views come from browse and suggested, not search.** 16M views in 2 months is far more than search alone could plausibly deliver for this question. Viral reach on the home feed is the most likely source. Upworthy and Yahoo wrote articles about the "what did ancient humans do all day" idea ([Upworthy](https://www.upworthy.com/what-did-ancient-humans-do-all-day-before-work-was-invented-it-wasnt-all-about-survival/), [Yahoo](https://ca.style.yahoo.com/did-ancient-humans-day-invented-220718980.html)), which shows the topic spread beyond YouTube. Note: Yahoo credits a video from a different creator (*Axen*), so the theme is popular across channels.
- 🔎 **The channel description promises "the science behind everyday things".** That's a consistent "everyday life" positioning, the same promise as the titles.
- 💡 **Metadata is not why the video got 16M views.** The topic, title, thumbnail and script retention drive browse traffic. Description and tags help YouTube understand **what** the video is about and **which videos to recommend it next to**. That's useful, but it's the smaller lever.

## 3. A reusable description formula (for this niche)

> **Line 1:** the title's question, restated in different words, with the main keyword.
> **Line 2:** the myth vs the twist, with 1–2 secondary keywords. *(Lines 1–2 are all that shows before "…more" and in search results, so they must make sense alone.)*
> **Body (3–5 short lines):** what the video covers, naming the real finds and places (entities), written like a teaser, not a keyword list.
> **Chapters:** 5–8 timestamps, each a mini-question.
> **Sources:** 3–6 real studies/finds (builds trust and supports monetization review).
> **One CTA:** a question to answer in the comments, plus a link to the next video or playlist.
> **Short note:** "Illustrations are artistic reconstructions."
> **3–4 hashtags** at the end.

**What to leave out:** keyword lists, "tags:" paragraphs, the title copied word-for-word several times, long social-media link lists, and more than ~5 hashtags. They make the description look spammy and help nothing.

## 4. Policy check (official YouTube guidance)

- ✅ **Hashtags:** if a video has **more than 60 hashtags, YouTube ignores all of them**, and **over-tagging may get the video removed from search** ([YouTube Help: hashtags](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658)). Hashtags must relate to the video and must not mislead.
- ✅ **Display:** YouTube shows up to **3 hashtags above the title**, taken from the description, so the first 3 are the ones that matter. *(Commonly reported behaviour; I couldn't open the Help page itself to confirm the exact wording.)*
- 💡 **Studio tags** are widely described as having a **small role** in discovery, mainly to catch misspellings, and the limit is commonly reported as **500 characters** for the whole tag field ([third-party summary](https://hashtagtools.io/blog/youtube-tags-limit-how-many-tags-2026)). I couldn't verify either against YouTube's own page, so the tags below stay well under the limit.
- **AI disclosure:** YouTube's "altered or synthetic content" label is for **realistic** content. Your stylised cartoons don't need it. An "artistic reconstructions" note is still a good accuracy practice.
- **Don't** put Ink Explainer's name (or any other channel's name) in your tags or hashtags. That's misleading metadata.
