## 1. Executive summary

All four thumbnails use the same strategy:

> **Take a feeling the viewer has today (no job, a rainy week, a cold night, "why me?"), put it on one simple stick-figure character in the ancient world, frame it with a warm fire against a cold world, and label it with 2–3 words of modern language.**

The viewer reads it in about one second as **"what was MY life like back then?"** That's a question about themselves, which is why it reaches far beyond history fans.

NO JOBS is the strongest of the four, and the only proven outlier. It has the biggest face, the simplest scene, the brightest colors, and a text line that pokes at a universal frustration: work.

## 2. Visual deconstruction

### 2.1 NO JOBS (the proven outlier)

| Element | What's there | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Main subject** | One caveman's head and body, filling the left ~45% of the frame. Eyes closed, smug smile, hands behind his head, leaning on a rock. He's cropped by the frame edges. | The cropping makes him huge, so he's readable at phone size. "Hands behind head, eyes closed" is a pose everyone recognizes instantly as *no stress at all*. |
| **Visual hierarchy** | 1st: the yellow "NO JOBS" (top-right). 2nd: the relaxed face. 3rd: tiny villagers relaxing in the background (fire, kids playing, people lying under a tree). | Text and face together tell the whole story. The background backs it up without competing: everyone is relaxed, not just him. |
| **Composition** | Face on the lower-left, text on the upper-right: a diagonal that leads the eye from one to the other. The sun sits at the centre. Background figures are small and placed on the right third. | Nothing overlaps the face or the text. The bottom-right corner (where YouTube puts the timestamp) has only grass. |
| **Colour** | Bright sky blue and grass green, yellow text with a black outline, a white face. | Most history and documentary thumbnails are dark, brown or sepia. A **bright daytime** image stands out strongly in a dark-mode feed. Yellow on blue is a near-complementary pair, the highest-contrast combination available. |
| **Typography** | 2 words, all caps, a heavy rounded font, ~25% of the frame height, thick black outline. | Readable at the smallest size. Rounded letters match the friendly cartoon tone; a sharp or serious font would clash. |
| **Expression** | Content, smug, eyes closed. | It's not a shocked "YouTube face". Low-key, relatable emotions are this genre's signature, and they make it feel calm instead of spammy. |
| **Negative space** | Large areas of empty sky around the text and above the background. | The text "breathes" and reads instantly. |
| **Left out on purpose** | No arrows, no circles, no realistic violence, no second headline, no logo. | Every element serves the single idea "they didn't work". |

## 3. Click psychology

| Mechanism | NO JOBS | RAINED ALL WEEK | Why Us? | COZY INSIDE? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity gap** | "No jobs? Then how did they eat?" | "What did they do for 7 days?" | "Why did the weak-looking one survive?" | "Did they really live inside a mammoth?" |
| **Emotional trigger** | Envy and wish-fulfilment (escape from work); mild humour | Boredom you can relate to; coziness | Mystery; a slight threat; identity | Absurd surprise; coziness |
| **Visual tension** | Low. The tension is between the image and the viewer's own life. | Cold vs warm; the food supply | 4 frowning species around 1 nervous one | A blizzard vs a fire inside a dead animal |
| **Story without the title** | Yes: a man with no stress | Yes: stuck inside by the rain | Partly: a strange lineup | Yes: sheltering inside a mammoth |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Bright daytime colours in a dark niche | The split composition | Two clashing art styles | An impossible image |
| **1-second read** | Yes, the clearest of the four | Yes | Slower, because of 5 figures | Yes |
| **Audience** | Anyone with a job, which is almost everyone (widest) | People who like cozy/rain content plus history fans | Science and evolution fans | Cozy-content fans plus history fans |
| **Promise** | "Ancient life had far more free time than yours" | "How they passed long rainy days" | "The reason your species won" | "The clever way they survived winter" |

**Why NO JOBS is probably the biggest:** its emotion (resentment of work and envy of freedom) is shared by the largest number of people, and its image is the simplest. The other three appeal to curiosity; NO JOBS appeals to a **personal grievance**, which drives both clicks and comments. The script makes the same move (*"your alarm screamed at you this morning"*).

## 4. How title and thumbnail work together

**NO JOBS + "What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Day?"** is the best pairing:
- The **title** asks a neutral question.
- The **thumbnail** gives a provocative partial answer ("no jobs") that the viewer half-doubts.
- **Left unresolved:** if they had no jobs, how did they survive, and was it really better than my life?
- They don't repeat each other. The thumbnail adds new information plus an emotion.

**RAINED ALL WEEK** is the weakest pairing, because the thumbnail repeats the title. A version with a twist could do more, for example "BORED?" or "4 DAYS LEFT", which hints at the dwindling food.

**Rule for Paper Origins:** the title asks the question, and the thumbnail gives a **surprising, emotional half-answer** or names a **fear**, never a copy of the title.

## 5. Competitive pattern and repeatability

- All four share one art style: round white stick-figure faces, fur clothing, clean outlines and detailed painted backgrounds. This consistency makes the channel recognizable in the feed. **It's their brand, so don't copy it closely.**
- **Fire appears in all four thumbnails.** It's the genre's symbol for safety and home, and the warm focal point.
- **The text is always a modern phrase or emotion** (jobs, a rainy week, cozy, us), never an archaeology term. Modern words on an ancient image create the "what would my life be like?" effect.
- **The formula is repeatable.** It depends on choosing a modern situation that millions of people know, not on a unique topic. That's why the channel can keep producing hits.
- **Alternative explanations for the views:** Ink Explainer had momentum (from ~51K to 152K subscribers across about 6 videos), strong scripts, and topics with universal appeal. The thumbnails help, but they're one part of the system.

**Your current thumbnails compared** (NO HOSPITAL?, NO GRAVE?, WHO'S WATCHING?):
- The text is about a quarter of the thumbnail width, while theirs spans most of the top. Yours is too small at phone size.
- "NO GRAVE?" uses a skull and dark colours, and "WHO'S WATCHING?" is a dark night scene. Theirs are mostly bright, or have one strong warm glow.
- "NO ___?" copies their text formula exactly, but your images are busier, so you get the resemblance without the clarity.

## 6. The formula, reverse-engineered

> **[A modern feeling the viewer has] + [one big, simple character showing it in an ancient setting] + [warm firelight vs a cold or bright world] + [2–3 words of modern language, often a question] + [one clean diagonal: face ↔ text] = "What would MY life have been like?"**

| Keep (the strategy) | Change for each video (the execution) |
|---|---|
| One dominant character face, at least 35–45% of the frame height | The scene, props and setting |
| A low-key, relatable emotion (worry, boredom, relief, confusion) | Which emotion |
| A warm/cold or bright/dark contrast with one clear focal glow | The colour of the cold side (snow, rain, night, dusk) |
| 2–3 words, a modern word or a question, never a copy of the title | The words |
| Bottom-right corner empty (the timestamp) | Where the text sits (top-left or top-right) |
| No arrows, circles or logos | Supporting background figures |

**What should be yours, not theirs (Paper Origins brand):**
- **The explorer**: your logo's character with the pith helmet and glasses, as a recurring guide in some thumbnails, especially "discovery" ones.
- **A text colour you own**: white with a thick black outline, plus **one key word in fire-orange**, instead of their all-yellow.
- **A subtle paper texture** over the whole image ("Paper Origins"), so it looks hand-drawn on paper. It's distinctive without adding clutter.
- **A different font**, for example **Lilita One** (free on Google Fonts): heavy and rounded like theirs, but not the same.

## 7. Three concepts for your next video

**Assumed video** (from the topic plan; tell me if it changes):
- **Title:** *What Did Ancient Humans Do When Someone Got Sick?*
- **Niche:** ancient humans and prehistory
- **Audience:** curious adults, mostly 18–44, who like history, science and "what would my life be like" content
- **Style:** flat 2D cartoon with clean outlines; Paper Origins explorer as the brand character
- **Summary:** the myth that the sick and injured were left behind, disproved with real evidence of care (for example, Shanidar 1 in Iraq, a man who lived for years with crippling injuries)

### Concept A: closest adaptation, "NO DOCTORS?"
- **Subject:** a caveman lying on a fur bed, his head and shoulders filling the left 45% of the frame. Feverish, with flushed cheeks, a leaf compress on his forehead, half-open tired eyes looking at the viewer.
- **Background:** a warm cave interior; an older woman healer crushing herbs in a stone bowl by the fire; the cold blue night visible through the cave mouth on the right.
- **Text:** "NO DOCTORS?", top-right, with "DOCTORS" in orange.
- **Emotion:** worry and empathy. Everyone has been sick.
- **Curiosity gap:** "What did they do instead, and did it work?"
- **Fit with the title:** the title asks what they did; the thumbnail adds what they were missing.
- **Weakness:** the closest to the NO JOBS pattern. It's safe but predictable. A sick face can look unpleasant, so keep it mild.

### Concept B: stronger curiosity, "LEFT BEHIND?" ⭐ recommended
- **Subject:** an injured caveman with a splinted leg (wrapped in leaves and wooden sticks, no blood) sitting alone in the left foreground, his face large and turned toward the viewer, worried.
- **Story:** the tribe walks away from him toward a warm fire and a sunset on the right, small in the background. **One child in the group has turned around and is reaching back toward him.**
- **Background:** cold blue shadow on his side; warm orange light on the tribe's side.
- **Text:** "LEFT BEHIND?", top-centre or top-right, with "BEHIND" in orange.
- **Emotion:** fear of abandonment, which is primal and universal, plus hope (the child).
- **Curiosity gap:** "Did they really leave the weak to die?" The image says yes, but the child says maybe not.
- **Fit with the title:** the title is neutral ("what did they do?"); the thumbnail names the worst possible answer, and the video disproves it.
- **Promise and payoff:** honest, because the video shows the evidence that they did care for the injured.
- **Weakness:** two areas of interest (him and the tribe). The man has to dominate, about 50% of the frame height, and the tribe stays small.

### Concept C: a different angle, "HE SURVIVED?"
- **Subject:** **the Paper Origins explorer** (pith helmet, round glasses) in the right foreground, holding a magnifying glass, shocked, looking at the viewer.
- **Supporting:** an ancient skeleton laid out in an excavation pit on the left, with one arm bone visibly healed (thickened and bent). The healed spot glows softly.
- **Background:** a warm, sandy dig site at golden hour, with a paper texture.
- **Text:** "HE SURVIVED?", top-left.
- **Emotion:** discovery and disbelief.
- **Curiosity gap:** "Survived what? And how could they tell?"
- **Fit with the title:** it shows the *evidence* angle that sets your channel apart from a story-only channel.
- **Weakness:** a skeleton is less emotional than a living face, and bones can look grim. Keep it clean and cartoon-like, not creepy. Its main value is that it **builds your brand character**.

**How to use these:** upload all three in **Studio → Test & Compare**. YouTube shows each version to different viewers and keeps the one that people watch the most.

## 8. Recommendation: Concept B, "LEFT BEHIND?"

- It has the strongest emotion (fear of abandonment) of the three, and the reader's guess is the opposite of the truth, which is the same move the script makes ("name the myth").
- It's the furthest from a copy of the references. It's a story scene with your own text style, not "NO + noun".
- The promise is honest, so viewers stay instead of feeling tricked. That protects retention, which is your biggest problem right now (about 80 seconds watched per view).

## 9. Production-ready prompt (Concept B)

Generate the image **without text**, then add the text in Canva or Photopea. AI image generators often misspell words.

```
YouTube thumbnail illustration, 16:9, 1280x720. Flat 2D cartoon style with clean bold black outlines, simple round-headed cartoon characters with large expressive faces and dot-and-line features, wearing rough brown fur clothing, flat colour with soft shading and a subtle warm paper-grain texture over the whole image.

FOREGROUND, LEFT HALF: an injured prehistoric man sits alone on cold grey stony ground, shown from the waist up, very large in frame — his head is about 45% of the image height, positioned in the left third, slightly below centre. His right leg is stretched out in front of him, wrapped in green leaves and tied to two straight wooden sticks as a splint (clean, no blood, no wound visible). He looks directly at the viewer with a worried, pleading expression: eyebrows raised in the middle, mouth slightly open, one hand resting on the splinted leg. He sits in cool blue evening shadow.

BACKGROUND, RIGHT HALF: in the far distance on a gentle grassy hill, a small group of five cave people walk away from him toward a glowing campfire and a large orange sunset. They are small (about 15% of the image height) and seen from behind. One small child at the back of the group has turned around and is reaching one arm back toward the injured man.

LIGHTING AND COLOUR: strong split. The left side is cool blue-grey dusk shadow; the right side is warm orange and gold sunset light and fire glow. The brightest point of the image is the campfire and sun on the right; the injured man's face is lit by a soft warm rim light from that side so it stays bright and readable.

COMPOSITION: keep the top 30% of the image as simple open sky (soft dusk gradient from blue on the left to orange on the right) with no clouds, birds or objects, because text will be added there. Keep the bottom-right corner simple grass with no detail. No other characters in the foreground.

EXCLUDE: no text, no letters, no logos, no blood, no visible wounds, no skulls, no weapons, no realistic gore, no photorealism, no clutter.
```

**Adding the text in Canva / Photopea:**
- "LEFT BEHIND?" in **Lilita One**, all caps. Place it in the top sky area across the centre-right, with letters about 160–190 px high on the 1280×720 canvas.
- "LEFT" in **white**, "BEHIND?" in **fire-orange (#FF8A1F)**, both with a **14–18 px black outline** and a soft drop shadow.
- Keep the text clear of the man's head. Keep the bottom-right 200×100 px empty (timestamp).
- **Phone check:** shrink the finished thumbnail to about 160×90 px (roughly the size in a mobile feed). You should still be able to read the words and see a worried face. If you can't, make the face bigger and cut background detail.

### The three most important improvements
1. **Face size.** On every thumbnail, the main face should be at least 40% of the frame height. This is the biggest difference between your current thumbnails and theirs.
2. **Text size and colour.** 2 words across most of the top, with your own white + orange colours, instead of small text squeezed into one corner.
3. **One story, read in a second.** Cut anything that doesn't serve the one question, especially dark, grim props like skulls, and keep one strong warm glow as the focal point.
