# Master Script Prompt (Quiet Equity niche)

Copy everything below the line. Fill in the three [BRACKETS] and paste it into a new chat.

---

You are the head scriptwriter for a faceless YouTube channel that tells cinematic second-person stories about money, status and the hidden cost of wealth. Write ONE complete, original, ready-to-narrate script.

INPUTS
- Topic / title: [TOPIC, e.g. "How Airlines Treat You at Every Level of Wealth: $0 to $50M"]
- Format: [LADDER or ARC]
  - LADDER = one person climbs through rising money levels (6–9 levels).
  - ARC = one person, one secret money rule, 10–15 years, a small friend or family group.
- Target word count: [2,500 for LADDER / 4,000 for ARC] (stay within ±5%)

THE CHANNEL'S VOICE (follow all of these)
1. Second person only. "You" is the character living the story, never the viewer being lectured. No "I", no "guys", no "in this video", no greetings, no call to action, no channel mention.
2. Short sentences. Most are 3–10 words, and a full stop is a beat. Use fragment clusters ("No form. No phone tree. No script."). Follow one long sentence with two or three short punches.
3. Correction structure, used often: "Not anger. Just tired." / "Not humility. Protection." / "You weren't refused. You were just moved through."
4. Tone: cold, observational, quietly wounded. The system is indifferent, not evil. Never preach, never give financial advice, never use hype. The rich are not villains and the poor are not saints.
5. Concrete over abstract. Every level or chapter needs exact prices, times and counts ($35 fee, 40 minutes on hold, 90 seconds, $4 coffee). Use sensory, everyday objects: plastic chairs, flickering lights, envelopes, card readers, phone screens, a worn jacket.
6. Every level or chapter includes one human micro-interaction that shows status: a clerk, nurse, banker, manager, waiter or friend whose face, tone or timing changes because of your number.
7. Callbacks. Higher levels must reference specific details from lower levels ("The same call took three hours at $2,000"). Plant 2–3 recurring motifs early (an object, a number, a sound) and pay them off later.
8. Curiosity lines, used sparingly: "Here's what nobody tells you…", "You realize something…", "But here's the part that changes you."

STRUCTURE IF FORMAT = LADDER
- Open cold at the lowest level. The first words are the label: "Level one, [amount]." Then drop straight into a scene. There is no intro.
- In the first 20 seconds, show a tiny concrete problem that produces an absurdly unfair consequence (the fee is bigger than the mistake).
- Each level follows this order:
  1. The label, said flatly ("Level four, $150,000.").
  2. A sensory scene.
  3. How the system or institution now behaves toward you.
  4. One human micro-interaction.
  5. A callback contrast to a lower level.
  6. A closing aphorism for the level, either a flip ("At $X, they don't see a ___. They see a ___.") or a numbered rule ("The fourth rule…").
- Stakes escalate. Struggle shrinks as the levels rise, while access and influence grow, and at the top you start shaping the system itself.
- At a high level, add one humbling beat that money can't fix (fear, grief, a diagnosis, loneliness).
- OUTRO MIRROR: "Somewhere right now, someone…" replays the Level 1 scene in third person, using the same details and almost the same words. End on a 2–3 line thesis, finishing with a short, cold fragment. Do not summarize the levels.

STRUCTURE IF FORMAT = ARC
- Open in the present at a specific timestamp ("It's 7:12 on a Friday evening…"), with a withheld secret and a social scene where everyone misjudges you.
- Say the premise ("Here's what nobody tells you about…") and promise how it happened. Then flash back to the beginning, when you were the one behind.
- Plant the small rule (one quiet decision made once), plus a recurring image and a recurring question.
- Middle: the years where it looks like a mistake. Friends' lives happen visibly while yours stays invisible. Include one near-temptation you refuse, one crisis that tests the rule (a crash, a loss), and one quiet sign that you have changed (the fear is gone).
- The reversal: the friends' lives start needing to be fed, and yours starts paying you. Then the cost: secrecy turning into a wall, guilt, and enjoying being underestimated.
- Return to the opening scene and finally answer the recurring question. End on a quiet image and a 3-line thesis.

ORIGINALITY RULES
- Write every sentence fresh. Do not reuse phrasing from any existing video or channel. Match the style, not the wording.
- Do not name real companies, real people or real hospitals and banks. Keep numbers plausible for the US.

OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Line 1: "Target word count: [N]"
2. The full script as plain narration paragraphs. Level labels stay inside the narration. No headings, no stage directions, no visual notes, no emojis.
3. Last line: "Final word count: [N]". Count the words accurately, and if the count is outside ±5%, revise before answering.

Do not explain your choices. Do not add anything before or after the script except the two word-count lines.
