#H2 Act Two: The Lies

#SC | INT. A BAR BEHIND THE POLICE BARRACKS | NIGHT
#SET Night, the day after Bayo's call. A small dark bar tucked behind the police barracks, patronised by off-duty officers. A low ceiling stained brown from smoke, a counter with a few dusty bottles, plastic chairs, a ceiling fan turning slowly, a TV in the corner showing a football highlight with the sound off. The light is amber and dim, one red bulb over the counter. The air is thick with cigarette smoke and the smell of stale beer. A radio plays old highlife.
MORENIKE sits at a corner table in a plain brown headscarf and dark glasses, her handbag clutched on her lap, her whole posture rigid and out of place. She has not touched the bottle of mineral water placed in front of her. Her eyes follow the door.
SERGEANT AJINIBU enters, off duty in a loose singlet, a toothpick in his mouth. He walks slowly, his thick shoulders rolling, taking his time. He pulls out the chair opposite her, scrapes it back, and sits heavily. He says nothing. He picks up her bottle of water, looks at it, puts it down.
She reaches into her handbag. She slides a thick brown envelope across the sticky table. It stops against his hand.

@AJINIBU (slow, lazy drawl, not touching the envelope, smiling with his teeth)
Madam, I do not know you.

@MORENIKE (low, flat, no trace of the sweetness she shows others)
There is a young man in your cell. He talks too much. Make him quiet.

@AJINIBU (leaning back, the toothpick shifting to the other side of his mouth)
Quiet? How?

@MORENIKE (taking off her dark glasses and meeting his eyes for the first time)
However you like.

#RX He looks at her for a long moment. His small eyes do not blink. The only movement is the toothpick, rolling slowly from one corner of his mouth to the other. Then he lifts the envelope, weighs it in his palm with a bounce of the thumb, and slides it into his pocket. He stands without hurry and pushes the chair back under the table.

@AJINIBU (a faint, courteous nod)
No wahala, Madam.

He walks out. Morenike stays where she is, her hands pressed flat on the table. Her fingers are trembling slightly. She puts her dark glasses back on. Behind the lenses, her eyes close. She exhales slowly.

#SC | INT. GWAGWALADA POLICE STATION, TOILET BLOCK AND CELL | DAY AND NIGHT
#SET Two scenes, one day and one night, in the damp back of the police station. The toilet block is a small tiled room with a cracked sink, a rusting bucket, a floor slick with grey water, and exposed iron pipes along one wall, one of them held in place with a loose collar. Yellow tube light, flickering. Dripping water. The cell by night is blue-grey, with a single thread of orange from the street lamp through the barred window.
DAY. BAYO and OBI mop the toilet block floor side by side. Obi pushes a wet mop in long slow strokes. Bayo wrings a dirty rag into a bucket and swats at flies. They are joking. Bayo says something low. Obi's shoulders shake with silent laughter. Bayo laughs too, a real laugh, head thrown back, his chipped tooth showing, the first honest sound he has made.
Their laughter fades. Bayo rests on his mop handle and looks at his reflection in a puddle.

@BAYO (quiet, serious, looking at the water)
Chief, when you leave tomorrow, go and see my mother. Tell her I am sorry for everything.

@OBI (resting a heavy hand on his shoulder, warm and firm)
You will tell her yourself.

#RX Bayo nods slowly but does not believe it. He bows his head and begins to mop again. Obi watches him, a crease deepening between his brows.

NIGHT. The cell is dark. Obi lies on his mattress, eyes open. Bayo lies curled on the other, breathing unevenly. A key turns. The door swings open with a rusty groan. SERGEANT AJINIBU stands in the doorway, backlit by the corridor tube light, a toothpick in his mouth. Two shadows stand behind him, CORPORAL DANLADI and CONSTABLE EKENE.

@AJINIBU (casual, almost bored)
You. Come and clean the other toilet.

Bayo sits up. His eyes find Obi's in the dark. Obi gives a tiny shake of the head. Bayo stands anyway. He walks past Ajinibu into the corridor with his arms stiff at his sides. The door closes. The key turns.
Obi sits upright on the mattress in the dark. The camera stays on his face. We hear boots on concrete, three sets, fading. We hear a distant door. A low voice. A flat, muffled blow. Another. A sound like metal on bone. A cry that is cut short. Then a long, awful silence. Obi's hands grip his knees. His eyes are wide and wet. His lips move. He is praying.
MORNING. Grey dawn in the barred window. Ajinibu walks slowly past the cell, wiping his hands with a folded white handkerchief, finger by finger. The toothpick is back in his mouth.

@AJINIBU (flat, not stopping, not looking at the cell)
The boy fell. It happens.

#RX Obi stares at his back. It is worse than anger. It is cold, patient certainty. His hands do not shake now. Slowly he stands.
The gate opens for Obi. The release has come through. A guard hands him a small bundle at the desk: a plastic wristband and a torn scrap of paper with an address in blue ink. Obi stands at the open door of the station with the bundle in his fist, the morning light on his face. He looks back down the corridor.

@OBI (very quiet, to the empty cell behind him)
Bayo. I will find them.

#TW PAYOFF: Morenike's accomplice is dead within days. One quiet sentence did it.

#SC | EXT. A VILLAGE NEAR GWAGWALADA | DUSK
#SET Late afternoon sliding into dusk. A small farming village of mud-brick houses with corrugated iron roofs, chickens scratching in the red dirt, smoke from cooking fires drifting through the trees. The sky is amber and rose. Harmattan haze softens the light. A distant radio plays a hymn. Goats bleat. Children's laughter drifts from somewhere out of sight.
OBI, now in a crisp navy agbada and a gold watch, steps from a taxi at the edge of the lane. He walks slowly between the houses, holding the wristband in his closed hand. He stops at a low mud house with a faded blue door. On a stool in front, MAMA BAYO sits shelling beans into a basin, her thin hands moving mechanically. A faded wrapper and headtie, a bent back.
Obi stops a respectful distance away and bows his head.

@OBI (gently, in a low voice)
Mama, I was with your son in the cell.

She does not speak. Her hands stop. The beans fall back into the basin. She raises her eyes slowly. Obi steps forward and places the wristband in her open palm. She looks at it. Her fingers close around it. Her face folds slowly, every line deepening, until she presses it against her chest with both hands and rocks forward.

#RX She makes no sound at first. Then a low keening rises in her throat. Obi lowers himself onto his heels beside her stool and waits, his big hands hanging between his knees. He does not touch her. He looks at the ground and breathes slowly through his nose.

@OBI (after a long silence, quietly)
He asked me to find you. I will find the people who did this. Did he leave anything behind?

She lifts her wet face and points with a trembling finger toward a small room built onto the side of the house. Its door is shut, its window covered with a sheet of cardboard.

@MAMA BAYO (thin, broken, almost inaudible)
His room. I have not entered it since he went to work in the city. I do not want to disturb anything.

@OBI (rising, nodding slowly)
Then do not. Someone will come for it, with the law.

#RX Mama Bayo clutches the wristband. She nods, over and over, without speaking. Obi lays a hand briefly on her bowed head, then turns and walks back down the lane, shoulders squared. Behind him, the keening begins again.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, SITTING ROOM | NIGHT
#SET Night, a week later. The informal sitting room on the ground floor, dimly lit by a single table lamp. Dark leather sofas, a glass coffee table, a wall of framed family photographs. A half-drunk glass of whisky sits untouched on a coaster. Rain has not begun yet, but a low wind moves the curtains, and distant thunder rolls over the city. The clock ticks.
TUNDE sits alone on the sofa in his shirtsleeves with his tie hanging loose and his elbows on his knees. His eyes are red. He is staring at the black screen of the phone in his hand. His thumb rubs the glass back and forth.
ADUNNI enters quietly in a house wrapper, her hair tied up. Her face is worried. She sits on the edge of the armchair opposite him, hands clasped.

@ADUNNI (gentle, hesitant)
Tunde, I heard Bayo was arrested. What happened?

@TUNDE (not looking up, voice flat and dangerous)
You tell me what happened.

He holds out the phone without raising his eyes. She takes it. The silent twelve-second clip plays. She watches. Her face goes from confusion to horror to a slackness that is almost faint. Her hand moves to her mouth.

@ADUNNI (whispering, voice cracking)
Where did you get this?

@TUNDE (dully)
Morenike showed me. She did not want to.

@ADUNNI (looking up, desperate, standing)
Tunde, this is twelve seconds. He grabbed my wrist. I threw a plate at his feet. Where is the rest?

@TUNDE (shaking his head slowly, voice breaking)
I saw what I saw. You did not move.

@ADUNNI (stepping toward him, voice rising and trembling)
I was in shock! Play it with sound! Someone has cut this.

@TUNDE (finally lifting his eyes, bloodshot and furious and heartbroken)
Who would cut it?

@ADUNNI (stopping, helpless)
I do not know. But my sister saw only part of it. Let me bring her. She will help us speak to your mother.

#RX Tunde looks at her for a long, long moment. His jaw works. He seems about to answer. He looks away, rubs his face with both hands, and exhales. He does not argue. He does not agree. He lets the silence say he is too tired to fight.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, LIVING ROOM | NIGHT, RAIN
#SET The formal living room, a few nights later. Heavy rain beats against the tall windows, rivulets running down the glass. Thunder rumbles. Every lamp is lit, throwing warm gold on the cream sofas and heavy drapes, a stark contrast with the blue-black storm outside. The wedding portrait above the fireplace looks down on four people sitting in a tense circle. A pot of untouched tea sits cooling on the coffee table.
TUNDE sits rigid in a high-backed chair, arms crossed, eyes on the floor. CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI sits beside him on the sofa, her red gele immaculate, hand fan folded in her lap, face unreadable. ADUNNI sits on the edge of a chair across from them, hands clenched in her lap, braids pinned, still in the clothes she wore all day. MORENIKE sits next to Adunni on the sofa, looking prim and concerned in a muted brown dress.

@ADUNNI (to Morenike, urgent, low, a hand reaching toward her sister)
Sister, please. Tell them what you saw. Tell them I am not what they think.

Thunder cracks. Morenike lowers her eyes. She stands slowly, smoothing her dress with both hands. She turns to face the older woman, her face full of pain and reluctance.

@MORENIKE (soft, trembling, performing with every breath)
Mama. Tunde. I know she is my sister. I love her with all my heart.

@CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI (cold, still, lowering her glasses)
Say it.

#RX Morenike takes a shuddering breath and presses her fingertips against her mouth. A tear slides out and she lets it fall. Then she lowers her hand and speaks.

@MORENIKE (voice breaking, quiet and clear)
Why are you still with her, Tunde? She cannot give you a child. I say this with tears. Five years. She told us herself. You are the only son. The family name is dying in this house.

#RX The words land like stones. Adunni's face goes white. She half rises. Her mouth opens, closes. Chief Mrs. Adebisi gives a single slow nod, almost to herself. Tunde's head lifts. He looks at Adunni, and in his eyes there is something terrible: hurt, shame, a decision forming.

@ADUNNI (staggered, whisper)
Morenike... what are you...

@MORENIKE (turning to her, wiping her cheek, voice trembling with sorrow)
I am not a wicked person. I am only telling the truth. First the video, and now this. Is she able?

Silence. The rain seems louder. Tunde looks at his mother. His mother looks at the floor. The clock ticks twice. Adunni stands with her hands open at her sides, her face crumpled.

@ADUNNI (rushing, voice thin and broken)
Tunde, I never betrayed you. I never...

@TUNDE (not meeting her eyes, voice dead flat)
Take your things and go.

@ADUNNI (taking a step toward him, desperate)
Tunde, listen to me, there is something about the child...

@TUNDE (shouting, standing so fast the chair crashes back, face contorted)
Go!

#RX The word echoes. Adunni stops dead. Her arms fall to her sides. The air leaves her. She looks at her husband's twisted face, at his mother's frozen mask, at her sister's lowered eyes, and she understands that the door has closed. She nods once, small and stunned, like someone who has been slapped. She turns, walks slowly to the foyer, and lifts a small cloth bag from the console beneath the crystal vase. She opens the front door. The storm roars into the hall.

#SC | EXT. ADEBISI MANSION GATE / INT. SUV / EXT. OGUNDELE MANSION | NIGHT
#SET Heavy tropical rain, black sky, the street lamps streaked with water. The iron gate and its gold spear tips drip and shine. The mansion glows warm and gold behind the bars. A black SUV waits across the road with its hazard lights blinking. Later, the Asokoro mansion appears out of the dark: lanterns lining a gravel drive, glowing amber in the rain. Thunder rumbles continuously.
The front door slams. ADUNNI steps onto the porch in her soaked lace wrapper, a single cloth bag in her hand, no shoes. She walks down the steps and across the drive in the pouring rain. Water streams down her face and soaks her braids. She reaches the gate. A security man pulls it open without meeting her eyes. She walks through. It slams behind her with an iron boom.
She turns. She grips the bars with both hands. Her knuckles are pale. Behind the gate, the mansion glows. A figure stands on the porch under the light: TUNDE, motionless, his face wet with rain he is refusing to wipe. Behind him, CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI stands in the doorway, arms folded, unmoving.
Adunni's chest heaves. Rain runs into her eyes. When she speaks, her voice is quiet, steady, carrying across the drive.

@ADUNNI (quietly, each word placed, her gaze locked on his)
You will find out one day who did this. And when you do, I want you to remember I never begged.

#RX Tunde does not move. Something in his face flickers and falls. He looks down at the wet marble step. The porch light buzzes. Adunni lets go of the bars. She turns her back on the house.

A pair of headlights cuts through the rain. A black SUV pulls up at the curb. The window lowers. MR. KUNLE the driver hurries out with an umbrella, bent against the wind. MR. HASSAN the security man follows, tall and broad, lifting her bag.

@KUNLE (breathless, holding the umbrella over her, voice anxious)
Madam, we heard on the radio. We came at once.

@ADUNNI (hoarse, dazed, her teeth chattering)
Take me to Asokoro.

@HASSAN (low, deep, a quick look at Kunle)
To the big house?

@ADUNNI (steadier now, lifting her chin)
To my house.

#RX Kunle opens the rear door. Adunni climbs in. Hassan wraps a dry blanket around her shoulders. She does not look back. Through the rear window, the gate and the glowing mansion shrink and blur and vanish in the rain.
From an upstairs window, MORENIKE watches the tail-lights shrink. She stands behind the curtain, one hand holding it aside. She smiles. It is slow and complete.

INT. SUV. The city slides past in streaks of colored light. Adunni sits in the back seat with the blanket round her, staring straight ahead. Her lips are moving silently. She is counting something, a prayer or a number. Kunle's worried eyes flick to the rear-view mirror again and again.

The SUV crosses the city and slows at a pair of towering cast-iron gates. They open automatically with a soft electric whine. A long gravel drive, lined with lanterns, glowing amber. A white mansion rises beyond it, three times the size of Tunde's, its curved front steps wet and shining. Staff in indigo uniforms with white aprons line the entrance in two rows, umbrellas held high, heads lowered.

@HOUSEKEEPER (an elderly woman at the head of the line, bowing, voice tender and formal)
Welcome home, Madam.

#RX Adunni stands in the rain beside the SUV and does not move. Her mouth opens slightly. She looks at the lanterns, the staff, the steps. The blanket slips from her shoulders. Then, with slow, uncertain steps, she climbs the stairs between the rows of bowing servants. Water drips from her hem onto the marble.
In the hallway, a huge oil portrait of IYA AGBA hangs above a round table of fresh flowers. The old woman in a white gele and deep-blue aso-oke gazes down with bright, knowing eyes and the ghost of a smile. On the table beneath it lies a sealed cream envelope, her name on the front in shaky handwriting.
Adunni picks it up with trembling fingers and breaks the seal. We hear the grandmother's voice, old, cracked, clear.

@IYA AGBA (V.O., reading, slow and warm, with a long patient breath between sentences)
My Adunni. If you read this, then someone has hurt you. Do not be bitter. Be useful. I raised you and your sister with one heart, but I saw early that your sister's heart has a hole in it. So I left this house, the workshop in Abeokuta, and the house in Wuse to you alone. When she proves she is a sister, share what I left. Until then, keep your peace.

#RX Adunni's knees give way. She sinks to the marble floor and sits there with the letter pressed to her chest, her wet wrapper pooling around her. The staff turn their faces away, giving her the dignity of privacy. The housekeeper quietly closes the front doors. Rain drums on the windows. In the portrait, the old woman's eyes seem to soften.
Adunni opens her cloth bag with shaking hands. Beneath her few clothes lies the sealed hospital envelope she brought from the jewellery box. She holds it in her lap. For the first time, she breaks the seal.

#TW PAYOFF: the hidden fortune. The deed and the weavers from the garden call mean something now.

#SC | INT. PRIVATE HOSPITAL, CONSULTING ROOM | DAY (FLASHBACK, SIX MONTHS EARLIER)
#SET Bright daytime in a calm private hospital consulting room. White walls, a polished desk, a poster of the human heart on one wall, a box of tissues, a green plant by the window. Cool air conditioning. A clock ticks softly. The light is clean and slightly blue, a washed-out memory colour.
ADUNNI sits in the chair opposite DR. IBIDUN COKER, a grey-streaked woman in a pressed white coat with round glasses. A thin folder lies on the desk between them. Adunni sits very upright, her handbag in her lap, her hands twisting the strap.

@DR. COKER (kind, formal, steady)
Mrs. Adebisi, your husband's results are low. His count is very low. You, on the other hand, are completely healthy.

#RX Adunni stops moving. Her lips part. A tremor passes over her face, then fades. She looks at the folder.

@ADUNNI (very quiet)
Does he know?

@DR. COKER (taking off her glasses)
Not yet. He asked me to give the report to you first, as his wife. He is too busy to attend.

Adunni takes the folder. She opens it slowly and reads. In her mind we see flashes: Tunde laughing in the kitchen with soup on his sleeve; Tunde unwrapping a car part with a boy's joy; Tunde standing proud on a stage. She closes the folder and holds it against her chest.

@ADUNNI (looking up, voice soft and certain)
Doctor, when he comes, tell him the problem is mine.

@DR. COKER (leaning forward, gently)
Mrs. Adebisi, that is not true.

@ADUNNI (shaking her head, eyes shining, a brave small smile)
Please. He will break. I would rather be blamed than watch him break.

#RX Dr. Coker looks at her for a long moment. Her face softens. She closes the folder on the desk with both hands, slowly, and nods. A tear slides down Adunni's cheek, but she is smiling.

BACK TO PRESENT. The hall of the Asokoro mansion. Adunni sits on the floor with the opened envelope in her trembling hands. We see the page: ADUNNI OGUNDELE-ADEBISI, FERTILITY: NORMAL. She presses the paper to her mouth. Her whole body folds around it. She weeps without a sound, shoulders shaking, rocking slightly, while the great portrait of Iya Agba looks down.

#TW PAYOFF: Adunni is fertile. Tunde is the one who cannot. She took the blame, and Morenike used it.

#SC | VARIOUS | MONTAGE (TWO YEARS)
#SET A montage across two years, in three braided threads: Adunni's rebuilding, Morenike's climb, and Adunni's healing in London. Each thread has its own palette. The workshop is dusty gold turning to clean white. The Adebisi world is cream and gold turning slowly grey. London is cold blue and rain. A warm, rising instrumental piece with talking drum and strings plays under all of it.
WORKSHOP, ABEOKUTA. Morning sun through louvre windows. BABA SULE pulls dust sheets off silent looms one by one, revealing carved wooden frames and half-finished cloth. ADUNNI stands among them in plain clothes, barefoot on the concrete, her hand trailing across a strip of indigo aso-oke. She sits at a loom. Her fingers fumble the thread, then find it. Her face concentrates, lips pressed. A smile appears.
Months pass. Sketchbooks fill. Patterns of indigo and gold spread across paper. A first order from a church choir arrives; Adunni and a small crew of weavers wrap it with ribbons. A second from an Abuja designer; a stack of orders grows on a desk. A third from a minister's wife; a courier van pulls up. A small showroom opens with fresh paint and a hand-painted sign. Then a larger showroom with glass doors and mannequins in tailored aso-oke.
A factory floor: rows of working looms, hundreds of weavers in indigo smocks, spools of thread whirling. ADUNNI walks down the aisle in a deep-indigo suit and a wine gele, her heels clicking, a heavy ring on her hand. The weavers stand as she passes. Some touch their chests in respect. She nods to each, her face calm and warm.
A magazine cover on a rack: AGBA ASO-OKE, THE QUIET EMPIRE. Her face, composed, looks out from it.
INTERCUT: THE ADEBISI WORLD. MORENIKE brings a covered dish to the mansion door, her face soft and humble. She sits with CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI in a church pew, head bowed, praying beside her. She holds TUNDE's hand at his late father's memorial service, lighting a candle with him. A few weeks later, she wears a small gold bracelet he gave her. Then a ring on her hand. Her hair is a little longer. Her dress is a little more expensive.
CHURCH. The wedding. TUNDE in white agbada stands at the altar, handsome and hollowed, his smile not reaching his eyes. MORENIKE walks down the aisle in ivory lace, radiant, wiping a tear with a finger, nodding at the congregation. Guests clap. CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI watches from the front pew with a small, approving nod.
INTERCUT: LONDON. A hospital room with a grey rain-streaked window. ADUNNI lies in a hospital bed in a plain gown, an ECG patch on her chest, a drip in her arm. She is thinner. DR. DAWSON stands beside the bed in a white coat, calmly adjusting a monitor. He does not speak. He glances at her, then at the screen. A TV bolted high in the corner plays Nigerian news. It shows a clip: the Adebisi wedding. TUNDE and MORENIKE smiling at the altar.

@ADUNNI (whisper, eyes on the screen, a tear running into her hairline)
Mama, I hope you can see.

#RX Dr. Dawson looks at her quietly. He reaches over and, without a word, switches off the TV. Then he pulls up a chair and sits beside the bed, his hands loose between his knees. He does not look away from her. Adunni closes her eyes. Her fingers, resting on the sheet, slowly curl around his.
