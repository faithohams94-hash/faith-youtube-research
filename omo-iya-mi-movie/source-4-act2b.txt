#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, NEW MASTER BEDROOM | NIGHT
#SET Late night, a year after the wedding. The master bedroom has been redecorated: heavier gold drapes, a bigger bed with a quilted headboard, a new dressing table with a wall of mirrors and too many perfume bottles. It is richer and colder. A brass lamp casts a hard yellow circle. Somewhere in the house a clock chimes eleven. Tunde is not home.
MORENIKE sits at the dressing table in a satin robe with her hair freshly done, applying a cream to her hands in slow circles. Her reflection in the three-way mirror looks satisfied, a little tired. She hums a church song. A diamond ring catches the light each time her hands move.
A knock at the bedroom door. Not a servant's knock. Soft, slow, deliberate. She stops humming. She rises and crosses to the door, tying her robe tighter. She opens it a crack.
SERGEANT AJINIBU stands on the landing, out of uniform in a loose shirt with a toothpick between his teeth, looking around the opulent corridor with open admiration.

@AJINIBU (drawling, a slow smile, a small bow)
Madam Adebisi. Congratulations.

@MORENIKE (a violent whisper, glancing down the corridor, pulling the door nearly shut)
How dare you come here?

@AJINIBU (unhurried, shifting the toothpick)
The police are beginning to talk. People ask who ordered the quiet. A man with my duties must prepare for questions.

#RX Morenike's hand tightens on the door. Her face is pale under the cream. A vein beats at her throat. She looks over his shoulder at the empty stairs, then back at him. Her voice drops to something very quiet and very cold.

@MORENIKE
How much?

@AJINIBU (pleasantly)
Ten million. Monthly.

#RX She stares at him. He chews the toothpick and waits, utterly at ease. After a long moment, she shuts her eyes and gives one small nod. He tips an invisible hat and strolls down the stairs, whistling softly. She closes the door and leans her back against it with her eyes shut, her breath coming short. Behind her the mirrors reflect her from three sides, a trapped woman multiplied.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MOTORS, TUNDE'S OFFICE | DAY
#SET Midday in Tunde's corner office, grey light through the glass. It looks less polished than before. A pile of unopened mail on the desk, a half-drunk cup of cold coffee, the silver model car now dusty. The wedding photograph of Adunni has been replaced by one of Morenike. The air conditioning is off, and the room is warm.
TUNDE sits behind his desk in a rumpled suit, a thick document open before him. He looks worn. MORENIKE leans against the desk beside him in a tailored cream suit, one hand resting lightly on his shoulder. Across the desk sits MICHAEL, groomed, in a pinstripe suit and white sneakers, smiling the practised smile of a man who sells hope.

@MORENIKE (warm, proud, patting Tunde's shoulder)
Tunde, this is my old school friend. Michael is a contractor with government links. He can give us a supply contract that will triple the company.

@TUNDE (not looking up, flat, turning a page)
Why have I never heard his name?

@MICHAEL (spreading his hands, easy, rich, confiding)
Because the best deals are not announced, sir.

Tunde reads. Michael talks smoothly about margins, timelines, logistics, government approvals. Tunde flips pages, nodding without conviction. On page seven he stops. His finger rests on a line.

@TUNDE (slowly, a frown deepening)
Six hundred million as an advance.

@MICHAEL (without a flicker, leaning back with an open smile)
For materials, sir. Fully refundable.

#RX Tunde looks at Morenike. She nods encouragingly, her eyes warm and trusting. He looks at Michael. Michael smiles. Tunde exhales, drags a pen from his pocket, and signs. The pen scratches. Morenike lets out a small breath, almost a sigh of relief, and squeezes his shoulder.
Michael and Morenike exchange a look. It lasts one second too long. His eyes drop to her mouth and rise. Her lips twitch. She looks away first. Tunde does not see it. He is looking at the signature he has just put on the page.

#TW PLANT: Michael is not just a contractor. He is her lover. And he has his own plan.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MOTORS | DAY (MONTAGE)
#SET A montage of decline across six months. The showroom floor at first gleaming, then dusty. The offices at first busy, then half-empty. The light turns greyer and flatter with each cut. A slow, sad piano plays under the images.
The showroom: a salesman polishes a car that no one comes to see. A sign on a window: SALE. A second sign: LAST UNITS. A third sign is half-peeled off.
The car park at the factory: rows of unfinished cars under tarps. A line of workers stands outside the gate with placards, arms folded. A security guard pulls the chain across the gate.
The corridor: CHIEFS in agbada murmuring in low voices, heads close together. They glance at Tunde's office door and fall silent when he appears.
TUNDE at his desk, tearing open a letter from the bank. He reads. His hand drops. He tears open another. Another. He pushes the pile away and lays his head on his arms.
In the next office, a young ACCOUNTANT in a short-sleeved shirt stares at a spreadsheet in red. He zooms in. He scrolls. He checks again. He picks up the office phone with a trembling hand and hangs up before it connects. He stands and walks across the corridor to Tunde's door.

@ACCOUNTANT (standing at the door, hesitant, whispering)
Sir, six hundred million has left, and I cannot trace where it went.

#RX Tunde lifts his head slowly. For a moment he looks at the young man as if he has not understood. Then his face goes blank. He rises very slowly and walks to the window. He stands with his hands behind his back, looking at the city.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, BEDROOM | NIGHT
#SET Night, two years into the second marriage. The bedroom looks cold and tidy. The drapes are half drawn, and a thin light from a lamp falls across a bed that is made on only one side. A faint rain starts against the window, light, not yet the storm. The silence is heavy.
MORENIKE sits on the edge of the bed in a silk nightdress, hands folded in her lap, her face composed into sorrow. TUNDE stands at the window with a glass in his hand, thinner than before, his back to her. He has not touched the glass.

@MORENIKE (tearful, soft, picking at the hem of her gown)
Two years, Tunde. Two years, and still nothing. What is wrong with this house?

@TUNDE (not turning, dull)
Do not start.

@MORENIKE (rising, moving toward him)
I only want to give you a son.

#RX She stops behind him. He is still. Outside the window a drop of rain slides down the glass. Tunde finally speaks, so quietly it is almost to himself.

@TUNDE (quietly, still facing the glass)
Adunni could not either. And now you. What are the odds?

#RX The room goes completely silent. Morenike's hand, reaching for his arm, stops in the air. Her face is caught between hurt and something sharper, a flicker of unease that she masks at once. She lowers her hand. She does not answer. He does not turn. In the dark glass, his reflection stares back at him, and for the first time, he looks like a man who is afraid of a question.

#TW PLANT: the question Tunde cannot answer yet. The audience already knows.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MOTORS, BOARDROOM | DAY
#SET Afternoon in the company boardroom. A long polished table with twelve leather chairs, a faded framed company logo on the wall, blinds half down. Papers and cold tea cups litter the table. The room smells of cigar smoke and stale air. The light through the blinds is striped and dusty. A wall clock ticks loudly.
TUNDE sits at the head of the table in a loosened tie, unshaven, with tired eyes. Two CHIEFS in expensive agbada sit on either side: CHIEF BALOGUN, heavy and bald, mopping his face with a handkerchief, and CHIEF OKORO, thin and watchful, tapping his pen on a file.

@CHIEF BALOGUN (heavily, sweating, shaking his head)
The banks will not move. We need a partner with cash.

@CHIEF OKORO (slowly, leaning forward, tapping the file)
Agba Aso-Oke Industries funds distressed partners. Their founder is a woman, and nobody has ever met her.

@TUNDE (incredulous, a short dry laugh)
Aso-oke for a car company?

@CHIEF OKORO (a thin smile, shrugging)
Cash has no tribe, Mr. Adebisi.

#RX Tunde looks at the two men. The clock ticks. His shoulders sag. He picks up a pen, turns it over twice, and drops it onto the file. He gives one slow nod. Chief Balogun sighs in relief and mops his face again.

#SC | INT. AGBA ASO-OKE INDUSTRIES, ADUNNI'S OFFICE | DAY
#SET A spacious, elegant corner office on the top floor of the Agba Aso-Oke tower. Floor-to-ceiling glass looks over Abuja. A huge desk in dark wood, a leather chair, a white orchid in a ceramic bowl, framed swatches of indigo cloth. On the wall behind the desk, a large framed portrait of Iya Agba, lit from the left. A low coffee table with a silver tray of water and glasses. The light is bright, warm and flattering. Quiet jazz plays from hidden speakers.
ADUNNI sits behind the desk in a deep-indigo tailored suit, a wine gele folded in a tall crown, a heavy ring on her right hand. She looks composed and powerful, and a little tired. She is reviewing a file. TEMI stands half a step behind her chair with a clipboard against her chest.

@TEMI (crisp, professional)
Madam, a partnership request. Adebisi Motors. They are asking for a six-hundred-million-naira bridge.

Adunni does not look up. She turns a gold pen once between her fingers.

@ADUNNI (calm, level, eyes still on the page)
Who signs for them?

@TEMI (a very small hesitation)
Mr. Tunde Adebisi.

#RX Adunni's pen stops. Her face does not change. For three full seconds she does not move at all. The jazz plays softly. Her eyes lift slowly to the portrait of Iya Agba on the wall. The old woman looks back, calm and patient. Adunni breathes in once through her nose and lets it out. Her hand resumes its small turning of the pen.

@ADUNNI (quiet)
Give him the loan.

@TEMI (startled, stepping forward)
Madam?

@ADUNNI (turning at last, level eyes, a faint steel in her voice)
No interest. But he repays in sixty days, or every asset he has pledged becomes ours. Put it in writing.

@TEMI (carefully)
That is very harsh.

@ADUNNI (closing the file, folding her hands on it, gently)
It is exactly fair.

#RX Temi looks at her for a long moment. She nods once, writes on her clipboard, and steps back. Adunni turns her chair slowly to the window. The sun lights her face from the side, the same side as the portrait. She is looking at the city, and her expression is unreadable.

#TW PAYOFF: she does not rescue him. She sets a clock.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, STUDY | NIGHT
#SET Night in a dim wood-panelled study with a leather chair, a globe, shelves of unread books, and a green banker's lamp over a large desk. The window is open a crack. Rain whispers outside. The air smells of cigar and varnish. A single lamp lights the faces from below.
MORENIKE stands by the desk in a silk robe, arms folded, her face drawn. MICHAEL leans on the desk in his shirtsleeves, collar open, fanning a stack of bank slips like a deck of cards. He is smiling.

@MICHAEL (lazily, snapping a slip against his thumb)
Your husband is a bad businessman, sweetheart. And you are a worse thief.

@MORENIKE (low, sharp, stepping toward him)
What have you done?

@MICHAEL (turning a slip toward her, the figure visible)
The bridge loan. Six hundred million. It left your account this evening and landed in mine.

#RX Morenike's arms drop. She snatches the slip and stares at it. Her lips move over the number. Her face loses color slowly, from the forehead down.

@MORENIKE (hissing, voice shaking, white knuckles on the paper)
That is Tunde's money! He signed it for the company!

@MICHAEL (shrugging, smile widening, straightening up and tucking the slips into his jacket)
Report me.

#RX She opens her mouth. No sound comes. She knows. She cannot. Every word she might say would cost her more than he has taken. Michael leans down and kisses her forehead like a parent leaving a child, whistles softly, and walks out of the study. The door clicks. Morenike stands alone in the circle of lamplight, the slip crushed in her fist, staring at the shut door with something close to horror and something close to respect.

#SC | INT. A VILLAGE HOUSE NEAR GWAGWALADA | DAY
#SET Late morning in the village. Bright sun, dust in the air, hens scattering. The small mud room beside Mama Bayo's house, unopened for years. When the door is finally opened, the light falls across a narrow iron bed with a thin mattress, a faded poster of a footballer, a plastic stool, a wall calendar from three years ago. Dust floats in the beam. A faint smell of old cloth and stillness.
MAMA BAYO stands in the yard with her hands clasped before her, shaking slightly. SEGUN ALADE stands at the open door of the small room with a torch, OBI behind him in his agbada, and two POLICE OFFICERS in uniform with a warrant paper in one hand. The officers are respectful. Mama Bayo nods to them to enter.
SEGUN steps inside, the torch beam sweeping the walls. He runs his fingers over the crumbling plaster, tapping gently. He crouches at the foot of the bed. He taps a brick near the floor. It sounds hollow. He works it with his fingertips and draws it out in one slow pull. Behind it, wrapped in a faded plastic bag, lies a cracked phone.

@SEGUN (soft, dry, lifting the bag with two fingers)
He hid it before he took the job. A careful boy.

OBI steps forward and takes the phone with both hands as if it might wake. He turns it over. He presses the power button. The screen flickers, shows a single battery bar, and glows. A folder of voice notes appears, each with a date. He thumbs one and presses play.
Bayo's voice fills the small room, young and bright, a little nervous, a little proud.

@BAYO (V.O., recording, cheerful, close to the mic)
Today I met Madam Morenike at the canteen. If anything happens to me, her name is Morenike Ogundele.

#RX Silence. A fly buzzes against the window. Mama Bayo covers her mouth with both hands. Her eyes go wide and wet. Slowly she sinks onto the plastic stool, rocking, tears running down her wrists. Obi lowers his head. Segun looks at the officers. One of them takes off his cap.

#TW PAYOFF: the evidence is a boy's quiet insurance, and it was in his room the whole time.

#SC | INT. FCT POLICE COMMAND, INTERROGATION ROOM | NIGHT
#SET Night in a bare interrogation room at the FCT Police Command. A steel table bolted to the floor, three chairs, a one-way mirror, a naked bulb behind a wire cage. The walls are cracked institutional green. A ceiling fan creaks. The air is warm, stale, and smells of sweat. The light is flat and white, with sharp shadows under every brow.
SEGUN ALADE stands at one end of the table. In front of him, in a clear plastic evidence bag, lies a short length of grey pipe with brown stains on its threads. Beside it, in a second bag, lies Bayo's cracked phone. Across the table, SERGEANT AJINIBU sits cuffed to a steel ring. Without his uniform shirt, he looks smaller and greyer, a pale stripe where his toothpick used to be. Three detectives in plain clothes stand behind Segun with folded arms.

@SEGUN (soft, dry, tapping the plastic with one finger)
Detachable. The cell block's toilet pipe. The officers took it out, used it and put it back. But they did not wipe the thread. And the boy recorded his own employer.

Ajinibu stares at the pipe. His jaw works. A bead of sweat runs down his temple. He says nothing.

@DETECTIVE (blunt, leaning over him)
Sergeant, you are looking at life. You can protect nobody. Name everyone.

#RX Ajinibu's gaze flicks from face to face. His breath quickens. His mouth opens, and the arrogance goes out of it, and what is left is an ordinary frightened man.

@AJINIBU (breaking, voice cracking, spit flying)
It was not my idea! A woman paid us. She came to the bar. We have her voice. I record everything I do, I am not a fool.

He gives the name. The room goes still. Segun sits back slowly. Behind him, a detective is already dialing the commissioner with his phone to his ear and his eyes on Ajinibu. Obi, watching through the one-way mirror from the other side, closes his eyes and lets out a long, shaky breath.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, SITTING ROOM | NIGHT
#SET Night, a week later. The sitting room, quiet, with the TV muted on a news channel. Lamplight falls across the sofa. A half-eaten plate of food sits on the coffee table. A mosquito coil smokes in a corner. The mansion feels hollow and under-furnished. Rain has returned in a thin steady patter at the window.
TUNDE and MORENIKE sit on the same sofa, a body-width apart, not looking at each other. Morenike scrolls on her phone. Tunde stares at the muted TV. On the arm of the sofa, her phone buzzes. The screen lights up: MICHAEL (CONTRACTOR).
She flips it face down. She does it a half-second too late.

@TUNDE (not turning his head, flat)
Who is that?

@MORENIKE (too light, rising to leave)
A friend.

@TUNDE (his eyes finally sliding to her)
At midnight?

@MORENIKE (not meeting his gaze, gathering her robe)
It is nothing.

#RX She walks out of the room. Tunde watches her go. His eyes follow the phone in her hand until the door closes behind her. He sits very still. A muscle works in his jaw. He turns off the TV. The remote clicks. In the silence, we hear the rain.

#SC | EXT. ADEBISI MANSION AND RESTAURANT | DAY
#SET Morning at the mansion's boundary wall, bright and clear. A narrow lane between two high walls, a neighbour's bougainvillea spilling over. Later, midday at a busy Abuja restaurant with white tablecloths, waiters in black waistcoats, big windows overlooking a car park. The light is clean daytime, with the soft clatter of cutlery and low conversation.
MORNING. TUNDE, in a plain shirt and no tie, walks next door and rings a bell at a neat bungalow. MR. OKAFOR comes out in a loose cream kaftan and slippers, a newspaper rolled in his hand.

@TUNDE (stiff, uncomfortable, lowering his voice)
Please, Mr. Okafor, may I park in your compound for a day? My wife must not see my car.

@MR. OKAFOR (peering at him, eyebrows climbing, delighted and alarmed)
She is hiding something from you?

@TUNDE (quietly)
I hope not.

Mr. Okafor hands him a bunch of keys without another word. Tunde climbs into Okafor's old Toyota, sinks low in the seat, and parks it across the road from his own gate.
LATER. A silver SUV pulls out of the Adebisi gate. MORENIKE is at the wheel in sunglasses. The old Toyota slips out behind it, keeping three cars back. Tunde grips the wheel with both hands, eyes locked on her tail-lights, jaw set.
INT. RESTAURANT. A corner table by a window. MORENIKE and MICHAEL sit with their heads close, speaking in low voices. At the next table, TUNDE sits with his back to them in a plain cap, a menu raised in front of his face. He lowers one corner of it. He sees them. He waves over LEKAN, a young waiter, and presses folded notes into his hand without looking at him.

@TUNDE (very low, urgent)
Stand at that table. Fill their water slowly. Listen to every word.

LEKAN nods, swallows, picks up a water jug. He walks to the corner table with a polite smile and begins to fill their glasses with painful slowness, his head tilted, his ears wide. Behind him, Morenike's voice carries, low and bitter, and Tunde's menu trembles.

@MORENIKE (O.S., low, tight)
...You swapped the page. Tunde thinks it is a refundable advance. The clause says it is a gift to your company. If he ever reads it...

@MICHAEL (O.S., easy, a short laugh)
He will not read. People do not read what they trust.

#RX Behind the menu, Tunde does not move. His face has gone to stone. Slowly a single tear escapes his eye and rolls down his cheek. It drops onto the white tablecloth and spreads into a small round stain. He sets the menu down with great care, as if it were glass.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, LIVING ROOM | NIGHT
#SET Night, the same day. The formal living room is dark except for one lamp by the sofa. The wedding portrait of Tunde and Morenike hangs over the fireplace, large and glossy and false. Rain hisses against the windows. Thunder rolls once, far away.
TUNDE sits in the armchair in the dark, a folder on his lap, completely still. MORENIKE enters in her coat with her keys in her hand, switches on the main light, and stops when she sees him.

@TUNDE (calm, quiet, not moving)
Who is Michael?

@MORENIKE (a quick polite smile, setting down her keys)
A school friend. I told you.

@TUNDE (opening the folder on his lap and turning a page)
I want to read the contract again. And I want to call him.

@MORENIKE (stepping forward, a little too fast)
You do not need to call.

@TUNDE (rising slowly, voice rising with him)
Do you think I am a fool?

@MORENIKE (stammering, hand rising to her collarbone)
I... I...

@TUNDE (his voice cracking, the folder shaking in his hand)
Six hundred million. From my own wife.

#RX Morenike's hand touches her collarbone and stays there. She blinks slowly, once, twice, the tell of a woman choosing a lie. She opens her mouth.
A hard knock at the front door. Another. Then a loud voice: "Police! Open up!"
They both freeze. The knocking grows insistent. Tunde walks to the foyer and opens the door. Six officers in tactical vests stand on the porch in the rain, their torches on him. A DETECTIVE in a plain jacket steps forward with a warrant paper held up to the light.

@DETECTIVE (formal, flat)
Morenike Adebisi, you are under arrest for the murder of Bayo Adeleke.

@TUNDE (dazed, turning slowly to look at Morenike, then back)
Murder? Who is Bayo Adeleke?

@DETECTIVE (looking at him steadily)
Sir, your former personal assistant. He died in custody three years ago.

#RX Tunde's face empties. The color drains out of it. He sways and puts a hand on the door frame. His lips move.

@TUNDE (barely a whisper)
I put him in that cell. I only wanted him punished.

Two officers move past him and take Morenike by the arms. They cuff her wrists behind her. She does not struggle. She turns her head slowly and looks at Tunde. Her expression is not fear and it is not shame. It is a cold, level contempt. She walks out into the rain between two officers without a word, her heels clicking on the marble and then on the wet stone.
Tunde stands in the open door with the rain blowing in over him. He does not move. Behind him, the wedding portrait looks down from the wall.

#TW PAYOFF: Tunde learns, in the same breath, that his wife is a killer and that his rage killed a man.
