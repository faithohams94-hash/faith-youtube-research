#H1 Screenplay

#H2 Act One: The Birthday

#SC | EXT. ADEBISI MANSION, GARDEN, MAITAMA, ABUJA | NIGHT (ADUNNI'S BIRTHDAY PARTY)
#SET A warm Saturday evening, just after sunset. The whole back garden is dressed for a celebration: a huge white marquee with gold drapes, round tables with champagne-colored cloths and gold chair covers, a hundred strings of fairy lights wrapped around the frangipani and hibiscus, and tall gold balloon arches on either side of a raised stage. A live highlife band with a talking drum, shekere, conga and electric guitar plays on the stage. Waiters in black waistcoats carry trays of small chops, jollof rice, suya and chilled drinks. Smoke from the suya grill drifts across the lawn. Two hundred guests wear matching aso-ebi in champagne and gold. Warm gold light, cheerful and loud. A four-tier cake with sugar flowers stands under a spotlight and reads HAPPY 30TH, ADUNNI.
Wide shot over the lawn. Women in towering gele hug and shout greetings. Men in agbada clap each other's backs. Children in matching outfits run between the tables, chased by a harried aunt. Someone is spraying naira notes over the band, and the notes flutter down like confetti.
ADUNNI stands at the center of it all in a champagne lace gown with a sweetheart neckline, a gold gele folded in a tall crown, her braids pinned beneath it. Her face is open and bright. She hugs an elderly relative with both arms, laughs with her whole shoulders, touches a friend's wrist as she leans in to hear a joke, and spins once so a child can see her skirt flare. She is entirely happy.
TUNDE stands on the far side of the lawn by the stage steps, in an embroidered navy agbada with gold thread. He is not talking to anyone. He is watching his wife. His smile is soft and a little stunned, the face of a man who cannot believe that this woman is his. When she laughs across the lawn, he looks down, shakes his head slightly and smiles at the grass.
Near the buffet table, two older women in matching gele lean toward each other, hands cupped near their mouths, plates of jollof forgotten in their other hands.

@GUEST ONE (low, admiring, with a small click of the tongue)
Tunde adores that girl. Five years, and not once has he looked elsewhere.

@GUEST TWO (lower still, eyebrows climbing, a sorrowful head shake)
Five years and no child, though. That poor Chief must be praying day and night.

#RX GUEST ONE nods slowly, mouth turned down. They both glance toward the stage where CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI sits at the head table. They look away quickly when she lifts her eyes.

CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI rises from the head table with a hand fan in one fist. She is dressed in a wine lace buba and wrapper with a stiff red gele, coral beads at her throat. She walks to the stage in slow, deliberate steps. The band's last chord dies when she touches the microphone. Two hundred faces turn toward her.

@CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI (slow, dry, each sentence final, glasses lowered on her nose)
Tonight my son celebrates his wife. I say only this. May next year's party hold the laughter of a child.

#RX A swell of applause and a few cries of "Amen!" Adunni's smile does not fall. It tightens. Her hands, folded in front of her, press together so hard her knuckles whiten. She keeps her eyes on the older woman and keeps smiling, and the smile trembles once at the corner. Tunde sees it from across the lawn. His own smile drops.

TUNDE strides to the stage, climbs the three steps in two long paces, and gently takes the microphone from his mother's hand.

@TUNDE (softly, jaw tight, a flash of embarrassment in his eyes)
Mama, tonight is for my wife.

@CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI (unbothered, lowering her fan, one eyebrow lifting)
I only pray, my son.

#RX She walks back to her seat without looking at anyone, spine perfectly straight. A few guests clap awkwardly. The band, relieved, strikes up a bouncy tune. The party rushes back to life.

At the garden gate, a small figure appears under the arch of fairy lights. MORENIKE stands there in a mustard dress that has been tailored a little too tight, a sleek bob wig, gold hoops, a handbag with a worn corner, and a small cheap gift bag in her other hand. She is a beat late. She takes in the lights, the marquee, the cake, the band, the money flying through the air. Her face is polite. Her eyes are not. They move across the lawn like a camera counting everything she does not have.
Adunni spots her and runs across the lawn, one hand holding up her skirt, the other already open for a hug.

@ADUNNI (delighted, breathless, voice rising)
Sister! You came!

#RX She wraps Morenike in both arms and rocks her side to side. Morenike's arms come up slowly, stiff for a moment, and then pat Adunni's back twice, like patting a stranger. Her chin rests on Adunni's shoulder. Over that shoulder, her face is blank.

@MORENIKE (bright, sweet, pulling back with a wide smile, tapping Adunni's cheek)
Would I miss my baby sister's birthday?

She hands over the little gift bag. Adunni peeks inside and pulls out a plain printed scarf. She presses it to her chest as if it were gold.

@ADUNNI (touched, eyes shining)
Ah, it is beautiful. Thank you, Sister.

#RX Morenike watches her wear the scarf over her shoulder and says nothing. Her smile has not moved at all.

The MC, a heavy man in a gold agbada, taps the microphone. Feedback squeals. The guests laugh.

@MC (booming, playful, grinning at the crowd)
Ladies and gentlemen, the husband has one more gift!

#RX Gasps. A drumroll starts from the talking drum. Adunni turns, hands to her chest, laughing and shaking her head. Tunde reaches the center of the stage and raises both hands for quiet.

@TUNDE (grinning like a boy, voice warm and playful)
Adunni, close your eyes.

She does, giggling like a girl. Her hands go over her face, fingers parted a little so she can peek. He chuckles and gently moves her hands down.

@TUNDE (laughing)
No peeping, Aya mi.

Tunde nods at the MC. At the far end of the long driveway, the iron gates swing wide. Headlights sweep across the lawn. A gleaming white SUV rolls up the drive, slow and silent, a huge red ribbon tied across its bonnet and a bow on its roof. The paint reflects every fairy light. Guests part to let it through. The crowd screams.

@TUNDE (into the microphone, proud, drawing out each word)
The newest model in Abuja. The first one in the country. It landed last week. Number plate, ADUNNI ONE.

#RX Phones rise in a forest of glowing screens. A dozen women scream and clutch each other. The band explodes into a victory rhythm. Adunni opens her eyes, sees the car, and freezes. Both hands fly over her mouth. Her eyes go huge and wet. She makes a small sound behind her palms and begins to cry and laugh at once. She turns and runs, skirt gathered in one fist, and throws herself into Tunde's arms. He lifts her off the ground and spins her.

@ADUNNI (sobbing and laughing, forehead against his neck)
What did I do to deserve you?

@TUNDE (face buried in her braids, voice rough with joy)
You married me.

Guests chant her name in rhythm: "A-DUN-NI! A-DUN-NI!" Fireworks open over the roof in gold and red. Everyone looks up.
Everyone except Morenike.
At the edge of the lawn, two young GUESTS in champagne aso-ebi stand with their backs to her, filming the SUV on their phones, not noticing her behind them.

@GUEST THREE (shouting over the music, thrilled)
Imagine! The older sister is still unmarried, and the younger one is being given cars!

@GUEST FOUR (laughing, nudging her)
Some people carry all the luck!

#RX Morenike does not turn. Her shoulders stay square. Her fingers tighten on her handbag strap until the knuckles show. Her lips stay shut, but the muscle at the corner of her jaw jumps once. She begins to clap, one beat too late, in a slow tight rhythm that does not match the band.

In her handbag, her phone vibrates against her ribs. She slides it out a few inches. The screen reads LANDLORD. She stares at the name for a long moment, then presses the red button and returns the phone to her bag without a change of expression. She keeps clapping.
TUNDE steps off the stage, scanning the crowd, and finds her. He crosses the lawn with an easy, open stride. His arm is still around Adunni's waist.

@TUNDE (warm, sincere, pressing a thick envelope into her palm and closing her fingers around it)
For your rent, Sister. Adunni told me.

#RX Morenike looks down at the envelope. Then she looks up, and her face has transformed: bright, grateful, a little teary.

@MORENIKE (soft, tearful, patting his arm twice)
God bless you, Tunde. You are too kind.

#RX He nods warmly and returns to his wife. The moment he turns away, the warmth drains out of Morenike's face like water out of a basin. She looks at the envelope in her hand. It sits there as heavy and warm as a coal.

Fireworks continue to blossom above the roof. Gold light and red light wash over the faces of the guests. Adunni spins in Tunde's arms, laughing, her gele crooked, tears and mascara on her cheeks. The white SUV stands in the middle of the lawn like a trophy. In its glossy door, we see Morenike's reflection, small, distorted and gold-lit. She is smiling, politely, brightly. Then the smile goes out. We hold on the face with no smile, with the fireworks flickering across it. It is not angry. It is very calm.

#TW PLANT: the whispers about the child, and the envelope for rent. Both return when Morenike chooses her weapon.

#TITLE ỌMỌ ÌYÁ MI. My Mother's Child.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, MASTER BEDROOM | NIGHT
#SET Past midnight. The party is long over, and the house is silent except for the grandfather clock downstairs and the far-off hum of the generator. The master bedroom is cream and gold: a wide bed with silk sheets, heavy drapes, a dressing table with a round mirror, a bedside lamp casting a small warm circle. The window is open a hand's width, and the curtains lift gently in the night air. A white lace gown hangs on the wardrobe door like a ghost.
TUNDE is asleep on his back in the wide bed, one arm stretched across the pillow where Adunni's head should be. His face is peaceful, mouth slightly open, cufflinks still on the nightstand.
ADUNNI sits at the dressing table in a satin nightdress, her gele removed and her braids loose across her shoulders. In the mirror, her face looks tired and soft. She removes her earrings one at a time and places them in a velvet tray. She hesitates. Then she opens the carved jewellery box and lifts out the top tray of gold. At the bottom, beneath a layer of tissue paper, lies a sealed hospital envelope, its paper creased and softened with years of handling.
She does not open it. She holds it flat against her chest with both hands. Her eyes close. Her lips move without sound, a prayer or a name. A single tear slides down her cheek and she lets it fall onto the envelope.

#RX She opens her eyes and looks at Tunde's sleeping face in the mirror. Her expression is full of love and an aching tenderness. She breathes in slowly, wipes her cheek with the heel of her hand, and tucks the envelope back beneath the tissue. She covers it with the gold tray. She closes the lid with great care so that it makes no sound.

She rises, walks silently to the door and steps into the corridor. A single lamp glows at the far end. Across the hall is a closed door, painted white. An Ankara cloth in blue and yellow hangs over its brass handle like a small flag. It has hung there so long that it is slightly faded.
Adunni touches the cloth. Her fingers rest on it for a long moment. She lifts them away, folds her hands, and walks past toward the stairs.

#TW PLANT: the envelope and the Ankara cloth. Do not show the envelope's contents. The cloth is not lifted until the final scene.

#SC | INT. MORENIKE'S FLAT, WUSE | NIGHT
#SET Midnight, forty minutes after the party. A cramped second-floor one-bedroom flat in an ageing block. Peeling cream paint with water stains in the corners. A wobbling ceiling fan clicks with each turn. A faded floral sofa, a plastic dining table with two mismatched chairs, a small wardrobe with a cracked mirror. On the wall, a framed photograph of Iya Agba with two small girls in white dresses. Sodium orange light seeps through the louvre windows from the street lamp outside. A neighbour's gospel music thumps faintly through the wall. A landlord's notice lies face-up on the floor where it was slid under the door.
The door unlocks. MORENIKE enters. She kicks off her shoes without bending, and one hits the wall. She drops her handbag and the envelope onto the plastic table. She does not turn on the light. She stands in the orange glow with her arms limp at her sides and her wig slightly crooked.
On the sofa, MICHAEL is stretched out with one foot on the armrest, a plastic bag of suya open on his chest, a toothpick between his teeth. He has his own key. He lifts his head with a lazy grin.

@MICHAEL (easy, teasing, in a rich smooth drawl)
You left that party early.

@MORENIKE (flat, tired, not looking at him, pulling off her earrings)
They gave her a car, Michael. A car with her name on the plate.

@MICHAEL (shrugging, chewing)
So? Your brother-in-law is rich.

@MORENIKE (turning at last, voice low and hard)
Do you know what a guest said while I stood there? "Some people carry all the luck." And me? I carry rent.

She picks up the envelope Tunde gave her. She opens the flap and counts the notes with a thumb, quick and bitter. She throws the envelope onto the table, where it slides and stops at the edge.

#RX Michael sits up slowly, the grin gone from his face. He watches her the way you watch a pot beginning to boil.

@MORENIKE (low, rising, hand pointing at the window as if the party were outside)
Five years, and there is no child in that house. His mother prays for one in front of two hundred guests. And Adunni stands there, smiling, holding her stomach like it is empty.

@MICHAEL (carefully, spreading his hands)
Everybody knows it is her problem. She told the whole family herself.

@MORENIKE (slowly, as if tasting the words)
Yes. She did.

#RX The room goes quiet. The fan clicks. Morenike turns toward the louvre window and stands very still with her hands behind her back. Orange light cuts across her face in stripes. She does not look angry. She looks calm, which is worse. We watch her thinking.

@MORENIKE (very quiet, to the glass)
A man like Tunde needs a push. A man like him believes whatever is shown to him. If I cannot enjoy a happy marriage, Adunni must not keep hers.

@MICHAEL (amused, leaning forward, elbows on knees)
What push?

@MORENIKE (turning, a small cold smile appearing)
A scandal.

She sits on the arm of the sofa, takes out her phone, and scrolls past the landlord's name to a contact saved as BAYO, CHOIR. She presses call. It rings. It rings a long time.

@MORENIKE (into phone, sweet and bright, a different woman entirely)
Bayo. Do you still want that job?

#RX Michael raises one eyebrow and leans back, settling in to watch the show. Morenike's reflection in the black screen of the window smiles back at her.

#SC | INT. BUKA CANTEEN | NIGHT
#SET A cheap roadside canteen near the Wuse market, open late. Plastic tables with peeling vinyl cloths, mismatched plastic chairs, a ceiling fan with a missing blade, a wall calendar from a soft drink company. A small TV bolted to the wall plays a football replay with the sound down. A woman at the counter ladles stew into plastic plates. The light is harsh white from a single tube. The smell of palm oil and fried plantain hangs in the air.
BAYO sits alone at a corner table in a faded polo shirt, hunched over a plate of beans and bread, eating quickly with a bent spoon. He is lean and tired, with a chipped front tooth. His phone, the screen cracked, sits beside the plate.
MORENIKE enters, scanning the room with distaste. She picks her way between the tables, holding her handbag against her stomach, and sits across from Bayo. She places a paper bag on the table with fingertips. She slides a folded CV beside it.

@BAYO (surprised, mouth full, then wiping his lips and sitting up straight)
Madam Morenike! Ah, good evening, ma.

@MORENIKE (brisk, pleasant, not touching the table)
Open it.

He peeks inside the paper bag. He pulls out a navy suit, still in its plastic. His eyes widen. He runs his thumb along the fabric, his face stunned.

@BAYO (breathless, half-laughing)
Madam, why are you doing this for me?

@MORENIKE (calm, leaning in)
Tunde Adebisi needs a personal assistant. You will be very good at it.

@BAYO (lowering his voice, a cautious grin spreading)
And what do I do for you?

@MORENIKE (smiling, her head tilted, her eyes completely still)
Be kind to my sister. Very kind.

#RX Bayo stares at her for a second, then laughs, short and delighted, rubbing his jaw. He thinks he understands. He leans back, chewing his bread with new confidence. Morenike watches him laugh. Her smile does not change. She lifts one finger and sets it on the CV, pushing it an inch toward him. He takes it with both hands, like a gift.

#SC | EXT. ADEBISI MANSION, GARDEN | DAY
#SET Bright mid-morning, a few days later. The garden after the party is quiet and clean, the marquee gone, only trimmed hibiscus hedges and a stone bench under a frangipani. A sprinkler ticks across the grass. Sunlight dapples the flowerbeds. A bird sings in the tree. A white SUV with the plate ADUNNI ONE is parked in the shade of the portico.
ADUNNI walks along the hibiscus hedge in a pale yellow lace blouse and wrapper, a phone to her ear, one hand trailing along the leaves. She is relaxed, brisk, and speaking in a lowered, confidential tone.

@ADUNNI (warm and firm, into the phone)
Baba Sule, pay the weavers first. Always the weavers first.

@BABA SULE (V.O., raspy, respectful, a little anxious)
Ma, the bank is asking whose name is on the deed.

@ADUNNI (stopping, lowering her voice even more, a glance over her shoulder)
Nobody's name is to be spoken. Mama said so. Not until the day comes.

Behind the hedge, a shadow moves. MORENIKE stands very still among the leaves, one hand holding back a branch. She has arrived early and walked around from the side gate. She has heard the last sentences. Her eyes narrow. She counts to three. Then she steps out into the sun with a wide, delighted smile.

@MORENIKE (bright, teasing, hands out)
Which deed?

#RX Adunni startles and presses the phone against her chest. Her eyes widen and she lets out a quick breathy laugh to cover it.

@ADUNNI (too quick, laughing)
My tailor. He is behind on rent.

@MORENIKE (kissing Adunni's cheek, pinching her chin)
You and your charity cases.

#RX Adunni laughs and waves it off, tucking her phone into her pocket. She does not see her sister's smile freeze in place for a full second after her back is turned, nor see Morenike's eyes drop to the pocket where the phone vanished. Then Morenike's face resets.

#TW PLANT: the deed, the weavers, the name nobody speaks.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, KITCHEN | DAY
#SET A large bright kitchen with white marble countertops, a gas hob big enough for a restaurant, copper pots on hooks, a window over the sink full of green leaves. A bowl of tomatoes, a chopping board, a half-peeled onion. A radio plays low gospel. The cook has been sent out. The air smells of onions, thyme and pepper.
ADUNNI chops spring onions on a wooden board with quick, practiced strokes. MORENIKE sits on a stool at the island, her handbag in her lap, her fingers twisting the strap. She stares at the board and says nothing for a while.

@MORENIKE (hesitant, soft, the voice of someone trying not to cry)
There is a boy. Bayo. His father is dead. He has five siblings and a mother who cannot walk.

@ADUNNI (the knife stopping, her head turning, eyes softening)
Ah. God.

@MORENIKE (tearful, eyes lowered)
He is from my choir. He is so clever. But nobody will give him a chance.

@ADUNNI (putting down the knife and wiping her hands on her apron, coming around the island)
What kind of work does he do?

@MORENIKE (looking up with wet eyes)
Tunde needs an assistant. Just a word from you.

#RX Adunni takes Morenike's hands in both of her own. Her face is full of tender concern. She nods several times.

@ADUNNI (soft, certain)
I will speak to him tonight.

Cut to:
INT. MASTER BEDROOM, THE SAME NIGHT. Lamp light. Tunde sits up in bed with a laptop on his knees. Adunni lies against his arm, tracing a pattern on the sheet with one finger. She tells him the story in low tones. Tunde listens with half a frown, then sighs, closes the laptop, and kisses the top of her head.

@TUNDE (indulgent, shaking his head slowly, a smile pulling at his mouth)
For you, Adunni. Anything for you.

#RX She beams up at him and kisses his jaw. He pulls her closer. The lamp clicks off. The room settles into warm darkness, and the only sound left is the soft tick of the grandfather clock downstairs.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MOTORS, HEADQUARTERS | DAY (MONTAGE)
#SET A gleaming glass-and-steel headquarters on the edge of Abuja. The ground floor is a showroom of polished cars on turntables. Upstairs, open-plan offices with workers at standing desks, a long glass corridor and Tunde's corner office with a view over the city. Bright daylight, cold white air-conditioning, upbeat office music.
A quick sequence, cut to a light, jaunty rhythm:
BAYO in his borrowed navy suit steps out of a lift, a folder pressed to his chest. His hair is slick, his smile is wide. He nods at the receptionist and she smiles back.
He stands when TUNDE enters a meeting. He knows every client's name. He holds the door. He fetches coffee exactly how Tunde likes it. He brings good news to the boardroom with a flourish and a grin.
TUNDE watches him through the glass wall of his office with approving nods, jotting a note.
A day later. ADUNNI enters the lobby with a lunch basket. BAYO is already at the lift, holding the door open with one arm and a bright, deferential smile.

@BAYO (warm, quick, bowing slightly)
Madam, welcome! Oga is in a meeting. Let me escort you.

A week later. In the canteen. Bayo buys her a small chocolate bar from the vending machine and presses it into her hand.

@BAYO (shy, lopsided grin)
Just because, Madam.

#RX Adunni laughs with her whole face and takes it. She slips it into her bag. TUNDE, walking past with two chiefs, sees the exchange, smiles and gives Bayo a thumbs up. Nobody notices that Bayo's eyes never leave Adunni's face when she walks away, and that his smile changes slightly, grows quieter, more calculating, as soon as she is out of sight.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, LIVING ROOM | DAY
#SET Mid-afternoon in the formal living room. Gold-trimmed cream sofas, heavy cream drapes held with gold tassels, a huge framed wedding portrait of Adunni and Tunde above the fireplace. A console table by the foyer door holds a crystal vase of white lilies. A small white CCTV dome sits in the ceiling corner above the foyer, easy to miss. The grandfather clock ticks. Sunlight slants across a patterned Persian rug.
MORENIKE sits on the edge of a sofa, a gift of kola nuts in a small basket on her knee. ADUNNI sits opposite, pouring tea into two china cups.

@MORENIKE (casual, bright)
Adunni, that fabric I asked you about, the green one with the gold thread. Is it upstairs?

@ADUNNI (rising, setting the teapot down)
In the wardrobe. Wait here.

She leaves the room and climbs the stairs. Her footsteps fade on the landing. A door opens upstairs, then another.
The instant the sound disappears, Morenike's face changes. She stands, quick and silent. She slides a hand into her handbag and pulls out a small black camcorder the size of a fist. She crosses to the console in three soft steps. She lifts the white lilies out of the crystal vase and sets them on the table. She wedges the camcorder inside the vase, lens pointing out through the glass toward the foyer door, then slides the lilies back in around it, hiding it. She adjusts the angle with one finger. She presses record.
A tiny red light blinks once and holds. It glints through the crystal like a small ruby.
Morenike leans close to the lens. Her face fills the glass, warped and large.

@MORENIKE (whispering, almost tender)
Smile, my sister.

#RX She straightens. She smooths her dress with both hands and returns to the sofa. She sits, crosses her ankles, and picks up her tea. By the time Adunni returns with the green fabric draped over her arm, Morenike is sipping calmly and smiling.

#TW PLANT: the camcorder, the cassette and the whisper "Smile, my sister." All three return in the suitcase scene.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MANSION, FOYER AND KITCHEN | DAY
#SET Late morning, three days later. The foyer is bright and quiet. Sun pours through the tall front windows across the black-and-white chequered floor. The crystal vase sits on the console, white lilies drooping a little. The tiny red light blinks. The kitchen door is open, and the smell of frying plantain drifts out. Tunde is at the office. The staff have the afternoon off.
A knock at the front door. ADUNNI crosses the foyer in a green lace blouse and wrapper, drying her hands on a cloth. She opens the door. BAYO stands on the doorstep in his navy suit holding a thin folder, grinning, with a faint sheen of sweat on his forehead.

@BAYO (polite, bright, bowing a little)
Madam, Oga asked me to drop these documents.

@ADUNNI (stepping aside, warm)
Come in. Wait, let me give you something.

He steps into the foyer and looks around slowly, taking it in. Adunni goes to the kitchen, returns with a tall glass of water on a tray, and hands it to him. He drinks it slowly, his eyes on her over the rim.

@BAYO (soft, admiring, lowering the glass)
Madam, you are too kind. Many people would not do this.

@ADUNNI (laughing it off, turning toward the kitchen)
Are you hungry? I have food.

She goes into the kitchen again. He follows to the doorway, leaning against the frame. She fills a plate with rice and fried plantain, sets it on the island, and turns to hand him a fork. As she places the plate, his hand shoots out and closes around her wrist.

@BAYO (low, intimate, leaning close)
Do you know you are very beautiful?

#RX Adunni freezes. Her eyes go to his hand, then to his face. Her mouth opens slightly. For a few seconds she does not move at all, caught between disbelief and the instinct to be polite. Her lips tremble. Her breath stops.

@ADUNNI (voice small and shaking, trying to pull her wrist back)
What are you doing? Let go of my hand.

@BAYO (lowering his voice, stepping closer, tilting his head)
Madam, you are lonely in this house. I can see it.

@ADUNNI (rising, fear giving way to anger)
What are you doing? What do you think you are doing?

She wrenches her arm back and grabs the plate. She hurls it at the floor between them. It explodes against the tiles, rice and plantain scattering across the white marble.

@ADUNNI (shaking, finger pointing at the door, voice thin and furious)
Get out of my house.

#RX Bayo backs away with his hands raised, still smiling as if he had lost nothing. He steps over the broken plate and walks out through the foyer, passing the console with the crystal vase. His eyes glance at it once and move on. Adunni stands in the kitchen with her hand pressed to her wrist, breathing hard, shaking. Slowly she slides down against the island and sits on the floor among the broken pieces with her face in her hands.
In the vase on the console, a tiny red light blinks.

#SC | INT. MORENIKE'S FLAT | NIGHT
#SET Night, a few hours later. The flat is dark except for the cold blue glow of a laptop on the plastic table and a single desk lamp aimed at the keyboard. The ceiling fan clicks. The street lamp paints orange stripes across the wall. A mosquito coil smokes in a saucer.
MORENIKE sits hunched in front of the laptop, headphones on, her wig removed and her natural hair in small flat twists. She is watching the footage from the camcorder, which is connected by a cable. On the screen, we see fragments: the foyer, Bayo's hand on Adunni's wrist, the plate shattering. We hear them in the tinny leak from her headphones. She watches without expression, her chin resting on one fist.
She scrubs the timeline forward, stops, and pauses on a single frame: Adunni standing frozen, her wrist in Bayo's hand, her mouth open, her eyes huge. In that frame, it could be shock, or invitation.
Morenike drags the clip into an editing window. She trims the beginning. She trims the end. She cuts the moment Adunni says "Let go of my hand." She deletes the audio track. She cuts out the sound of the plate. She leaves twelve seconds of silent footage. She plays it. It looks like a woman who does not resist.
She exports the clip. A progress bar crosses the screen. At 100 percent, she exhales through her nose, a small satisfied click of the tongue.
She disconnects the camcorder and ejects the mini-DV cassette. She holds it up to the lamp, turns it, looks at the handwritten date on the label. Her lips press together as she considers. Then she crosses to the bed, drags an old brown suitcase from underneath it, unzips it, and finds a corner of the lining. With a nail file she unpicks a few stitches, slides the cassette into the gap, and runs her finger along the seam. She zips the suitcase and pushes it back under the bed with her foot.

#RX She sits on the edge of the bed, looks at the suitcase for a moment, and picks up her phone. She sends the twelve-second clip to herself and then to a second number. Her thumbs move steadily. Her face is a mask.

#TW PLANT: the cassette in the suitcase lining. Keep the shot on it for a full beat. This is the film's biggest payoff.

#SC | INT. ADEBISI MOTORS, TUNDE'S OFFICE | DAY
#SET Late morning in Tunde's glass-walled corner office. A wide dark desk with a leather chair, a silver model of a car on a stand, a framed photograph of Adunni and Tunde on their wedding day, shelves of trophies, a window overlooking Abuja's skyline in hazy light. The air conditioning hums. Outside the glass wall, employees move without noise.
TUNDE stands behind his desk, buttoning his jacket. MORENIKE sits in the visitor's chair opposite, back straight, hands folded over her handbag, eyes already glistening.

@MORENIKE (soft, trembling, not looking up)
I did not want to be the one to show you this.

She slides her phone across the desk. Tunde looks at her for a beat, picks it up, presses play.
The silent clip plays: Adunni frozen in the kitchen, Bayo's hand around her wrist, his face inches from hers. Her mouth open. Her eyes wide. No sound. No struggle.
Tunde watches. His jaw tightens. His chest rises and falls. He plays it again. He plays it a third time, one finger pressed against his lips. He sets the phone down very gently on the desk, as if it might break.

@TUNDE (hoarse, barely audible, staring at the blank screen)
Why is she standing there?

@MORENIKE (careful, low, as if afraid)
Tunde, I do not know. Maybe it was shock. Maybe... I do not want to say.

@TUNDE (turning, eyes hard)
Say it.

@MORENIKE (looking up, tears sliding, a tremor in her voice)
She did not pull away. Twelve seconds, Tunde. I counted.

#RX Tunde grips the edge of the desk until his knuckles go grey. A vein rises on his temple. His whole body goes rigid. His eyes squeeze shut. He is breathing through his nose in short hard bursts. When he opens his eyes, they are wet and red. He snatches his own phone from his pocket and dials with a stabbing thumb.

@TUNDE (into phone, voice shaking with fury, spacing every word)
Commissioner. Yes. I want a man arrested. Tonight. He is my employee, Bayo Adeleke. He put his hands on my wife.

He hangs up. He stands with both fists on the desk, head bowed. Morenike lowers her eyes so he cannot see what is in them. When she speaks again her voice is gentle and worried.

@MORENIKE (soft, tentative)
Is it not better to handle this quietly?

@TUNDE (lifting his head, shouting)
Quietly? That man stood in my house!

He storms out. The door slams so hard the model car rocks on its stand. Morenike remains in the chair, completely still, looking at the phone on the desk. Her face: relief, and underneath it, the first thin line of fear. She reaches out and slowly retrieves her phone. She stares at the black screen, at her own faint reflection, and her lips move in a single silent word that we cannot read.

#SC | INT. GWAGWALADA DIVISIONAL POLICE STATION, CELL | NIGHT
#SET Night. A single cell at the back of a low concrete police station on the edge of Abuja. Peeling blue paint, a stained concrete floor, a single high barred window with a sliver of orange sky, a bucket in the corner, two thin foam mattresses. A tube light overhead flickers and buzzes. Water drips in the corridor beyond the bars. A radio somewhere plays a faint news bulletin.
BAYO sits on the floor with his back against the wall and his knees drawn up. His navy suit jacket is gone, his shirt torn at the collar, his lip split and swollen. His left eye is closing. His hands shake. He stares at the opposite wall.
Across the cell, MR. OBI sits on a mattress in a faded white undershirt, a tin cup of water in his big hand. Silver hair at his temples, a tired kind face, a heavy body folded against the wall. He watches Bayo for a long time over the rim of the cup.

@OBI (slow, gravelly, Igbo-inflected, gently)
Young man, what did you do?

@BAYO (hoarse, not looking up, a bitter laugh)
I only did what I was paid for.

@OBI (setting the cup down)
Paid by who?

Bayo says nothing. His jaw works. A POLICEMAN appears at the bars, tapping them with a baton. He holds out a cheap phone through the gap.

@POLICEMAN (bored, low)
Two minutes. Do not make me regret it.

Bayo snatches the phone. His fingers are unsteady. He dials a number from memory. He turns his body toward the wall and cups his hand over his mouth. Obi pretends to look at the ceiling.

@BAYO (into phone, voice thin and cracking, a whisper)
Madam. You promised me. You said the woman would be blamed, not me. Now I am in a cell.

@MORENIKE (V.O., sharp, soothing, tinny through the speaker)
Bayo, listen...

@BAYO (louder, desperate, a sob in his throat)
No, you listen. Get me out by tomorrow, or I tell Mr. Adebisi everything. The camera, the clip, all of it.

#RX He presses the red button and holds the phone against his forehead, squeezing his eyes shut. His shoulders shake. Obi looks at him with calm, careful eyes and says nothing. He has just heard everything.

Cut to:
INT. MORENIKE'S KITCHEN, THE SAME MOMENT. A tiny kitchen, one bare bulb. Morenike stands by the sink with the phone pressed against her mouth. The call has ended. The kitchen is silent. A tap drips. She stares at the wall. Her hand is shaking. She puts the phone down on the counter, then picks it up again, then puts it down. She presses both palms flat on the counter and breathes in once through her nose. When she lifts her head, her face is cold and still again.
