Overview

Logline

On the night of Adunni's birthday, a jealous elder sister watches her younger sister receive the biggest car in Abuja and decides to destroy her marriage. She succeeds, and then marries the husband herself. But every lie she tells is recorded somewhere, and years later the sister she threw into the rain owns his mansions.

Synopsis

Adunni Ogundele-Adebisi is married to Tunde Adebisi, the owner of the biggest auto company in Abuja. Their marriage is loving, but after five years there is still no child. Years ago Adunni quietly told the doctor, and then the whole family, that the fault was hers. The truth is that Tunde has a low sperm count. She took the blame to protect his pride, and she never told him otherwise.

At Adunni's birthday party, Tunde surprises her with the newest car in Abuja. Her older sister, Morenike, unmarried and consumed by envy, watches from the lawn and decides that night to take the marriage apart and take Tunde for herself. She plants a charming stranger, Bayo, as Tunde's personal assistant, and hides a camera in the house. Bayo makes a move on Adunni. Morenike edits the footage so it looks like Adunni did not resist, and shows it to Tunde. Tunde jails Bayo. When Bayo threatens to expose Morenike, she pays corrupt policemen to silence him, and he dies in custody.

Morenike then uses the weapon Adunni handed her. In front of Tunde and his mother, she says that Adunni cannot give a child. Tunde throws Adunni out into the rain. Adunni drives to a mansion, a workshop and a house her late grandmother secretly left her. Her grandmother raised both sisters, and she left everything to Adunni because she had seen Morenike's nature when Morenike was a child. Within a few years Adunni builds a luxury aso-oke empire. Morenike marries Tunde.

The marriage poisons itself. A blackmailing policeman drains Morenike. Her lover, Michael, swindles Tunde and then swindles Morenike. A prisoner Bayo befriended spends his freedom hunting the truth, and an investigator finds Bayo's secret phone. Tunde's collapsing company takes a rescue loan from Adunni's company, not knowing whose it is. When he cannot repay, Adunni buys out his creditors and becomes the owner of his mansions. While packing, Tunde finds a cassette in Morenike's suitcase. It shows him everything he got wrong.

Format and Tone

Feature-length drama written for YouTube release. Target runtime 95 to 105 minutes. Dialogue is in English with short Yoruba phrases (translated in the script). The tone is Nollywood melodrama with a thriller engine: big emotions, heavy silences, cliffhangers before every ad break, and reversals that arrive just after the audience feels certain.

Setting

Abuja, Nigeria, present day, covering roughly five years. The Location Bible in this document describes every principal location in detail.

Directing Rule for Shocks

Each time the audience believes it knows what happens next, give it something else. The script does this by building a clear expectation, letting it settle, and then breaking it with a fact that has been quietly planted earlier. No twist arrives without a plant. The Shock Map in the next section lists them.

The Shock Map

Each entry lists what the audience expects, what actually happens, and where the reveal was planted. Scenes are named, not numbered, so the list stays correct if scenes move.

SHOCK 1 (the birthday party). Expect: the biggest night of Adunni's life is a celebration. Get: it is the night her sister decides to destroy her. The camera finds Morenike's reflection in the car door, smiling and then not smiling.

SHOCK 2 (the edited clip). Expect: Tunde sees the whole truth. Get: he sees twelve silent seconds in which Adunni appears to freeze, and the lie takes hold. Plant: Morenike tucks the original cassette into her suitcase lining.

SHOCK 3 (the toilet block). Expect: Morenike's accomplice will blackmail her later. Get: she has him killed within days, and the order is a single quiet sentence.

SHOCK 4 (the hidden mansion). Expect: Adunni, thrown out of her home, is poor and alone. Get: she owns a mansion with staff, a workshop and a house. Plant: the phone call to Baba Sule in the garden.

SHOCK 5 (the flashback in the hall). Expect: Adunni cannot have children. Get: Adunni is fertile. Tunde is the one who cannot. She took the blame. Plant: the sealed envelope in her jewellery box.

SHOCK 6 (the blackmail and the swindle). Expect: Morenike has won. Get: the policeman who killed Bayo now owns her, and then her lover robs her too.

SHOCK 7 (the loan). Expect: Adunni will forgive Tunde and rescue him for free. Get: she grants the loan on a sixty-day clause.

SHOCK 8 (Bayo's old room). Expect: the evidence is a recording Morenike can destroy. Get: Bayo hid a phone in a wall of his room before he died, and it recorded her.

SHOCK 9 (the arrest). Expect: Tunde catches Morenike stealing and the story ends in a fraud arrest. Get: the police arrive for murder, and Tunde learns for the first time that Bayo is dead.

SHOCK 10 (the boardroom). Expect: Tunde knows who is lending him money. Get: he has been begging his own ex-wife, and she has bought out every creditor he has.

SHOCK 11 (the suitcase). Expect: the story is over once Morenike is jailed. Get: Tunde finds the original cassette and learns he threw out an innocent wife. Payoff: the tucked-away cassette from the edit scene.

SHOCK 12 (the TV in the shop). Expect: Adunni lives alone with her grief. Get: she has a husband, Dr. Dawson, and triplets, and Tunde realises in one second that it was never her.

SHOCK 13 (the courtroom). Expect: Morenike's lawyer will find a loophole. Get: Michael turns state witness, and Bayo's own phone speaks from the grave.

SHOCK 14 (the post-credit scene). Expect: the story is closed. Get: the grandmother left a second sealed letter, addressed to Morenike.

How to Use This Script for Visuals

This edition is written for people and tools that will turn each scene into video or still images. Every scene is built from the same layers, so nothing has to be guessed.

SETTING blocks (grey panel): the physical world of the scene. Where it is, the time of day, the light, the colors, the textures, the weather, the sounds in the background and the mood.

ACTION lines: what the camera sees, in order. Each line describes movement, props, facial expression and body language, not just plot.

DIALOGUE with delivery notes: who speaks, and in the parenthesis, how. Delivery covers pace, volume, pitch, breath and the expression on the speaker's face.

REACTION blocks (indented, italic): what happens in the seconds after a line or an action. Faces falling, hands stopping, the room changing. These are the beats most tools skip, so they are written out.

DIRECTOR'S NOTES: plants and payoffs for the twists. These are for the editor and director, not the camera.

Visual Continuity Rules

Light follows the story. The Adebisi mansion is warm gold while the marriage is happy, then turns cool blue and grey after the eviction. Adunni's new world is deep indigo with warm lantern light. Morenike's world is sodium orange and harsh fluorescent.

Each main character keeps one signature color. Adunni: champagne and indigo. Morenike: mustard and gold. Tunde: charcoal and white, then oil-stained grey. Chief Mrs. Adebisi: deep red and coral.

Rain only appears twice: the eviction and the last scene. It is the film's rhyme.

Keep the same face for each actor and the same wardrobe for each timeline stage. The Character Bible lists the stages.

How to use the prompts. Each character has an image prompt and a voice prompt. To keep faces consistent across shots, generate one hero portrait per character first, then use it as the reference image for every later prompt. Add this style line to the end of every image prompt:

Principal Characters

ADUNNI OGUNDELE-ADEBISI

Role. Protagonist. Wife of Tunde, younger sister of Morenike, secret heiress, later founder of Agba Aso-Oke Industries.

Age. 30 at the start. 35 at the end.

Appearance. Slim, graceful, dark-brown skin, soft round face with a gap-toothed smile, long neck, hair in neat braids or under a gele. Wardrobe stages: (1) the marriage, pastel lace, champagne gowns, simple gold studs; (2) the eviction, a soaked lace wrapper and bare feet; (3) the empire, sharp deep-indigo aso-oke suits, a wine gele, a single heavy ring; (4) the new family, soft cream knitwear and a simple headscarf.

Body language and expressions. She walks with a straight spine and small, quick steps when happy, and slows to a glide when hurt. She touches people when she speaks: a hand on an arm, a squeeze of a wrist. She laughs with her whole shoulders and covers her mouth with both hands when moved. When she is wounded she goes very still, her lower lip trembles once, and she swallows before speaking. Her eyes fill without a sound. In the empire years her stillness becomes a weapon: a level gaze, hands folded, a very slight lift of the chin.

Voice and speech. Soft mezzo, warm and unhurried, with a gentle Yoruba lilt. She rises at the end of a sentence when teasing, and drops lower and slower when she is hurt. She never shouts. She says "Ah, Tunde" and "Mama" with affection. Her laughter is breathy and short. In the boardroom her voice becomes flat and calm, each word placed like a coin.

Nature. Warm, humble, proud in a quiet way. She will give up her reputation for love, and then be unmovable once trust is gone. She never uses the word revenge.

MORENIKE OGUNDELE

Role. Antagonist. Elder sister of Adunni. Unmarried. Later Tunde's second wife and then a convicted murderer.

Age. 37 at the start. 42 at the end.

Appearance. Taller than Adunni, striking rather than soft, high cheekbones, defined brows, lips usually pressed into a polite smile. Wardrobe stages: (1) the party and the flat, a mustard dress, a sleek bob wig, gold hoops, a handbag with a worn corner; (2) the wife, real designer wear that does not suit her, too many bangles; (3) the prison, a plain grey wrapper, hair tied under a faded headtie.

Body language and expressions. She stands very upright with her shoulders back and her handbag held against her stomach like a shield. Her smile appears fast and disappears fast, like a light switched on and off. Her eyes stay still when her mouth smiles. She claps late. She tilts her head to listen and never interrupts. When she lies, she blinks slowly and touches her collarbone. When she is angry, she goes quieter, not louder. In prison she becomes stone: hands folded, spine straight, no blink.

Voice and speech. Crisp alto, controlled and polite, with careful diction. In public she is sweet: "my dear", "Sister", "Mama", with a bright rising tone. In private she is flat and dry, almost bored. She uses long pauses to make others fill the silence. She never raises her voice, even when ordering a killing. Her laugh is a single dry breath.

Nature. Intelligent, patient, charming in public. She believes she is owed a life.

TUNDE ADEBISI

Role. Adunni's husband, the only son of the Adebisi family, founder and CEO of Adebisi Motors.

Age. 34 at the start. 39 at the end.

Appearance. Tall, broad-shouldered, clean-shaven, a confident jaw and tired eyes. Wardrobe stages: (1) the marriage, tailored dark suits, crisp white shirts, an embroidered navy agbada at the party; (2) the fall, a rumpled suit and loosened tie, thin face, shadows under the eyes; (3) the shop, an oil-stained polo shirt and plastic sandals.

Body language and expressions. He takes up space: wide stance, hands on hips, a big open laugh. When moved he looks up and blinks fast. When angry his jaw locks and a vein shows at the temple, and he points with a straight arm. When ashamed he turns his body away, rubs the back of his neck and cannot hold eye contact. In the shop his shoulders curve inward and his hands, once clean, are black with oil.

Voice and speech. Rich baritone, confident and generous, a deep laugh. He says "Aya mi" (my wife) tenderly. In anger he speaks in short, hard commands and his voice cracks upward at the end. When he breaks, the voice becomes a hoarse whisper. In the shop his pitch is lower and slower, a man who has stopped giving orders.

Nature. Generous and proud. He loves Adunni sincerely but cannot bear to be seen as less than a man, and that pride is his tragedy.

CHIEF MRS. FOLASADE ADEBISI

Role. Tunde's mother. A matriarch who wants grandchildren and believes the wrong sister.

Age. 63.

Appearance. Small, upright, formidable. A stiff gele in deep red, heavy coral beads, rimless glasses on a chain, a wine lace buba and wrapper, a beaded fan she keeps in one hand.

Body language and expressions. She moves slowly and never hurries. She folds her arms when displeased and taps one finger against the opposite elbow. She rarely blinks. She looks over her glasses when she is judging. When she is wrong, she takes the glasses off and holds them in both hands. In the ending her shoulders sag and the fan finally rests in her lap.

Voice and speech. Low and dry, slow, each sentence ending like a closing door. She speaks formal English with Yoruba proverbs and calls people "my daughter" or by their full name. She never raises her voice because she never needs to.

Nature. Protective and old-fashioned. She is not a villain. She is wrong, and when she learns how wrong she was, it breaks her.

BAYO ADELEKE

Role. The planted personal assistant. Morenike's tool and her first victim.

Age. 28.

Appearance. Lean, handsome, quick smile, one chipped front tooth, sleek short hair. He wears a borrowed navy suit that fits slightly wrong and a thin tie. In the cell he wears a torn white shirt and has a swollen left eye and a split lip.

Body language and expressions. He is always moving, shifting weight, tapping a foot, adjusting a cuff. He smiles first and thinks second. He leans in when he talks, with a head tilt that feels like friendliness. When scared his smile stays but his eyes widen and his hands shake. In the cell he curls against the wall and his bravado drains out of him.

Voice and speech. A quick bright tenor with street charm, sprinkled with Pidgin: "Madam, ah!", "No wahala." He laughs in short bursts. When afraid his voice thins and speeds up. By the cell he is hoarse and slow.

Nature. Charming and desperate. He supports a sick mother and thinks the job is easy money. He is not evil, only greedy and weak, and that costs him everything.

DR. DANIEL DAWSON

Role. Cardiologist, later Adunni's husband.

Age. 41.

Appearance. Calm, short neat beard, thin wire glasses, a soft voice and steady hands. White coat at work, a plain grey knit sweater at home.

Body language and expressions. He stands still and listens with his whole face. He takes his glasses off to rub the bridge of his nose when he is moved. His hand finds the small of Adunni's back without being asked. He never hurries toward a crying person. He sits near them and waits.

Voice and speech. Deep soft baritone, measured, with a faint British-trained edge on a Nigerian base. Each sentence lands slowly. He pauses before the kind thing.

Nature. Patient and grounded. He does not rescue Adunni. He stays beside her while she rescues herself.

Supporting Characters

MR. OBINNA NWOSU ("OBI")

Role. Wealthy businessman jailed on a false fake-alert charge. Befriends Bayo, is released the day Bayo dies, and spends his freedom pursuing justice.

Age. 52.

Appearance. Heavy-set, silver at the temples, tired kind eyes, a faded prison undershirt in the cells and a crisp navy agbada afterwards with a thick gold watch.

Act One: The Birthday

1EXT. ADEBISI MANSION, GARDEN, MAITAMA, ABUJA — NIGHT (ADUNNI'S BIRTHDAY PARTY)
Setting. A warm Saturday evening, just after sunset. The whole back garden is dressed for a celebration: a huge white marquee with gold drapes, round tables with champagne-colored cloths and gold chair covers, a hundred strings of fairy lights wrapped around the frangipani and hibiscus, and tall gold balloon arches on either side of a raised stage. A live highlife band with a talking drum, shekere, conga and electric guitar plays on the stage. Waiters in black waistcoats carry trays of small chops, jollof rice, suya and chilled drinks. Smoke from the suya grill drifts across the lawn. Two hundred guests wear matching aso-ebi in champagne and gold. Warm gold light, cheerful and loud. A four-tier cake with sugar flowers stands under a spotlight and reads HAPPY 30TH, ADUNNI.

Wide shot over the lawn. Women in towering gele hug and shout greetings. Men in agbada clap each other's backs. Children in matching outfits run between the tables, chased by a harried aunt. Someone is spraying naira notes over the band, and the notes flutter down like confetti.

ADUNNI stands at the center of it all in a champagne lace gown with a sweetheart neckline, a gold gele folded in a tall crown, her braids pinned beneath it. Her face is open and bright. She hugs an elderly relative with both arms, laughs with her whole shoulders, touches a friend's wrist as she leans in to hear a joke, and spins once so a child can see her skirt flare. She is entirely happy.

TUNDE stands on the far side of the lawn by the stage steps, in an embroidered navy agbada with gold thread. He is not talking to anyone. He is watching his wife. His smile is soft and a little stunned, the face of a man who cannot believe that this woman is his. When she laughs across the lawn, he looks down, shakes his head slightly and smiles at the grass.

Near the buffet table, two older women in matching gele lean toward each other, hands cupped near their mouths, plates of jollof forgotten in their other hands.

GUEST ONE
(low, admiring, with a small click of the tongue)
Tunde adores that girl. Five years, and not once has he looked elsewhere.
GUEST TWO
(lower still, eyebrows climbing, a sorrowful head shake)
Five years and no child, though. That poor Chief must be praying day and night.

Reaction. GUEST ONE nods slowly, mouth turned down. They both glance toward the stage where CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI sits at the head table. They look away quickly when she lifts her eyes.

CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI rises from the head table with a hand fan in one fist. She is dressed in a wine lace buba and wrapper with a stiff red gele, coral beads at her throat. She walks to the stage in slow, deliberate steps. The band's last chord dies when she touches the microphone. Two hundred faces turn toward her.

CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI
(slow, dry, each sentence final, glasses lowered on her nose)
Tonight my son celebrates his wife. I say only this. May next year's party hold the laughter of a child.

Reaction. A swell of applause and a few cries of "Amen!" Adunni's smile does not fall. It tightens. Her hands, folded in front of her, press together so hard her knuckles whiten. She keeps her eyes on the older woman and keeps smiling, and the smile trembles once at the corner. Tunde sees it from across the lawn. His own smile drops.

TUNDE strides to the stage, climbs the three steps in two long paces, and gently takes the microphone from his mother's hand.

TUNDE
(softly, jaw tight, a flash of embarrassment in his eyes)
Mama, tonight is for my wife.
CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI
(unbothered, lowering her fan, one eyebrow lifting)
I only pray, my son.

Reaction. She walks back to her seat without looking at anyone, spine perfectly straight. A few guests clap awkwardly. The band, relieved, strikes up a bouncy tune. The party rushes back to life.

At the garden gate, a small figure appears under the arch of fairy lights. MORENIKE stands there in a mustard dress that has been tailored a little too tight, a sleek bob wig, gold hoops, a handbag with a worn corner, and a small cheap gift bag in her other hand. She is a beat late. She takes in the lights, the marquee, the cake, the band, the money flying through the air. Her face is polite. Her eyes are not. They move across the lawn like a camera counting everything she does not have.

Adunni spots her and runs across the lawn, one hand holding up her skirt, the other already open for a hug.

ADUNNI
(delighted, breathless, voice rising)
Sister! You came!

Reaction. She wraps Morenike in both arms and rocks her side to side. Morenike's arms come up slowly, stiff for a moment, and then pat Adunni's back twice, like patting a stranger. Her chin rests on Adunni's shoulder. Over that shoulder, her face is blank.

MORENIKE
(bright, sweet, pulling back with a wide smile, tapping Adunni's cheek)
Would I miss my baby sister's birthday?

She hands over the little gift bag. Adunni peeks inside and pulls out a plain printed scarf. She presses it to her chest as if it were gold.

ADUNNI
(touched, eyes shining)
Ah, it is beautiful. Thank you, Sister.

Reaction. Morenike watches her wear the scarf over her shoulder and says nothing. Her smile has not moved at all.

The MC, a heavy man in a gold agbada, taps the microphone. Feedback squeals. The guests laugh.

MC
(booming, playful, grinning at the crowd)
Ladies and gentlemen, the husband has one more gift!

Reaction. Gasps. A drumroll starts from the talking drum. Adunni turns, hands to her chest, laughing and shaking her head. Tunde reaches the center of the stage and raises both hands for quiet.

TUNDE
(grinning like a boy, voice warm and playful)
Adunni, close your eyes.

She does, giggling like a girl. Her hands go over her face, fingers parted a little so she can peek. He chuckles and gently moves her hands down.

TUNDE
(laughing)
No peeping, Aya mi.

Tunde nods at the MC. At the far end of the long driveway, the iron gates swing wide. Headlights sweep across the lawn. A gleaming white SUV rolls up the drive, slow and silent, a huge red ribbon tied across its bonnet and a bow on its roof. The paint reflects every fairy light. Guests part to let it through. The crowd screams.

TUNDE
(into the microphone, proud, drawing out each word)
The newest model in Abuja. The first one in the country. It landed last week. Number plate, ADUNNI ONE.

Reaction. Phones rise in a forest of glowing screens. A dozen women scream and clutch each other. The band explodes into a victory rhythm. Adunni opens her eyes, sees the car, and freezes. Both hands fly over her mouth. Her eyes go huge and wet. She makes a small sound behind her palms and begins to cry and laugh at once. She turns and runs, skirt gathered in one fist, and throws herself into Tunde's arms. He lifts her off the ground and spins her.

ADUNNI
(sobbing and laughing, forehead against his neck)
What did I do to deserve you?
TUNDE
(face buried in her braids, voice rough with joy)
You married me.

Guests chant her name in rhythm: "A-DUN-NI! A-DUN-NI!" Fireworks open over the roof in gold and red. Everyone looks up.

Everyone except Morenike.

At the edge of the lawn, two young GUESTS in champagne aso-ebi stand with their backs to her, filming the SUV on their phones, not noticing her behind them.

GUEST THREE
(shouting over the music, thrilled)
Imagine! The older sister is still unmarried, and the younger one is being given cars!
GUEST FOUR
(laughing, nudging her)
Some people carry all the luck!

Reaction. Morenike does not turn. Her shoulders stay square. Her fingers tighten on her handbag strap until the knuckles show. Her lips stay shut, but the muscle at the corner of her jaw jumps once. She begins to clap, one beat too late, in a slow tight rhythm that does not match the band.

In her handbag, her phone vibrates against her ribs. She slides it out a few inches. The screen reads LANDLORD. She stares at the name for a long moment, then presses the red button and returns the phone to her bag without a change of expression. She keeps clapping.

TUNDE steps off the stage, scanning the crowd, and finds her. He crosses the lawn with an easy, open stride. His arm is still around Adunni's waist.

TUNDE
(warm, sincere, pressing a thick envelope into her palm and closing her fingers around it)
For your rent, Sister. Adunni told me.

Reaction. Morenike looks down at the envelope. Then she looks up, and her face has transformed: bright, grateful, a little teary.

MORENIKE
(soft, tearful, patting his arm twice)
God bless you, Tunde. You are too kind.

Reaction. He nods warmly and returns to his wife. The moment he turns away, the warmth drains out of Morenike's face like water out of a basin. She looks at the envelope in her hand. It sits there as heavy and warm as a coal.

Fireworks continue to blossom above the roof. Gold light and red light wash over the faces of the guests. Adunni spins in Tunde's arms, laughing, her gele crooked, tears and mascara on her cheeks. The white SUV stands in the middle of the lawn like a trophy. In its glossy door, we see Morenike's reflection, small, distorted and gold-lit. She is smiling, politely, brightly. Then the smile goes out. We hold on the face with no smile, with the fireworks flickering across it. It is not angry. It is very calm.

Director's note. PLANT: the whispers about the child, and the envelope for rent. Both return when Morenike chooses her weapon.

2INT. ADEBISI MANSION, MASTER BEDROOM — NIGHT
Setting. Past midnight. The party is long over, and the house is silent except for the grandfather clock downstairs and the far-off hum of the generator. The master bedroom is cream and gold: a wide bed with silk sheets, heavy drapes, a dressing table with a round mirror, a bedside lamp casting a small warm circle. The window is open a hand's width, and the curtains lift gently in the night air. A white lace gown hangs on the wardrobe door like a ghost.

TUNDE is asleep on his back in the wide bed, one arm stretched across the pillow where Adunni's head should be. His face is peaceful, mouth slightly open, cufflinks still on the nightstand.

ADUNNI sits at the dressing table in a satin nightdress, her gele removed and her braids loose across her shoulders. In the mirror, her face looks tired and soft. She removes her earrings one at a time and places them in a velvet tray. She hesitates. Then she opens the carved jewellery box and lifts out the top tray of gold. At the bottom, beneath a layer of tissue paper, lies a sealed hospital envelope, its paper creased and softened with years of handling.

She does not open it. She holds it flat against her chest with both hands. Her eyes close. Her lips move without sound, a prayer or a name. A single tear slides down her cheek and she lets it fall onto the envelope.

Reaction. She opens her eyes and looks at Tunde's sleeping face in the mirror. Her expression is full of love and an aching tenderness. She breathes in slowly, wipes her cheek with the heel of her hand, and tucks the envelope back beneath the tissue. She covers it with the gold tray. She closes the lid with great care so that it makes no sound.

She rises, walks silently to the door and steps into the corridor. A single lamp glows at the far end. Across the hall is a closed door, painted white. An Ankara cloth in blue and yellow hangs over its brass handle like a small flag. It has hung there so long that it is slightly faded.

Adunni touches the cloth. Her fingers rest on it for a long moment. She lifts them away, folds her hands, and walks past toward the stairs.

Director's note. PLANT: the envelope and the Ankara cloth. Do not show the envelope's contents. The cloth is not lifted until the final scene.
3INT. MORENIKE'S FLAT, WUSE — NIGHT
Setting. Midnight, forty minutes after the party. A cramped second-floor one-bedroom flat in an ageing block. Peeling cream paint with water stains in the corners. A wobbling ceiling fan clicks with each turn. A faded floral sofa, a plastic dining table with two mismatched chairs, a small wardrobe with a cracked mirror. On the wall, a framed photograph of Iya Agba with two small girls in white dresses. Sodium orange light seeps through the louvre windows from the street lamp outside. A neighbour's gospel music thumps faintly through the wall. A landlord's notice lies face-up on the floor where it was slid under the door.

The door unlocks. MORENIKE enters. She kicks off her shoes without bending, and one hits the wall. She drops her handbag and the envelope onto the plastic table. She does not turn on the light. She stands in the orange glow with her arms limp at her sides and her wig slightly crooked.

On the sofa, MICHAEL is stretched out with one foot on the armrest, a plastic bag of suya open on his chest, a toothpick between his teeth. He has his own key. He lifts his head with a lazy grin.

MICHAEL
(easy, teasing, in a rich smooth drawl)
You left that party early.
MORENIKE
(flat, tired, not looking at him, pulling off her earrings)
They gave her a car, Michael. A car with her name on the plate.
MICHAEL
(shrugging, chewing)
So? Your brother-in-law is rich.
MORENIKE
(turning at last, voice low and hard)
Do you know what a guest said while I stood there? "Some people carry all the luck." And me? I carry rent.

She picks up the envelope Tunde gave her. She opens the flap and counts the notes with a thumb, quick and bitter. She throws the envelope onto the table, where it slides and stops at the edge.

Reaction. Michael sits up slowly, the grin gone from his face. He watches her the way you watch a pot beginning to boil.

MORENIKE
(low, rising, hand pointing at the window as if the party were outside)
Five years, and there is no child in that house. His mother prays for one in front of two hundred guests. And Adunni stands there, smiling, holding her stomach like it is empty.
MICHAEL
(carefully, spreading his hands)
Everybody knows it is her problem. She told the whole family herself.
MORENIKE
(slowly, as if tasting the words)
Yes. She did.

Reaction. The room goes quiet. The fan clicks. Morenike turns toward the louvre window and stands very still with her hands behind her back. Orange light cuts across her face in stripes. She does not look angry. She looks calm, which is worse. We watch her thinking.

MORENIKE
(very quiet, to the glass)
A man like Tunde needs a push. A man like him believes whatever is shown to him. If I cannot enjoy a happy marriage, Adunni must not keep hers.
MICHAEL
(amused, leaning forward, elbows on knees)
What push?
MORENIKE
(turning, a small cold smile appearing)
A scandal.

She sits on the arm of the sofa, takes out her phone, and scrolls past the landlord's name to a contact saved as BAYO, CHOIR. She presses call. It rings. It rings a long time.

MORENIKE
(into phone, sweet and bright, a different woman entirely)
Bayo. Do you still want that job?

Reaction. Michael raises one eyebrow and leans back, settling in to watch the show. Morenike's reflection in the black screen of the window smiles back at her.

4INT. BUKA CANTEEN — NIGHT
Setting. A cheap roadside canteen near the Wuse market, open late. Plastic tables with peeling vinyl cloths, mismatched plastic chairs, a ceiling fan with a missing blade, a wall calendar from a soft drink company. A small TV bolted to the wall plays a football replay with the sound down. A woman at the counter ladles stew into plastic plates. The light is harsh white from a single tube. The smell of palm oil and fried plantain hangs in the air.

BAYO sits alone at a corner table in a faded polo shirt, hunched over a plate of beans and bread, eating quickly with a bent spoon. He is lean and tired, with a chipped front tooth. His phone, the screen cracked, sits beside the plate.

MORENIKE enters, scanning the room with distaste. She picks her way between the tables, holding her handbag against her stomach, and sits across from Bayo. She places a paper bag on the table with fingertips. She slides a folded CV beside it.

BAYO
(surprised, mouth full, then wiping his lips and sitting up straight)
Madam Morenike! Ah, good evening, ma.
MORENIKE
(brisk, pleasant, not touching the table)
Open it.

He peeks inside the paper bag. He pulls out a navy suit, still in its plastic. His eyes widen. He runs his thumb along the fabric, his face stunned.

BAYO
(breathless, half-laughing)
Madam, why are you doing this for me?
MORENIKE
(calm, leaning in)
Tunde Adebisi needs a personal assistant. You will be very good at it.
BAYO
(lowering his voice, a cautious grin spreading)
And what do I do for you?
MORENIKE
(smiling, her head tilted, her eyes completely still)
Be kind to my sister. Very kind.

In the wardrobe. Wait here.

9INT. ADEBISI MANSION, FOYER AND KITCHEN — DAY

The staff have the afternoon off.

BAYO
(polite, bright, bowing a little)
Madam, Oga asked me to drop these documents.
ADUNNI
(stepping aside, warm)
Come in. Wait, let me give you something.

BAYO

ADUNNI

BAYO

Do you know you are very beautiful?

ADUNNI

What are you doing? Let go of my hand.

ADUNNI

ADUNNI
(shaking, finger pointing at the door, voice thin and furious)
Get out of my house.

10INT. MORENIKE'S FLAT — NIGHT
Setting. Night, a few hours later. The flat is dark except for the cold blue glow of a laptop on the plastic table and a single desk lamp aimed at the keyboard. The ceiling fan clicks. The street lamp paints orange stripes across the wall. A mosquito coil smokes in a saucer.

MORENIKE sits hunched in front of the laptop, headphones on, her wig removed and her natural hair in small flat twists. She is watching the footage from the camcorder, which is connected by a cable. On the screen, we see fragments: the foyer, Bayo's hand on Adunni's wrist, the plate shattering. We hear them in the tinny leak from her headphones. She watches without expression, her chin resting on one fist.

She scrubs the timeline forward, stops, and pauses on a single frame: Adunni standing frozen, her wrist in Bayo's hand, her mouth open, her eyes huge. In that frame, it could be shock, or invitation.

Morenike drags the clip into an editing window. She trims the beginning. She trims the end. She cuts the moment Adunni says "Let go of my hand." She deletes the audio track. She cuts out the sound of the plate. She leaves twelve seconds of silent footage. She plays it. It looks like a woman who does not resist.

She exports the clip. A progress bar crosses the screen. At 100 percent, she exhales through her nose, a small satisfied click of the tongue.

She disconnects the camcorder and ejects the mini-DV cassette. She holds it up to the lamp, turns it, looks at the handwritten date on the label. Her lips press together as she considers. Then she crosses to the bed, drags an old brown suitcase from underneath it, unzips it, and finds a corner of the lining. With a nail file she unpicks a few stitches, slides the cassette into the gap, and runs her finger along the seam. She zips the suitcase and pushes it back under the bed with her foot.

Reaction. She sits on the edge of the bed, looks at the suitcase for a moment, and picks up her phone. She sends the twelve-second clip to herself and then to a second number. Her thumbs move steadily. Her face is a mask.

Director's note. PLANT: the cassette in the suitcase lining. Keep the shot on it for a full beat. This is the film's biggest payoff.
11INT. ADEBISI MOTORS, TUNDE'S OFFICE — DAY
Setting. Late morning in Tunde's glass-walled corner office. The air conditioning hums. Outside the glass wall, employees move without noise.

TUNDE stands behind his desk, buttoning his jacket. MORENIKE sits in the visitor's chair opposite, back straight, hands folded over her handbag, eyes already glistening.

MORENIKE
(soft, trembling, not looking up)
I did not want to be the one to show you this.

She slides her phone across the desk. Tunde looks at her for a beat, picks it up, presses play.

The silent clip plays: Adunni frozen in the kitchen, Bayo's hand around her wrist, his face inches from hers. Her mouth open. Her eyes wide. No sound. No struggle.

Tunde watches. His jaw tightens. His chest rises and falls. He plays it again. He plays it a third time, one finger pressed against his lips. He sets the phone down very gently on the desk, as if it might break.

TUNDE
(hoarse, barely audible, staring at the blank screen)
Why is she standing there?
MORENIKE
(careful, low, as if afraid)
Tunde, I do not know. Maybe it was shock. Maybe... I do not want to say.
TUNDE
(turning, eyes hard)
Say it.
MORENIKE
(looking up, tears sliding, a tremor in her voice)
She did not pull away. Twelve seconds, Tunde. I counted.

Reaction. Tunde grips the edge of the desk until his knuckles go grey. A vein rises on his temple. His whole body goes rigid. His eyes squeeze shut. TUNDE
(into phone, voice shaking with fury, spacing every word)
Commissioner. Yes. I want a man arrested. Tonight. He is my employee, Bayo Adeleke. He put his hands on my wife.

MORENIKE
(soft, tentative)
Is it not better to handle this quietly?
TUNDE
(lifting his head, shouting)
Quietly? That man stood in my house!

Her face: relief, and underneath it, the first thin line of fear. She reaches out and slowly retrieves her phone. She stares at the black screen, at her own faint reflection, and her lips move in a single silent word that we cannot read.

12INT. GWAGWALADA DIVISIONAL POLICE STATION, CELL — NIGHT

BAYO
(quiet, serious, looking at the water)

OBI

(his voice shaking, his hand tightening on the phone)

MORENIKE
(flat, cold, tilting her head)
You were always a fool, Tunde.
TUNDE
(raw, rising)
You are a wicked woman. You will die in this prison.
MORENIKE
(quietly, her eyes steady)
Then I will die as the woman you chose.

Reaction. The words hit him. His face contorts. He slams the receiver down onto the counter, and it clatters. He stands up so fast the stool scrapes. He grips the edge of the counter with both hands, his whole body shaking, tears running down his face. He opens his mouth, closes it, and finds he has nothing left to say. Morenike watches him through the glass without moving, without remorse, her hand still holding the phone. When he finally turns and stumbles toward the door, she does not look away first. She watches him all the way out.

43INT. ABUJA HIGH COURT, COURTROOM 4 — DAY
Setting. A packed courtroom, mid-morning. Dark wood panelling, a raised bench with the national coat of arms, a witness stand to one side, a long wooden dock, rows of crowded benches. A ceiling fan turns slowly. A TV monitor on a trolley stands to one side for exhibits. Grey light falls through high windows; warm lamps pool over the bench. The gallery is full: reporters, relatives, curious onlookers, Obi in his navy agbada, Mama Bayo in a clean wrapper, Segun with his satchel on his knee, and in the last row, ADUNNI in a deep-indigo suit with DR. DAWSON beside her. A low murmur runs through the room.

HON. JUSTICE AMINAT BELLO presides, in a black robe and white collar bands, reading glasses low on her nose. MORENIKE sits in the dock in a plain dress, her hair neatly tied, her back straight, her face composed into a polite blank. BARRISTER IBE stands to cross-examine, a file in one hand.

BARRISTER IBE
(smooth, theatrical, pacing)
Sergeant Ajinibu, you admit you were in debt?
AJINIBU
(hunched in the witness stand, hoarse)
Yes.
BARRISTER IBE
(turning to the gallery, one finger raised)
So you would say anything to protect yourself?
AJINIBU
(lifting his head, almost pleading)
I recorded her voice, my lord. I recorded every call.

A recording plays through the court speakers, thin and distorted. We hear Morenike's voice, low, cold and unmistakable: "He talks too much. Make him quiet."

Reaction. A gasp ripples across the gallery. Heads turn. A woman puts her hand over her mouth. Barrister Ibe stops pacing. He turns to Morenike and bends to whisper. She shrugs without looking at him. Her face does not change.

The prosecutor, a tall man in a grey suit, rises.

PROSECUTOR
(formal, ringing)
The state calls Mr. Michael Okpara.

The double doors open. MICHAEL walks in, groomed and calm, in a dark suit and polished shoes. He walks to the witness stand without looking at the dock. He takes the oath. His voice is smooth.

MORENIKE half rises from the dock. For the first time, her composure splits. Her eyes blaze.

MORENIKE
(standing, loud, shaking)
You rat!
JUDGE
(sharp, striking the bench)
Order!

Reaction. A bailiff touches Morenike's shoulder. She sits slowly, breathing hard, her eyes fixed on Michael with pure venom. He does not look at her.

MICHAEL
(turning to the court, easy, regretful)
My lord, I was Madam Adebisi's contractor and her partner. I am here under an immunity agreement. She ordered the killing. She told me, in bed, three times. She laughed about it.

He produces a small voice recorder and a bound ledger and hands them to the clerk. A long hour passes in quick cuts: Barrister Ibe cross-examines, his tone sharper, then more desperate, loosening his collar. Michael answers each question in the same calm voice. Barrister Ibe's confidence cracks and fades. He finally sits.

The prosecutor lifts a small black cassette in a clear evidence bag.

PROSECUTOR
(to the bench)
My lord, Exhibit Nine. A cassette recovered by the accused's own husband.

The cassette plays on the monitor. The whole courtroom watches the same image Tunde watched: Morenike's face filling the screen, leaning toward the lens.

MORENIKE
(on tape, whispering)
Smile, my sister.

Reaction. The gallery turns as one to the dock. Morenike does not move. Her face is carved from stone. In the last row, Adunni's hand finds Dr. Dawson's and grips it.

The prosecutor lifts Bayo's cracked phone in its bag.

PROSECUTOR
(quiet, weighty)
Exhibit Ten. Recovered from the victim's room by Mr. Segun Alade, with a warrant.

The phone is played in open court. A young voice, bright and a little nervous, fills the room.

BAYO
(V.O., recording)
If something happens to me, the woman's name is Morenike Ogundele.

Reaction. In the gallery, MAMA BAYO closes her eyes. Her lips move. Obi puts one big hand on her shoulder. In the dock, Morenike's composure finally cracks. Her mouth moves. No sound comes out. Her hands, folded in her lap, begin to tremble, and she presses them hard against her knees.

JUDGE
(laying down her pen, looking over her glasses, grave and clear)
The accused is found guilty on all counts. The court sentences Morenike Ogundele to life imprisonment.

Reaction. A sound rolls through the courtroom, part gasp, part sob, part relief. Two WARDERS step to the dock and take Morenike by the arms. She stands. She does not resist. At the rail of the dock she turns, scanning the faces, searching. She finds Adunni in the last row. Their eyes meet across the whole length of the room. Morenike's lips shape two words, silently.

MORENIKE
(mouthing)
Ọmọ ìyá.

Reaction. Adunni does not look away. She does not nod. She does not answer. Her eyes fill and a single tear runs down her cheek, but her face remains still. Morenike is led out through the side door. It closes behind her.

44EXT. COURTHOUSE STEPS — DAY
Setting. Midday on the wide stone steps of the Abuja High Court. The sky is bright and high with a few white clouds after the morning's grey. Crowds of reporters and onlookers drift down the steps. Camera flashes pop. A street vendor sells bottled water at the bottom. Warm sun, the wind lifting dust and scraps of paper across the plaza. A pigeon walks along a railing.

ADUNNI stands alone at the top of the steps, one hand resting on the stone railing, her face turned toward the sun. DR. DAWSON comes up behind her and wraps one arm around her shoulders. She leans her head against him.

ADUNNI
(quiet, looking out over the plaza)
I wanted her to ask for forgiveness.
DR. DAWSON
(gently)
Maybe one day she will.
ADUNNI
(turning her head to look at him)
And if she does?
DR. DAWSON
(steadily, holding her gaze)
Then you will decide that day. Not today.

Reaction. She looks at him for a moment and nods. She lays her head on his shoulder and closes her eyes. Behind them, the crowd breaks and flows. The sun comes out fully from behind the building and lights the two of them in warm gold. A breeze lifts the edge of her headscarf. She breathes out a long, slow breath.

45INT. MALLAM GARBA'S CAR-PARTS SHOP — DUSK
Setting. Closing time at the car-parts shop. Orange dusk light slants through the half-closed shutter. The shelves are cast in long shadows. The old TV is off. The air smells of oil and dust. Outside, horns fade as the road empties. A moth circles the bare bulb. The sound of the city is soft and far.

TUNDE sweeps the oil-stained floor alone, slowly, in long tired strokes. He is thin and stooped. His polo shirt hangs loose on his shoulders. A first drop of rain patters against the tin roof. Then another. He stops sweeping. He lifts his head, listening. The rain thickens, drumming across the roof.

He walks to the shutter and peers through the gap. In the street, a woman with a cloth bag in her hand walks quickly through the rain with her head down. For one second, his heart stops. He takes a step forward. She passes under a street lamp. It is not her. His shoulders fall.

He turns and sits on an upturned crate. The bulb flickers. His eyes lose focus. The shop dissolves into warm gold light.

FLASHBACKS. ADUNNI laughing in the kitchen with soup on his sleeve, shoulders shaking, the sound clear and bright. ADUNNI's face on the night of the birthday car, covering her mouth, eyes shining. ADUNNI standing at the gate, drenched, saying quietly: "I never begged." The flashbacks speed up, overlap, then slow.

The rain hammers. TUNDE puts his face in his hands. His shoulders shake. A sob breaks out of him, then another, and he cannot stop.

TUNDE
(broken, over and over, barely audible)
Adunni. I am sorry. I am so sorry.

Reaction. The only sound is the rain on the tin roof and his crying. The bulb buzzes. Slowly the camera rises through the roof, over the dripping tin, over the wet rooftops, over the lights of Abuja coming on one by one. Far across the city, in a lit window in Asokoro, a woman sits beside a sleeping child, her hand resting lightly on its back. The rain falls between them.

FADE TO BLACK.

46INT. AGBA ASO-OKE INDUSTRIES, WORKSHOP — NIGHT
Setting. Night in the old workshop in Abeokuta, preserved beside the new factory. A long tin-roofed hall of wooden looms in rows, spools of indigo, gold and wine thread glowing in the lamplight, the floor swept clean. Moonlight comes in through the louvre windows in silver bars. The air smells of cloth and old wood. A single lamp is lit near the far wall, where a large portrait of Iya Agba hangs. The silence is deep. Far off, a night bird calls.

ADUNNI walks slowly through the rows of silent looms in a soft cream wrap, trailing her fingertips along the wooden frames. She stops before the portrait of IYA AGBA. The old woman gazes down with bright, patient eyes. The light on the portrait, for the first time, falls from the right side, not the left. Adunni does not notice.

She turns toward a tall wooden cabinet beside the portrait. She opens its lower door and kneels. She moves aside a bolt of cloth and a basket of spindles. At the back, wedged in a corner, she finds a small tin box she has never seen, rusted at the edges. She sets it on the floor and opens the lid.

Inside lie a small brass key and a second cream envelope. On the front, in the same shaky handwriting as her own letter, is a single line: For Morenike.

Reaction. Adunni stares at the envelope. She reads the name once. She reads it a second time. Her hand trembles slightly as she touches the corner of the paper. She does not break the seal.

ADUNNI
(whispering, to the portrait)
Mama. Even now, you were planning for her.

Reaction. She holds the letter against her chest for a long moment, looking up at her grandmother's face. Then she slides it carefully into her handbag. She puts the key in her pocket. She rises, brushes dust from her knees, and walks slowly toward the lamp. She turns it off. The workshop goes dark except for the moonlight on the looms. In the dark, the portrait seems, for just a second, to smile.
