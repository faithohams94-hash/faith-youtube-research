Overview

Logline

On the night of Adunni's birthday, a jealous elder sister watches her younger sister receive the biggest car in Abuja and decides to destroy her marriage. She succeeds, and then marries the husband herself. But every lie she tells is recorded somewhere, and years later the sister she threw into the rain owns his mansions.

Synopsis

Adunni Ogundele-Adebisi is married to Tunde Adebisi, the owner of the biggest auto company in Abuja. Their marriage is loving, but after five years there is still no child. Years ago Adunni quietly told the doctor, and then the whole family, that the fault was hers. The truth is that Tunde has a low sperm count. She took the blame to protect his pride, and she never told him otherwise.

At Adunni's birthday party, Tunde surprises her with the newest car in Abuja. Her older sister, Morenike, unmarried and consumed by envy, watches from the lawn and decides that night to take the marriage apart and take Tunde for herself. She plants a charming stranger, Bayo, as Tunde's personal assistant, and hides a camera in the house. Bayo makes a move on Adunni. Morenike edits the footage so it looks like Adunni did not resist, and shows it to Tunde. Tunde jails Bayo. When Bayo threatens to expose Morenike, she pays corrupt policemen to silence him, and he dies in custody.

Morenike then uses the weapon Adunni handed her. In front of Tunde and his mother, she says that Adunni cannot give a child. Tunde throws Adunni out into the rain. Adunni drives to a mansion, a workshop and a house her late grandmother secretly left her. Her grandmother raised both sisters, and she left everything to Adunni because she had seen Morenike's nature when Morenike was a child. Within a few years Adunni builds a luxury aso-oke empire. Morenike marries Tunde.

The marriage poisons itself. A blackmailing policeman drains Morenike. Her lover, Michael, swindles Tunde and then swindles Morenike. A prisoner Bayo befriended spends his freedom hunting the truth, and an investigator finds Bayo's secret phone. Tunde's collapsing company takes a rescue loan from Adunni's company, not knowing whose it is. When he cannot repay, Adunni buys out his creditors and becomes the owner of his mansions. While packing, Tunde finds a cassette in Morenike's suitcase. It shows him everything he got wrong.

Format and Tone

Feature-length drama written for YouTube release. Target runtime 95 to 105 minutes. Dialogue is in English with short Yoruba phrases (translated in the script). The tone is Nollywood melodrama with a thriller engine: big emotions, heavy silences, cliffhangers before every ad break, and reversals that arrive just after the audience feels certain.

Setting

Abuja, Nigeria, present day, covering roughly five years. Principal locations: the Adebisi mansion in Maitama; Adebisi Motors headquarters; the Ogundele family mansion in Asokoro and Agba Aso-Oke Industries; a divisional police station in Gwagwalada; the FCT Police Command; a CCTV repair shop in Wuse; a car-parts shop in Utako.

Directing Rule for Shocks

Each time the audience believes it knows what happens next, give it something else. The script does this by building a clear expectation, letting it settle, and then breaking it with a fact that has been quietly planted earlier. No twist arrives without a plant. The Shock Map in the next section lists them.

The Shock Map

Each entry lists what the audience expects, what actually happens, and where the reveal was planted. Scenes are named, not numbered, so the list stays correct if scenes move.

SHOCK 1 (the birthday party). Expect: the biggest night of Adunni's life is a celebration. Get: it is the night her sister decides to destroy her. The camera finds Morenike's reflection in the car door, smiling and then not smiling.

SHOCK 2 (the edited clip). Expect: Tunde sees the whole truth. Get: he sees twelve silent seconds in which Adunni appears to freeze, and the lie takes hold. Plant: Morenike tucks the original cassette into her suitcase lining.

SHOCK 3 (the toilet block). Expect: Morenike's accomplice will blackmail her later. Get: she has him killed within days, and the order is a single quiet sentence.

SHOCK 4 (the hidden mansion). Expect: Adunni, thrown out of her home, is poor and alone. Get: she owns a mansion with staff, a workshop and a house. Plant: the phone call to Baba Sule in the garden.

SHOCK 5 (the flashback in the hall). Expect: Adunni cannot have children. Get: Adunni is fertile. Tunde is the one who cannot. She took the blame. Plant: the sealed envelope in her jewellery box.

SHOCK 6 (the blackmail and the swindle). Expect: Morenike has won. Get: the policeman who killed Bayo now owns her, and then her lover robs her too.

SHOCK 7 (the loan). Expect: Adunni will forgive Tunde and rescue him for free. Get: she grants the loan on a sixty-day clause.

SHOCK 8 (Bayo's old room). Expect: the evidence is a recording Morenike can destroy. Get: Bayo hid a phone in a wall of his room before he died, and it recorded her.

SHOCK 9 (the arrest). Expect: Tunde catches Morenike stealing and the story ends in a fraud arrest. Get: the police arrive for murder, and Tunde learns for the first time that Bayo is dead.

SHOCK 10 (the boardroom). Expect: Tunde knows who is lending him money. Get: he has been begging his own ex-wife, and she has bought out every creditor he has.

SHOCK 11 (the suitcase). Expect: the story is over once Morenike is jailed. Get: Tunde finds the original cassette and learns he threw out an innocent wife. Payoff: the tucked-away cassette from the edit scene.

SHOCK 12 (the TV in the shop). Expect: Adunni lives alone with her grief. Get: she has a husband, Dr. Dawson, and triplets, and Tunde realises in one second that it was never her.

SHOCK 13 (the courtroom). Expect: Morenike's lawyer will find a loophole. Get: Michael turns state witness, and Bayo's own phone speaks from the grave.

SHOCK 14 (the post-credit scene). Expect: the story is closed. Get: the grandmother left a second sealed letter, addressed to Morenike.

Character Bible

How to use the prompts. Each character below has a ready-to-paste image prompt. To keep faces consistent across shots, generate one hero portrait per character first, then use it as the reference image for every later prompt. Add this style line to the end of every prompt:

STYLE LINE: Photorealistic cinematic still, Nigerian feature film, natural skin texture with visible pores, shallow depth of field, soft film grain, 35mm lens, warm Abuja color grade, 16:9.

Principal Characters

ADUNNI OGUNDELE-ADEBISI

Role. Protagonist. Wife of Tunde, younger sister of Morenike, secret heiress, later founder of Agba Aso-Oke Industries.

Age. 30 at the start. 35 at the end.

Appearance. Slim, graceful, dark-brown skin, soft round face with a gap-toothed smile, long neck, hair in neat braids or a gele. In the marriage she wears gentle pastel lace and simple gold studs. After the exile she wears sharp, deep-colored aso-oke tailoring and a single heavy ring. In the boardroom she is composed and rarely raises her voice.

Nature. Warm, humble, proud in a quiet way. She will give up her reputation for love, and then be unmovable once trust is gone. She never uses the word revenge.

Portrait of a 30-year-old Nigerian woman, dark brown skin, soft oval face, small gap between her front teeth, slender neck, hair in neat box braids, wearing a champagne lace gown and simple gold stud earrings, gentle warm smile, fairy lights glowing behind her at a garden party.
MORENIKE OGUNDELE

Role. Antagonist. Elder sister of Adunni. Unmarried. Later Tunde's second wife and then a convicted murderer.

Age. 37 at the start. 42 at the end.

Her clothes are expensive imitations at the start (well-cut but a little too tight, a handbag with a worn corner). As Mrs. Adebisi she wears real designer wear that does not suit her. Nature. Intelligent, patient, charming in public. She believes she is owed a life. She never raises her voice, even when she orders a killing.

Screenplay

Act One: The Birthday

1EXT. ADEBISI MANSION, GARDEN, MAITAMA, ABUJA — NIGHT (ADUNNI'S BIRTHDAY PARTY)

Fairy lights cover every tree. A live highlife band plays under a white marquee. Two hundred guests in lace and agbada fill the lawn. A four-tier cake reads HAPPY 30TH, ADUNNI.

ADUNNI, in a champagne lace gown, moves between tables laughing and hugging. TUNDE watches her from across the lawn like a man who still cannot believe his luck.

Two older women in matching gele whisper near the buffet.

GUEST ONE
Tunde adores that girl. Five years, and not once has he looked elsewhere.
GUEST TWO
Five years and no child, though. That poor Chief must be praying day and night.

CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI takes the microphone. The band stops.

CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI
Tonight my son celebrates his wife. I say only this. May next year's party hold the laughter of a child.

Applause. Adunni's smile holds, barely.

TUNDE
(softly, taking the microphone)
Mama, tonight is for my wife.
CHIEF MRS. ADEBISI
I only pray, my son.

A gate near the road opens. MORENIKE enters in a mustard dress, a small gift bag in her hand. Adunni runs across the lawn and wraps her in a hug.

ADUNNI
Sister! You came!
MORENIKE
Would I miss my baby sister's birthday?

The MC taps his microphone.

MC
Ladies and gentlemen, the husband has one more gift.

Tunde takes the stage. The band begins a slow drumroll.

TUNDE
Adunni, close your eyes.

She does, giggling. Tunde nods. The iron gates at the end of the drive swing wide. Headlights sweep across the lawn. A gleaming white SUV rolls up the drive, a red ribbon across its bonnet. The crowd screams.

TUNDE
(into microphone)
The newest model in Abuja. The first one in the country. It landed last week. Number plate, ADUNNI ONE.

Phones go up. Adunni opens her eyes, gasps, and covers her mouth with both hands. She runs to Tunde.

ADUNNI
What did I do to deserve you?
TUNDE
You married me.

Guests chant her name. Fireworks open over the roof.

At the edge of the lawn, two GUESTS stand near Morenike, not noticing her.

GUEST THREE
Imagine. The older sister is still unmarried, and the younger one is being given cars.
GUEST FOUR
Some people carry all the luck.

Morenike claps one beat too late. In her handbag, her phone buzzes. The screen reads LANDLORD. She declines the call and keeps clapping.

Tunde crosses to her through the crowd and presses an envelope into her palm.

TUNDE
For your rent, Sister. Adunni told me.
MORENIKE
(warm)
God bless you, Tunde. You are too kind.

The envelope lies in her hand like an insult.

Fireworks light the sky. Adunni spins in Tunde's arms. In the glossy white door of the SUV, we see Morenike's reflection: smiling, then not smiling.

Director's note. PLANT: the whispers about the child, and the envelope for rent. Both return when Morenike chooses her weapon.
ỌMỌ ÌYÁ MI. My Mother's Child.
2INT. ADEBISI MANSION, MASTER BEDROOM — NIGHT

Tunde sleeps, one arm over the pillow where Adunni lay. ADUNNI sits at the dressing table in her nightdress. From the bottom of her jewellery box she lifts a sealed hospital envelope, soft with age. She does not open it. She holds it to her chest, then puts it back and covers it with gold.

Across the corridor, a door with an Ankara cloth hung over the handle. A nursery that has never been used. She touches the cloth and walks past.

Director's note. PLANT: the envelope. Do not show its contents.
3INT. MORENIKE'S FLAT — NIGHT

A cramped one-bedroom flat, a ceiling fan that wobbles. MORENIKE enters at midnight, kicks off her shoes, and drops the envelope onto the table. A hard-faced notice from the landlord lies on the floor where it was slipped under the door.

MICHAEL is already inside, lying on her sofa with a bag of suya. He has his own key.

MICHAEL
You left that party early.
MORENIKE
They gave her a car, Michael. A car with her name on the plate.
MICHAEL
So? Your brother-in-law is rich.
MORENIKE
Do you know what a guest said while I stood there? "Some people carry all the luck." And me? I carry rent.

She picks up the envelope Tunde gave her. Counts it. Throws it on the table.

MORENIKE
Five years, and there is no child in that house. His mother prays for one in front of two hundred guests. And Adunni stands there, smiling, holding her stomach like it is empty.
MICHAEL
Everybody knows it is her problem. She told the whole family herself.
MORENIKE
(slowly)
Yes. She did.

She walks to the window. Below, the street is dark and dirty. In her reflection, her face does not look angry. It looks calm. That is worse.

MORENIKE
A man like Tunde needs a push. A man like him believes whatever is shown to him. If I cannot enjoy a happy marriage, Adunni must not keep hers.
MICHAEL
(amused)
What push?
MORENIKE
A scandal.

She takes out her phone and scrolls to a name from her old church choir.

MORENIKE
(into phone)
Bayo. Do you still want that job?
4INT. BUKA CANTEEN — NIGHT

A cheap canteen, plastic chairs, the sound of a TV. BAYO eats beans with a spoon bent from use. MORENIKE slides a CV and a paper bag of folded clothes across the table. Inside the bag is a navy suit.

BAYO
Madam, why are you doing this for me?
MORENIKE
Tunde Adebisi needs a personal assistant. You will be very good at it.
BAYO
And what do I do for you?
MORENIKE
(smiling)
Be kind to my sister. Very kind.

Bayo laughs, thinking he understands. He does not.

5EXT. ADEBISI MANSION, GARDEN — DAY

ADUNNI walks among the hibiscus with a phone to her ear.

ADUNNI
(into phone)
Baba Sule, pay the weavers first. Always the weavers first.
BABA SULE
(V.O.)
Ma, the bank is asking whose name is on the deed.
ADUNNI
Nobody's name is to be spoken. Mama said so. Not until the day comes.

A shadow in the hedge. MORENIKE has arrived early and has heard the last words. She steps out smiling.

MORENIKE
Which deed?
ADUNNI
My tailor. He is behind on rent.
MORENIKE
You and your charity cases.

She kisses Adunni's cheek. Adunni does not see her sister's smile freeze and then recover.

Director's note. PLANT: the deed, the weavers, the name nobody speaks.
6INT. ADEBISI MANSION, KITCHEN — DAY

MORENIKE sits at the kitchen island. ADUNNI chops vegetables.

MORENIKE
There is a boy. Bayo. His father is dead. He has five siblings and a mother who cannot walk.
ADUNNI
Ah. God.
MORENIKE
Tunde needs an assistant. Just a word from you.
ADUNNI
(soft)
I will speak to him tonight.

Cut to the bedroom. Tunde, in bed, yields in two sentences to his wife's pleading voice.

TUNDE
For you, Adunni. Anything for you.
7INT. ADEBISI MOTORS, HEADQUARTERS — DAY (MONTAGE)

BAYO in his navy suit: fast, polite, always smiling. He stands when TUNDE enters. He knows every client's name. He brings coffee. He brings good news.

Whenever ADUNNI visits her husband's office, BAYO is there. A door held. A joke told. A sweet bought from the canteen "just because."

TUNDE watches, grateful. ADUNNI laughs. Neither notices that Bayo's eyes never leave her.

8INT. ADEBISI MANSION, LIVING ROOM — DAY

MORENIKE visits with a gift of kola nuts.

MORENIKE
Adunni, that fabric I asked you about, the green one with the gold thread. Is it upstairs?
ADUNNI
In the wardrobe. Wait here.

Adunni goes up. The moment her footsteps fade, Morenike pulls a small camcorder from her bag. She sets it into a crystal vase on the console, angled at the doorway. She presses record. A red light blinks once and holds.

She leans toward the lens and whispers.

MORENIKE
(whispering)
Smile, my sister.

She sits down calmly. Adunni returns with the fabric.

Director's note. PLANT: the camcorder, the cassette, and the whisper. All three return in the suitcase scene.
9INT. ADEBISI MANSION, FOYER AND KITCHEN — DAY

A knock. ADUNNI opens the door. BAYO stands holding a folder.

BAYO
Madam, Oga asked me to drop these documents.
ADUNNI
Come in. Wait, let me give you something.

She hands him a glass of water. He drinks too slowly.

BAYO
Madam, you are too kind. Many people would not do this.
ADUNNI
Are you hungry? I have food.

She serves a plate. As she sets it down, he catches her wrist.

BAYO
(soft)
Do you know you are very beautiful?

She freezes.

ADUNNI
What are you doing? Let go of my hand.
BAYO
Madam, you are lonely in this house. I can see it.
ADUNNI
What are you doing? What do you think you are doing?

She pulls free and throws the plate at his feet. It shatters.

ADUNNI
(shaking)
Get out of my house.

He backs off, smiling, as though he has lost nothing. In the vase on the console, a tiny red light blinks.

10INT. MORENIKE'S FLAT — NIGHT

MORENIKE watches the footage on a laptop, headphones on. We hear it only in fragments: Adunni's voice, a plate breaking. She pauses on the frame where Adunni stands frozen, her wrist in Bayo's hand. She trims the clip: twelve seconds, no sound.

On screen it looks like consent. Morenike exports the file with a small satisfied click.

She ejects the mini-DV cassette from the camcorder. She looks at it for a moment. Then she opens her old suitcase, unpicks a corner of the lining, and tucks the cassette inside. She zips it shut and pushes the suitcase under the bed.

You want a reason that will make it hurt less. There is none.
ADUNNI
Try.
MORENIKE
You had everything. A husband who loved you. A car with your name on the plate. And Iya Agba. Iya Agba left you everything.
ADUNNI
You knew about the house?
MORENIKE
I did not know about the house. I knew she loved you more. I always knew. I watched it from the corner of every room for thirty years.
ADUNNI
She loved you too.
MORENIKE
Then why did she leave me a plot of land in a village that does not exist on any map?

Adunni takes a long breath. When she speaks, her voice does not shake.

ADUNNI
You are my sister. I still love you. I always will. But I will not save you.

She stands.

ADUNNI
I have hired the best lawyer in Abuja. He will make sure you get a fair trial. Nothing more.
MORENIKE
(almost smiling)
A fair trial. For a murderer.
ADUNNI
Ọmọ ìyá mi. My mother's child. Good luck.

Adunni leaves. Morenike stays behind the glass, her expression unreadable.

41INT. VISITING ROOM, KUJE CORRECTIONAL CENTRE — DAY

TUNDE sits where Adunni sat. MORENIKE on the other side. A long silence.

TUNDE
I found the tape.

Morenike's eyes flicker, once.

TUNDE
Smile, my sister. That is what you said to her.
MORENIKE
You were always a fool, Tunde.
TUNDE
You are a wicked woman. You will die in this prison.
MORENIKE
Then I will die as the woman you chose.

Tunde stands, shaking. He grips the edge of the table. Tears run down his face. Morenike watches without moving, without remorse. She does not look away first.

42INT. ABUJA HIGH COURT, COURTROOM 4 — DAY

A packed gallery. HON. JUSTICE AMINAT BELLO presides. MORENIKE sits in the dock, composed and upright. BARRISTER IBE stands to cross-examine.

BARRISTER IBE
Sergeant Ajinibu, you admit you were in debt?
AJINIBU
Yes.
BARRISTER IBE
So you would say anything to protect yourself?
AJINIBU
I recorded her voice, my lord. I recorded every call.

A recording plays. We hear Morenike: "He talks too much. Make him quiet." The gallery gasps.

Barrister Ibe turns to Morenike, whispers. She shrugs.

The prosecutor calls the next witness.

PROSECUTOR
The state calls Mr. Michael Okpara.

MICHAEL walks in, groomed and calm. He does not look at Morenike.

MORENIKE
(standing, loud)
You rat!
JUDGE
Order!
MICHAEL
(to the court)
My lord, I was Madam Adebisi's contractor and her partner. I am here under an immunity agreement. She ordered the killing. She told me, in bed, three times. She laughed about it.

He produces a small voice recorder and a ledger. A long hour of cross-examination, and Barrister Ibe cannot move him.

The prosecutor lifts a small black cassette.

PROSECUTOR
My lord, Exhibit Nine. A cassette recovered by the accused's own husband.

The cassette plays on a monitor. The courtroom watches Morenike lean to the lens and whisper: "Smile, my sister." The gallery turns. Morenike does not move.

Then the prosecutor lifts Bayo's cracked phone.

PROSECUTOR
Exhibit Ten. Recovered from the victim's room by Mr. Segun Alade, with a warrant.

The phone is played in open court. We hear Bayo's own voice: "If something happens to me, the woman's name is Morenike Ogundele."

MAMA BAYO, in the gallery, closes her eyes. Morenike's composure finally cracks. Her mouth moves. No sound.

JUDGE
The accused is found guilty on all counts. The court sentences Morenike Ogundele to life imprisonment.

A warder takes her arm. At the gallery rail she turns to look for Adunni. Adunni is seated in the last row. Morenike mouths two words.

MORENIKE
(mouthing)
Ọmọ ìyá.

Adunni does not look away. She does not answer.

Director's note. PAYOFF: Michael turns on Morenike, the cassette plays, and Bayo speaks from beyond his death.
43EXT. COURTHOUSE STEPS — DAY

ADUNNI stands on the steps with her hand on the railing. DR. DAWSON comes up behind her and wraps his arm around her.

ADUNNI
I wanted her to ask for forgiveness.
DR. DAWSON
Maybe one day she will.
ADUNNI
And if she does?
DR. DAWSON
Then you will decide that day. Not today.

She leans her head against his shoulder. The sun comes out behind the courthouse.

44INT. MALLAM GARBA'S CAR-PARTS SHOP — DUSK

Closing time. TUNDE sweeps the floor alone. Rain begins to patter against the tin roof. He stops sweeping.

He sees the street through a gap in the shutter: a woman carrying a cloth bag, head down. For one second he thinks it is her. It is not.

He sits on an upturned crate. Flashbacks, in warm gold light:

ADUNNI laughing in the kitchen with soup on his sleeve. ADUNNI's face on the night of the birthday car. ADUNNI standing at the gate, wet, saying: "I never begged."

The tin roof drums. TUNDE puts his face in his hands and sobs.

TUNDE
(broken)
Adunni. I am sorry. I am so sorry.

The camera rises slowly through the roof, over the rain, over the lights of Abuja. Far across the city, in a lit window in Asokoro, a woman sits beside a sleeping child.

FADE TO BLACK.

Post-Credit Scene

45INT. AGBA ASO-OKE INDUSTRIES, WORKSHOP — NIGHT

The looms are silent. ADUNNI stands alone before IYA AGBA's portrait. From the back of a cabinet, behind a bolt of cloth, she finds a small tin box she has never opened. Inside: a key, and a second letter. On the envelope, in shaky handwriting: For Morenike.

She reads the name twice. She does not open it.

ADUNNI
(whispering)
Mama. Even now, you were planning for her.

She slides the letter into her handbag and turns off the lights.

Director's note. PAYOFF: the grandmother's second letter and the unfinished story of the sisters.
