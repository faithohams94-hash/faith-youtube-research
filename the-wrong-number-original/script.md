# THE WRONG NUMBER
## "She Texted the Wrong Number… 10 Years Later, the Stranger Appeared at Her Wedding"

*A Nigerian drama adapted from the original treatment. 58 scenes. About 50 minutes. All dialogue, no narrator. Text messages are spoken as voice-over and shown on screen as chat bubbles. The only text that isn't spoken is short visual notes for the AI scenes.*

---

## CAST

- **CHLOE ADEYEMI** (33 → 43). A beautiful, intelligent woman in Abuja and a trained interior designer who stopped designing. She has learned to apologise even when she has done nothing wrong.
- **KUNLE ADEYEMI** (38). Chloe's husband, a respected businessman. Charming in public and controlling in private. He uses money, criticism and intimidation, and he doesn't believe he's doing anything wrong.
- **EMMA WILLIAMS** (35 → 45). Department manager at an AI production studio in Lagos. Single, witty and blunt. Kind without being sentimental.
- **ADA** (7 → 17) and **TOBI** (4 → 14). Chloe's children.
- **BISI**. Emma's colleague and friend.
- **HALIMA**. Chloe's old university friend.
- **ZAINAB**. Chloe's lawyer.
- **TUNDE BALOGUN**. An architect, warm and patient.
- **MAMA EMMA**. Emma's mother.
- **AUNTY BOLA**. Kunle's elder sister.
- **GUESTS**, **CLIENT**, **COUNSELLOR**, **JUDGE**, **MC**, **KUNLE'S LAWYER**.

## RECURRING DETAILS TO KEEP CONSISTENT

- **Friday, 2:19 PM.** The minute the first reply arrived. Keep the same notification sound every time.
- **"Jollof or fried rice?"** A question that once terrified her and finally sets her free.
- **"Friday Stranger".** The name Chloe saves Emma under.
- **The portfolio.** Dusty on the top shelf at the start, and her business by the end.
- **The blue notebooks.** Chloe's hidden record of every shout and insult, kept under her lipsticks. It becomes her evidence in court.
- **"Sorry."** Chloe says it constantly at the start. By the end she has stopped.
- **"Still here."** Two words that hold ten years together.

## ACT ONE — THE MESSAGE THAT WENT ASTRAY

## SCENE 1 — "JOLLOF OR FRIED RICE?"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE, ABUJA — KITCHEN — FRIDAY, 2:14 PM — YEAR ONE**
*(~0:00–0:59)*

*Abuja, early afternoon. The camera glides through the gates of a large white house in Asokoro, past a manicured lawn and a parked black SUV, into a spotless kitchen. The extractor fan hums. A pot of rice simmers. A woman moves between the stove, the fridge and her children with the careful speed of someone who cannot afford a mistake.*

*A beautiful, expensive kitchen with marble counters, fresh flowers and everything perfect. Everything except the woman standing in it. CHLOE ADEYEMI (33) stirs a pot while ADA (7) does homework at the counter and TOBI (4) pushes a toy car across the floor. Chloe's phone rings: "KUNLE."*

**KUNLE (ON PHONE):** Chloe. I hope you've started preparing my dinner.

**CHLOE:** Yes, darling. I'm just trying to remember which—

**KUNLE (ON PHONE):** You mean you've forgotten what we discussed this morning?

**CHLOE:** No, no, I remember. I just wanted to be sure.

**KUNLE (ON PHONE):** It's amazing how you can spend the whole day in this house and still not remember one simple instruction.

*Click. Chloe stares at the phone.*

**ADA:** *(not looking up)* Mummy, are you in trouble?

**CHLOE:** No, sweetheart. Mummy just forgot something.

**ADA:** You always say sorry to Daddy.

*Chloe freezes. The words land harder than they should.*

**CHLOE:** Finish your homework, darling.

**TOBI:** Mummy, I want jollof!

**CHLOE:** *(a tired smile)* Everybody wants something today.

*She picks up a brand-new phone. The old one died this morning. She types Kunle's number from memory, her fingers trembling. One digit is wrong.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Jollof rice or fried rice? Sorry, I forgot which one you wanted.

*Send. 2:17 PM. She grips the counter and waits. 2:19 PM. Buzz.*

**UNKNOWN (TEXT):** Wrong receiver, but jollof rice with turkey sounds better 😂

*Chloe blinks and looks at the number. Oh no. A stranger.*

**CHLOE:** *(whispering)* Wrong receiver?

*And for the first time all afternoon, a tiny smile escapes her. Then a car pulls up outside. A door slams. The smile vanishes.*

**ADA:** *(quietly)* Daddy's home early.

## SCENE 2 — WRONG RECEIVER
**INT. AI PRODUCTION STUDIO, LAGOS — BREAK ROOM — SAME FRIDAY, 2:20 PM**
*(~0:59–1:42)*

*Lagos, the same afternoon. Horns blare on Admiralty Way as the camera rises to the third floor of a glass building. Inside the AI production studio, young creatives in headphones sit at glowing screens, and someone laughs in the corridor. The break room smells of takeaway jollof and coffee.*

*A busy, colourful studio with screens showing half-finished animated scenes. EMMA WILLIAMS (35) eats rice from a takeaway pack while reviewing a sequence on her tablet. She's grinning at her phone. BISI, her colleague, leans over.*

**BISI:** What's funny?

**EMMA:** Someone just asked me whether to cook jollof or fried rice.

**BISI:** And what did you say?

**EMMA:** Jollof with turkey, obviously.

**BISI:** You're advising strangers now?

**EMMA:** Food is a serious matter, Bisi. Somebody has to help. *(beat)* She sounded scared, though.

**BISI:** Scared? Of rice?

**EMMA:** *(looking at the message again)* "Sorry, I forgot which one you wanted." *(beat)* Who says sorry to their own husband about rice?

**BISI:** *(shrugging)* Lagos people say sorry for everything.

**EMMA:** *(putting the phone down)* Hmm.

*A colleague calls from the door: "Emma! The client wants the dragon to be purple now!"*

**EMMA:** *(groaning)* Purple. Of course. Why not.

## SCENE 3 — TOO MUCH PEPPER
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — DINING ROOM — SAME NIGHT**
*(~1:42–2:20)*

*Night falls over Abuja. Inside the Adeyemi dining room, a crystal chandelier hangs over a long, polished table. The only sounds are cutlery against china and the ticking of a wall clock. Chloe sits at the far end, her back straight and her plate barely touched.*

*A long, polished table. Kunle eats in silence. Chloe watches every spoonful. The children eat quietly.*

**KUNLE:** *(putting his spoon down)* Chloe.

**CHLOE:** Yes, darling?

**KUNLE:** What is this?

**CHLOE:** Jollof rice. You said this morning—

**KUNLE:** I know what I said. I'm asking what this is. *(He pushes the plate an inch away.)* Too much pepper. Too much oil. Is it so difficult to follow one instruction?

**CHLOE:** I'm sorry. I'll make it again tomorrow. Less pepper.

**KUNLE:** You always say tomorrow.

*He stands and leaves the table. Ada looks at her mother's untouched plate.*

**ADA:** *(whispering)* Mummy, I liked it.

**CHLOE:** *(squeezing her hand)* Thank you, baby.

**TOBI:** *(mouth full)* Me too! Jollof is the best!

*Chloe laughs softly, then stops herself and looks toward the door Kunle went through.*

## SCENE 4 — THE MORNING INSPECTION
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — DINING ROOM / FRONT DOOR — MONDAY, 6:45 AM**
*(~2:20–3:06)*

*Monday dawn. Pale light spreads across the marble floor of the entrance hall. Kunle comes down the staircase in a crisp suit, buttoning his cuffs, and every footstep echoes. At the bottom, his family waits in a neat line, like staff before an inspection.*

*Kunle, dressed for work, checks his shirt cuffs. Chloe hands him a lunch box and his briefcase. Ada and Tobi, in school uniforms, stand in a line like soldiers.*

**KUNLE:** *(inspecting Ada's collar)* Who ironed this?

**CHLOE:** I did, darling.

**KUNLE:** It has a crease. *(to Ada)* Tell your mother that a crease on your collar is a crease on my name.

**ADA:** *(quietly)* Yes, Daddy.

**KUNLE:** *(holding out his hand)* The receipts from Saturday's market.

*Chloe hands him a small bundle of receipts. He flicks through them.*

**KUNLE:** Eight thousand naira for tomatoes?

**CHLOE:** Prices have gone up, darling. Everybody is complaining.

**KUNLE:** Everybody isn't spending my money. *(He pockets the receipts.)* Next time, bring the change and the receipt together. *(He kisses Tobi's head.)* Be good, champion.

*The door closes. Chloe exhales for the first time in ten minutes. Ada looks at her.*

**ADA:** Mummy, I'll iron my own collar from now on.

**CHLOE:** *(kneeling, fixing it)* No, baby. Your collar was perfect. *(beat)* It was perfect.

## SCENE 5 — "YOU SIMPLY HAVE BAD TASTE"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — BEDROOM / INT. EMMA'S DESK, LAGOS — INTERCUT — THE NEXT FRIDAY, 2:19 PM**
*(~3:06–3:51)*

*The next Friday. Afternoon sun falls through the bedroom curtains onto a bed covered in folded laundry. Chloe works in silence. In Lagos, Emma sits at a cluttered desk covered in sticky notes, a film sequence paused on her monitor.*

*Chloe folds laundry on the bed. Her phone lies beside her. She scrolls back to the stranger's message and reads it again. She hesitates, then types fast before she can change her mind.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Jollof rice was the wrong option. Next time try fried rice.

*In Lagos, Emma's phone buzzes on her desk. She grins.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** No. The person simply has bad taste 😂

**CHLOE (TEXT):** You don't know him.

**EMMA (TEXT):** True. But anyone who complains about good jollof is already suspicious.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** You're funny.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Only on Fridays. Monday to Thursday, I'm a very serious woman.

*Chloe laughs. Not a polite laugh. A real, unexpected, beautiful laugh. Ada appears in the doorway.*

**ADA:** Mummy! You're laughing!

**CHLOE:** *(surprised at herself)* Yes, baby. I suppose I am.

**ADA:** Why?

**CHLOE:** Someone said something funny.

**ADA:** Who?

**CHLOE:** *(looking at the phone)* Nobody, really. A stranger.

**ADA:** Can the stranger say something funny again tomorrow?

**CHLOE:** *(smiling)* Maybe next Friday.

## SCENE 6 — FRIDAY STRANGER
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — BEDROOM — LATE NIGHT**
*(~3:51–4:10)*

*Late night. The house is dark except for the blue glow of a phone screen. The air conditioner hums. Kunle sleeps on his back on the far side of a wide bed. Chloe sits on the very edge, as far from him as the mattress allows.*

*The children are asleep. Kunle snores on the far side of the bed. Chloe sits on the edge of the bed with her phone glowing in the dark. She opens the unknown number, taps "Add contact" and types a name: "FRIDAY STRANGER." She looks at it for a long time.*

**CHLOE:** *(whispering to herself)* What are you doing, Chloe?

*Kunle shifts in his sleep.*

**KUNLE:** *(mumbling)* Who are you talking to?

**CHLOE:** *(locking the phone instantly)* Nobody, darling. Go back to sleep.

*She lies down, holding the phone against her chest.*

## SCENE 7 — "WHO IS MAKING YOU SMILE?"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — SITTING ROOM — EVENING — WEEKS LATER**
*(~4:10–4:39)*

*Early evening, weeks later. Golden light comes through the tall windows of the sitting room. The television plays the news on low. Chloe curls up on the cream sofa with her feet tucked under her, a small, rare smile on her face. Behind her, the front door opens quietly.*

*Chloe sits on the sofa reading a message and smiling. She doesn't notice Kunle standing in the doorway, watching.*

**KUNLE:** Who is making you smile like that?

**CHLOE:** *(locking the phone, too quickly)* Nobody. It's… a recipe group. Women share recipes.

**KUNLE:** *(walking slowly toward her)* A recipe group. *(beat)* Then why don't your recipes improve?

*He holds out his hand for the phone. Chloe's heart pounds. Then his own phone rings: "CHIEF OKEKE." He looks at it, then at her.*

**KUNLE:** *(answering, suddenly warm)* Chief! Good evening, sir!

*He walks out of the room, laughing on the call. Chloe sits frozen with the phone against her chest.*

*She opens FRIDAY STRANGER.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** I have to go. Talk next Friday.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Everything okay?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Yes. Sorry.

*She deletes the conversation from her screen, then opens the contact and looks at the name: "FRIDAY STRANGER." She doesn't delete that.*

## SCENE 8 — RICE WOMAN
**MONTAGE — LAGOS AND ABUJA — FRIDAYS OVER THREE WEEKS**
*(~4:39–5:18)*

*A sequence of Fridays. The camera cuts between two cities: yellow danfo buses crawling through Lagos traffic, wide clean Abuja roads under blue sky, Emma striding into meetings, Chloe packing lunch boxes. Each Friday, a phone lights up.*

*FRIDAY ONE. Lagos traffic and Abuja sunshine. Emma in a meeting, Chloe packing school lunches.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Serious question. Is pineapple on pizza a crime?

**EMMA (TEXT):** An international offence. Who committed it?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** My daughter.

**EMMA (TEXT):** She's young. Rehabilitation is possible.

*FRIDAY TWO. Emma arguing with an editor over a film scene, then checking her phone and smiling.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** How was your week, Rice Woman?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Don't call me that 😂

**EMMA (TEXT):** Then tell me your name.

*Chloe hesitates. She looks around the empty kitchen as if someone might be watching.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Chloe.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Emma. Nice to meet you, Chloe of the disputed jollof.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Nice to meet you, Emma who gives unsolicited rice advice.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Solicited. You texted ME, madam.

## SCENE 9 — THE PORTFOLIO
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — BEDROOM / EMMA'S APARTMENT, LAGOS — INTERCUT — FRIDAY THREE, NIGHT**
*(~5:18–5:59)*

*Friday night. The Adeyemi house is asleep. A single bedside lamp throws a small circle of light on the carpet where Chloe sits cross-legged, her back against the wardrobe. In Lagos, Emma lies on a worn sofa in a small, cosy flat, eating cereal from the box.*

*Chloe sits on the bedroom floor in the dark. Emma lies on her sofa in Lagos, eating cereal for dinner.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Can I ask you something that isn't about food?

**EMMA (TEXT):** That would be a first. Go on.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Do you think people can forget who they used to be?

*Emma stops chewing. She sits up.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Yes. But I don't think the old person disappears completely.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** How do you find them again?

*A long pause. Emma thinks carefully.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Perhaps you start by remembering one thing that used to make you happy.

*Chloe looks up. On the top shelf of her wardrobe, behind old handbags, is a dusty folder. She stands on a chair and pulls it down. She blows off the dust. The first page reads: "CHLOE ADEYEMI — INTERIOR DESIGN PORTFOLIO." Sketches of beautiful rooms, colour palettes and photographs. Her own handwriting from another life. She runs her fingers over the letters.*

**CHLOE:** *(whispering)* Hello, you.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** I think I remember something.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Then that's something worth keeping.

*Chloe holds the portfolio against her chest like something she has rescued.*

## SCENE 10 — THE FIRST CALL
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — LAUNDRY ROOM / EMMA'S BALCONY, LAGOS — SATURDAY AFTERNOON**
*(~5:59–6:58)*

*Saturday afternoon. Kunle's car pulls out of the gate, golf clubs rattling in the boot. The house relaxes. In the laundry room at the back, the washing machine rumbles through its cycle. Chloe closes the door behind her and leans against it.*

*Kunle has gone to play golf. The washing machine rumbles. Chloe stands among piles of laundry with her phone.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Random question. Can I hear your voice? Just to prove you're not an AI. I work in an AI studio. I'm suspicious of everybody.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** 😂 Okay. Once. Quickly.

*Chloe presses call. It rings once.*

**EMMA:** *(laughing)* Hello? Is this the famous Rice Woman?

**CHLOE:** *(shy, giggling)* This is Chloe.

**EMMA:** Oh! You have a lovely voice! I was expecting someone much more… guilty about rice.

**CHLOE:** *(laughing)* And you sound exactly like your texts. Loud.

**EMMA:** Lagos made me loud. You can't survive Third Mainland Bridge whispering.

*They both laugh. Then a short silence.*

**CHLOE:** *(quietly)* It's strange. Hearing you. It makes you… real.

**EMMA:** *(softly)* I am real, Chloe. Every Friday. I'm a real person on the other side of this phone.

*The washing machine stops. Silence. Chloe glances at the door.*

**CHLOE:** I should go.

**EMMA:** Go. Same time next Friday?

**CHLOE:** Same time next Friday.

## ACT TWO — THE WOMAN BEHIND THE MESSAGES

## SCENE 11 — "SHE WOULD FORGET HER OWN HEAD"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — DINING ROOM — FRIDAY NIGHT — NINE MONTHS LATER**
*(~6:58–7:41)*

*Nine months later. A Friday-night dinner party. The dining room glows with candles, white lilies stand in tall vases, and every chair is filled. Three businessmen in agbada and their wives in shimmering lace laugh around the table. Chloe, in a simple dress, moves silently from guest to guest with serving dishes, refilling, clearing and smiling.*

*A dinner party. Kunle entertains three business associates and their wives. Chloe has cooked every dish, arranged the flowers and set every glass. She moves around the table serving.*

**GUEST:** Kunle, this egusi is excellent! Your wife should open a restaurant!

**KUNLE:** *(smiling warmly)* Please, don't encourage her. If you leave Chloe alone, she'd forget her own head. I have to supervise everything in this house.

*Everyone laughs. Chloe smiles too.*

**GUEST'S WIFE:** Chloe, didn't you study design? I remember your wedding. Those decorations!

**CHLOE:** *(brightening)* Yes! Interior design. I used to love—

**KUNLE:** *(cutting in smoothly)* That was a long time ago. Chloe's full-time job now is making sure I don't starve. *(Laughter.)* Darling, the dessert.

**CHLOE:** *(the light going out of her face)* Yes, darling.

## SCENE 12 — "I THINK I'M AFRAID OF HIM"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — BEDROOM — SAME NIGHT, LATER / INT. EMMA'S APARTMENT — INTERCUT**
*(~7:41–8:54)*

*Midnight. The last guest's car leaves through the gate. The candles have burnt down and the dirty plates are stacked in the kitchen. Upstairs in the bedroom, Kunle stands at the mirror, his reflection stern under the bright ceiling light, while Chloe hovers by the door.*

*The guests are gone. Kunle loosens his tie in front of the mirror.*

**KUNLE:** Why did you interrupt when I was explaining the contract to Chief Okeke?

**CHLOE:** I was only answering his wife's question—

**KUNLE:** You always have an explanation.

*Chloe looks down at the carpet.*

**CHLOE:** I'm sorry.

*He walks out. Chloe sits on the edge of the bed and opens FRIDAY STRANGER.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Emma, can I ask you something?

**EMMA (TEXT):** Anything.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Can someone make you feel useless without ever saying the word "useless"?

*In Lagos, Emma puts down her dinner.*

**EMMA:** *(quietly)* Oh, Chloe.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Yes. How someone treats you matters as much as the words they use.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** What if everyone thinks he's wonderful?

**EMMA (TEXT):** They're judging the person they see. You're living with the person you know.

*A long silence. Then Chloe types the words she has never said out loud.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** I think I'm afraid of my husband.

*Emma doesn't reply immediately. She stands up and paces her living room. Then she types.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Chloe, are you and the children safe right now?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Yes. He's asleep.

**EMMA (TEXT):** I'm glad. You don't have to decide anything tonight. But you shouldn't have to live in fear. Is there anyone in Abuja you trust enough to talk to?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** I used to have friends.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Perhaps one of them is still waiting to hear from you.

*Chloe scrolls through her contacts to a name: "HALIMA (UNI)." The last message between them is nearly three years old. Her thumb hovers over the call button. It hovers. It hovers.*

## SCENE 13 — "WHO IS SHE?"
**INT. AI STUDIO, LAGOS — EMMA'S DESK — NEXT MORNING**
*(~8:54–9:52)*

*Morning in Lagos. The studio is waking up: coffee machines hiss, monitors flicker on, and someone plays Afrobeats from a laptop. Emma sits slumped at her desk with dark circles under her eyes, staring at her phone. Bisi walks over carrying two paper cups.*

*Emma hasn't slept much. Bisi drops a coffee on her desk.*

**BISI:** You look like you edited all night.

**EMMA:** I didn't sleep. The rice woman.

**BISI:** The Abuja one? You're still texting her? It's been nine months, Emma.

**EMMA:** She told me last night she's afraid of her husband.

*Bisi slowly sits down.*

**BISI:** *(serious now)* Emma. Be careful. You don't know these people. You don't know her. You don't even know her surname.

**EMMA:** I know she says "sorry" more than anybody I've ever met. *(beat)* I know she laughs like she's not allowed to.

**BISI:** And what can you do from Lagos?

**EMMA:** *(quietly)* Listen. That's all. I can listen.

**BISI:** *(after a pause)* Then listen properly. And make sure she has real people there, not just a phone.

**EMMA:** *(nodding)* That's exactly what I told her.

## SCENE 14 — EMMA'S BIRTHDAY
**INT. EMMA'S APARTMENT, LAGOS — NIGHT — HER 36TH BIRTHDAY**
*(~9:52–10:38)*

*Night in Lagos. Emma's small apartment is quiet. Rain taps on the window. She has laid out one plate, one cupcake and one candle on a small round table. She sits down alone in front of them.*

*Emma sits alone at her small dining table with one cupcake and one candle. Her phone rings: "MUMMY."*

**MAMA EMMA (ON PHONE):** Happy birthday, my daughter! Thirty-six! *(beat)* Emma, when are you going to bring a husband home? Your cousin Funmi has three children now.

**EMMA:** *(sighing)* Mummy, I have a career.

**MAMA EMMA (ON PHONE):** A career won't hold your hand when you're old.

**EMMA:** *(quietly)* I know, Mummy.

*She hangs up. She lights the candle and looks at it. Her phone buzzes again: a voice note from FRIDAY. She plays it. Chloe's voice is singing softly, a little off-key, in a whisper.*

**CHLOE (VOICE NOTE):** *(whispering, singing)* Happy birthday to you… happy birthday, dear Emma… *(She giggles.)* Sorry, I have to whisper, the children are asleep. *(beat)* You remembered my daughter's birthday last month. I wasn't going to forget yours. *(beat)* Make a wish. A big one.

*Emma laughs out loud, alone in her flat, and wipes her eyes. She blows out the candle.*

**EMMA:** *(whispering to the empty room)* Thank you, Rice Woman.

## SCENE 15 — "PLEASE DON'T DISAPPEAR"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE / AI STUDIO, LAGOS — INTERCUT — FRIDAY — ONE YEAR AFTER THE FIRST MESSAGE**
*(~10:38–11:34)*

*One year after the first message. A Friday. In Lagos, Emma rushes from one glass-walled meeting room to the next with a laptop under her arm, her phone face down in her pocket. In Abuja, Chloe stands alone at the kitchen counter, staring at her phone, waiting.*

*Emma is buried in back-to-back meetings with her phone face down. In Abuja, Chloe waits at the kitchen counter.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Emma? You there?

*One hour passes.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Sorry. You're probably busy.

*Another hour. Chloe stares at the phone.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Forget I disturbed you.

*In Lagos, Emma finally comes out of a meeting and turns her phone over. Three messages. Her face falls.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Chloe! I've been in meetings all day. Is everything okay?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Yes. Sorry.

**EMMA (TEXT):** That's three apologies, and you haven't done anything wrong.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Habit, I suppose.

**EMMA (TEXT):** You don't need to apologise for wanting to talk to your friend.

*Chloe reads one word again and again: "friend."*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Are we friends?

**EMMA (TEXT):** After surviving almost a year of your questionable food opinions? I should think so.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Thank you.

**EMMA (TEXT):** For what?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** For not making me feel like a burden.

*Emma stares at her phone, deeply moved.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Chloe, friendship isn't a burden.

*A few minutes later:*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Same time next Friday?

**EMMA (TEXT):** As often as life allows. And if one of us is busy, it doesn't mean the friendship has disappeared.

*Chloe smiles. In her world, people who go quiet usually come back angry. This matters more than Emma will ever know.*

## SCENE 16 — A HOUSE WITHOUT WINDOWS
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — CHILDREN'S ROOM — AFTERNOON — YEAR TWO**
*(~11:34–12:12)*

*Year Two. A school bus stops outside the gate. Ada, now eight, runs up the driveway with her backpack bouncing and a rolled-up paper in her hand, and bursts into the children's room where Chloe is folding tiny school uniforms.*

*Ada (8) comes home from school and proudly hands Chloe a drawing: a beautiful house surrounded by flowers, a sun and a tree. But there are no windows.*

**CHLOE:** It's beautiful, sweetheart. *(beat)* But where are the windows?

**ADA:** I forgot them.

**CHLOE:** *(pointing to one room)* Even this room?

**ADA:** That's your room, Mummy.

**CHLOE:** My room?

**ADA:** The one where nobody disturbs you. *(beat)* So nobody can see in and shout.

*Chloe's breath catches. Her eight-year-old has drawn her a gift: a room where her mother can finally have some peace.*

**CHLOE:** *(pulling her close)* Thank you, my darling. It's the best room I've ever seen.

**ADA:** Mummy, when I grow up, I'll build you a real one.

**CHLOE:** *(whispering into her hair)* Maybe Mummy will build it first.

## SCENE 17 — "REMOVE THAT SORRY"
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — DINING TABLE — NIGHT / EMMA'S APARTMENT — INTERCUT**
*(~12:12–12:59)*

*Night. Everyone else is asleep. The dining-room light is on low. Chloe sits at the long table, where she usually serves, with her old portfolio open, pencils scattered around her, sketching with a concentration we haven't seen before.*

*Everyone is asleep. Chloe sits at the dining table with her old portfolio open, sketching a new house: light, air, big windows and doors that open onto a garden. Her phone buzzes.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Friday question. If money and fear weren't involved, what would you do tomorrow?

**CHLOE (TEXT):** I'd go back to designing homes.

**EMMA (TEXT):** Then tell me about one.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Open-plan. Warm lighting. Adire fabric on the cushions. A reading corner by a big window, so the morning comes in before anybody wakes up. And no locks on the inside doors, because nobody in that house would need to hide.

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Sorry 😂

**EMMA (TEXT):** Remove that sorry immediately.

## SCENE 18 — THE CALL TO HALIMA
**INT. ADEYEMI HOUSE — GARDEN — MORNING — YEAR THREE**

**EXT. WEDDING RECEPTION — CONTINUOUS**
*(~47:34–48:04)*

*The silence stretches. Every guest is looking around. Chloe stands at the microphone, her smile slowly fading.*

*Chloe's phone lies beside her bouquet. It lights up with a new message.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Still here ❤️

*Chloe gasps and looks up. At the back of the reception, a woman in an elegant emerald-green outfit slowly rises from her seat. Emma. She's smiling and crying.*

**EMMA:** *(calling out gently)* Chloe?

*Chloe puts a hand over her mouth.*

**CHLOE:** Emma?

**EMMA:** You still haven't learned how to cook the right rice.

*The entire reception erupts in laughter. Chloe laughs through her tears. She steps down from the platform, gathering her dress. Emma walks toward her. They stop, facing each other. Two women who have shared ten years of their lives, who know each other's fears, humour, hopes and hard days, and who have never been in the same room.*

**CHLOE:** You came.

**EMMA:** Of course I came.

*Chloe starts to cry.*

**CHLOE:** I don't even know what to say.

**EMMA:** Well, that's a first.

*They laugh and embrace. Chloe holds her tightly.*

**CHLOE:** Thank you.

**EMMA:** For what?

**CHLOE:** For answering.

**EMMA:** *(looking into her eyes)* Thank you for texting back.

## SCENE 56 — AUNTY FRIDAY
**EXT. WEDDING RECEPTION — CONTINUOUS**
*(~48:04–49:18)*

*Applause fills the garden. The two women hold each other in the middle of the aisle while guests wipe their eyes. Then small footsteps come running across the grass.*

*Ada comes forward and wraps her arms around Emma.*

**ADA:** You're Aunty Friday?

**EMMA:** *(laughing)* I suppose I am!

**ADA:** Mummy talks about you all the time. *(beat)* You're the reason she started laughing again. I was seven. I remember.

*Emma can't speak. Tobi joins the hug. For the first time, Emma sees the children whose school stories and birthdays she has followed for ten years.*

**TOBI:** You look exactly like your texts.

**EMMA:** *(laughing through tears)* What does that even mean?

**TOBI:** Funny.

*Tunde approaches, smiling.*

**TUNDE:** So you're the famous Emma.

**EMMA:** And you're the man who likes both kinds of rice?

**TUNDE:** Guilty.

**EMMA:** I think we're going to get along.

*The guests applaud. Chloe goes back to the microphone, still holding Emma's hand.*

**CHLOE:** I want to say one last thing. Sometimes, when you're going through something terrible, you start to believe nobody cares. That nobody sees you. That nobody would understand. *(beat)* But please don't give up on the possibility of kindness. Talk to someone you trust. Accept help. And if someone reaches out to you, don't underestimate what it means to listen.

**CHLOE:** Because ten years ago, one wrong digit sent my message to the wrong number. And somehow… *(She looks at Emma.)* …it reached exactly the person I needed.

*The guests stand and applaud. Emma wipes away her tears.*

## SCENE 57 — THE FIRST DANCE
**EXT. WEDDING RECEPTION — DANCE FLOOR — LATER THAT NIGHT**
*(~49:18–49:45)*

*Later that night. The band plays upbeat highlife and the dance floor is packed. Aunties are dancing, children are spinning, and Tunde is attempting a terrible shoki. At the edge of the floor, by the food table, two women stand side by side with full plates.*

*Music. Everyone dances. Emma and Chloe stand at the edge of the dance floor with plates of food, laughing.*

**EMMA:** *(looking at her plate)* Your caterer made jollof AND fried rice.

**CHLOE:** I couldn't decide.

**EMMA:** *(mock horror)* After ten years?

**CHLOE:** *(smiling)* No. I decided. I wanted both. *(beat)* And nobody in this garden is going to tell me that's wrong.

*Emma looks at her friend, really looks at her, then raises her plate like a toast.*

**EMMA:** To both.

**CHLOE:** To both.

*They clink plates. Tunde pulls Chloe onto the dance floor. She reaches back and drags Emma in too. Halima, Ada and Tobi join. Everyone dances under the lights.*

## SCENE 58 — JOLLOF WITH TURKEY
**INT. CHLOE AND TUNDE'S KITCHEN, ABUJA — FRIDAY, 2:19 PM — ONE WEEK AFTER THE WEDDING**
*(~49:45 — END)*

*One week after the wedding. Friday afternoon. Sunlight floods a bright kitchen with a huge window and plants on every sill, a room Chloe designed herself. Soft music plays. The back door is open, and laughter drifts in from the garden.*

*Chloe stands in the kitchen of the home she shares with Tunde and the children. Sunlight fills the room. Music plays softly. Everything is warm, relaxed and lived-in. Tunde walks in.*

**TUNDE:** Darling, what are we having for dinner?

**CHLOE:** I haven't decided.

**TUNDE:** Anything you make is fine with me.

*He kisses her cheek and leaves, humming. Chloe pauses. She remembers another kitchen. Another Friday. A woman trembling as she typed. She looks around her bright kitchen and smiles. Her phone buzzes.*

**EMMA (TEXT):** Happy Friday, Rice Woman! What's cooking? 😂

**CHLOE (TEXT):** I haven't decided. Jollof or fried?

**EMMA (TEXT):** Whatever YOU want, Chloe.

*Chloe stares at the words. Her smile widens.*

**CHLOE:** *(whispering)* Whatever I want.

*She looks out the window. Then she types.*

**CHLOE (TEXT):** Jollof rice with turkey, obviously ❤️

**EMMA (TEXT):** Finally. Ten years to learn 😂❤️

*Chloe laughs.*

**TITLE CARD:** *"Sometimes, the people who change our lives arrive without an introduction." One wrong number. One unexpected friendship. Ten years of showing up.*

**END.**
