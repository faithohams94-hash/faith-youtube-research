**You aren't monetized because the channel is about 0.1% of the way to the threshold. You haven't been penalized or rejected.** YouTube hasn't found an audience for these videos yet. Across 5 videos, you have about 154 views in total.

You won't reach 4,000 hours in 12 weeks from where you are, so **plan for 8,000.** That works out to roughly **120,000 views at ~4 minutes watched each, within 12 months**: about 10,000 views a month, against roughly 60 a month now.

The lower tier (fan funding and shopping, no ad revenue) needs 500 subscribers plus 3,000 hours. It's a realistic first milestone.

## What you're doing wrong

### 1. Changing formats before YouTube learns who you're for
Arc → arc → arc → ladder → ladder. Every switch makes YouTube start looking for a new audience from zero. A new channel needs **10–20 videos in one clear lane** before the algorithm learns who to show it to. "Every Level of Wealth" is also one of the most-copied formats on the platform, so you're competing with channels that have millions of views on the same title.

### 2. Thumbnails: busy scenes and hidden faces
- **The "YOU" plus red arrow** is on 3 of 5 thumbnails. On the family one, it points at the **back of your character's head**. The face, your biggest click driver, is turned away.
- **6–8 characters per thumbnail.** At phone size (where most viewers see it), they blur into noise. Two or three elements at most: one face, one emotion, one prop or number.
- **Text no one can read:** the paper in the hospital thumbnail and the phone message in the parents one. If text can't be read at 160×90 px, it's wasted.
- **Misery without a payoff:** the brother thumbnail shows him getting yelled at, which says "sad story". It doesn't show the 24% secret, which is the whole reason to click.
- The divorce thumbnail is the exception: a split frame, two big numbers, a clear before and after. **That's your template.**

### 3. Titles: too long, no twist, sloppy
- *"…from -$412 to $60 …"* gets cut off on screen, and "-$412" means nothing to a cold viewer.
- *"POV: You are 40 and You Still Work for your Younger Brother"* has no secret, no money, no twist. Its capitalization is also inconsistent ("You are", "your").
- *"Divorced at 55, had $0, then At 56, $1.4M"* has the right idea but reads like a draft. Try: **"POV: Divorced at 55 With $0. At 56, You Had $1.4 Million."**
- Small errors like these make a channel look low-effort, both to viewers and to YouTube's reviewers later.

### 4. Your character doesn't match your stories
The thumbnail says **AGE 55**, but the character looks about 20. The same chibi face plays 30, 40 and 55. People who click "divorced at 55" stories are mostly 40–65, and they don't see themselves. Keep the brand traits (glasses, deadpan look, black outfit), but **age him** per story: gray streaks, lines, a heavier build.

### 5. The first minute loses people
Your scripts reveal the premise **80–120 seconds in** (see `why-no-views.md` §1). By then most cold viewers are gone. With 15–25-minute videos, an early drop-off means almost no watch hours, so length can't save you.

### 6. Who is watching
The screenshot is from an account **subscribed to Somber Journal**. The red progress bars on two thumbnails show that account watched them, and there are about 13 YouTube tabs open. If most views and subscribers come from you, friends, or WhatsApp shares, those viewers aren't the real audience. They click and leave quickly, and YouTube learns the wrong targeting. **Stop sending videos to friends and family. Don't play them from other accounts.** The next 1,000 views need to be strangers.

### 7. The second gate: the monetization review
Even after you reach the numbers, a reviewer checks the channel against the **"inauthentic content"** policy (clarified July 2026). AI images, an AI voice, and repeated templates ("Every Level of Wealth", the same stock phrases in every script) are exactly what gets channels like this rejected.
- **Your advantage:** your scripts are genuinely original long-form writing. Protect that.
- **To pass review:** use a human voice (yours or a hired narrator), vary phrasing between videos, add real editing (sound design, music, on-screen numbers), and avoid uploading near-identical "levels" videos.

## What you're doing right
- A clear brand: name, banner promise ("The money stories nobody tells you"), a consistent character, and "new stories every week".
- Original, well-written scripts. This is the hardest part, and the thing AI-farm channels don't have.
- Organized production (prompt system, beat timings, consistent style).

The problem is **packaging, focus and the opening minute**, not effort or writing quality.

## The 90-day plan

**Format lane (pick one and stay in it for 12 videos):**
> *"POV: [Underestimated at age X, with a dollar amount] → [secret or comeback at age Y, with a dollar amount]"*
> Midlife and family underdogs with a hidden money twist. Examples: divorce at 55, the brother who owns 24%, the "failure" who paid off his parents' house.

**Every video:**
1. **Title:** a before-and-after with numbers, under 60 characters, clean grammar.
2. **Thumbnail:** a split frame, one face per side showing the right age, two big numbers, nothing else. Make 3 versions and run **Test & Compare** in Studio.
3. **Opening 15 seconds:** state the secret, the injustice and the payoff ("Everyone in this courtroom thinks you just lost everything. You didn't."). Put the full premise before 0:30.
4. **Length:** 10–13 minutes (~1,800–2,100 words) until retention data says people stay longer.
5. **Voice:** a human narrator, around 2.8 words per second.
6. **Shorts:** cut 2–3 vertical Shorts of 30–50 seconds from each video (the reveal moment), linked to the full video.
7. **Cadence:** 2 long videos a week if you can sustain it, or 1 at minimum, on the same days.

**Targets:**
- By day 30: one video above 1,000 views, CTR above 4%, more than 60% of viewers still watching at 0:30.
- By day 90: 100+ subscribers from strangers, around 500 views a month per video.
- After that, the 500-subscriber / 3,000-hour tier, then 1,000 / 8,000.
