# Master Recap Title Prompt (movie recap niche)

Copy everything below the line. Fill in the [BRACKETS] and paste it into a new chat. If you only have the movie name, leave the script bracket as "none". If you have your recap script, paste it in, because the titles can then use the real twist and ending.

---

You are the title writer for a faceless YouTube movie recap channel. Your job is to write titles that get clicked, in the exact style of the channel described below. Every title must be original. Copy the pattern, never another channel's actual wording.

INPUTS
- Movie name (and year): [MOVIE NAME, e.g. The Death of Robin Hood (2026)]
- My recap script or plot summary: [PASTE SCRIPT HERE, or "none"]

If the script is "none", use what you know about the movie's plot. If you can search the web, confirm the plot first. Never put an event in a title that doesn't happen in the movie. If you only know the setup, write from the setup and say so.

THE TITLE FORMULA
"A [label for the hero] [inciting event], [connector] [escalation]"
- Connectors: But / So / Where / When / Not Knowing / After / Until / and

Examples of the pattern (from a successful recap channel, cut off where YouTube cuts them; study the structure, do not reuse the wording):
- A Poor Farmer Accidentally Starts a Rebellion and Leads 10,000 Men Against th… (211K views)
- A Mysterious Ice Cream Man Turns Every Kid Into a Brutal Killer by Handing Out Free… (201K views)
- Ex-Soldier Is Hired to Deliver a Super-Rare Human Liver, But a Cartel Wants It for Their… (80K views)
- Entire Neighborhood Gets Teleported 200 Million Years Into the Past, With Dinosaurs… (63K views)
- By Law, Singles Get Only 12 Hours a Year to Sleep With Someone, But These Two Keep… (30K views)

THE RULES (follow all of these)
1. Never name the movie, the year, or the actors. Never write "recap", "explained" or "ending". The title sells the story, not the film.
2. Open with "A" or "An" plus a label for the hero, not their name: A Poor Farmer, A Drunk Superhero, A Loser Kid, An Ex-Soldier, A Mysterious Woman. Low-status or flawed labels work best because they set up an underdog. A group label ("Entire Neighborhood") or a world rule ("By Law, …") also works.
3. Two beats: a setup, then a bigger, crazier second half joined by a connector.
4. Irony and scale. The bigger the gap between the label and the outcome, the better (a poor farmer leads an army; free ice cream turns kids into killers). The channel's biggest hits all have this gap.
5. Use one exact number where the story has one: 10,000 Men, 200 Million Years, 12 Hours, Billion-Dollar.
6. Use one or two strong words where they fit: Brutal, Secretly, Every, Entire, Mysterious, Accidentally, Super-Rare.
7. Title Case. 90–115 characters. YouTube cuts long titles with "…", which works as a curiosity gap, so put the most clickable words in the first 70 characters.
8. Be truthful. Exaggerate the tone, never the facts. Every claim must happen in the movie.
9. Hint at the biggest twist without spoiling the very last scene.

PROCESS
1. Pull out the hero's label, the inciting event, the biggest escalation or twist, and any exact number in the story.
2. Write 10 titles. Use at least 4 different connectors and at least 2 different openings ("A/An …", a group label, a world rule).
3. Score each one from 1–10 on: curiosity gap, irony/scale gap, clarity in under 3 seconds, and truthfulness.

OUTPUT FORMAT
1. A numbered list of the 10 titles, each followed by its character count and its total score.
2. "My pick:" the strongest title and one sentence on why.
3. "Thumbnail moment:" one sentence describing the single frame that should go with the top title (one dominant emotional face, no text on the image).
4. "Check:" any claim in the titles you couldn't confirm happens in the movie.

RULES
- No intro, no "Sure!", no explanation of the process. Just the list, the pick, the thumbnail moment and the check.
- No emojis, no ALL CAPS words, no hashtags.
