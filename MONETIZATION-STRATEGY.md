# Paper Origins: Monetization Strategy

Starting point (Studio, Oct 29, 2026): **1 subscriber, 63 views and 1.4 watch hours in 28 days, 3 videos**, all on ancient humans.

| Video | Age | Views |
|---|---|---|
| How Did Ancient Humans Give (Birth)… | 8 days | 65 |
| What Did Ancient Humans Do (When Someone Died)… | 4.5 days | 19 |
| How Did Ancient Humans Sleep… | 13 hours | 6 |

The thresholds below come from creator-industry reporting. I couldn't open YouTube's own Help Center from the research environment, so check them in **YouTube Studio → Earn**.

---

## 1. The goal, honestly

| | Until Jan 31, 2027 | From Feb 1, 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Ad revenue (full YPP) | 1,000 subs + **4,000** watch hours (12 months) | 1,000 subs + **8,000** watch hours |
| Fan funding (memberships, Super Thanks) | 500 subs + 3,000 hours + 3 uploads in 90 days | reported unchanged |

Reports say an application that reaches YouTube **by Jan 31** is judged against the old 4,000-hour bar.

**What it takes:** 4,000 hours is about **50,000 views** on 12-minute videos when viewers watch 40%. You're at 63 views a month, so a steady climb won't get there in 13 weeks. It needs **one breakout video**. That's how channels in this niche actually grow: Ink Explainer's videos get 19K to 16M views each.

The good news is that one video does most of the work:

| Outcome by Jan 31 | Watch hours | Result |
|---|---|---|
| No breakout, slow growth | 200–800 | Apply for nothing yet. Keep going toward 8,000 hours (realistic by summer 2027). |
| One video reaches 25K–30K views | ~2,000–2,500 | Close. A second hit or a strong follow-up gets you to the fan-funding tier in Feb–Mar. |
| One video reaches 60K+ views | 4,000+ | Apply before Jan 31 and you're monetized in February. |

**So the whole strategy is: give yourself as many good chances at a breakout as possible before Jan 24, and double down the moment one hits.**

---

## 2. What your 3 videos tell us

1.4 hours from 63 views is about **80 seconds watched per view**. On a 10–15 minute video, most people are leaving in the first minute or two. That's the first thing to fix, and the new script prompt targets it.

Check these 3 numbers in **Studio → Analytics → (each video)** before uploading again. They show exactly where the problem is:

| Number (where to find it) | Healthy | If it's low, the problem is… |
|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** (Reach tab) | 1,000+ in the first week | YouTube hasn't tested the video yet. Normal for a new channel, so keep uploading. |
| **Impressions click-through rate** (Reach tab) | 4%+ | The thumbnail or the title. |
| **Average percentage viewed** (Engagement tab) | 35%+ | The script, especially the first 30 seconds. Open the retention graph and note the second where the line drops hardest. |

Other things to fix:

- **Sleep competes head-on with Ink Explainer's "What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Night?"** (100K views). YouTube will almost always send people to the bigger video. **Don't make topics they've already made.** Make the *next* question their viewers would ask (see section 4).
- **Your thumbnails copy Ink Explainer's "NO ___?" pattern.** The pattern works, but right now a viewer can't tell your channel from theirs. You have a ready-made difference: **the explorer in the pith helmet from your logo.** Put that character in every thumbnail and in every video as the guide, at least in the opening and ending. People subscribe to a character, not to a topic.
- **"WHO'S WATCHING?" thumbnail** is dark and the person is small. Thumbnails that get clicks show **one big face with a strong emotion, a bright background, and 2–3 large words.** Check that the text can be read at phone size, about 2 cm wide.
- **Birth and death topics** are fine for advertisers if visuals are not graphic: no blood, no visible wounds, no corpses in thumbnails. Keep it that way, or videos get limited ads once you're monetized.

## 3. The weekly system (Oct 30 – Jan 31)

### Uploads
- **2 long videos a week** (for example, Tuesday and Friday at the same time). Minimum 1 if production can't keep up, but never skip a week.
- Each one written with `MASTER-SCRIPT-PROMPT-ANCIENT.md`, at 1,900–2,200 words (12–14 minutes).
- **1 Short a day**, cut from the long videos: the single most surprising fact, 30–45 seconds, ending on an open question with the full video linked in "Related video". Shorts don't count toward watch hours, but they're the cheapest way to get the **1,000 subscribers**, and they're a breakout chance too.
- That's about **25 long videos and 90 Shorts by Jan 31**.

### Every video gets
- **3 thumbnails in Test & Compare** (Studio → video → Thumbnail → Test & Compare).
- A **full title under 60 characters** so the whole question shows on phones.
- An **end screen** pointing to your best-performing video, and every video added to a **playlist** by format (DAY / WHY / LADDER). When people watch the next video, those hours count too.
- A **pinned comment** that asks a question viewers will answer ("Would you have survived in this tribe? What job would you have had?"). Comments help the video get recommended.

### Every Monday: 15-minute review
1. For each video from the last 2 weeks, write down impressions, CTR and average percentage viewed.
2. CTR below 4% → change the thumbnail first, then the title. You can change both on old videos at any time.
3. Average percentage viewed below 35% → look at the retention graph. Rewrite that part of the next script (usually the opening).
4. Any video at **2× your channel median after 48 hours** → follow the breakout plan below.

### Breakout plan (when a video takes off)
- Within **7 days**, publish a follow-up on the same theme, with the same title pattern and a similar thumbnail ("What Did Ancient Humans Do When Someone Got Sick?" → "…When Someone Was Born Disabled?").
- Pin a comment on the hit linking the follow-up, and add the follow-up to the hit's end screen.
- Cut 3–5 Shorts from the hit.
- Pause any other plans. For the next 3 weeks, at least half of uploads should be related to the hit.

## 4. Topic strategy

Pick the topics that sit **next to** the big channels' biggest videos, not on top of them. When someone finishes Ink Explainer's "What Did Ancient Humans Do For Pleasure?" (277K views), YouTube looks for a related video to recommend. You want it to be yours.

| Their hit | Your next-question topic |
|---|---|
| …Actually Do All Day? (16M) | What Did Ancient Humans Do With Old People? / What Did Ancient Children Do All Day? |
| …Do For Pleasure? (277K) | How Did Ancient Humans Fall in Love? / What Did Ancient Humans Do for Fun? *(keep it advertiser-friendly)* |
| …Survive the Deadliest Predators (155K) | What Did Ancient Humans Fear the Most? / What Did Ancient Humans Do When Food Ran Out? |
| …All Night? (100K) | *(don't make a sleep video; it's covered)* What Did Ancient Humans Dream About? |
| Your own best (birth, 65 views) | What Did Ancient Humans Do When Someone Got Sick? / How Did Ancient Humans Fix Broken Bones? |

**Your own lane, which the big ancient channels don't do:** the money/status LADDER format. *Life at Every Level of Wealth in Ancient Rome*, *…in Ancient Egypt*. It's a known high-performing format (the "every level of wealth" videos) moved into the ancient world, and finance-adjacent topics pay more per view once you're monetized. **Make at least 2 LADDER videos in November** to test it.

Topic order for the next 8 uploads:
1. What Did Ancient Humans Do When Someone Got Sick? *(follows your best video)*
2. Life at Every Level of Wealth in Ancient Rome *(LADDER test)*
3. What Did Ancient Humans Do With Old People?
4. What Did Ancient Humans Do When Food Ran Out? *(winter is already covered by a big channel)*
5. Life at Every Level of Status in Ancient Egypt *(LADDER test 2)*
6. How Did Ancient Humans Fall in Love?
7. Why Did Ancient Humans Paint Inside Dark Caves? *(WHY test)*
8. Whichever of 1–7 did best → its follow-up

## 5. Checkpoints

| Date | Check | If you're behind |
|---|---|---|
| **Dec 1** (~8 more videos) | At least one video over **1,000 views**. CTR 4%+ and average percentage viewed 35%+ on the newest videos. | Packaging is the problem. Redesign thumbnails around the explorer character, and test a new title pattern. |
| **Jan 1** (~17 videos) | **300+ subscribers** and at least one video over **10,000 views**. | Move most uploads to whichever format (DAY / WHY / LADDER) has the best average percentage viewed. |
| **Jan 24** | 1,000 subs + 4,000 hours? | **Apply that day.** Don't wait for "safety". |
| **Jan 31** | Last day for the 4,000-hour bar. | If you have 500+ subs and 3,000+ hours, **apply for the fan-funding tier.** Otherwise continue to 8,000 hours, since the same system still works. |

## 6. Do this week (setup)

- [ ] **2-Step Verification** on the Google account, and **create and link an AdSense account** (Studio → Earn). This avoids weeks of delay later.
- [ ] Studio → Earn → turn on **"Notify me when I'm eligible"**.
- [ ] **Channel description and banner.** Say what viewers get: *"How ancient humans really lived, and what it says about your life today. New videos every Tuesday and Friday."*
- [ ] Make the 3 playlists (DAY / WHY / LADDER) and add the 3 existing videos.
- [ ] Test new thumbnails on the 3 existing videos (big explorer face, bright colors, 2–3 words).
- [ ] Write the next 2 scripts with the ancient prompt, check the SOURCES list, and record.

## 7. Don't

- **Don't buy views or subscribers, and don't do "sub for sub".** Fake viewers don't watch, which tells YouTube your videos are bad. YPP reviewers also reject channels for it.
- **Don't delete or hide videos with low views.** Their watch hours still count.
- **Don't copy Ink Explainer's wording, fact sequences or topics.** The inauthentic content policy demonetizes channels that look mass-produced or copied. Real sources on screen, your own character, and (ideally) **your own voice** are the best protection when YPP reviewers look at your channel.
