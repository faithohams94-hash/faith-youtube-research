A Poor Girl Helped a Billionaire CEO Grandpa Walk Again

A broke waitress named Ireti, whose name means "hope", has one year to raise ₦38 million to save her dying mother. She takes the job nobody survives: caretaker to a bitter billionaire who lost his legs, his family and his will to live. He insults her every day. She answers with love every day. Neither of them knows that their families met once before, sixteen years ago, inside a burning car.

The man who has everything has given up. The girl who has nothing refuses to.

Production rules for every scene

  • Establish. Open on scenery for 3–5 seconds so the viewer knows where we are before anyone speaks.
  • The scene. Clean, cinematic shots with the emotion clear on every face.
  • Aftermath. Show the consequence: the reaction, the silence, the decision.
  • Fade. Every scene ends on a fade, so cuts never feel abrupt.
  • Same face, every shot. Use one reference image per character (see the cast pages) and check hands, props and on-screen text before upload.

Establish

On screen · gold text over the shot

Inside, a huge bedroom. On a silver tray sits a small birthday cake with one unlit candle and a card: "HAPPY 75TH, DADDY — FROM ALL OF US." It's untouched. CHIEF BABATUNDE ADENUGA stands at the window in a white kaftan, leaning on an ivory-handled cane, holding a phone on video call. On screen is SEGUN, in a busy Abuja office, adjusting his tie.

SEGUN
Happy birthday, Daddy! Did the wine arrive? I sent the expensive one.
CHIEF
The wine arrived. (beat) My son did not.
SEGUN
(laughing it off) Daddy, please, not today.
CHIEF
When are you bringing the twins? I last saw them when they were seven. They're twelve now. I know them from photographs, Segun. Like strangers in a newspaper.
SEGUN
It's a bad month. The merger, the Senate committee…
CHIEF
Every month is a bad month.
SEGUN
(glancing off-screen; someone is calling him) Christmas. I promise. We'll all come at Christmas.
CHIEF
You promised last Christmas.
SEGUN
Daddy, they're calling me in. I love you. Bye-bye!

The screen freezes, then: "CALL ENDED — 1:12". Chief stares at it. His jaw tightens. His hand shakes. Then he hurls the phone onto the marble floor, and glass sprays everywhere.

NURSE GRACE rushes in, in navy scrubs.

GRACE
Sir! Sir, is everything okay?
CHIEF
(not looking at her) Leave my room.
GRACE
Sir, your blood pressure. Please, let me help you to the chair… (she gently takes his elbow)
CHIEF
(shoving her hard; she stumbles into the dresser) Don't touch me! Why are you here? Mm? Because I pay you! Everybody in this house is paid to smile at me. My children too. Paid with my money to forget me! (pointing his cane at her) Go home to your own father, if he's still alive, and see whether you'll even visit him.

Silence. Grace straightens up slowly with tears in her eyes.

GRACE
(quietly) My father is dead, sir.

Chief's face flickers with shame. He opens his mouth, but no apology comes out. Grace unpins her name badge, places it on the dresser and walks out.

AUNTY RONKE appears in the doorway with a silver tray: a glass of water and one small BLUE TABLET.

RONKE
Your evening tablet, sir. You've been shouting. It's for your pressure.

Chief swallows it without looking. Ronke watches him swallow, a beat too long. Then she closes the door slowly.

Chief stands alone, breathing hard. He looks at Grace's name badge. His face softens. He limps out onto the landing with his cane.

CHIEF
(calling down the stairs, almost gentle) Grace… Grace, come back. I didn't mean…

He reaches the top of the grand curved staircase, 22 marble steps. His POV starts to BLUR. The chandelier swims. The steps tilt.

CHIEF
(blinking hard) Grace…?

His cane slips on the marble. In slow motion he falls, tumbling down the staircase. The cane clatters down after him. He lands at the very bottom beneath the giant oil portrait of his late wife, MAMA ABIKE, smiling in gold gele.

Aftermath

Silence, except the rain. Chief lies still. His fingers twitch, reaching weakly toward the portrait. Upstairs, a door opens a crack, and we see half of Aunty Ronke's face looking down. She doesn't scream. She doesn't run. She waits.

FADE TO BLACK.

2. "HE WILL NOT WALK AGAIN."

INT. PRIVATE HOSPITAL, IKOYI VIP SUITE — NIGHT
Establish

A gleaming private hospital at night, with an ambulance's blue light flashing in the rain at the entrance.

Chief lies unconscious in a bed with tubes and a monitor beeping. FEMI ADENUGA bursts in, tie loose and two phones in his hand. GABRIEL is right behind him. DR. HASSAN BELLO holds up an X-ray.

FEMI
Doctor, talk to me. How bad?
DR. HASSAN
He fractured his hip and the fall bruised his lower spine. (He points at the X-ray.) There's severe swelling around the nerves. Mr. Adenuga, I'll be honest with you. From what I see tonight, your grandfather will not walk again.

Femi's phones slowly go quiet in his hands.

FEMI
There must be something. Germany. America. I'll fly in any specialist…
DR. HASSAN
You can fly him anywhere in the world, but the spine doesn't read passports. He'll need a wheelchair, full-time care and physiotherapy to keep him comfortable. We'll repeat the scans when the swelling goes down. Until then… prepare him.

Chief's eyes open slowly. He has heard everything. Femi sits on the bed and takes his grandfather's hand in both of his.

FEMI
Grandpa. I'm here. Everything will be fine. I promise you.

Chief looks at the hand holding his. Then, slowly and deliberately, he turns his face away to the wall. Femi is left holding a hand that doesn't hold back.

The door opens. MR. LEKAN BAMIDELE enters in a black agbada with a giant bouquet of white lilies.

LEKAN
Chief! Ah! God forbid bad thing! (He puts the flowers down and pats Chief's blanket.) Rest, Chief. Rest. Femi and I will carry the company. You don't need to worry about anything. Anything at all.

In the corner, holding Chief's overnight bag, stands AUNTY RONKE. Lekan's eyes meet hers. The smallest nod passes between them, and nobody else sees it.

Aftermath

In the corridor, Femi is on the phone, pacing.

FEMI
Dad, he fell down twenty-two steps. (beat) No, the doctor says he can't walk. (beat) You're not coming?
SEGUNV.O., on the phone
Hire the best caretaker in Lagos, Femi. Pay anything. Money is not the problem.

Femi lowers the phone.

FEMI
(to Gabriel, bitter) Money is the only thing in this family that's never the problem.

Gabriel puts a hand on his shoulder. Through the glass, Chief stares at the wall.

FADE.

3. ONE YEAR

INT. PUBLIC HOSPITAL, IKEJA FEMALE WARD — DAY
Establish

A crowded public hospital: a peeling green sign, women waiting on benches, a ceiling fan turning slowly in a long ward of twelve beds.

MAMA FUNMI lies in the last bed, thin and smiling. IRETI sits beside her in a faded yellow blouse, braiding her mother's hair. DAYO sits on the floor reading a JAMB past-question book.

DAYO
Sister, what's the past tense of "seek"?
IRETI
Sought.
DAYO
And the past tense of "money"?
IRETI
"Finished." (Mama Funmi laughs, then coughs. Ireti's smile drops.) Dayo, go and buy pure water. Two sachets. Take this. (She gives him a crumpled ₦100 note.)
DAYO
(standing) Two sachets of water for a hundred naira. This country.

Dayo leaves. Ireti takes her mother's hand and hums an old Yoruba lullaby. Mama Funmi closes her eyes.

DR. OKORO appears in the doorway with a brown file.

DR. OKORO
Miss Alade. Can I talk to you?

In the corridor, he closes the file before he speaks. That's how she knows.

DR. OKORO
The growth on your mother's heart is bigger than we thought. It's pressing on the valve. Without surgery… (beat) I'd give her one year. Maybe less.

Ireti's hand goes to the wall to steady herself.

IRETI
One year.
DR. OKORO
There's a hospital in Chennai, in India, that does this operation every week. Very good results. We can book it, the surgery, the flight, everything. But it costs about thirty-eight million naira.
IRETI
(a small, broken laugh) Thirty-eight million.
DR. OKORO
I'm sorry, my dear. There's nothing more we can do for her here.

He touches her shoulder and walks away. Ireti slides down the wall onto the floor and bursts into tears, covering her mouth so the ward won't hear.

Back inside, she wipes her face hard and fixes on a smile. Mama Funmi opens her eyes.

MAMA FUNMI
Why are you crying?
IRETI
I'm not crying, Mummy. Dust.
MAMA FUNMI
(quietly) Since when does dust enter a hospital?
IRETI
(fluffing her pillow) This hospital? Everything enters this hospital. Even goats.

Mama Funmi laughs. Ireti laughs with her, and behind her mother's head, her face crumples.

Aftermath

That night, alone on a hospital bench, Ireti writes in a small notebook:

On screen · her handwriting

"Surgery: ₦38,000,000. My salary: ₦40,000 a month. = 79 years."

She stares at it, then underlines: "Mummy has ONE."

FADE.

5. THE SWEET FOOD

INT. THE ALADE HOME, MUSHIN EVENING
Establish

A face-me-I-face-you compound at dusk: children playing barefoot, a woman frying puff-puff, a generator roaring.

Ireti pushes the door open, exhausted, and stops. Mama Funmi, discharged and wrapped in a shawl, is at the kerosene stove stirring a pot of jollof rice. Fried fish sizzles beside it.

IRETI
Ah! Mummy! What are you doing? You're supposed to be resting!
DAYO
(mouth full) Sister, sit down and taste it first. This food is SWEET.
IRETI
(not sitting) Where did the money for fish come from?

DAYO
(proudly) Me. I help people in my class fill their workbooks. Five hundred naira per book. I did twelve this week.
IRETI
(sharp) You did WHAT?
DAYO
It's just homework…
IRETI
Don't ever do that again! Do you hear me? You're writing JAMB in two months. Read YOUR books! Not other people's!
DAYO
(standing, hurt) Sister, I see how you're struggling. You walk home to save transport. You eat once a day. You think I'm blind?
IRETI
It's my responsibility, Dayo. Not yours.
DAYO
(quietly) No problem.
MAMA FUNMI
Ireti. Why are you shouting? At least thank your brother. He brought food into this house.
IRETI
(turning to her mother) Mummy, you know what the doctor said. You're not supposed to be standing at a hot stove!
MAMA FUNMI

DAYO
Mummy… what do you mean, "the time you have"?

(too quickly) Nothing. Ireti, go and bathe. Then come and join us. The rice is getting cold.

He's very, very rich. (beat) And money can't buy legs.

DAYO
Sister, with that money, I'd be sad in a jet.

MAMA FUNMI
(whispering) I heard the doctor. Hospital walls are thin.
IRETI
Mummy…

No.
MAMA FUNMI

No, Mummy. I'll find a way.
