# Episode 1 · Image and Video Prompts

**They Pitied Me for Bringing Leftovers to Work... Until I Bought the Building**

182 shots · **150 images (85% of runtime)** · **32 video clips (15% of runtime)** · cold open 58% video · runtime ≈ 15:06 at 2.6 words per second

## How to use this book

1. **Make the reference sheets first** (section 1) and attach them as character or style references to every shot where your generator allows it.
2. **IMAGE shots:** generate the image from its prompt. In the edit, give every still a slow zoom or pan so nothing on screen is ever frozen.
3. **VIDEO shots:** generate the image first (it's the start frame), then feed it to your image-to-video tool with the video prompt. Generate the clip length shown and trim it to the narration time.
4. **Timecodes** are calculated from the narration at 2.6 words per second. Once your voiceover is recorded, slide each shot to its line.
5. **ON SCREEN** items are text you add in CapCut on top of that shot. Section 2 has the design for each kind.
6. **Captions:** keep word-by-word captions like your previous videos, in the bottom 15% of the frame (every prompt keeps that area clear).

**Negative prompt (add to every image if your tool supports it):** no photorealism, no 3D render, no anime style, no extra fingers, no distorted hands, no garbled text, no watermark, no logo, no speech bubbles, no captions baked into the image

**Lighting rule:** the past is cold slate-blue fluorescent; the present is warm gold. The palette warms year by year as the money grows, so the color shift tells the story.

## Google Flow and Grok: uploading references

1. Make the 7 reference images first (section 1) and save them with the names shown: REF-1 to REF-5 for the characters, LOC-1 and LOC-2 for the two main locations.
2. For each shot, look at its UPLOAD FIRST line and attach exactly those images before you paste the prompt. In Google Flow, add them as ingredients or reference images; in Grok Imagine, upload them with the prompt.
3. Attach only the images listed. Extra references confuse the generator and can mix characters' faces.
4. IMAGE shots: generate, pick the best result, and download it with its shot number in the file name (e.g. shot-023.png) so the edit stays in order.
5. VIDEO shots: first make the start-frame image the same way. Then start an image-to-video generation (in Flow, the option that animates from a starting frame), use that image as the first frame and paste the video prompt.
6. If a face drifts off-model, regenerate with the reference re-attached rather than editing the prompt.

## Policy check (Google Flow and Grok)

- Every character is fictional. There are no real people, celebrities, politicians or public figures, and no children or teenagers.
- No violence, injuries, blood, weapons, nudity, sexual content, drugs, hate symbols or harassment. The tensest moments are emotional (a stamp, a stare, sweat, a choice).
- No brand names or logos in any image or video prompt. Cars and products are described generically (a luxury sedan, a compact sedan, unbranded delivery bags). The narration can still say the brand names.
- The one fire reference (the Episode 2 teaser) is only an orange glow through window blinds and a phone call. No one is shown in danger.
- Upload only images you created yourself (the REF and LOC sheets). Never upload photos of real people as references.
- No prompt imitates a named living artist, studio or franchise. 'Somber Journal style' refers to your own channel.
- If a generation is still blocked, it's usually one word: rephrase that line more neutrally and try again. Don't try to get around a block by spelling words differently.

_I checked every prompt against the content rules Google (Flow) and xAI (Grok) publish for image and video generation, as I understand them. Their filters change over time, so treat this as a strong first pass rather than a guarantee._

## 2. ON SCREEN text design

### Clock card
- **Look:** Top-left corner. White digital-clock font (CapCut: a monospace or LCD-style font), about 9% of frame height, thin dark drop shadow. Hold for the whole shot.
- **Animation:** Snap on with a 2-frame flicker, like a digital display waking up.
- **Sound:** A single soft clock tick, and a low heartbeat bass underneath the cold open.

### Stamp card
- **Look:** Center, slightly rotated 6°. Heavy condensed red letters (CapCut: Anton or Bebas Neue) inside a rough red rectangle border, like a rubber stamp. The folder image already shows the stamp; this card repeats it big for one beat.
- **Animation:** Slam in from 120% to 100% scale in 4 frames with a tiny camera shake.
- **Sound:** A heavy rubber-stamp thud.

### Year / net-worth card
- **Look:** Center-left. 'YEAR N' in gold heavy condensed caps (Anton or Bebas Neue); the net-worth line underneath in white, with the dollar figure in bright green. Behind the text, a dark-green rounded panel at 70% opacity.
- **Animation:** Year text slides up and fades in; the dollar figure counts up from $0 to its value over about 1 second.
- **Sound:** A soft whoosh, then a cash-register 'cha-ching' when the count lands.

### Money math card
- **Look:** Upper third, centered. White heavy condensed caps with a thick black outline; every dollar figure in bright green or gold. Split long lines at the ' · ' marks into two or three stacked lines.
- **Animation:** Each part pops in one after another (scale 80% to 100%), about 0.3 seconds apart.
- **Sound:** A light pop for each part and a coin clink on the final figure.

### Character stat card (Marcus)
- **Look:** Right side, like a video-game stat panel. Name in white caps; SALARY in white; NET WORTH in bold red with a small down-arrow. Thin red border.
- **Animation:** Slides in from the right; the net-worth number glitches briefly into place.
- **Sound:** A low error-buzz on the red number.

### Character stat card (you)
- **Look:** Left side, mirroring Marcus's card. Name in white caps; SALARY in white; INVESTING in bold green with a small up-arrow. Thin green border.
- **Animation:** Slides in from the left; the investing number ticks up.
- **Sound:** A clean, bright chime.

### Rules card
- **Look:** Use the shot's own image of the handwritten index card full screen, then add each rule as a typed white line on the right as it's mentioned, like the plane video's safety card. For rule 4 alone, put it center screen in large handwritten-style white text.
- **Animation:** Each rule types on letter by letter.
- **Sound:** A marker squeak per line; for rule 4, a single low piano note.

### Choice card (like the plane video's 'OPEN YOUR EYES / KEEP THEM SHUT')
- **Look:** Two pill-shaped buttons stacked in the lower-middle above the captions: EXTEND THE LEASE (white pill, dark text) and LET THEM GO (red pill, white text), with a small 'or' between them. Darken the image 25% behind them.
- **Animation:** Both buttons fade in, then pulse gently twice while the narrator weighs it; the chosen one (EXTEND) glows when he uncaps the pen.
- **Sound:** A ticking clock underneath, cutting to silence on the pen cap.

### End card
- **Look:** Center. 'EPISODE 2' in gold heavy caps, 'COMING SOON' in white below. Keep the late-night phone shot visible behind it.
- **Animation:** Fade in on the last beat and hold 3 seconds before the end screen.
- **Sound:** A low boom, then silence.

_Optional background image for the year and money cards: a minimal dark forest-green textured paper card with a faint gold border and soft vignette, empty center, flat 2D illustration style, 16:9, no text._
